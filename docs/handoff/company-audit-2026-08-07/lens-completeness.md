# What 235 Findings Could Not See: A Completeness Critique of the Muntin Audit

## Thesis
This audit is unusually accurate about the machine and structurally silent about the business. I independently re-checked ten findings against the code: six reproduced to the digit, two are right in substance but wrong in magnitude, and two headline claims are contradicted by their own cited evidence — an 80% substantive survival rate that is genuinely good, with both failures pointing in the sensational direction. But accuracy is not the problem. Across 235 findings and 15 domains there is not one finding about market attractiveness, willingness to pay, channel strategy, support capacity, insurance, or whether the food-cost number the product exists to print is accounting-correct. The sharpest evidence: ADR-020 — the decision to defer all revenue by twelve-plus months, the largest strategic act in the company's history — is 80 lines that contain the words "market," "competitor," "revenue," and "customer" exactly zero times, and no domain flagged that. Meanwhile the product repo already holds a dated 12-competitor market analysis naming two live dislocations (Hubdoc shut 2026-05-08; Toast bundling invoice scanning to ~120K restaurants) that appears in zero findings, and the storefront holds a strategy doc that named the company's biggest untested assumption and observed that "nobody proposed to test it" — still true after thirty agents. Finally, the audit is repo-bound in a way it never states: I could not resolve a hostname, read a traffic dashboard, or see a bank balance, and neither could it. Roughly six facts only Don possesses would move more of this audit's conclusions than any of the 118 critical-and-high findings it produced.

**Right in substance, wrong in magnitude (2).** The `-49.4%` produce driver: the audit says 69 pages; the real count is **69 EN + 69 ES = 138** (`grep -rl --include="*.html" -e "-49.4%"`). **Headline contradicted by its own evidence (2).** See §6.

**Calibrated confidence:** treat CONFIRMED file:line evidence as near-certain, treat every *count* as ±40%, and treat every *duration*, *rate*, or *aggregate business inference* as unverified until you check it. The auditors were rigorous where they read a file and loose where they summarized.

## The adversarial layer added findings; it almost never subtracted — and that is a design flaw, not an agent failure
1 REFUTED out of 235 is 0.43%. My own sample refuted 2 of 10 headlines. Even allowing that I sampled for checkability, the gap is roughly an order of magnitude.

The cause is structural. 82 of 235 findings (34.9%) carry verdict ADVERSARY — meaning the adversary's output was measured in *findings added*, not *findings killed*. An agent scored on what it discovers has no incentive to spend its budget disproving its partner. The one REFUTED finding (design-ux, "101 Cost Index pages draw no price history") was killed only because the adversary happened to find a *better* version of the same complaint (79 of 101 render a season curve; the real gap is the share card and the dark-mode chart contrast). Refutation happened as a side effect of a more interesting discovery, not as a goal.

The orchestrator's own pass proves the point: it personally refuted a whole class of phase-1 claims ("the deploy is red because of cost-index sync") in one command, and found that the 5-seat Team overcharge the adversary asserted was correct against a note elsewhere saying Team caps at 1 seat. One hour of hostile reproduction killed more bad claims than fifteen adversary agents.

**Consequence for phase 3:** do not treat the 118 critical+high list as a backlog. It is a *hypothesis set* with an ~80% base rate. A one-person company that tries to close 118 items will close none. The correct move is to pick the handful whose failure mode is irreversible, reproduce each personally, and delete the rest of the list rather than track it — because `orchestrator-verified.md` V8 already documents that the 2026-07-28 scan's findings are still live ten days later.

## The hole where the market should be — and the company already wrote the analysis the audit didn't
Zero of 235 findings address: market size, buyer willingness to pay, why restaurants churn on software, the purchase-decision calendar, or channel. I checked: `grep -oiE "willingness|price sensitivity|Hubdoc|pre-post anomaly"` over findings.json returns 0 for each; `\bTAM\b` returns 0 with word boundaries.

This is not because the material doesn't exist. **`Muntin-Invoice-Decoder/runbooks/qbo-xero-roadmap.md` (2026-05-25) is a real market document** — a 12-competitor capability matrix, an explicit white-space finding ("Pre-post anomaly intercept — **Nobody**"; "Vendor scorecard — **Nobody**"), the insight that the *bookkeeper* is the underserved recommender who "wins the account," and two dated dislocations: Hubdoc shut down 2026-05-08 leaving Xero restaurant operators actively shopping, and Toast launched AI Invoice Scanning bundled to ~120K US restaurants, with the window "measured in months." It appears in zero findings.

Likewise `potentially-profitable/docs/strategy/2026-07-28-legitimately-useful.md:122` frames the wedge precisely — "Between 'free arithmetic' and '$350 a month' there is nothing" — and at **line 213** names the company's biggest untested assumption: *"Market data actually moves a price for a one-location independent … That last one is the biggest unaudited assumption in the entire packet. It sits under every strategy's payoff and nobody proposed to test it."* Thirty agents later, still nobody proposed to test it.

**The sharpest instance.** `docs/editorial/decisions/ADR-020-…md` is 80 lines deferring all revenue to 2027. `grep -i "market|competitor|revenue|customer"` returns **zero matches**. The company deferred twelve-plus months of revenue, walked past both dislocations its own market doc dated, and extended an uncapped perpetual $19 price-lock by a year — on grounds that never once mention a buyer. Fifteen domains read that ADR and none said so.

## Four business functions with no findings at all
**1. Customer support and success does not exist as a function, and the site already sold it.** `apps/web/lib/copy.ts:3185` publishes "We read every message and reply within 24 hours, in your language"; `:3201` offers "Your phone (we will call within 24 hours)"; `:4023` promises security replies in five business days; `security/index.html` promises 24 hours "weekends included"; `.well-known/security.txt` says two business days; the product's `docs/security.txt` says three. The security domain caught the *inconsistency*. Nobody costed the *obligation*: bilingual 24-hour reply plus phone callback, from a full-time FOH manager. The ops-capacity domain's ~35 h/month maintenance figure contains zero support hours because there are zero customers — which means the arithmetic that already fails will fail worse the moment it succeeds. Ten customers at one ticket each per week is 40 tickets/month against a 24-hour published SLA.

**2. Accounting-domain correctness of the number the product exists to print.** The extraction engine is careful — `services/extract/engine_pkg/assemble.py:137` implements a credit-memo sign flip preserving `total == subtotal + tax + fees`, and `constants.py:304` sets a 10¢ tax-reconciliation hard block. But downstream, `apps/api/src/lib/inventory-purchases-store.ts:92-99` computes Purchases as `SUM(unit_price_cents * quantity)` over observations `WHERE pc.catalog_item_id IS NOT NULL`. Freight, fuel surcharge, delivery fees and tax are excluded; so is every SKU not yet canonicalized. That flows straight into `Beginning + Purchases − Ending = Usage` and into food-cost %. Meanwhile `inventory-reconcile.ts:128-134` reports coverage as `totalItems / countedItems / estimatedItems` — **item counts, never dollar coverage**. So the operator can be shown a food-cost % built on a materially incomplete purchases denominator, with a coverage badge that cannot detect it, and the number will not tie to what their bookkeeper calls food purchases. For a product whose promise is "it does not guess," that is a first-order correctness question and it drew zero findings.

**3. Insurance and personal financial exposure.** `docs/legal-counsel-kickoff.md:150-155` files cyber insurance as a *question for counsel*, noting the founder "will engage a broker (Embroker, Vouch, etc.)" — 88 days ago, and `grep -i insurance` over findings.json returns 0. More striking: `runbooks/v6-pivot-plan.md:70` states "revenue projection ~$109k ARR; Year-1 net burn **~$70-115k from founder equity**." I searched both repos: nothing derives either number. A solo founder is on record planning to put up to $115k of personal money into a venture whose demand is untested, with no entity confirmation (per the legal-ip domain), no insurance, and no LLC liability shield validated. The finance-runway domain audited a $164/month Fly bill and never touched the $115k.

**4. Channel.** The Accountant tier is priced at $150 + $30/seat and the company's own roadmap says the bookkeeper "wins the account." There is no accountant-channel plan, no distributor/POS partnership analysis, and no restaurant-association path in either repo. The audit noted the missing UI; nobody asked whether the cheapest customer-acquisition route for a person with no marketing budget is one accountant with eight restaurant clients.

## Where I would bet against this audit
**"Zero confirmed users after ~15 months of build" (customer-growth, CRITICAL).** The evidence block cites five sources for *zero users* — all solid — and **nothing at all** for *15 months*. `git log --reverse` on `Muntin-Invoice-Decoder` starts **2026-06-01**, 582 commits to 2026-07-31. The storefront checkout starts 2026-07-05 (shallow, 343 commits). The duration is an unsourced number in an audit of a company whose defining discipline is that every number must be registered, cited, or labeled illustrative. It matters because "15 months building something nobody wanted" and "2-6 months" produce opposite emotional responses in the reader.

**"No working analytics on either surface — CAC cannot be measured at all" (finance-runway).** Refuted by its own evidence, which states the storefront proxy forwards to plausible.io SaaS with no env dependency. That means storefront analytics *work*. The true finding is narrower and different: coverage is 21%, and `docs/sub-processors.md:69` tells customers Plausible is self-hosted on Fly and "no data leaves our Fly instance" — which is false for the storefront. That is a real disclosure defect. "CAC cannot be measured" is not.

**"Tacombi invoices … under an MIT grant" (legal-ip, CRITICAL #1).** The fixtures are real (`fresco_lineitems.json` etc., I confirmed Tacombi strings in 4 of 7 real_fixtures). But the ops-capacity adversary's own GitHub API read establishes `Muntin-Invoice-Decoder` is **private**. MIT grants rights to "any person obtaining a copy"; in a private repo, nobody has. The license is inert and the framing "can end the venture and the day job in one move" is overstated *today*. The live risk is one visibility toggle or one contractor invite away — serious, but a config risk, not a legal grant.

**Severity inflation on zero-counterparty claims.** Four CRITICALs sit on the ToS, the lifetime price lock, and unwritten terms. With no customers, no billing path and `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` unset, none of these is enforceable against him this month; they are pre-launch cleanup. Meanwhile the exposure that is live *right now* — 172 tracked files, **3.1 MB** of internal strategy in a **public** repo, including `docs/handoff/strategic-council-board.md` (161 KB), `board-archive.md` (141 KB) and `docs/editorial/the-hundred.md` (418 KB), readable by MarginEdge today — is rated merely HIGH. The severity ladder is calibrated to legal category, not to whether anything can actually happen.

**"No automation can clear the red deploy."** True for 5 of 8 gates; 3 self-heal in the deploy chain, and the orchestrator empirically moved 312/320 → 317/320 by running five builders. The fix is a documented sequence of about an hour, not an impasse.

## The question nobody asked: what is this for, and is stopping on the table?
Every one of the fifteen domains assumes the goal is a launched, paid, growing software company. The repos never say that. `runbooks/v6-pivot-plan.md:70` implies a venture posture ($109k ARR, $70-115k personal burn, $0 marketing). `ADR-020` implies a data-publisher posture with no revenue mechanism at all — `cost-index/methodology/index.html` explicitly forecloses sponsorship and paid placement. `docs/handoff/founder-vision.md` describes an emotional and craft ambition ('the same mechanism that built cult followings for Superhuman, Linear, Ramp') that reads like neither. These three documents describe three different companies, and no finding notes the conflict.

The strategic options the audit never enumerates, all of which a one-employed-person zero-revenue company should have on the table:

- **Sell or license the Cost Index dataset.** It is the only genuinely non-reproducible asset — 8,576 published revisions, a written record of what it cannot measure, a CC0/CC-BY split the legal domain calls better than most funded companies. It is currently free by policy, which forecloses the one asset with an obvious buyer (a trade publication, an association, a food-cost tool vendor who needs prices and will never publish their own error record).
- **Open-source the extraction engine and sell only the hosted seam.** The deterministic no-LLM engine is the technical differentiator and is already MIT'd by accident; doing it deliberately turns an exposure into distribution.
- **Partner rather than compete.** `runbooks/qbo-xero-roadmap.md` identifies displaced Hubdoc customers and a bookkeeper-shaped wedge — both are *channel* opportunities (an accounting firm, a Xero app-store listing) that need no consumer funnel, no billing UI on 1,368 pages, and no $19 price lock.
- **Stop the storefront.** 262 of 1,368 pages are noindex-frozen and still fully maintained, translated, gated and narrated. The audit treats that as waste to be pruned. The stronger version is that the whole 1,368-page corpus is a cost center with unmeasured return, and the honest test costs one afternoon in Search Console.
- **Stop entirely, or shrink deliberately to a lifestyle scope.** Nothing in this audit — or in the repos — establishes that a restaurant operator will pay for this. The founder has a full-time job, a Front-of-House career, and no external capital. "Keep the Cost Index as a public good, keep the day job, ship nothing paid" is a legitimate outcome that no domain priced.

What the audit did not ask, and what should be asked first: *what does Don want this to be in three years?* Every recommendation in 235 findings changes depending on the answer, and the answer is not in either repository.
