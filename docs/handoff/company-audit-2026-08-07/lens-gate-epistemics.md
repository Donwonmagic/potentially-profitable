# What Does Green Mean? — Gate Epistemics at Muntin, and the Redesign

## Thesis
I hand-classified 40 of the 169 gates and ran the full suite: 27 of 40 assert a SHAPE (a string is present, two committed files agree, a count matches a manifest), 9 assert GROUNDING and can fail, and 4 assert grounding but are wired so they cannot fail. Corroborating across all 169: zero of the 129 storefront gates ever perform network I/O, only 10 consult wall-clock time, and 56 of check-all's 320 entries are pure tautologies — the Cloudflare deploy runs the builder and then check-all asks the builder whether its own output matches. The sharpest structural finding is that the two meta-gates are each other's blind spot: check-gate-coverage enumerates `scripts/check-*.mjs`, check-idem-coverage enumerates builders already inside check-all, and neither can see the 33 builders on disk that ship a `--check` mode nobody runs. That hole is not theoretical — `build-cost-index-pages.mjs:43` says in its own header that it was written to be wired "into check-all in --check mode" and it is wired nowhere, which is why /about/ publishes "Eggs +71.7% as of 2026-07-21" while today's data says +148.2% as of 2026-08-04, and the Mon/Wed/Fri bot rebuilds the correct number and throws it away on every run. This is the company's own "a scanner is only as good as its root list" lesson recurring for a fourth time, inside the two gates written to enforce that lesson. The cost side is real too: check-all takes 186 seconds, 83 of its entries are idempotence checks, and 3 of the 8 reds a founder sees locally are phantoms the deploy heals — a 37.5% false-alarm rate on his only feedback loop. The prescription is to cut the tautologies out of check-all, close the meta-gate root list, and add five gates on the ways this business can actually be hurt that currently have zero coverage; net gate count goes down while coverage goes up.

## The classification: 40 gates read by hand, 67.5% shape
I read the assertion of 40 gates across both repos (not just the header — the comparison itself) and classified each as SHAPE (a file exists / a string is present / two committed artifacts agree / a count matches a manifest) or GROUNDING (the thing is actually current, actually reachable, actually executed, or actually true against something outside the repo).

GROUNDING, can fail (9): `check-tests.mjs` (spawns node:test, actually executes); `check-claim-usage.mjs` (a `used_in` slug must resolve to a file on disk — real reachability); `check-og-images.mjs` (every og:image href resolves to a file); `check-headers-license.mjs` (expands a `_headers` glob and reads each matched file's in-band license); `check-contrast.mjs` (computes real WCAG ratios from token values rather than asserting a token is present); product-side `check-coverage-floor.mjs` (reads output from a real pytest/vitest run), `check-typecheck-baseline.mjs` (runs tsc), `check-cost-index-snapshot-fresh.mjs` and `check-cost-pressure-snapshot-fresh.mjs` (Date.now() against a vendored asOf — and the first parses `STALE_AFTER_DAYS` out of the resolver so gate and code cannot drift, the single best-designed gate in either repo).

GROUNDING but defanged (4): `check-cost-index-series-freshness.mjs`, `check-cost-index-freshness.mjs`, and `check-pressure-freshness.mjs` are all listed in check-all as `(warn)` and run without `--check` (7 such entries total in scripts/check-all.mjs), and `check-competitor-claims.mjs` is the only gate in either repo that actually fetches a URL — its own header says "INFORMATIONAL ONLY. It never blocks CI." I ran the series gate: it prints "16 unexpected, 10 known-latent" frozen price feeds and exits 0.

SHAPE (27): the rest — `check-security-claims.mjs` (count of `class="security-claim"` blocks == length of a JSON array), `check-experiments-parity.mjs` (JSON keys appear as substrings in worker.js), `check-tool-verified-stamp.mjs` (a sentinel and a parseable ISO date exist — never that the date is recent), `check-counts-coherence.mjs`, `check-tokens-sync.mjs`, `check-locale-parity.mjs`, `check-image-dimensions.mjs`, `check-fabrications.mjs` (regex blocklist), `check-tool-no-fetch.mjs` and `check-sheet-no-fetch.mjs` (grep for the literal `fetch(` — they never execute the tool), product-side `check-view-rls.mjs` (SQL text contains `security_invoker = true`; no SQL ever runs), `check-funnel-no-pii.mjs` and `check-demo-no-persistence.mjs` (grep call-sites), and both `check-gate-coverage.mjs` implementations.

Ratio: 9 / 4 / 27 → 22.5% grounding that can fail, 10% grounding defanged, 67.5% shape. Mechanical corroboration across all 169: 0 of 129 storefront gates perform network I/O; 10 of 129 consult wall-clock; 3 of 40 product gates consult wall-clock; roughly 6 execute their subject. The sample's ratio is if anything generous — the ceiling on grounding across all 169 is about 12%.

## The tautology class: 56 gates that cannot fail by construction, 61 counting the refresh workflow
`wrangler.jsonc` `build.command` runs 76 builders and then runs `scripts/check-all.mjs`. check-all's 320 entries include 83 labelled `(idem)`, each invoking a builder with `--check` and asserting the on-disk output matches what the builder would produce. I intersected the two lists: **56 of the 83 idem builders are run by the deploy immediately before check-all checks them.** In the only context where check-all blocks anything, those 56 are a closed loop — run the builder, then ask the builder whether the builder's output is the builder's output. Zero information, 17.5% of the suite.

The same pattern is inside the automation. `.github/workflows/cost-index-refresh.yml:229-235` rebuilds five derived artifacts and then `--check`s the same five in the same step, under the comment "Every derived artifact rebuilt above must re-derive byte-identical." It cannot do otherwise.

The 27 idem entries the deploy does NOT run are the opposite problem and are exactly what `check-idem-coverage.mjs` was written for — 5 of today's 8 reds are in that set. Worth being precise about which reds are real: I ran the suite (312 of 320 pass, 186 seconds) and intersected the failures with the deploy's builder list. `inject-css-cache-bust`, `build-sitemap`, and `inject-glossary-article-schema` are phantom reds — the deploy runs those builders and heals them. `build-themes-review-board`, `build-theme-story-pages`, `build-cuisine-landing-pages`, `build-ingredient-state-record`, and `check-cost-index-picker` are real: **the production deploy is red right now.** I reproduced the picker failure and its cause — running `build-cost-index-picker.mjs` produces a real 3-line diff in `data/cost-index-picker.js`, and `cost-index-refresh.yml:302-311` omits that file from `git add` while line 313's `git checkout -- .` deletes it.

Tautology is not the same as worthless — an idem check does catch "an input changed and nobody re-ran the builder" when run BEFORE the builders. The defect is running them after. Move the 56 to a pre-merge job that does not run builders and they become informative; leave them in the deploy and they are 56 lines of green that mean nothing.

## The unreached class: the two meta-gates are each other's blind spot
`check-gate-coverage.mjs:194` enumerates `readdirSync(scripts/)` filtered to `check-*` — its own comment at line 145 says "only check-* scripts count." `check-idem-coverage.mjs` enumerates the `(idem)` entries already present in check-all. Neither root list contains *builders on disk that support `--check` and are not in check-all*. I enumerated that set: **33 builders**, and both meta-gates report green.

Split by consequence:
- **5 are run by a live workflow with nothing verifying their output**: `build-cost-index-pages.mjs`, `build-ingredient-yield-pages.mjs`, `build-cost-index-archive.mjs`, `build-blog-index.mjs`, `inject-about-cost-read.mjs`. These build the Cost Index (200 pages), the yield library, the citation archive, and /about/.
- **15 are run by nothing and checked by nothing**: including `inject-glossary-autolinks.mjs`, `build-llms-full.mjs`, `inject-glossary-sitemap.mjs`, `stamp-release-gate.mjs`.

The consequence is live and I reproduced it. I ran the 35 builders `cost-index-refresh.yml` runs, on a clean tree, and diffed. Seven files change that the workflow's `git add` allowlist does not cover: `about/index.html`, `es/about/index.html`, `open/index.html`, `es/open/index.html`, `open/seasonality/index.html`, `es/open/seasonality/index.html`, and `data/cost-index-picker.js`. The `/about/` diff is not cosmetic:

```
-<span class="ar-mover__nm">Eggs</span>… ▲ +71.7% above baseline
+<span class="ar-mover__nm">Eggs</span>… ▲ +148.2% above baseline
-<span class="ar-state ci-asof" data-asof="2026-07-21">as of 2026-07-21</span>
+<span class="ar-state ci-asof" data-asof="2026-08-04">as of 2026-08-04</span>
```

The founder's own "numbers I check on my shifts" page publishes an egg movement off by a factor of two, 17 days stale, on a cost-intelligence company's About page. The bot computes the right number three times a week and discards it. `/open/index.html` is a 357-line diff. No gate covers any of it, because `build-cost-index-pages.mjs:43` — which says in its own header it was written for "check-all in --check mode" — is invisible to both meta-gates.

A second unreached example, product-side: `apps/api/src/lib/stripe-tiers.ts:119` exports `monthlyTotalCents()`, the one function in the codebase that prices a tier correctly. I grepped every `.ts` outside tests: **zero callers.** `apps/api/src/routes/billing.ts:174-175` builds the Stripe line item by hand as base-price × seats. `check-pricing-consistency.mjs:305` does read billing.ts — but only to look for price-ID env names. It exits 0 and prints "solo $25 / team $60 / accountant $150 consistent across stripe-tiers and the copy bundles" while a 5-seat Team checkout bills $300.

## Coverage holes by consequence: six ways to be hurt, five with zero gate
Inverting the question — here is how this business actually gets hurt, and whether anything would catch it.

**1. A published number that is false because the data moved. ZERO COVERAGE.** `blog/cost-index-2026-07/index.html` carries 164 `<!-- src: ... -->` annotations naming live mutable keys. Line 632 publishes "refuses a posture on 73 of 100" annotated `<!-- src: cost-lockfloat.json counts.withhold/catalog -->`; `data/cost-lockfloat.json` today reads `{lock:37, cushion:19, float:7, withhold:37}` — the published figure is wrong by a factor of two, in the paragraph whose entire subject is honest refusal. That file has 14 commits since 2026-07-01. No gate anywhere greps for `<!-- src:`. Meanwhile `<!-- count:KEY -->` sentinels ARE enforced (`check-counts-coherence.mjs:72`). The company built enforcement for the low-stakes sentinel class and none for the high-stakes one.

**2. A generated page publishing a stale number. ZERO COVERAGE.** See /about/ above.

**3. An overcharge at checkout. ZERO COVERAGE.** `check-pricing-consistency.mjs` validates the declared-price registry against the copy strings and prints a standing "NOT BILLABLE TODAY — founding ($19/per_location)" line — genuinely good work — but never evaluates the arithmetic the checkout route performs.

**4. A dead product CTA. ZERO COVERAGE.** `https://ledger.muntin.digital/` appears on **405 storefront HTML pages** (injected by `scripts/inject-ledger-cta.mjs:51`). The product repo routes `app.muntin.digital/*` (`apps/web/wrangler.jsonc:28`); `ledger.muntin.digital` appears in the product repo only inside runbooks, never in a route pattern. Seventeen storefront gates call `existsSync` on an href, all for local paths; none checks a cross-repo hostname. `check-cross-repo-seams.mjs` exists on the product side and enforces `?source=ledger` attribution and a shared `@id` — it does not enforce that the hostname resolves.

**5. A stale trust artifact. ZERO COVERAGE.** `docs/warrant-canary.md:3` is dated 2026-05-10 — 89 days, on a monthly cadence — and no `check-*.mjs` in either repo greps for "canary." `llms.txt:25` still tells every AI crawler the Index "publishes weekly wholesale reference prices"; the founder made it monthly on 2026-07-09 and no gate reads llms.txt's prose. `check-tool-verified-stamp.mjs` proves a "last verified" date is *parseable*, never that it is *recent* — a stamp gate that cannot detect staleness.

**6. A tenant leak. PARTIAL.** `check-view-rls.mjs` covers views from migration 0039 forward and is well-reasoned, but its subject is SQL *text* and no production SQL executes in CI. Nothing enumerates org-scoped tables and demands a policy, which is why the `documents` table still has none 42 migrations after 0015 deferred it.

**7. A dormant cross-repo dependency. COVERED — and this is the model.** `check-cost-index-snapshot-fresh.mjs` exists precisely because this failure already happened; it parses the resolver's own constant so gate and code cannot drift. I ran it: OK, freshest 10d — that phase-1 finding is now refuted, the founder fixed it. Its sibling `check-cost-pressure-snapshot-fresh.mjs` exits 1 right now. This is what a grounding gate looks like; there should be five more and 56 fewer tautologies.

## The cost side: 169 gates is now a liability, and the alarm fatigue is quantifiable
check-all spawns 320 node processes and takes **186 seconds** on every deploy. 83 of those entries are idempotence checks and 56 of the 83 are provably uninformative in that context.

The false-alarm rate is measurable. A founder running `node scripts/check-all.mjs` locally today sees 8 reds; **3 of them (37.5%) are phantoms the deploy will heal.** That is the mechanism by which "known (idem) noise, never chase them" became a filing category — `check-idem-coverage.mjs`'s own header records that themes-review-board, theme-story-pages, and cuisine-landing-pages sat under exactly that label for months. All three are red again today. The gate written to stop that recurrence documents it correctly and it recurred anyway, because the gate reports that the *registry* is complete, not that the *reds* are cleared.

Waiver inventory across the 129 storefront gates: 15 `MANUAL` entries, 14 `UNWIRED`, 12 `ALLOW`, 9 `HISTORICAL_WAIVERS`, 9 `KNOWN_SOURCE_LATENT`, 6 `BASELINE_COUNT`, 6 `ALLOWLIST`, 5 `DEDUP_ALLOW`, 5 `BASELINE_ALLOW_NONIDEM`, 3 `BASELINE_DENYLIST`, plus a `.typecheck-baseline.json` on the product side. Two of the four storefront `UNWIRED` entries are documented-failing (`check-css-drift.mjs` — "FAILS — 505 drift issues"; `check-reply-time-canon.mjs`). Credit where due: that registry is genuinely well-run — each entry carries a date, a status, and a reason, and the rule "do not resolve a failing unwired gate by wiring it" is correct. The problem is not dishonesty, it is volume: 21 MANUAL idem builders are 21 standing human obligations for one employed person, and `check-idem-coverage.mjs` itself names `inject-knit-rail.mjs` as the highest-churn one.

Naming matters more than it looks. `check-staleness-honesty.mjs` prints "0 overdue · every published label honors the staleness ceiling" while 16 price feeds are 58–67 days frozen. That is not a bug: `tools/_shared/cost-staleness.js:10-14` deliberately compares two frozen dates and "never against 'today'," deferring the current-ness question to the freshness heartbeat. But the heartbeat is `(warn)` in check-all and the series gate exits 0 everywhere, including at `cost-index-refresh.yml:225`. Each link in the chain correctly disclaims the question and the last link cannot fail — so a gate named *staleness honesty* is structurally incapable of detecting staleness, and its green line is what a reader of the log sees. To be fair to the data: I checked the 11 series past 45 days and every one publishes at `directional`, the lowest confidence tier. The data is honest even where the gate is not.

One more instance of the same coupling: `check-cost-index-dispatch-fresh.mjs` — which CLAUDE.md calls "the intended reminder" to write each month's edition — reports "edition of 2026-07-09, data asOf 2026-08-05 (27d lag, ≤ 38)" and exits 0. It measures dispatch-date against *data asOf*, not against today. August's edition is 7 days late by the calendar; the gate compares two committed files and will never notice, and if the data feed stalls the publication alarm goes green permanently.

## Where the storefront's enforcement actually lives — and why it is the wrong place
All 129 storefront gates run in exactly one place: inside `wrangler.jsonc` `build.command`, at deploy time. The three PR-triggered workflows — `lighthouse-ci.yml:41`, `playwright.yml:48`, `window-a11y.yml:56` — are all `continue-on-error: true`. So no gate can block a merge; a violation does not get blocked, it gets the entire site frozen after the fact. Today that is not hypothetical: the deploy is red on 5 checks, and 4 of them need a human to run a builder and commit.

This interacts badly with the tautology finding. The 56 self-healing idem checks are the ones that DO run in the enforcing position (the deploy, after their builders); the 27 that can genuinely go red are precisely the ones with no healer. The deploy gate's composition is inverted: the entries that cannot fail always run in the blocking slot, and the entries that can fail can only be cleared by hand.

The product repo is materially healthier — 22 real blocking CI jobs in `ci.yml` (privacy-gates, db-verify, determinism-gate, no-llm-import-gate, coverage-floor, apps-api-tests, template-tenant-isolation-gate), an `UNWIRED` registry that is empty, and `check-gate-coverage.mjs` running first in the privacy job. But it inherits the same blind spot in a different form: its gate-coverage root list is also `check-*` only, and its CI is red today on `check-cost-pressure-snapshot-fresh.mjs` (exit 1, 18 days old against a 21-day cliff), whose fix requires both repos on the founder's laptop.
