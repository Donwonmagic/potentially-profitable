# How Muntin Dies: A Pre-Mortem from December 2027

## Thesis
Muntin does not die of any of the 235 defects in this audit. It dies of a ratchet: a maintenance obligation that only grows — 320 storefront gates, 1,368 pages, 21 manual builders, 8 public cadences, ~35 founder-hours/month — against a capacity fixed at post-shift hours and about to shrink for coursework. ADR-020 then removed the only deadline the company had, and nothing was ever built that could show the founder a single human being who wants this: `listFounding()` has zero callers, every founding lead is stamped with a retired product line's name, 775 of 777 subscribe forms produce a source the monthly broadcast filter excludes, and 79% of pages emit no analytics. The death is quiet and it has already started: the storefront deploy exits 1 right now on a machine-caused failure hand-cleared twice without fixing the pattern; the board records PR #536 merged "with three reds the founder accepted"; and the monthly dispatch promised on 83 pages as "the first Tuesday of each month" did not appear on 2026-08-04. The honesty brand is the second casualty, not the first, and it does not detonate in public — nobody is reading. It rots privately, where the founder is the only witness, and that is exactly what makes it feed the abandonment. Every other kill path here is a rider on those two.

## The ranking: what actually kills it, by probability × lethality
I rank seven paths. The top two are not defects at all; they are rates of change.

**KP1 — The maintenance ratchet → red-normalization → the instrument stops being trusted.** P: very high. Lethality: total. *Already in progress.*

**KP2 — Quiet abandonment for want of any human signal.** P: very high (the solo-venture base rate, and ADR-020 removed the forcing function). Lethality: total. *Leading indicators firing now.*

**KP3 — The data line retires itself on a dated schedule.** P: high. Lethality: high but survivable. *Fires 2026-09-08, then mass-fires 2026-09-29.*

**KP4 — The deploy is a hard stop and the bot re-creates it every Mon/Wed/Fri.** P: certain (true at this moment). Lethality: medium. *Fixable in one hour.*

**KP5 — The founding-cohort obligation.** P: medium. Lethality: medium-high, and it is the ONE path where the honesty brand dies loudly rather than quietly. *Worsens daily.*

**KP6 — The Stripe overcharge surviving to launch.** P of damage today: near zero. P of surviving to launch: high, because the gate written to catch it is blind to it. Lethality if it fires: high.

**KP7 — Key-person catastrophe.** P: low per year. Lethality: total and *irreversible*. Cheap insurance, but do not let it displace KP1/KP2.

The crossing point of KP1 and KP2 is the death. Everything else is a rider.

## KP1 — The ratchet: the defense machinery is the largest recurring liability
**Trigger:** every gate, page and cadence this company adds is permanent. Nothing in either repo retires one.

**The arithmetic.** `scripts/check-all.mjs` now runs **320 checks** (I ran it: `312 of 320 passed`, exit 1). `check-idem-coverage.mjs` verifies 83 builders, **21 of them manual** — 8 requiring the founder's Mac and API keys the container does not have. Eight public recurring cadences, **four currently overdue**: the warrant canary is 88 days and three cycles past its own stated monthly signing (`Muntin-Invoice-Decoder/docs/warrant-canary.md:3`), the Q2 transparency report is 36 days past its published date, the changelog is 40 days stale while `methods/index.html:470` tells readers changes show up there, and the August dispatch is missing. Phase-1 built the maintenance total from these registries, not from the air: **~35 h/month, none of it revenue-producing.** Against that: a full-time FOH manager at a two-location brand, with **34% of product commits and 25% of storefront commits landing between 00:00 and 05:00**. That is not spare capacity; that is sleep debt. The board records the founder is starting a college course.

**The chain.** Obligations grow monotonically → capacity is fixed then shrinks → maintenance crosses 100% of discretionary hours → the founder starts accepting reds. That last step is not a forecast. `docs/handoff/strategic-council-board.md` already records it: *"#536 merged with three reds the founder accepted."*

**Point of no return:** not the first false published number. It is the moment red becomes the normal state of the board, because after that the 320 gates stop being an instrument and become wallpaper — and this company's entire epistemology is that the instrument is trustworthy. That moment is roughly now.

**Cheapest prevention:** a declared maintenance budget in hours, and retire to it. Concretely: drop the two cadences nobody can serve (the canary was never published anywhere — `grep -ril canary` across 1,368 storefront pages returns nothing — so deleting it costs zero trust); stop maintaining the 262 noindex-frozen pages still translated, still gated, still carrying audio and earning nothing; and refuse to merge a new gate without naming the gate it replaces. One founder decision, one afternoon.

## KP2 — Quiet abandonment: the company cannot show its founder one human being
This is the likeliest single cause of death and it is almost entirely a *measurement* failure, which makes it the cheapest to fix.

**The chain.** ADR-020 (2026-07-30) moved the paid launch to "sometime in 2027, date deliberately unset" — the honest call, but it deleted the only deadline. What remains as motivation is the work itself, and the work returns nothing legible:

- **The funnel has no mouth.** I counted it: **4 files** on the entire storefront carry `action="/api/waitlist"` (index.html, es/index.html, ledger/index.html, es/ledger/index.html). Zero across 142 cost-index pages, 221 library, 172 glossary, 40 sheets, 8 tools, 20 blog.
- **The audience that exists cannot be reached.** 777 pages carry `action="/api/subscribe"`. The monthly broadcast at `src/worker.js:8793` sends only where `sub.source === 'cost-index'`. The live source values are: **613 `footer`, 162 `cost-index-ingredient`, 2 `cost-index`.** The dispatch reaches two pages' worth of subscribers — while **83 Cost Index pages promise in their own footer** "the monthly read — the first Tuesday of each month." The promise and the send are one string apart, and no gate compares them.
- **The leads that arrive are mislabeled.** `src/worker.js:8921` clamps `source` to a four-value enum from the *retired web-studio line*; the live forms send `home-founding` and `ledger-pricing`, neither in it. 100% of founding leads land in the product DB as `source='studio'`.
- **The cohort cannot be counted.** `waitlist-store.ts` implements `listFounding()` — "operator pull at launch" — and grep returns its declaration, its two implementations, and **nothing else**. Migration `0030` even built the index for it.
- **/try exists and is invisible.** The live "read your own invoice" surface with founding capture at the moment of delight has **zero inbound links** from 1,368 pages.

**Point of no return:** the first missed obligation that produces no consequence. 2026-08-04 came and went; nothing happened, and nothing will. A 7-day gap (the product repo's current state — last commit 2026-07-31) becomes 6 weeks becomes never. Compounding it: `potentially-profitable` is a **public** repo, and GitHub disables scheduled workflows in public repos after 60 days of inactivity — so the cron keeping the data alive is switched off by exactly the absence the two alarm workflows were written to detect.

**Cheapest prevention:** roughly three hours of string-level work — align the subscribe source with the broadcast filter, add the two live values to both waitlist allowlists, wire `listFounding()` to one admin read, link /try from the Cost Index hub. After that, a number goes up when the founder writes something. Today no number does.

## KP3 — The data line retires itself on 2026-09-08 and 2026-09-29
This is the only kill path with a calendar date, and I computed it from the repo rather than trusting the board.

Running `node scripts/check-cost-index-series-freshness.mjs`: **74 of 100 fresh, 16 unexpected-frozen, 10 known-latent.** NOAA has been dark since 2026-06-01 — **67 days** — across 15 seafood series, keyless, so no secret rotation surfaces it. `scripts/fetch-cost-index-sources.mjs:126-142` records the diagnosis ("the NOAA family was 0-for-15 while AMS was 74-for-74") and the reporting fix landed 2026-07-30; **the outage itself remains undiagnosed.**

The 120-day builder cliff converts a frozen series into a terminal "this series stopped publishing" page. Adding the gate's own remaining-days to today:

- **2026-09-08** — yellow-squash, the first terminal page ever rendered
- **2026-09-12** — cherry-tomato
- **2026-09-16** — collard-greens, napa-cabbage
- **2026-09-29** — **seventeen series at once**, including shrimp (w 0.04), vegetable-oil (w 0.12), salmon-fillet (w 0.03) — **0.19 of 1.07 declared weight, ~18% of the basket**

ADR-021's terminal render has never been exercised on a real feed (`data/cost-index-retired.json` holds only `_doc`/`_lastReviewed`/`retired` scaffolding). So the first production exercise of an untested path is seventeen simultaneous page transitions on the asset ADR-020 bet the company on.

The cross-repo bleed is already live and unnamed by either CI: `packages/cost-alerts/data/ingredient-aliases.json` declares 32 market-prior slugs; `apps/api/src/data/cost-index-snapshot.json` carries 24. The missing 8 are exactly the frozen seafood set. **25% of the paid product's market-prior universe is already gone** because of an upstream outage in a different repository, and an operator counting shrimp gets "uncovered" with no explanation.

**Cheapest prevention:** one afternoon. Diagnose NOAA or move the 15 to `KNOWN_SOURCE_LATENT` with a dated reason, and *exercise the terminal path once on a single real slug* before September makes it seventeen.

## KP4 — The deploy is red right now, and the bot re-creates it three times a week
I reproduced this and it refines the orchestrator's ground truth. The cost-index gates the orchestrator checked do pass — but `check-all.mjs` **exits 1** (312/320), and `check-cost-index-picker.mjs` fails **deterministically, 3 runs of 3**: `✗ grapefruit: dollarRef false != recomputed true`.

The mechanism is exactly as described. `.github/workflows/cost-index-refresh.yml` runs `node scripts/build-cost-index-picker.mjs` at ~line 99, under a comment that *explains this very failure* — "a refresh that adds or drops an ingredient (or flips a firm-$ reference) leaves it stale and trips check-cost-index-picker on the next PR unless it's rewritten here." The `git add` allowlist at 302-311 does not contain `data/cost-index-picker.js`. Line 313's `git checkout -- .` then discards it. The file's last commit is **2026-07-28** ("fix(ci): regenerate lockfloat + picker stale after the main merge") while the seed it derives from was rewritten 07-31, 08-03 and 08-05.

Because `check-all` runs inside `wrangler.jsonc`'s `build.command`, **no content change can deploy today.** Three of the last four commits are bot data commits; the last human commit in either repo is 2026-07-31.

What makes this a *kill path* rather than a bug: the comment block above the allowlist documents two prior instances of this identical failure, each fixed by appending one filename. This is the third. A recurring, machine-caused, hand-cleared red is the training signal that produces KP1's red-normalization. It is also the single cheapest fix in the audit — stop enumerating outputs; stage everything and reset the short list that must not travel. **~1 hour, permanent.**

I also observed `check-all` returning 317, 317 and 312 across three consecutive runs on a clean tree. A non-deterministic gate suite is worse than a failing one: it teaches the operator that reds are noise.

## KP5 — The founding-cohort obligation, and the one way the honesty brand dies loudly
Every other honesty failure here rots in private. This one has a counterparty.

The offer — "three months free, then $19 a month per location **for as long as you stay**" — is published on **51 surfaces** per its own `sourced-claims.json` `used_in` array, in EN and ES. It is granted **by default, twice**: `src/worker.js:8947` forwards `founding_member=true` for an absent field (`String(undefined) !== 'false'`), and `apps/api/src/routes/waitlist.ts:97-103` stores true for an empty string. The route is mounted public and unauthenticated at `index.ts:326` with 5 req/min/IP and **no proxy-key check** — the `x-muntin-proxy-key` header the storefront sends is never validated. An email address and a curl enrols a perpetual price lock.

The qualifying terms do not exist. `runbooks/launch-plan-2026-11-13.md:35-37` names them as TODO — "counsel to draft the 'locked until cancellation, non-transferable' terms" — and `grep -rn "non-transferable"` across the whole repo returns that one line. `find apps/web/app -ipath "*founding*"` returns nothing. No cap, no cohort limit, no definition of "staying", no rule for a change of ownership or added locations. ADR-020 extended the accrual window by **12+ months at zero marginal cost to enrol**.

At launch there are two doors. Honor it: ARPU on the first cohort is $19/location — ~24% below Solo — in perpetuity, to a cohort nobody can enumerate, with `per_location` and `per_account` bases that do not reconcile (a 3-location founding member pays $57 against a $60 Team plan for 2-5 seats, and no rule resolves it). Or walk it back: a company whose only asset is "we do not publish what we cannot stand behind" publicly retracts a published commitment. **That is the loud death.**

**Cheapest prevention, and it must happen before more people enrol, not at launch:** flip the default to opt-in (10 minutes), require the proxy key (30 minutes), publish `/legal/founding-member-terms` with a cohort cap and a definition of "staying" (4 hours). Counsel redlines it later; an unbounded promise cannot be redlined retroactively.

## KP6 — The Stripe overcharge is harmless today and lethal at launch, because the gate cannot see it
`apps/api/src/routes/billing.ts:174-175` sets `line_items[0][price]` to the base tier price ID and `line_items[0][quantity]` to `seats`. Against `stripe-tiers.ts` (Team base $60, `seatPriceCents: 0`, maxSeats 5; Accountant base $150, seat $30, maxSeats 50), a 5-seat Team bills **$300 against a posted $60**; a 50-seat Accountant bills **$7,500 against $1,620**. `monthlyTotalCents()` at `stripe-tiers.ts:119` computes it correctly and checkout never calls it. Two pricing engines; checkout uses the wrong one.

Zero damage exists today: `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` is unset, no `apps/web` surface calls `/v1/billing/checkout/begin`, `/settings/billing` is a non-interactive "Soon" row, and the success/cancel URLs default to `localhost:3000`. That is precisely why it will survive: nothing exercises it, and the gate built on 2026-07-30 *specifically to catch posted-price drift* — `check-pricing-consistency.mjs` — has an unconditional `basis === "per_seat"` pass and checks one of the three conditions its own registry defines as billable. It prints green.

This is the audit's own diagnosed pattern reproducing **inside the fix for the previous instance of the pattern**: a gate measuring the shape of a declaration rather than the behavior of the code. If it fires on day one of paid GA, the headline writes itself against a company whose entire brand is arithmetic honesty. **One line to fix (call `monthlyTotalCents`, or quantity=1 plus a seat line item) and ~2 hours to make the gate compute rather than declare.** Free now, a refund event later.

## The honesty brand: closer to the line than the founder thinks, but it will not detonate — it will rot
The asymmetry is the finding. `<!-- count:KEY -->` sentinels are machine-enforced. `<!-- repro:value -->` sentinels are machine-enforced. `<!-- src: -->` annotations are **not enforced by anything** — `grep -rln 'src:' scripts/check-*.mjs` returns nothing. There are **164 of them, all in one file**: the flagship July dispatch. The one I checked is wrong by roughly 2×: `blog/cost-index-2026-07/index.html:632` publishes "refuses a posture on **73 of 100**" annotated to `cost-lockfloat.json counts.withhold`; the live file reads **`{lock: 37, cushion: 19, float: 7, withhold: 37}`**. That source file is rewritten on every measured read. Every one of those 164 annotations points at mutable data.

Alongside it: `llms.txt:25` tells every AI crawler the Index "publishes **weekly**" — a cadence the founder retired on 2026-07-09. `/security/integrity.txt` publishes 6 SHA-256 digests and invites the reader to verify; all 6 fail. `/never/` promise #2 says the engine "never learns from your corrections" while the landing page sells the opposite and the code ships it. The published privacy policy asserts a 24-hour deletion reaper that is double-locked off — and the API's own response copy branches on the flag and says the honest thing, so the *code is more honest than the document that governs it.*

But assess this honestly against the lens. A reputational detonation requires an audience, and there is no audience: zero confirmed users (asserted in three independent repo artifacts), 79% of pages emitting no analytics, and the monthly dispatch reaching two pages' worth of subscribers. **Nobody is going to catch Muntin publishing 73 when the answer is 37.**

Which is why the real damage runs the other way. The founder built a 320-gate instrument whose entire purpose is to make his published numbers true. When he discovers — as this audit just did — that the flagship post's headline is wrong, that his warrant canary is three cycles past a schedule whose own text says silence is the signal, and that his monthly promise on 83 pages went unmet three days ago with no consequence, the thing that breaks is not his reputation. It is his belief that the instrument works. **For this company the honesty brand's failure mode is not a scandal; it is the founder losing faith in his own machine — and that is the direct on-ramp to KP2.**

## What I dismiss, and why — plus the burn nobody is watching
**Incumbents crushing it: dismissed.** MarginEdge, R365 and Toast do not know Muntin exists and never will. The real version of this risk is self-inflicted and already shipping: `apps/web/app/(marketing)/vs/marginedge/page.tsx:57` — indexed, sitemapped, `lastFactChecked 2026-05-21` — tells the highest-intent traffic the company will ever get that "MarginEdge runs inventory, recipe costing… **We do not yet do the rest**," at prices ($25/$60/$150) the company does not charge. All of it is false: `routes/inventory.ts` is 1,100 lines, plus `recipes.ts`, `sales-mix.ts`, a Square connector and 16 shipped `/insights/*` surfaces. Muntin is not being crushed; it is conceding ground it already won. A KP2 rider, and a 1-day rewrite.

**GDPR/CCPA/MODPA fines: dismissed.** Zero customers, no EU signups accepted, revenue far under every statutory threshold. The compliance findings are real but they are *truthfulness* failures, which route to the honesty section. Do not spend the counsel budget here; spend it on the founding-member terms.

**Security breach: dismissed as a present danger, retained as a launch blocker.** `POST /v1/erasure` requiring only a session and a typed phrase (so a `read_only` member can destroy a workspace and rewrite its audit chain), `requireRole` applied to 1 of 69 route files, and `documents` having no RLS 42 migrations after 0015 deferred it, are all real. With no customer data, none is live exposure. They become live on the first paid signup. Correctly deferred — but they belong on the launch gate, not in a backlog.

**Running out of money: not a killer, but a demotivator worth cutting.** `services/docling/fly.toml:29-56` runs **two always-on `performance`/2-CPU/8GB machines** — ~$164/month at list — serving **zero customers**, against a budget doc saying $10-15/mo and a public page saying ~$34/mo. Over 17 months to an undated 2027 launch that is roughly **$2,800-3,500 of personal cash from an FOH manager's salary**. Survivable, but it is the kind of monthly line item that makes a tired person ask what they are doing. Nothing in either repo measures the bill.

**Key-person catastrophe: real, irreversible, but not the likely death.** Both repos on a personal GitHub account, `CODEOWNERS = * @donwonmagic`, zero occurrences of "succession" or "bus factor" in either repo, and `runbooks/backup-restore-drill.md:65` stating a lost KMS key makes ciphertext unrecoverable — against a drill never run. Low annual probability, total loss, catastrophic *for customers* the moment there are any. Buy the insurance, but do not let it displace KP1 and KP2, which are the ones actually happening.

## The counterfactual: what December 2027 looks like if Muntin lives
The survival scenario is not "ship faster." It is **subtract**.

Muntin lives if, in August 2026, the founder does three things that all point the same direction. First, he stops the bleeding that produces false red signals — one hour on the refresh workflow's staging pattern buys back a permanently-green board, which is the precondition for the 320 gates meaning anything again. Second, he closes the loop between the 1,368 pages and a human: three hours of string work makes the subscribe source match the broadcast filter, makes the waitlist source survive the proxy, and gives `listFounding()` a caller, so that writing an article produces a number that moves. Third — and this is the actual survival decision, not a task — he declares a maintenance budget in hours and retires everything above it: the unpublished canary, the quarterly transparency report nobody has read, the 262 noindex-frozen pages still being translated and audio-narrated for no one, and a standing rule that a new gate must name the gate it replaces.

The honest reading of ADR-020 is that it was the right call made for the right reason and then left half-finished: it deferred the *launch* without deferring any of the *obligations*. A company that has decided not to sell anything for eighteen months should be carrying less machinery, not more. Right now it is carrying more.

And one thing worth saying plainly, because a pre-mortem that only enumerates deaths is useless: what this company is short of is not discipline, competence, or ideas. `apps/api` is 93,382 LOC against 82,503 LOC of tests with 16 TODOs in the entire product tree. The extraction engine is deterministic with a passing no-LLM CI gate. The encryption is real. The valuation ladder withholds a number rather than guessing. 1,058 of 1,066 indexable pages carry valid JSON-LD. This is better engineering than most funded seed companies produce with ten people. What it is short of is **one operator who is not Don Goldstein, using the product, on purpose.** The corpus that could produce that operator — 919 real invoices from his own two-location restaurant — is being fed to CLIs on his Mac and has never once been pushed through the product's own activation path. Not by anyone. Not by him. That is the single most consequential fact in this entire audit, and it costs a week to fix.
