# The Capacity Arithmetic: 53 hours of obligation against "a few hours a week"

## Thesis
Muntin's recurring obligations total ~53 founder-hours/month against a capacity the founder himself wrote down as "~a few hours/week" (docs/seo-handoff-both-repos.md:23, 2026-07-05) — 13-26 h/mo. That is a 2.7x deficit, and maintenance alone (31 h/mo, before a single word is written) is 1.6x capacity. You do not have to trust my capacity estimate, because the failures measure it for you: in the 30 days to 2026-08-07 roughly 3 of 31 maintenance-hours-due were actually paid (~10%), while the warrant canary went 3 monthly signings overdue, the Q2 transparency report went 37 days past its published date, unexpected frozen price feeds doubled 8→16, and the six ADR-013 government datasets registered as a recurring manual chore were fetched exactly once, ever. The realized split is ~88% creation / ~10% maintenance — which is the right instinct for a pre-revenue company and the wrong one for this one, because everything decaying is a TRUST artifact, and trust is the entire differentiator. The obligation side is also compounding faster than capacity: the same doc that recorded "a few hours/week" also recorded "~90 checks"; check-all now runs 320, a 3.5x in 33 days. Right now the Cloudflare deploy is red on 8 checks, 5 of which no automation can clear — I verified the deploy's build.command runs three of the eight and none of the other five. And the one document about buying leverage, docs/hiring/project-manager-scope.md, is a fossil: all three of its hiring triggers are denominated in the retired web-studio revenue line, at $3,467-4,117/month against zero revenue.

## Capacity: don't argue the number, read it off the failures
Three independent bases, in order of authority.

**(a) The founder's own written figure.** `docs/seo-handoff-both-repos.md:23`, authored by Donwonmagic on 2026-07-05: "**Operator reality:** Don Goldstein, solo, **~a few hours/week**." That is 3-6 h/wk = **13-26 h/mo**. Take the midpoint: 20.

**(b) Observed authorship.** Non-bot active days per month: storefront 16 in July, **0 in August**; product 26 in June, 12 in July, **0 in August**. The last non-bot commit in *both* repos is 2026-07-31 — **7 consecutive days of zero authored work** while `muntin-cost-index-bot` kept committing on 2026-08-03 and 2026-08-05. This is not an anomaly to explain away; it is what a full-time FOH manager's August looks like.

**(c) The job.** A front-of-house manager across two locations (Bethesda + Arlington) works 45-55 h/wk, evening- and weekend-loaded — the hours that overlap exactly with when discretionary work would happen. And `docs/handoff/strategic-council-board.md:158` records the founder "is starting a college course, with little time or money for a launch." Capacity is scheduled to shrink, not grow.

**The deficit: 53.2 ÷ 20 = 2.7x. Maintenance alone: 31.2 ÷ 20 = 1.6x.**

If you find 20 h/mo too pessimistic, run the sensitivity: at the ops-capacity auditor's more generous 15-20 h/wk (65-87 h/mo), obligations consume 61-82% of everything, leaving no slack for the audit you just commissioned, the strategy pivot in ADR-020, or the billing bug. But the generous number is refuted by evidence, not opinion. **Back capacity out of completions instead.** In the 30 days to 2026-08-07, of the 31.2 maintenance-hours due, what was actually paid: the snapshot re-vendor (~1 h) and the one-time ADR-013 fetch (~2 h). That is **~3 of 31 hours, or 9.6% of the maintenance ledger**. Everything else went unpaid, and the unpaid items are visible with dates: canary 68 days past, transparency 37 days past, unexpected frozen feeds doubled 8→16 in the seven days after the roster gate that would notice them was written, six gov datasets fetched once and never again, 5 unhealed deploy reds sitting in `main`.

The 2.7x is not a forecast. It is a description of a system already 90% in arrears on one of its two ledgers.

## Maintenance vs creation: the split is 88/10, and it is inverted from what the company needs
Realized capacity in the last 30 days went almost entirely to creation: the July dispatch (2026-07-09), two new library articles, a data-viz honesty sweep with three new `viz-*` families, `viz-bars` rule 9, `check-claim-usage` (a whole new bidirectional gate), ADR-020, and two new absence-alarm workflows. That is genuine, high-quality output — nothing here is laziness.

But the arithmetic of what it *created* is the trap. `docs/seo-handoff-both-repos.md:23` — the same line that says "a few hours/week" — also tells the next session that `check-all.mjs` "must stay green (**~90 checks**)." I ran it today: **320 checks in 3m09s**. The gate surface grew 3.5x in 33 days.

Every gate is creation that converts, permanently, into maintenance. Every one adds a way for the deploy to go red, a registry that must be curated, a waiver that must be re-dated. The company's own CLAUDE.md warns about this shape — "a builder that check-all verifies but nothing re-runs turns drift into a red deploy no automation can clear" — and then 21 of 83 builders were registered as MANUAL anyway, and 8 of those 21 document a fetch that has happened once.

So the split that matters is not creation-vs-maintenance in hours spent. It is: **creation is running at ~88% of capacity and is manufacturing new maintenance faster than the remaining ~10% can absorb.** Maintenance at 31.2 h/mo already exceeds total capacity by 1.6x. That is the definition of structural decline — not because the founder is failing, but because the system's carrying cost passed his available hours somewhere around mid-July and nothing in the process notices a *cumulative* budget. Each individual gate, ADR, runbook and translation is defensible on its own; the sum is not payable.

And the decay is landing on exactly the wrong assets. The company's differentiator is not the code — it is the honesty machinery. The items going unpaid are the warrant canary, the transparency report, the promises timestamps, the price feeds, the vendored snapshot: **every one of them a trust artifact.** A lapsed canary is worse than no canary, because its own text (`docs/warrant-canary.md:33`) instructs the reader to treat silence as a signal.

## What NOT to cut — three things that look expensive and are load-bearing
**The 645 Spanish pages. HOLD AND INSTRUMENT — cutting them today would be a mistake.**
The raw number is alarming: `es/` is 26.9% of all file-touches in the storefront (19,547 of 72,757) and 57 of 129 gates reference it. But measure before amputating. First, creation is nearly free — `docs/editorial/the-hundred.md:49` states plainly that "the ES pipeline uses machine translation" (`scripts/lib/translate.py` → Cloudflare Workers AI Llama), so most of that churn is machine-generated, not founder-hours. Second, the technical work is *done and correct*: `check-hreflang-orphans` is clean across 1,326 pages, the sitemap carries 540 EN + 518 ES with zero noindex and zero dead URLs. Third, in a DMV restaurant market it is the one differentiator MarginEdge, R365 and Toast do not have. The honest problem is that **518 of 651 ES pages carry no analytics at all**, so nobody knows whether anyone reads them. Cutting on no evidence would destroy real work; the correct move is one Plausible script and a decision in 60 days on data. Reclaim ~1 h/mo now by freezing ES only for the frozen content in cut #1.

**The gate families. DO NOT CUT — BUDGET THEM.**
Every one of the 15 domain postures says the same thing: the honesty machinery is the company's most defensible asset. Deleting gates would destroy the moat to buy hours. The correct control is a **gate budget**: no new `check-*.mjs` ships unless it is self-healing (its builder runs in the deploy `build.command`) or it retires an existing one. That converts the 90→320 growth curve from a compounding liability into a flat one, without losing a single existing protection.

**The 6 on-funnel free tools and the 98 live glossary terms.** `cost-pulse`, `margin-math`, `menu-engineering`, `plate-cost`, `vendor-benchmark` are the funnel under ADR-020, and the live glossary is the AI-citation surface plus the autolink graph that the library articles depend on. The 73 frozen glossary terms go with cut #1; the other 98 stay.

## The compounding term — why this gets worse without a decision
Two curves are moving in opposite directions and both are measurable in-repo.

**Obligation is growing.** 90 → 320 check-all checks in 33 days (`docs/seo-handoff-both-repos.md:23` dated 2026-07-05 vs today's run). 21 MANUAL idem builders where the doc's own header says the safe number is zero. 39 ADRs and 237 markdown docs, each of which must not be allowed to start lying. 23 open-data datasets. Nothing in the process tracks a cumulative carrying cost, so every individually-correct decision to add a gate, a dataset, a locale or a runbook silently draws on an account nobody balances.

**Capacity is shrinking.** Non-bot active days: 26 (June, product) → 12 (July, product) → 0 (August, both). Coursework starting (`strategic-council-board.md:158`). The FOH job does not get lighter.

And there is a hard deadline stack in the next 60 days that the ledger cannot absorb: the next monthly dispatch is due ~2026-08-09; `check-cost-index-dispatch-fresh` reds around 2026-08-16; the vendored product snapshot (`_asOfRange` 2026-07-18..07-28, `STALE_AFTER_DAYS = 30`) goes stale and fails closed in the second half of August, silently disabling the inventory market prior exactly as it did for 42 days in June-July; and ten known-latent price series hit the 120-day builder cliff in 53 days, at which point `salmon-fillet` — a basket contributor that dates the published headline — is dropped and its page orphaned.

One more structural item that costs zero hours and should be fixed this week regardless: **GitHub disables scheduled workflows in a public repository after 60 days without commit activity.** Both repos are public and on a single personal account, and `muntin-cost-index-bot` commits are not user activity for that timer. The two absence-alarm workflows the founder built specifically to survive his absence are themselves switched off by the mechanism they were built to defeat. A term of coursework is longer than 60 days.

The good news is genuinely good: the Cost Index data engine compounds unattended on a Mon/Wed/Fri cron off free public inputs and degrades to last-good. ADR-020's judgement — that a data line which survives neglect is the right thing to own during a year of coursework — is correct. It just has to be *the only thing*, and today it is one of twenty.
