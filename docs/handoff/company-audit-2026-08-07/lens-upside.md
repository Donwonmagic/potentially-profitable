# The Upside Case: What Muntin Already Owns and Is Not Cashing

## Thesis
Muntin has built two genuinely uncommon assets and is cashing neither: a CC0 restaurant-price archive with a published, self-falsifying calibration record, and a determinism guarantee that is verifiable in thirty seconds by a stranger. Both are stronger than the phase-1 defect list implies — and weaker in one specific way the company itself already discloses honestly: 83,695 of the 85,720 published observations (97.6%) are marked `reconstructed:true`, an after-the-fact backfill any competitor with the same USDA/BLS/FRED feeds can rebuild. The unfabricatable asset is not the 25-year depth; it is the 2,025 live-captured points that began 2026-05-01, the 10,294-entry append-only revisions ledger, and the dated publication chain — all of which compound only if the cadence never breaks, and 26 of 100 series have already been frozen 58–77 days. The single highest-leverage fact in this audit: ADR-020 made the data line "the company for the next year" and gave it no revenue mechanism, while the methodology page's independence contract forecloses only *placement and influence* — it does not foreclose selling analysis, service, or expertise over data that stays free. Two paths to a first dollar therefore need zero billing code and zero new product: a paid monthly read over the free index, and paid cost work inside the founder's own vendor network. Meanwhile the machinery to reach an audience is built and disconnected — 162 ingredient pages promise a monthly email that a five-element allowlist in `src/worker.js:8746` makes it impossible to send, one embeddable card exists out of 82, and a complete DataCite record sits with `"doi": null`. This company's problem is not that it lacks assets. It is that every distribution surface it built stops one line short of working.

## The asset inventory, graded honestly — three real, three overrated
**GENUINELY DEFENSIBLE.**

*(1) The calibration record.* `cost-index/calibration.json` publishes a split/EnbPI conformal band with 79,086 scored steps, pooled coverage 0.767 against 0.80 nominal, `underCovering: 0` across 75 items — and, in the same file, a direction-call hit rate of **0.506 against a 0.502 baseline over 34,029 calls**. That is a company publishing, on its own front page, the finding that its own directional signal is at chance. I have not seen a pre-revenue company do this. It is the single most credible artifact in either repo and it costs nothing to promote.

*(2) The determinism guarantee.* `Muntin-Invoice-Decoder/scripts/no-llm-ci.sh` runs clean (I executed it: `no-LLM gate: OK`, exit 0) over every tracked `.ts/.tsx/.js/.mjs/.py/.toml/.yaml/.ipynb`, blocking LLM imports, LLM HTTP destinations, and LLM-only env vars. The repo is MIT-licensed and the methodology page asserts the build is open. In a category where every competitor says "AI-powered," a buyer can falsify or confirm this claim in under a minute. That is rare and it is being buried.

*(3) The 919-invoice validation corpus.* `services/extract/VENDOR-TEMPLATE-KEY.md:203-236` reports per-vendor reconcile rates measured on 919 real invoices from named distributors — i_halper 93.2% (n=73), chefs_warehouse 92.1% (n=214), baldor 89.7%, pat_lafrieda 87.5%, down to cozzini_bros 20.8%. A funded competitor can buy engineers; it cannot buy an FOH manager's four years of real Mid-Atlantic distributor invoices. `CONFIDENCE-CALIBRATION.md:150` even documents the *negative* result — that confidence is flat-to-inverted against reconciliation, so no confidence gate was shipped.

**OVERRATED — say it plainly.**

*(4) The "25-year archive."* I counted every published `series.json`: 82 files, 85,720 observations, 2001-03-28 → 2026-08-04. Of those, **2,025 are `reconstructed:false` (live-captured at publication) and 83,695 are `reconstructed:true`** — the company's own `reconstructedNote` says "a monthly backfill reconstructed from public sources after the fact — not figures published live on that date." The disclosure is exemplary. The consequence is that 97.6% of the archive is re-derivable by anyone with the same USDA AMS / LMR / BLS / FRED access. This is not a moat; it is a well-built public-data mirror.

*(5) The 1,314-page corpus.* Real, bilingual, well-gated — but `256` of those pages carry `noindex`, and the marketing-SEO domain confirmed the five free tools carry zero analytics. A corpus nobody measures and a fifth of which is frozen is a maintenance liability as much as an asset (ops-capacity priced the whole maintenance load at ~35 founder-hours/month).

*(6) The honesty machinery as a *product* feature.* The gates are excellent as engineering. But "we withhold rather than guess" is a purchase reason only for someone already worried about being lied to. With zero users, it has never been tested as a sales argument. Treat it as a credibility asset, not a demand generator.

## The archive thesis: the compounding clock started 2026-05-01, and 26% of it is already stopped
The right way to think about the archive is not "25 years deep." It is: **a live, timestamped, third-party-verifiable publication record began on 2026-05-01 and is 98 days old.**

The arithmetic. `data/cost-index.json` holds 1,343 measured `points` across 100 ingredients over 35 distinct `asOf` dates from 2026-05-01 to 2026-08-04 — and `points` is a **rolling window capped at 18 per ingredient** (min 1 / median 17 / max 18), not an append-only store. What persists is (a) the per-ingredient `series.json` with `reconstructed:false` flags — 2,025 rows, median 26 per ingredient — and (b) `cost-index/revisions.json`, a genuinely append-only ledger of **10,294 entries**, each recording a change to a previously-published reading (`R:bell-pepper:2026-05-13:2950->2550`). *That* is the artifact a competitor cannot fabricate retroactively: a dated record of what you said and when you corrected it.

At the current rate — 2,025 live points in 98 days across 82 ingredients — the live record grows ~7,500 rows/year. Reaching two unbroken years of live weekly capture across 100 ingredients (~10,400 rows) takes roughly 16 months of uninterrupted cadence. **That clock is the whole asset, and it is already broken for 26 of 100 series.** Running `scripts/check-cost-index-series-freshness.mjs` today: 16 unexpected frozen feeds (butter-lettuce 77d, green-beans 72d, shrimp/vegetable-oil/squid/clams/pork-belly 67d, ground-beef 58d) plus 10 known-latent, against a 120-day builder cliff that starts dropping ingredients in 43–62 days. Two of the frozen ones — shrimp and vegetable-oil — are basket contributors, which is why `basket.asOf` reads 2026-06-01 under a `_lastReviewed` of 2026-08-05.

One operational constraint that bounds any archive strategy: `.git` is **1.16 GiB** for a 343-commit shallow clone, because every measured read commits a 12.6 MB `data/cost-index.json` (12 such commits since 2026-07-10). Whatever else gets built on the archive, the storage pattern needs a decision before it needs a feature.

## Built and one line short of shipping — four eight-inch putts
Every one of these is an existing, working asset whose distribution stops at the last step.

**(1) The email that cannot be sent.** `src/worker.js:8746` defines `SUBSCRIBE_SOURCES = new Set(['footer','article-end','workshop-empty-state','window','cost-index'])`; `:8863` clamps anything outside it to `'footer'`; `:8793` filters the Cost Index broadcast to `sub.source === 'cost-index'`. I counted the hidden inputs across the built site: **162 pages send `source="cost-index-ingredient"`** and only **2** send `cost-index`. So every subscriber captured on the deepest-intent surface on the site is filed as `footer` and can never receive the monthly read those same pages promise them. Two independent phase-1 adversaries found this (marketing-seo CRITICAL, finance-runway CRITICAL). From the upside lens it is the most important single line in either repo: it is the only thing standing between a working Resend broadcast lane and an owned audience.

**(2) The embed built once.** `cost-index/ribeye/embed.html` exists — a 30 KB self-contained card. It is the only one: `ls cost-index/*/embed.html` returns **1 of 82**. `cost-index/feed.json` advertises an embed for 1 of its 82 entries, and `llms.txt:38` tells every AI crawler that the feed carries "links to its full series **and embeddable card**" for every shipping ingredient. Embeds are the canonical distribution mechanic for a data publisher: every placement is a backlink, a citation, and a crawler-visible attribution. The builder exists; 81 files and a one-paragraph "embed this" section are missing.

**(3) The DOI that was prepared and never minted.** `cost-index/menu-pricing/study/datacite.json:2` — "PREPARED, NOT MINTED. doi is a null placeholder." `CITATION.cff` ships with `# doi: pending`. The field report grounds itself in **36 sources with 20 distinct DOIs**, has a `datapackage.json` (tabular-data-package profile), a CHANGELOG, and CC-BY 4.0. A minted DOI is what makes it findable in scholarly indexes and citable by a journalist's fact-checker. Everything but the mint is done.

**(4) The archive with no front door.** Per-ingredient `Dataset` JSON-LD is complete and correct — I parsed `cost-index/ribeye/index.html` and it carries `temporalCoverage: 2001-04-18/2026-08-04`, both `DataDownload` distributions, CC0 license, `variableMeasured`, and a `citation`. But the top-level `DataCatalog` on `cost-index/index.html:33` declares only **two** distributions: `index.csv` and `index.json` — the 81-row *current snapshot*. There is no combined multi-ingredient timeseries download anywhere, no Zenodo/HuggingFace/data.world listing, no `identifier`. CORS is already open on `/cost-index/*.json` and `*.csv` (`_headers:131-135`), so the plumbing for third-party notebooks is done. A single `series-all.csv` plus one catalog distribution entry converts 85,720 rows from "82 files you'd have to know to loop over" into a dataset that Google Dataset Search and researchers can actually find.

## The mispriced thing: what the independence contract actually forbids
The lens question is whether giving away the data and monetizing extraction is the wrong allocation. My answer is: the *architecture* is right and the *sequencing* is wrong, and the contract permits more than the company thinks.

Read `cost-index/methodology/index.html:~515` precisely. The independence section forecloses four things, all of them **inputs and influence**: no submitters (no market participant supplies a price); **no paid placements — "no vendor feed, no sponsorship, no pay-to-be-listed. An ingredient is added, weighted, or held back only by the mechanical shippable bar"**; firewalled from Ledger ("No Ledger relationship can move a published reading"); independent and reproducible.

What that **forbids**: sponsorship of the index or its newsletter, any vendor paying for coverage/weighting/omission, exclusivity or embargo on a published reading, any customer-data path into the index, a paid tier that sees numbers the public tier does not.

What it **does not touch**: charging for *service* over data that remains free and CC0 (SLA'd delivery, alerting, custom cuts, support); charging for *analysis* — Don's interpretation is not a published reading and moves nothing; charging for *expertise* — cost reviews, vendor-negotiation work, bespoke benchmark analysis for an operator or a group.

So the honest monetization set is narrow and specific: **service, analysis, and expertise — never placement, never influence, never exclusivity on the number itself.** CC0 means you can never sell access; you can only sell convenience, interpretation, and judgement. That is a real business (it is how open-data companies work) but it is a *labour* business, not a licensing one — which fits a one-person company far better than it fits a fundable one.

The crucial consequence: ADR-020 (`docs/editorial/decisions/ADR-020-…md`) declared the data line "the company for the next year" and, by its own text, deferred all billing work. But **analysis and expertise revenue requires no billing engine and no Ledger launch.** ADR-020 did not close the revenue door on the data line; nobody has opened it.

## Three cheapest credible paths to a first paying customer
Ranked by (assets already built) ÷ (evenings required). All three respect one employed person and zero revenue.

**A. A paid monthly read over the free index — "the Muntin Read."** Everything exists but the audience: the dispatch scaffolder (`scripts/build-cost-index-dispatch.mjs`), the Resend broadcast lane (`src/worker.js:8763-8800`, idempotent per `asOf`, capped at 90/day), the editor's-note gate, the EN/ES surfaces, and 162 pages already asking for the email. The free index stays free and CC0 — the paid thing is Don's read on it, which the independence contract does not govern. Blockers, in order: fix `SUBSCRIBE_SOURCES` (one line), fix `llms.txt:25` still telling crawlers "weekly" (the founder made it monthly 2026-07-09), then run free for 3–4 editions to build a list before charging. Revenue mechanism can be a Stripe Payment Link and a tagged list — no `apps/api` billing work at all. **Time to first dollar: 3–4 months, gated on list size, not code.**

**B. Paid cost work inside his own network — the fastest path, and the only one with zero technical prerequisites.** He is FOH manager at a two-location brand, buying from i_Halper, Chef's Warehouse, Baldor, Pat LaFrieda, Fresco, Edward Don. He has measured reconcile rates for each. A "vendor cost review" — run their last 90 days of invoices through the engine on his Mac, produce a report against the Cost Index, name what they overpaid — is billable today, needs no product, no billing, no launch, and no ADR-020 exception, and it does the validation work the customer-growth domain says has never been done (`docs/transparency-2026q2.md:38`: no live customers; `CONFIDENCE-CALIBRATION.md:150`: correction log still empty pre-pilot). **Time to first dollar: weeks.** It also doubles as the design-partner pipeline for path C.

**C. Ledger Solo at $25 for a hand-picked cohort of 3–5.** Worth stating precisely because the phase-1 headline obscures it: the checkout bug at `apps/api/src/routes/billing.ts:174-175` (base price × `seats`) **only overcharges multi-seat**. `stripe-tiers.ts` gives Solo `maxSeats: 1` and `basePriceCents` 2500, so a 1-seat Solo checkout bills $25 correctly. Turning on revenue for a Solo-only cohort needs `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY`, a checkout surface (nothing in `apps/web` currently calls `/v1/billing/checkout/begin`), and real URLs replacing the `http://localhost:3000` defaults — not the $19-per-location SKU, not the multi-seat fix, not the trial. **But this is the one path that contradicts ADR-020**, whose reasoning (don't sell "it does not guess" on a data line with silent dead feeds) is still valid while 26 of 100 series sit frozen. Do this only as an explicitly-priced private beta, and only after the frozen-feed count is back to single digits.

Break-even context: finance-runway computed the deployed docling pool at roughly $164/month against a $10–15 budget doc — two always-on `performance`/2-CPU/8192 MB machines with `auto_stop_machines = false` and `min_machines_running = 2` (`services/docling/fly.toml:29-56`). Path B covers that with a single engagement. Path C needs 7 Solo seats. Path A needs a list.

## The founder's seat is the most under-harvested asset in the company
He is not a founder who *researched* restaurants. He is inside one, at two locations, holding the invoices. The audit found that seat producing exactly one output — engine truth — and none of the other four it could produce.

**Being harvested:** the 919-doc corpus, via CLIs on his Mac. It produced the vendor-trust enablement order, the confidence-is-not-a-gate negative result, and `tests/golden/real_fixtures`. This is real and it is good engineering.

**Not harvested — design-partner loop.** customer-growth's verdict is that an operator who signed up would not survive 60 days: a new vendor produces `drift:new_layout` (`services/extract/drift.py:134`), auto-confirm needs 3–4 prior confirmations (`template_store.py:100`), vendor-trust ships in shadow mode by default. That is a diagnosis derived from code, not from watching anyone. He can watch himself — pushing his own 919 invoices through the *product's* activation path (not the CLI) would measure the real onboarding cliff in one weekend, and it is the only measurement that would tell him whether path C is sellable.

**Not harvested — case study.** The per-vendor reconcile table is a marketing asset a competitor structurally cannot match: "here are eleven real distributors and how often we reconcile each, including the three where we do badly." Publishing the *aggregate* table (rates and vendor names, no invoice contents) is the honesty-machine argument made concrete. **Caveat, and it is not optional:** legal-ip confirmed `services/extract/tests/golden/real_fixtures/fresco_lineitems.json:390` carries "Tacombi Bethesda," real invoice numbers, Net-45 terms and negotiated unit prices in an MIT-licensed repo, while two sibling fixtures in the same directory are properly shape-redacted. Redaction must land before any of this is publicized, because publicity is what makes someone look.

**Not harvested — distribution.** He has professional relationships with the buyers at those distributors and with other DMV operators. Nothing in either repo indicates a single outbound conversation. Path B is entirely built on this, and it is free.

**Worth, if used deliberately:** the seat converts three of the company's hardest unknowns — does onboarding survive contact, will an operator pay, does the Cost Index read match a real invoice — into answerable questions at a cost of zero dollars and a handful of evenings. It is the highest information-per-hour resource the company has, and it is currently pointed only at parser accuracy.

## What NOT to do — protecting the one thing that compounds
An upside memo has to name the negative space, because a part-time founder's scarcest resource is attention.

**Do not add surface area to the corpus.** 1,314 pages, 256 noindex, 171 glossary terms, EN+ES with audio in two languages, ~35 maintenance-hours/month against 15–20 available. Every new article widens the treadmill. The corpus is already large enough to do its job.

**Do not build a paid data tier.** CC0 is irrevocable for released versions; a paid tier that showed the same numbers earlier or in bulk would read as exclusivity and would strain the independence contract's spirit even where its letter allows it. Sell interpretation, not access.

**Do not take sponsorship, ever, on any surface adjacent to the index.** The methodology page uses the word explicitly. The credibility that makes paths A and B sellable is precisely what a sponsor logo cancels.

**Do not treat cadence as maintenance.** The 16 unexpected frozen feeds are not a hygiene item; they are the crown jewel decaying in real time. The whole archive thesis rests on an unbroken live capture chain, and the chain is 98 days old. Between "write this month's dispatch" and "re-source butter-lettuce," re-source butter-lettuce.

**Do not fix the multi-seat billing bug first.** It is genuinely serious (a 5-seat Accountant bills $750 against a correct $270 from `monthlyTotalCents()` at `stripe-tiers.ts:119`) — but `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` is unset, no web surface reaches checkout, and ADR-020 defers the launch. It is latent, not live. It must be fixed before any multi-seat customer exists; it should not consume an evening this month.
