# Does auditing change anything here? A ledger of 49 prior findings, measured against git

## Thesis
Auditing at Muntin has a 26% close rate and a 2-day median time-to-close — and those two numbers are the same fact seen twice. I built a ledger of 49 named findings across eight prior audit artifacts (2026-05-20 → 2026-07-31) and checked each against git. Fourteen were fixed by the same agent session that found them: 100% closed, 0 days. Thirty-eight outlived their session; ten of those were ever fixed, and every single one was closed either by that thread continuing, by the *next* audit session, or by an unrelated build thread that happened to touch the file. **Zero closures in the entire history came from anyone working an audit's list.** The bottleneck is not recording (the board is 1,488 lines and immaculate), not prioritization (the unfixed items are explicitly ranked "non-negotiable" and "NEEDS THE FOUNDER"), and not founder typing speed (80–84% of all commits in both repos are authored by "Claude"). The bottleneck is that **only an agent session produces work, and no session has ever been pointed at the backlog** — there have been zero human commits in either repo for seven days while three bots kept committing. The company's own doctrine — "any failure you do not turn into a permanent test, you will meet again" — is half-executed: it converts findings into *registries* faster than into *fixes*, and a registry entry has exactly the enforcement power of the document it replaced. The proof is on the deploy right now: `scripts/check-idem-coverage.mjs:16-19` says verbatim that themes-review-board, theme-story-pages and cuisine-landing-pages "were filed for months under 'known (idem) noise', which is exactly how a red deploy gets ignored" — and ten days later those exact three builders are three of the eight checks failing on a byte-clean HEAD, blocking production.

## Diagnosing the bottleneck: it is not recording, not prioritizing, and not typing
Ruling out each candidate with evidence:

**Not recording.** `docs/handoff/strategic-council-board.md` is 1,488 lines, dated, with named commits, an ADR series, and a "read first in a fresh context" contract. Four 2026-07-28 syntheses are committed verbatim *specifically because* the container is ephemeral. Recording is the strongest muscle in the company.

**Not prioritizing.** The unfixed items carry the strongest possible labels the company has: "not optional," "non-negotiable," "NEEDS THE FOUNDER, do not paper over," "highest risk-reduction per hour," "Do this Monday." Ranking is not the missing step.

**Not founder throughput at the keyboard.** Storefront authorship: **274 of 343 commits by "Claude"**, 11 by Cursor Agent, 12+4 by bots, 37 by Donwonmagic (predominantly merges), 5 by Don Goldstein. Product: **489 of 582 by "Claude"**, 88 Donwonmagic. The founder's role is direction and the merge button. Agents are the entire production capacity.

**The actual constraint: agent sessions are the only unit of work, and no session has ever been chartered against a backlog.** Every closure in the ledger came from a session that was *already touching that code*. The last human commit in either repo is 2026-07-31 (`460c5bf55`, PR #537 merge). Since then: three bot commits in the storefront (`ed3cc1704`, `841222923`, `7808827cc`), **zero in the product repo in seven days**. The Mon/Wed/Fri refresh, meanwhile, has committed without fail.

Which means the answer to "is fixing harder than finding?" is: **finding is nearly free and fixing costs a session.** Between 2026-07-28 and 2026-07-30 the storefront gained **9 new `check-*.mjs` gates in three days** (7 on 07-28, 2 on 07-30) while closing **0 of the 5 Rung-0 content corrections** those same audits called mandatory. The company reliably converts a finding into a gate or a registry entry — artifacts an agent can produce inside the session — and reliably fails to convert one into a prose correction or a data re-source, which requires a founder decision or a keyed run on his Mac. The backlog is not a symptom. It is the output.

## What would make THIS audit different — the unflattering answer
**The base rate applied to this audit predicts near-total failure.** 235 findings at a 26% survivor close rate would predict ~61 fixes. That is not the honest forecast, because the 26% was measured on audits producing ~5–13 findings each. This audit is roughly 20× the size of the largest prior one and the absorption capacity is identical — one employed person about to start coursework, with zero human commits in the last week. The realistic forecast is: **whatever gets fixed before this session's context ends, plus approximately nothing.**

**The report is the wrong artifact, and I am about to produce one.** The measured delta between 100% and 26% is not rigor, insight, or ranking — every unfixed finding above was already correctly found, correctly ranked, and correctly written down. The delta is *whether the fix happened before the agent's context ended.* If this audit ships a document into `docs/`, it will sit next to four documents from 2026-07-28 whose "not optional, do this Monday" list is 0-for-5 ten days later, and it will do exactly as well.

**The audit's own reflex — "add a gate" — is the over-applied move, not the missing one.** There are 129 storefront gates and 40 product gates. The deploy is red on five builders nobody runs, and the gate written expressly to catch that (`check-idem-coverage.mjs`) reports green while naming those exact builders in its own header comment. More enforcement over content nobody has time to correct produces a permanently red deploy, and a founder who cannot deploy stops reading gate output — which destroys the one mechanism that does work.

**Three things would actually move the number, and only three.**

1. **Spend the remaining session budget fixing rather than reporting.** The highest-value work in this entire audit is six shell commands: run the five unrun builders and commit; add them to `wrangler.jsonc` `build.command`; append the missing files to the refresh workflow's staging list. That turns a live production block into green and closes the third instance of a twice-hand-patched bug. It is worth more than a 31st agent.
2. **Fix the pattern, not the instance.** The `git add` allowlist should become a denylist (`git add -A -- data/ cost-index/ es/cost-index/ sitemap.xml` minus explicitly excluded paths), because the allowlist has now failed three times in the same way and each fix was one filename.
3. **Cap the deliverable at five items and mark the rest explicitly not-expected-to-be-read.** A 235-item list with no triage is indistinguishable, in outcome, from no list. Naming five and archiving 230 under an honest header is the only version of this report whose predicted close rate exceeds 26%.

**And one ordering constraint that is not optional:** CLAUDE.md forbids wiring a currently-failing gate. So `check-src-sentinel` cannot precede correcting `llms.txt:25` and the four-plus wrong numbers in `blog/cost-index-2026-07/index.html`. That is precisely why the 07-28 audit bundled them into one rung — and precisely the bundle that did not ship.
