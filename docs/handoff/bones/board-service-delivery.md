# Service Delivery, Support & Operations at $600/month

**Verdict: MAJOR_REVISION**

## Thesis
The verdict sells a signed, dated deliverable and the deliverable does not exist as an object anywhere in the product. `PeriodSnapshot` (`apps/api/src/lib/inventory-period-store.ts:14-38`) has no signature, no close date, no `delivered_at`, no version and no `supersedes`; it is written as a synchronous side effect of the operator pressing submit (`apps/api/src/routes/inventory.ts:608`), so there is no seat in the flow where a human reviews, withholds, or signs — the thing being sold is the one thing not built. The only Ed25519 signing in the codebase signs an invoice-package manifest (`apps/api/src/routes/accountant-handoffs.ts:858-870`), attesting "these are the files," not "these four legs foot," and the `pdfs` and `accountant` delivery shapes still return 503 (`accountant-handoffs.ts:51,289`). Second, the schedule is the worst one a one-person shop can choose: "your month closes on the second Tuesday" synchronizes all forty closes into the same 5-9 day window after month-end counts — the verdict's own 13.3 h/month budget is 1.9 hours a day for seven consecutive days, in the same week as the founder's own restaurant month-end, and 13.3 h is 51-102% of the 13-26 h/month that must also cover onboarding, support, sales, engineering, the Cost Index and the storefront. Third, the commercial argument — "half the labor at nearly twice the sticker," the count shrunk by Tier-2, Tier-3 and the market prior — has no code path: nothing substitutes a derived value for a counted ending QUANTITY (the market prior only prices what was already counted), and the honesty ladder actively punishes the attempt, since any estimated item forces `confidence = "medium"` and `countedShare < 0.5` forces `"low"` (`inventory-reconcile.ts:296-313`). So the plan's own machine will stamp "medium" on a deliverable sold as an audit opinion, every month, for every customer. Fourth, the amortization claim that justifies DMV clustering — "marginal cost scales with distinct vendor layouts, not with customers" — is false as built: templates are keyed `(org_id, vendor_id, layout_hash, field_path)` (`services/extract/template_store.py:73`) and line-item rules need four confirmations to promote (`template_store.py:100`), so every new customer re-pays 32-48 hand-reviewed invoices for vendors Muntin has already templated hundreds of times; the only cross-org path is an opt-in-both-ways community pool that is off by default (`apps/api/src/routes/community-templates.ts:19-30`). Fifth, onboarding is roughly 60-75 days to a first correct close and nobody scoped it — the baseline count sheet is built only from purchases inside the window (`inventory.ts:295-330`), so a real Beginning leg needs one full purchase month first — while `docs/tos.md:68` already promises pro-rated annual refunds, meaning a customer can cancel before the first deliverable and recover most of a $6,000-$12,000 prepay after the founder has spent the entire onboarding cost. Sixth, there is no support function and the runbooks say so out loud: `runbooks/slos.md:78` "we do not yet sell SLAs," `runbooks/incident-response.md:22` "founder is sole on-call" with the pager to be configured "on first day of paid beta," and both incident and extraction drills logged as "Not yet executed" — a P2 one-hour first response is a promise a man on a restaurant floor cannot keep. The good news is that the desk load is far more compressible than the verdict assumes: 44.5% of the entire exception queue comes from 12.6% of documents, all of them non-food vendors (Cintas 54.3%, Staples 37.5%, Cozzini 20.8% — `VENDOR-TEMPLATE-KEY.md:219-228`), and two measured, tested flags that cut desk minutes are simply off in production. Fix the schedule, build the close object, scope the ledger to food, flip the flags, and this is deliverable at ten to twenty locations; leave them and the second Tuesday is the date the company misses in public.

## Neglected
- **[DISQUALIFYING] The signed, dated close — the entire unit of value — does not exist as an object, and there is no review-or-sign seat in the flow.**
  - Evidence: `apps/api/src/lib/inventory-period-store.ts:14-38` defines PeriodSnapshot with no signature, no close_date, no delivered_at, no version and no supersedes field. `apps/api/src/routes/inventory.ts:608` writes it synchronously when the operator submits the count — no human step between count submit and the number existing. The only Ed25519 signing in the product is over an invoice-package manifest (`apps/api/src/routes/accountant-handoffs.ts:858-870`), which attests file contents, not that the four legs foot. The `pdfs` and `accountant` delivery shapes return 503 (`accountant-handoffs.ts:51,289`). Delivery today is a single-use, 7-day-TTL token (`accountant-handoffs.ts:38-45`) that 410s on second open — shorter than a close cycle and hostile to a CPA who re-opens during review.
  - Consequence: The product cannot produce the artifact the price is attached to. Worse, the first ten closes would be hand-assembled outside the system, which means the delivery cost is founder-hours with no software leverage and no audit trail — the exact opposite of what is being sold.
- **[DISQUALIFYING] "Your month closes on the second Tuesday" synchronizes the entire book into one week and consumes 51-102% of total founder capacity before support, onboarding, sales or engineering.**
  - Evidence: 13-26 founder-hours/month ÷ 40 locations = 19.5-39 minutes per location per month for every function combined. The verdict's own budget of ~13.3 h/month of close review is 19.9 min/location — 102% of the low bound and 51% of the high bound, for close review alone. Because all counts land at month-end and all closes land on one date, that 13.3 h compresses into 5-9 days: ~1.9 h/day for seven consecutive days, in the same week the founder's own FOH job is closing its month. `runbooks/incident-response.md:22` confirms there is no second person: "Sprint 0: founder is sole on-call."
  - Consequence: The promise breaks first at 15-20 locations, not 40, and it breaks publicly on a date the company chose and published. The audit already established that a missed obligation reads worse than an absent one; a synchronized deadline converts a smoothable workload into a guaranteed annual sequence of public misses.
- **[DISQUALIFYING] "The count is shrunk, not performed" — the whole TCO argument — has no code path, and the product's own confidence ladder penalizes the attempt.**
  - Evidence: Nothing substitutes a derived value for a counted ENDING QUANTITY. The market prior is a valuation fallback only (`estimated = valuationSource !== 'invoice'`, `inventory-reconcile.ts:245-249`); Tier-2 theoretical is a variance comparison; Tier-3 pars are reorder suggestions. The only count-shrinking mechanism that exists is `mode: "value"` (sort by descending spend, `inventory.ts:328-334`), which is free today. And `inventory-reconcile.ts:296-313` forces `confidence = "medium"` whenever `estimatedCount > 0` and `"low"` whenever `countedShare < 0.5` in non-spot mode — so shrinking the count and using market priors mechanically downgrades the label on the deliverable.
  - Consequence: The customer pays $600/month PLUS their own $100-150 of floor labor, and receives a statement stamped medium-or-low confidence. Every close becomes a support conversation explaining why the audit opinion is qualified. "Half the labor at nearly twice the sticker" is unsupported by any shipped mechanism, and it was the commercial argument.
- **[DISQUALIFYING] Time-to-first-correct-close is roughly 60-75 days and nobody scoped it, while the ToS already promises pro-rated annual refunds against a prepay taken up front.**
  - Evidence: `buildCountSheet` (`inventory.ts:295-330`) builds rows from purchases in the window plus prior count lines; on a baseline count `priorLines` is null, so slow-moving stock (dry goods, spices, wine) is simply not on the first sheet — a real Beginning leg needs one full purchase month first. Line-item rules require four confirmations to promote (`template_store.py:100`), so 8-12 vendors means 32-48 hand-reviewed invoices before the engine is trained for that org. `apps/api/src/routes/onboarding.ts:50-56` measures a completely different journey (passkey, recovery phrase, subscription, first extraction, first posting) — none of the close prerequisites: no catalog mapping, no opening inventory, no net-sales source, no stations. `docs/tos.md:68`: "Refunds: pro-rated on cancellation for annual plans."
  - Consequence: The riskiest 60-75 days of the relationship are unmeasured, unpromised and unpriced, and a customer who walks in month two recovers ~10/12 of a $6,000-$12,000 prepay after the founder has already spent the full onboarding cost. At ten pilot locations onboarding lands as an unbudgeted spike in the same months as the first closes.
- **[MAJOR] "Marginal cost scales with distinct vendor layouts, not with customers" is false as built — templates are org-scoped, so onboarding cost is roughly constant per customer, not decaying.**
  - Evidence: `services/extract/template_store.py:73` keys templates on `(org_id, vendor_id, layout_hash, field_path)`; the store's read paths filter `t.org_id == org_id` (`:669,751`). The only cross-org sharing is `/v1/community-templates`, described in its own header as "the opt-in cross-tenant template pool" where "the caller's org MUST be opted in (the read side is a contribution; symmetry keeps the opt-in meaningful)" (`apps/api/src/routes/community-templates.ts:19-30`) — off by default and requiring the customer to consent to contributing redacted rules.
  - Consequence: The DMV-clustering rationale and the 40-location ceiling both assume an amortization the code does not provide. Customer #20 pays nearly the same setup cost as customer #1 for Chefs' Warehouse, a vendor the corpus has already seen 214-248 times. This is the difference between a business with operating leverage and a services practice.
- **[MAJOR] 44.5% of the exception queue comes from 12.6% of documents — all non-food vendors that have no business in a food-cost close — and two measured, ready flags that cut desk minutes are off in production.**
  - Evidence: From the per-vendor table at `services/extract/VENDOR-TEMPLATE-KEY.md:219-228`: weighted reconcile across the ten vendors is 83.1% (n=533, ~90 failures). Cintas (54.3%, n=35), Staples (37.5%, n=8) and Cozzini Bros (20.8%, n=24) are 67 documents (12.6%) but 40 of the ~90 failures (44.5%). Excluding them lifts reconcile to 89.3%. Separately, `EXTRACT_MULTI_TABLE_HARVEST` defaults to False (`services/extract/engine_pkg/orchestrate.py:1112`) and `services/extract/README.md:138` records that with it off "chefswarehouse loses ~67% of its rows" — the #2 vendor in the buyer's own defined mix — while flipping it recovered +1011 line items with the reconcile rate unchanged. `EXTRACT_VENDOR_TRUST_ACTIVE` (`services/extract/main.py:120`) holds a measured 24%→62% first-pass improvement and requires `=="1"`, set nowhere.
  - Consequence: The verdict sized the exception desk off an aggregate (84-87%) that includes linen, knife-sharpening and office-supply routes which contribute nothing to food cost, and it never touches the two switches that most reduce the founder's minutes. Roughly half the desk load is self-inflicted and removable before a single customer signs.
- **[MAJOR] There is no support function, no sellable SLA, no drilled on-call and no status component for the thing being sold.**
  - Evidence: `runbooks/slos.md:78-80`: "Per-customer SLAs. We do not yet sell SLAs; the private-beta SLOs are operator-facing best-effort." `runbooks/incident-response.md:12-16` posts P1 15-minute / P2 1-hour / P3 4-business-hour first responses; `:22-26` says the founder is sole on-call and that the BetterStack pager and SMS backup "are set up in BetterStack on first day of paid beta" — i.e. not set up. Both `incident-response.md` and `extraction-degradation.md` log "Last drill: Not yet executed." `infra/status-page/components.yaml` registers ingest, extraction and review components with latency monitors, but nothing that says "your close shipped on time." Support is one inbox: `hello@muntin.digital` (copy.ts:4317), unstaffed and unmeasured.
  - Consequence: At forty customers depending on a monthly number, inbound will be dominated by three predictable classes — "why is my number qualified," "this vendor's invoice is wrong," and "my CPA has a question" — each landing in the close week, the one week with zero slack. Publishing response times a person on a restaurant shift cannot honor converts a support gap into a broken promise.
- **[MAJOR] No restatement policy, credit memos structurally arrive after the close date, and the per-location price sells into the one shape that is arithmetically broken.**
  - Evidence: `PeriodSnapshot` has no `supersedes` or version column (`inventory-period-store.ts:14-38`); `write()` appends and `latestForLocation()` returns most-recent, so a corrected close silently shadows the signed one with no restatement record. Credit memos are already dropped from `line_item_observations` (documented defect), and a credit issued days after month-end cannot land before a second-Tuesday signature. `apps/api/src/lib/inventory-purchases-store.ts:19-20` states plainly "No location filter: invoices are not tied to a location_key," while counts and count sheets are per-location — and the verdict prices per location at a 1-3 location buyer.
  - Consequence: Every signed close is provisional in a product whose promise is that it is not, with no mechanism to say so. And because quantity = locations, revenue scales precisely with the defect: the modal two-location customer's close mixes the other location's invoices into this location's usage on day one, at $1,200/month.

## Recommendations
- [founder] **Kill the synchronized "second Tuesday." Replace it with a per-customer close slot assigned at signature — "your close is delivered within five business days of us receiving your count" — and spread slots across the month so no more than two or three closes fall in any week. Publish the customer's own date on their account page, not a company-wide date on the storefront.** — A single public date compresses the entire book into the one week the founder has least capacity, in the same week his employer closes its month. Staggering is why accounting firms have staggered year-ends. It costs nothing, preserves the whole positioning (a fixed, promised date MarginEdge's rolling turnaround cannot match), and converts a guaranteed public miss into a smoothable load.
- [agent] **Instrument `desk_minutes_per_close` before selling anything. Add `review_started_at`/`reviewed_at` to the extractions store and a `prepared_at`/`signed_at`/`delivered_at` triple to the close record, and report minutes per close from customer one. Today those fields exist nowhere in `apps/api/src`.** — The verdict names `desk_minutes_per_close` as "the one number that decides whether it is more than" a $288K business, and then adds no apparatus to measure it. Every capacity claim in the plan — 13.3 h/month, forty locations, the honest one-founder ceiling — is an unmeasured assertion in a company whose brand is not publishing numbers it cannot stand behind.
- [agent] **Build the close as a first-class object before the pilot opens: a `closes` row with period, location, `prepared_at`, `reviewed_by`, `signed_at`, `signature`, `withheld[]` with reasons, `supersedes_close_id`, `delivered_at`, and a durable (not 7-day, not single-use) share link for the CPA. Count submit produces a DRAFT close; only a signature publishes a number. Restatement is a new close that names its predecessor.** — The unit of value has no representation in the schema, the count-submit path prints a number with no human in it (`inventory.ts:608`), and there is no restatement record — which credit memos guarantee will be needed every month. Selling an audit opinion without a signing step, a withhold record and a restatement chain is selling the one thing that cannot be produced.
- [agent] **Scope the food-cost ledger to food vendors. Classify vendors at onboarding and exclude linen, uniform, knife-route, office-supply and equipment vendors from the Purchases leg, the count sheet and the exception desk — showing them in a separate, unpriced "other spend" list.** — Cintas, Staples and Cozzini Bros are 12.6% of documents and 44.5% of exception failures (VENDOR-TEMPLATE-KEY.md:219-228); removing them lifts reconcile from 83.1% to 89.3% and cuts desk minutes roughly in half at zero cost to the number being sold. It also makes the Purchases leg correct: knife sharpening is not food cost, and including it inflates usage.
- [founder] **Flip `EXTRACT_MULTI_TABLE_HARVEST` and `EXTRACT_VENDOR_TRUST_ACTIVE` in a measured pilot on the founder's own corpus during the Phase-0 walk, and record the desk-minute delta. Both are built, tested and validated; both default off (`orchestrate.py:1112`, `main.py:120`).** — With multi-table harvest off, Chefs' Warehouse — the second-largest vendor in the buyer profile the verdict itself defines — drops ~67% of its rows into manual review (README.md:138). Vendor trust holds a measured 24%→62% first-pass improvement. These are the two largest single levers on delivery cost in the entire company and they appear in none of the verdict's four phases.
- [founder] **Publish an honest onboarding term and stop charging against it: the annual term starts at the FIRST SIGNED CLOSE, not at signature. Months 0-1 are a named, unbilled ramp with published milestones (vendors templated, catalog mapped, baseline count taken, net-sales source connected). Rewrite `docs/tos.md:61-69` so the pro-rated-refund clause cannot be exercised against work already spent.** — A real Beginning leg requires one full purchase month before the baseline count is complete (`inventory.ts:295-330`), and 8-12 vendors need 32-48 hand-reviewed invoices to train (`template_store.py:100`) — so the first correct close is ~60-75 days out. Taking $6,000-$12,000 up front for a deliverable that cannot arrive for two months, against a pro-rated refund promise, is the single largest uncompensated risk in the plan and it is entirely avoidable.
- [founder] **Design the minimum support function and publish only what a person on a shift can honor: one channel (hello@), next-business-day response, two-business-day during close week, an explicit "we do not operate a pager" statement, and a make-good defined as a free month rather than a cash credit. Delete or scope the P1/P2/P3 response times in `incident-response.md` to internal use until a second person exists.** — `slos.md:78` already admits no SLA is sold; `incident-response.md:22` admits a single on-call whose pager is unconfigured and whose drills read "Not yet executed." Publishing 15-minute and 1-hour first responses is the same class of error as publishing a BLS index as $393/lb — a number the company cannot stand behind. An honest, unimpressive support promise is worth more here than an impressive one that fails in the close week.
- [founder] **Cap the pilot by CONCURRENT CLOSES, not by locations-ever. Start at five locations, gate every expansion on two measured facts — desk minutes per close and minutes of inbound support per close — and only then decide the ceiling. Separately, put the scale question to the founder in these terms: an audit opinion requires a signer and therefore cannot scale past one calendar; a closed month can. If the ambition is scale, the signature must move to the METHOD (the limits register, the gates, the published refusals) rather than to each month's number.** — At 20 min/close the ceiling is 40 locations only if support, onboarding, sales and engineering are all zero. At a realistic 45-90 min the honest ceiling is 10-20 locations, i.e. $72K-$144K/year — which changes the answer to the unresolved scale question in both directions. Neither branch is served by an ops model whose only throughput lever is the founder's calendar, and nobody has said so.

## Would change my verdict
The single fact that would most change this verdict is **measured desk minutes and support minutes for one real close of a customer who is not Don** — specifically: (a) minutes of founder time to clear the exception queue, review the reconciliation notes, decide withhold-vs-publish, and sign; (b) minutes of inbound questions in the fourteen days after delivery. It can be obtained cheaply and almost immediately: during the Phase-0 walk the founder should run his own two Tacombi locations through the PRODUCT path (not the Mac CLIs) with a stopwatch and a plain text log, then repeat it once with a single friendly second restaurant at zero price. That is roughly two evenings plus one month of elapsed time, and it costs nothing but the founder's attention. If desk time lands under ~25 minutes per close and support under ~15 minutes per customer-month, forty locations is defensible and my verdict softens to ENDORSE_WITH_CONDITIONS on the delivery items alone. If it lands north of ~60 minutes — which the org-scoped template training, the non-food exception concentration and the medium-confidence explanations all suggest it will for the first year — then the honest ceiling is ten to twenty locations, the second-Tuesday promise must be staggered before it is ever published, and the price/cohort arithmetic in the verdict needs rebuilding rather than repair.
