# Venture Ambition with a Team of One-Plus-Agents

## Thesis
Surface area is nearly free; promises are not — and Muntin did not build 1,368 pages, it built 1,368 obligations. Craigslist serves an enormous page count on a tiny team because no Craigslist page asserts anything about tomorrow, whereas Muntin attached a dated promise to almost every surface it shipped (a "first Tuesday" dispatch on 83 pages, a warrant canary, `<!-- src: -->` cites, count sentinels, EN↔ES parity), which is why obligations reached ~53 h/month against 13–26 available. The commit history proves the deeper problem is not volume but shape: 266 of 344 commits in the last 60 days landed on 8 days, and 24 of 60 days produced nothing — work at Muntin is not a rate, it is an impulse that exists only while a session is running, while decay runs on all 60 days. The fix is not a better backlog, because the audit already proved backlogs do not work here (26% close rate, zero closures ever from working a list); it is to make the machine produce *reviewable units* during the gaps and reduce the founder's job to yes/no. Muntin has already built that mechanism four times — mutmut-nightly, flake-tracker, competitor-claims, ocr-bench all run on a cron, diff against yesterday, and open a labelled Issue — but every one of them stops at the Issue, because no scheduled agent session has ever been pointed at the queue. On review: the answer is not more gates (129 storefront gates, 27 of 40 asserting shape, zero doing network I/O, 56 of 320 tautologies, 37.5% phantom reds on the founder's only feedback loop — adding a gate is now negative-value by default); it is the exact adversarial-verification architecture this company ran on itself today, where a second agent must try to refute the first, the founder reviews only the surviving claim plus a random sample, and the sample produces a published calibration number so the filter's own error rate is measured rather than trusted. Determinism is the hidden asset in all of this: `no-llm-ci.sh` is filed as a privacy guarantee, but its larger value is that a deterministic read path is *simulatable*, which is what let FoundationDB's small team ship a near-zero-bug database and is what will let one person safely accept high-volume agent output on the product. Fundraising is premature and possibly unnecessary — the binding constraint is founder-attention and zero confirmed users, and money buys neither — but if the operating system above works, the operating system itself is the venture story, more so than the restaurant tool.

## Mechanisms
### WhatsApp
**What:** Ran a service at massive scale on an engineering team of roughly 32–35 at the 2014 acquisition, serving ~450M users (VERIFIED: Sequoia's own post and multiple engineering write-ups corroborate ~32 engineers / 450M users; the exact headcount varies 32–35 by source). The leverage came from a product with almost no states: one screen, one verb, no settings worth supporting, no content moderation surface, no enterprise tier, and a runtime (Erlang/BEAM) whose scaling unit was machines rather than people.

**Why it worked:** Support burden was ENGINEERED to zero rather than staffed. A product where the user can only be in one of a few states generates almost no tickets, so headcount never had to track users. Every unit of scale was absorbed by infrastructure the team did not have to grow to operate.

**For Muntin:** The infrastructure half transfers cleanly and already exists: static HTML on Cloudflare, a Worker API, a deterministic Python extractor — marginal cost per user is near zero. The support half does NOT transfer and Muntin should stop hoping it will. An extraction error is a dispute about a number, and this company's entire brand is that its numbers are right, so every wrong read is a ticket only a human can settle. The honest adaptation is to cap the beta at a number of operators Don can personally serve (10–25) and treat the existing `needs_review` queue AS the support surface, rather than pretending a support-free product is achievable here.

### Craigslist
**What:** Sustained an enormous page count and a national footprint on a famously tiny staff, for decades, by making refusal the core competency: refused redesigns, refused most advertising, refused feature requests, refused to monetize most categories. (UNVERIFIED: specific headcount figures for Craigslist are widely repeated but I did not confirm them; the refusal posture itself is well documented.)

**Why it worked:** Page count is not maintenance load — PROMISES are. A Craigslist page makes no assertion about tomorrow, so a million of them cost the same as one. Refusal was not asceticism; it was the mechanism that kept obligation flat while surface grew.

**For Muntin:** This is the sharpest reframe available for Muntin's 1,368 pages. The pages are not the problem; the dated promises attached to them are. 83 Cost-Index pages promise a monthly email; a warrant canary promises a monthly signature (89 days overdue); `<!-- src: -->` cites promise a number is current (at least 72 now false). Muntin's job is not to delete pages — it is to audit every page for what it PROMISES and either automate the promise, downgrade it to undated prose, or retract it. Retracting a promise is honest; letting it rot is the thing that actually breaches the brand.

### SQLite
**What:** A team of roughly three maintains one of the most widely deployed pieces of software in the world with a ~590:1 test-code-to-source ratio, ~51,445 TCL test cases plus ~50,362 TH3 cases, four independent test harnesses, and a public commitment to 100% branch and MC/DC coverage maintained on every release since 3.6.17 (2009). (VERIFIED against sqlite.org/testing.html and TH3 documentation.)

**Why it worked:** The mechanism is not 'many tests' — it is ONE published, ratcheting number that cannot go down. Coverage is measured, stated publicly, and treated as a floor, so no change can quietly weaken verification. A tiny team can review enormous change volume because the verification substrate, not the human, is what certifies each change.

**For Muntin:** Muntin has 169 gates and no ratcheting number. Pick exactly one per repo that cannot go down and publish it: for the product, mutation-kill rate on `services/extract` (mutmut already runs nightly); for the storefront, the fraction of published numeric claims that a gate can independently re-derive from frozen data — which today is close to zero, since `<!-- count: -->` and `<!-- repro: -->` are machine-enforced but `<!-- src: -->` is not. A single ratcheting number is worth more than the 191st gate, and it is agent-maintainable.

### FoundationDB
**What:** Built a deterministic simulation framework BEFORE building the database — a single-process simulator that reproduces a network of interacting nodes with injected disk, process, network, and request failures, all replayable from a seed. A small team shipped a near-zero-bug distributed database at speed, and the team's later company (Antithesis) exists to sell this mechanism. (VERIFIED: Will Wilson's 2014 Strange Loop talk and the FoundationDB paper.)

**Why it worked:** The verification substrate could explore more failure scenarios per hour than the team could introduce bugs. Determinism is the precondition — a replayable seed turns 'a flaky bug we cannot reproduce' into 'a test case', which is the only way a small team survives a large state space.

**For Muntin:** This is the single most under-recognized asset in the company, and it is filed under the wrong heading. `no-llm-ci.sh` is marketed as a privacy guarantee; its larger value is that a deterministic read path is SIMULATABLE in a way no LLM-based competitor's is. Muntin already has golden fixtures, `first_try_corpus.py`, a nightly OCR A/B bench, and mutation testing — the pieces of a simulator without the harness. An agent session can build a seeded fuzzer over synthetic invoice layouts that replays from a seed, and from then on every agent-authored extractor change is certified by the simulator rather than by Don reading Python. That is what makes high-volume agent output on the PRODUCT safe.

### Muntin itself (the 2026-08-07 audit)
**What:** Ran 38 agents — 15 domain auditors, 15 adversarial verifiers instructed to REFUTE their counterpart, 8 cross-cutting lenses — then had an independent critic re-check 10 findings, producing a stated calibration: 6 reproduced exactly, 2 overstated, 2 contradicted by their own evidence. The adversary downgraded 54 findings and upgraded 9. The orchestrator personally reproduced the highest-stakes claims.

**Why it worked:** Agent-on-agent refutation converts unbounded output into a small set of survivors, and the sampling step converts trust into a MEASURED error rate rather than a hope. Critically, the adversary's downgrades (54) vastly outnumbered upgrades (9), which is exactly the asymmetry a review filter needs: it is cheap to suppress noise and expensive to miss a real defect, and the numbers show the filter is tuned that way.

**For Muntin:** This is the review architecture, and Muntin proved it works on its own codebase today. Generalize it from a one-off audit into the standing PR pipeline: every agent-authored PR carries an adversary agent's verdict plus an explicit list of what the adversary COULD NOT check, and Don reviews only what survives refutation. Then add the piece the audit had and the pipeline currently lacks — a weekly re-review of a random 5% of auto-merged PRs, published as a running false-negative rate. Without that sampling loop the whole architecture is faith; with it, it is an instrument.

### Midjourney
**What:** Reached annualized revenue reported significantly above $200M with roughly 40 employees and zero outside funding since founding in 2021, profitable within roughly six months of public launch. (PARTIALLY VERIFIED: the ~40 employees / $0 outside funding / >$200M annualized figures trace to The Information via multiple secondary reports; the widely circulated $500M ARR figure is secondary-source only — treat as UNVERIFIED.)

**Why it worked:** Distribution was rented, not built (Discord), the product had one input and one output, and profitability arrived before any structural need for capital. Never raising meant never acquiring the obligations — board, growth targets, headcount — that force a company off a small-team operating model.

**For Muntin:** The direct lesson is that not raising keeps the small-team model available, and Muntin's cost base (Cloudflare static, one Worker, one Python service) plus the founder's salary make that genuinely viable. The distribution lesson is sharper: Midjourney did not build a storefront, it went where the audience already was. Muntin built 1,368 pages and 4 waitlist forms. The CC0 index, a DataCite record sitting with `"doi": null`, and 1 embeddable card out of 82 are the equivalent of an unshipped Discord — the audience-reaching surfaces are built and disconnected.

### 37signals / Basecamp
**What:** Stayed deliberately small for two decades by treating scope as a standing refusal: no enterprise tier, no acquisitions-driven roadmap, posted pricing, published opinions about what they will not do. Their operating cadence (fixed appetite, shape-then-build, cycles that end) is designed so that unfinished work is dropped rather than carried.

**Why it worked:** A published refusal is enforceable in a way a private intention is not — customers, and more importantly the team, can hold you to it. Fixed appetites make 'we ran out of time' a normal outcome rather than a failure, which prevents the accretion of half-finished obligations.

**For Muntin:** Muntin retires things (the course, /method/, the web-studio line) so the muscle exists — but it accretes far faster than it retires: `check-all` went from ~90 checks to 320 in 33 days and NOTHING in either repo has ever retired a gate. Make refusal mechanical: every gate registry entry, page cadence, and manual-builder entry gets a required `expires` field, and an expired entry reds CI until it is renewed with a reason or deleted. That converts scope discipline from a virtue into a gate — and it is the only structural fix for the ratchet the audit measured.

### Plenty of Fish
**What:** Markus Frind built and ran the site alone from a Vancouver apartment, taking no venture capital and retaining full ownership, and sold to Match Group for $575M in 2015. (VERIFIED: the $575M sale and zero outside funding.) IMPORTANT CORRECTION: he hired his first employee in 2008 and the company had roughly 100 employees at the time of sale — the solo phase was the EARLY phase, not the scale phase.

**Why it worked:** The solo years worked because the product was a two-sided marketplace with user-generated inventory and ad-based monetization — near-zero content cost, near-zero support expectation, and no promise of accuracy about anything. Once the business acquired obligations (moderation, trust and safety, ad sales), headcount followed and could not be avoided.

**For Muntin:** Cite this one as a caution, not a template. The widely repeated 'solo at huge scale' story is a compression that drops the 100 people. The transferable half is the pre-2008 half: keep obligations low enough that one person plus machines can carry them, and recognize the exact moment the obligation curve turns — for Muntin that moment is the first paying operator whose invoice number is disputed.

### Instagram
**What:** Roughly 13 people at the $1B acquisition with tens of millions of users, on managed AWS infrastructure, having previously killed a broader product (Burbn) down to a single verb: post a square photo with a filter.

**Why it worked:** The scope reduction came FIRST and was violent — a working product was cut to one feature. Leverage was a consequence of that cut, not a cause. Managed infrastructure meant scaling was a bill, not a hiring plan.

**For Muntin:** Muntin has never performed the Burbn cut. It has retired lines that were already dead (course, method, web studio) but has not cut anything alive. The candidate cut is real and uncomfortable: of six app shells, only two are substantial (`apps/api` ~590 files, `apps/web` ~361); desktop (16 files), ios (10), email-worker (11) and mobile (55) are crypto-parity and queue stubs carrying real CI cost — mobile.yml, visual-regression, e2e — against zero users. Freeze the four, keep the crypto parity vectors as tests, and reclaim the CI surface and the review attention they consume.

## Recommendations
- **Build the Nightly Agent Shift: a scheduled agent session that consumes a labelled Issue queue and returns PRs, never merges. The four existing nightly jobs (mutmut, flake-tracker, competitor-claims, ocr-bench) already produce labelled Issues; the storefront produces exactly one (freshness-heartbeat) across 129 gates. Add issue-emission to the storefront's decay-detecting gates, then point a nightly session at the `agent-ready` label.**
  - This is the direct fix for the audit's central finding. The 26% close rate with zero closures ever from working a list is not a discipline failure — it is that only an agent session produces work and no session has ever been pointed at the backlog. The commit data confirms the shape: 266 of 344 commits in 60 days fell on 8 days, and 24 of 60 days produced nothing. Decay runs on all 60 days; production runs on 8. A cron-triggered session inverts that without adding a single founder-hour. The company invented this exact pattern four times in the product repo and stopped one step short of the payoff every time.
  - Agent-executable: Fully agent-executable, and the scheduling primitives already exist in the founder's own tooling (scheduled sessions / cron-fired agent runs). One session writes: (a) a shared `lib/emit-issue.mjs` that dedupes on a stable key, modeled on freshness-heartbeat.yml's existing rolling-issue logic; (b) issue-emission wired into the ~10 storefront gates that detect DECAY rather than shape (freshness, dispatch lag, canary age, frozen feeds, stale manual builders); (c) a `.github/workflows/agent-shift.yml` that fires a session nightly with a fixed prompt: take the top 3 `agent-ready` issues, one PR each, ≤200 lines, each with a test that fails before and passes after, never merge. Founder does nothing but approve the first workflow file.
- **Replace the daily backlog with a 20-minute merge window, and auto-close anything that survives two windows unmerged. Founder reviews the CLAIM and the receipt, never the diff.**
  - A list item requires the reader to reconstruct context, decide, and execute — three acts, which is why 38 findings outlived their session and only 10 ever closed, all incidentally. A PR requires one act: yes or no. The auto-close rule is the part that matters most and will feel wrong: it makes non-closure visible and cheap, so the queue stops lying about what will never be done. Today the board is 1,488 immaculate lines of things that will not happen; a queue that expires tells the truth about capacity.
  - Agent-executable: Agent-executable. A session adds a PR template with three required fields — the claim in one sentence, the command a skeptic runs to refute it, and what the adversary could not check — plus a `stale-pr` workflow that closes agent-authored PRs after 48h with a comment. NOT delegable: the yes/no itself. That is the founder's job and it is the only recurring one he should keep daily.
- **Make every agent PR carry an adversarial verdict. A second agent, instructed to refute the first, must attempt the claim and report both its verdict and its blind spots. Route by blast radius: only published-number changes and product read-path changes reach the founder; parity, formatting, and internal-doc changes auto-merge on a green adversary.**
  - This is the answer to the panel's hardest question, and Muntin proved it works on itself today: 15 adversarial verifiers downgraded 54 findings and upgraded 9 — exactly the asymmetry a filter needs. Blast-radius routing is Chromium/Google's rationing model and it fixes the real scarcity, which is attention rather than time: a 4,000-line ES parity refresh deserves zero human review if a gate proves parity, while a 3-line change to a published price number deserves full attention. Today those two get identical treatment, which is why the important one gets the same shrug as the trivial one.
  - Agent-executable: Agent-executable. One session writes a `blast-radius.mjs` classifier over changed paths (tier 1: `data/cost-index*.json`, `sourced-claims.json`, any file with `<!-- src: -->`, `services/extract/**`, `apps/api/src/routes/**`; tier 2: everything else) and an `adversary.yml` workflow that fires a refutation session on every agent PR and posts its verdict as a review. Founder approves the tier-1 path list once — that list IS the decision, and it should never be delegated.
- **Add the calibration loop: re-review a random 5% of auto-merged PRs weekly and publish the running false-negative rate internally. If it degrades, tighten the routing; if it holds at zero for a quarter, widen it.**
  - Without this, layers 0–3 are faith. The audit itself modeled the discipline — 10 findings re-checked, 6 exact, 2 overstated, 2 contradicted, an 80% substantive survival rate stated openly — and that single number is what makes the rest of the audit usable. The same instrument applied to the merge pipeline turns 'I think the agents are producing correct work' into a measured claim, which is the only version of that statement this company is allowed to make. It is also the number an investor should be shown, because it is the proof that the operating model is real.
  - Agent-executable: Agent-executable end to end. A weekly workflow samples merged PRs, spawns a fresh session with no memory of the original work to independently verify each one's claim, and appends the result to `docs/ops/calibration.jsonl`. Founder reads one number per week. NOT delegable: the decision to tighten or widen — that is a risk-appetite call.
- **Mutation-test the gates themselves: corrupt each gate's input and assert the gate reds. A gate that does not fail when you break its input is not a gate. Then delete or fix everything that survives, and stop adding gates.**
  - The audit hand-classified 40 gates and found 27 assert shape; zero of 129 storefront gates do network I/O; 56 of check-all's 320 entries are tautologies where the deploy runs a builder then asks the builder whether its own output matches. The cost is not neutral: 186 seconds, and 3 of 8 local reds are phantoms the deploy heals — a 37.5% false-alarm rate on the founder's only feedback loop, which is precisely how red becomes background noise and how PR #536 got 'merged with three reds the founder accepted'. One meta-gate would have caught the `<!-- src: -->` hole, the idem-coverage substring-match bug, and the tautologies in a single pass. mutmut already does exactly this for the extract service nightly — the technique is in-house and just needs porting.
  - Agent-executable: Agent-executable. A session writes `check-gates-are-grounded.mjs`: for each wired gate, mutate a copy of its input fixtures (flip a number, delete a sentinel, stale a date), run the gate, and assert non-zero exit. Emits a table of which gates are inert. NOT delegable: deciding whether an inert gate gets fixed or deleted — but with the table in hand that is a 20-minute pass, and deletion should be the default.
- **Run a promise audit and pay down or retract the dated promises, starting with the warrant canary. Then install the retirement ratchet: every gate, cadence, and manual-builder entry carries a required `expires` field, and expiry reds CI until renewed with a reason or deleted.**
  - The 53 h/month is a promise bill, not a page bill — Craigslist's page count proves surface is nearly free when nothing asserts a future. The canary is the most dangerous artifact in either repo and the clearest retirement candidate: 89 days unsigned, and a stale canary is legitimately readable as a signal that a warrant arrived, so neglect actively transmits a false message. Either automate the ceremony or retract it publicly — both are honest; drift is not. The ratchet is the only structural fix for ~90 checks becoming 320 in 33 days with nothing ever retired, and it converts scope discipline from a virtue that decays into a gate that does not.
  - Agent-executable: Agent-executable inventory and enforcement: a session greps every dated promise on every indexed page ("first Tuesday", "monthly", "updated weekly", canary language, `<!-- src: -->`) and produces a table of promise → who pays it → last paid → automatable yes/no; then adds the `expires` field and the CI check. NOT delegable: the retract-or-automate call per promise, and specifically the canary decision, which is a legal-adjacent judgement Don must make personally.
- **Build the deterministic simulator for the extract engine and reframe `no-llm-ci.sh` internally as a verification asset, not just a privacy one. Seeded synthetic invoice generation, replayable failures, and a mutation-kill floor that ratchets.**
  - FoundationDB's small team shipped a near-zero-bug distributed database because the simulator explored more failure space per hour than the team could write bugs; SQLite's three people hold 100% MC/DC. Both mechanisms require determinism, and Muntin has it — build-gated, falsifiable by a stranger in 30 seconds. Every competitor saying 'AI-powered' has forfeited this capability permanently, which makes it a durable structural advantage rather than a positioning line. Practically: this is what makes agent-authored changes to the read path safe to merge without Don reading the Python, which is the only way the product survives an agent workforce.
  - Agent-executable: Agent-executable, and the substrate is already there — `services/extract/tests/golden/real_fixtures`, `first_try_corpus.py`, `fixtures/`, the nightly OCR A/B bench, mutmut. A session writes a seeded synthetic-invoice generator over the existing layout/vendor-template space, wires replay-from-seed into the golden harness, and adds a mutation-kill-rate floor to CI that can only go up. NOT delegable: nothing here, once the founder approves the floor number.
- **Do not raise yet. Spend the next quarter buying the three facts an investor actually needs — 25 operators who ran an invoice and came back, a first dollar from paid analysis over the free index, and an unbroken six-month publication chain — and start by becoming your own first customer this week.**
  - The company is not fundable in its current shape and money would not relieve the binding constraint. Zero confirmed users after ~15 months, the founder has never run the product end to end, 547 product CTAs across 405 files point at a hostname neither repo routes, and ADR-020 — the decision to defer all revenue 12+ months, the largest strategic act in company history — contains the words market, customer, revenue and competitor exactly zero times, while the product repo already holds a 12-competitor analysis naming two live dislocations (Hubdoc's shutdown; Toast bundling invoice scanning) that appears in zero findings. An investor's objections write themselves: bus factor plus attention (24 zero-commit days in 60 is the evidence, not the headcount), 1,368 pages before one customer, and a moat that is 97.6% reconstructed backfill any competitor can rebuild from the same USDA/BLS/FRED feeds. The honest moat is the 2,025 live-captured points, the 10,294-entry revisions ledger and the dated publication chain — real, but three months old and worthless the moment cadence breaks. Note the genuine tension rather than resolving it falsely: the one thing a raise would buy is Don's Tacombi hours, and those hours are the source of the 'written from behind a real pass' asset. If the operating system above works, IT is the more interesting pitch — but only with the calibration number to prove it.
  - Agent-executable: Mostly NOT agent-executable, which is the point. Agents can clear the path: resolve the hostname question and set `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL` beside its guarded sibling, widen the broadcast filter at `src/worker.js:8793` so the 613 `footer` and 162 `cost-index-ingredient` subscribers stop being silently skipped, add waitlist forms beyond the 4 of 1,368 pages that have one, fix the `home-founding`/`ledger-pricing` enum so founding signups stop being relabelled with the retired studio line's name, and mint the DataCite DOI sitting at null. But running one of his own Tacombi invoices end to end, talking to the first 25 operators, and asking for the first dollar are founder-only — and the first of those is one shift away.

## Antipatterns
- Do not answer this audit with another registry. The audit's own sharpest observation is that Muntin converts findings into registries faster than into fixes, and a registry entry has exactly the enforcement power of the document it replaced. There must be no 'review architecture registry', no 'promise registry board section', no ADR that records the intent to build the queue. If a recommendation here does not become a workflow file or a PR within two sessions, it has already failed in the documented way — and the predictable fate of the 2026-08-07 audit was to become finding #50.
- Do not add gates. This is counterintuitive for a correctness brand and it is still correct: 129 storefront gates, 27 of 40 asserting shape, zero doing network I/O, 56 of 320 check-all entries tautological, 186 seconds, and 37.5% phantom reds on the founder's only feedback loop. Gate #191 has negative expected value because its marginal detection is near zero and its marginal contribution to red-as-background-noise is not. Mutation-test what exists and delete the inert ones before writing another.
- Do not cite Craigslist as license for neglecting craft. The founder has explicitly committed to design as the proof of capability, and that is the right call for a company with no brand legacy. Craigslist's transferable mechanism is refusal of scope, not refusal of quality — importing the aesthetic would contradict the vision doc's entire empowerment thesis and would read as smallness, which is the exact discount the founder is trying to avoid.
- Do not plan on WhatsApp's zero-support model. It is not available here. Their product had almost no states a user could be confused about; Muntin's product prints a number an operator will dispute, and the dispute is precisely the moment the brand is tested. Any plan whose capacity math assumes near-zero support is arithmetic fiction. Cap the beta instead.
- Do not repeat the Plenty of Fish solo-to-exit myth. Frind was solo until 2008 and the company had roughly 100 employees at the $575M sale. The compressed version of that story is a trap for a founder deciding how long one person plus agents can carry a business with real support obligations. Use the pre-2008 phase as the model and name the moment the obligation curve turns.
- Do not let 'capable company / we' drift into implying a team. The founder-vision doc already draws this line correctly and it must hold under growth pressure: declining to advertise headcount is honest; 'our engineers' is not, and it is trivially falsifiable from /about/, sourced-claims.json, and a public repo where 80–84% of commits are authored by Claude. One such line detonates the entire honesty brand, which is the only asset here a competitor cannot buy.
- Do not adopt any growth mechanism requiring overclaim, urgency manufacture, or paid placement. The Cost Index methodology page forecloses sponsorship and placement in writing, and the fact gate enforces the rest. Concretely this rules out the standard indie-hacker playbook items — fabricated social proof, invented user counts, 'trusted by N restaurants' before there are N restaurants, and launch-day metrics that cannot be traced. The audit found zero confirmed users; any surface implying otherwise is disqualifying.
- Do not expand the 645 Spanish pages, or any parity surface, until the EN promise bill is paid. Each translated page inherits every dated promise of its source, so ES parity doubles an obligation the company is already paying at roughly 10%. Parity is a virtue when the base cadence holds and a multiplier on decay when it does not.
- Do not use the archive's 85,700-observation headline in an investor or press context without the 97.6%-reconstructed caveat. It is accurate and it is not defensible as stated — any competent diligence rebuilds the backfill from the same public feeds. The honest and still-strong version names the 2,025 live-captured points, the revisions ledger, and the dated chain, and concedes the moat is young. Overstating this one would be caught by exactly the audience whose trust is the whole strategy.
- Do not build a fifth and sixth nightly detector before wiring the four that already exist to a consumer. mutmut-nightly, flake-tracker, competitor-claims and ocr-bench all correctly detect, correctly diff, and correctly open an Issue that no session has ever been assigned. Adding detection to a system whose failure is consumption is the same error the company has already made with gates, boards, and audits.
