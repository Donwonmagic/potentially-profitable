# Domain Correctness — is the food-cost number the product exists to print actually right?

## Thesis
The math is not wrong because anyone was careless; it is wrong because a component built correctly for one purpose was reused, without re-deriving its contract, as the input to a second purpose where every one of its conservative choices inverts into an error. `line_item_observations` was engineered for PRICE intelligence — it deliberately drops credit memos, drops $0 promo lines, drops any line whose pack token will not parse, and stores the invoice's printed `unit_price × quantity` rather than its printed `line_total`. Each of those is the right call for a price median. Every one of them is a defect when that same table becomes the Purchases leg of `Beginning + Purchases − Ending = Usage`, and nothing in the repo re-examined them at that seam. Layered on top is a second class of defect that is worse because it is invisible: the DEFAULT count mode writes every un-entered item at expected-on-hand (`prior ending + purchases`), which makes that item's usage arithmetically ZERO, so the headline "Your real food cost" is structurally biased low by whatever the un-counted tail actually consumed; and the Purchases roll-up carries no location predicate at all while sessions, counts, food cost and the location picker are all per-location — meaning in a two-location org, which is precisely Don's own Tacombi shape, each location's usage includes the other location's invoices. The honesty spine is real but narrower than it reads: the percent IS withheld when net sales is null, but a POS window with only 12 of 30 days settled sails straight through and prints against a partial denominator; the reconciliation flag DOES flag rather than fix, but it still sums the impossible number into COGS and still prints the percent at 48px; and `inventory-reconcile.ts` contains an empty `if (estimated) { }` block whose comment asserts the estimate is "counted toward confidence below" when it is not. The engineering quality here is genuinely above commercial norm — the fail-closed market prior, the pack parity vectors, the pure deterministic engines, the written rationale for every exclusion — which makes the gap all the more dangerous, because the disclosures are so credible that a bookkeeper will trust the number they surround. And that is the strategic collision: the GTM panel already established that the accounting firm is the only channel this price can afford, and the accounting firm is the exact buyer who will ask "does this include the other location's invoices, and where is my catch-weight meat?" The correctness debt and the channel decision are the same problem, and the number must be right before the channel is opened, not after.

## Portrait

In `value` mode (the default per ADR-006), `draftLines()` writes every un-entered row at `counted_base_qty = r.expected_base_qty`, and `expectedOnHandBaseQty` returns `prior ending + purchases in window`. Reconcile then computes `usage = beginning + purchases − ending`, which for those rows is identically 0. The tail's entire consumption disappears from COGS and its entire purchase value stays parked in Inventory value. The negative-usage checksum can never fire on a tail row either, because 0 is not negative — so the least-verified items are the ones the safety net structurally cannot catch. Direction: understates food cost %; overstates ending inventory. Magnitude scales with the tail's share of usage (the 80/20 framing implies roughly a fifth of dollars).

## Implications
- **Treat the Purchases leg as a NEW consumer of `line_item_observations` and re-derive its contract from scratch, rather than inheriting the price-intelligence contract. Concretely: carry `line_total` into the observation and value the leg from it; stop filtering out negative lines (credits must reduce Purchases) and $0 lines (free goods are on the shelf); stop dropping `base_uom IS NULL` items from the VALUE leg even where they must stay out of the QUANTITY leg. Write the seam down as an ADR so the next reuse re-derives too.**
  - Every one of those four exclusions is correct for a price median and wrong for an accounting identity, and no artifact in the repo records that the seam was re-examined when the table was reused. This is the single generative cause behind roughly half the defect list.
- **Change the value-count tail from 'fill at expected on-hand' to 'leave uncounted and withhold', or at minimum surface the tail's implied-zero usage as its own line on the food-cost screen. Do not ship a default mode that silently zeroes a component of the headline number.**
  - Filling the tail at expected on-hand makes its usage arithmetically zero, biases the flagship 'Your real food cost' percentage downward by the tail's real consumption, and makes the negative-usage checksum structurally incapable of firing on exactly the rows nobody counted. A product that withholds rather than guesses cannot have its default mode guess zero.
- **Either add a location predicate to the Purchases leg (which requires attributing invoices to a location — a real product decision, not a query fix) or disable the multi-location picker on count/food-cost/variance until that attribution exists. Do not leave a per-location screen fed by an org-wide numerator.**
  - The store's own header documents the single-location assumption; the picker shipped anyway. In the founder's own two-location shape each location's food cost is overstated and the two numbers double-count — and the first customer profile the GTM work points at (a bookkeeper carrying several restaurants) is multi-entity by construction.
- **Make the food-cost percentage WITHHELD, not merely low-confidence, in three named states: the reconciliation flag is set, POS sales coverage is incomplete for the period, or valued items fall below a stated share of spend. Print the four identity legs including Beginning on screen, and make them foot.**
  - The brand claim is 'holds a number back rather than print a guess.' Today an impossible count still prints at 48px with a footnote, a 12-of-30-day POS window still prints, and the stored legs do not sum to the stored usage. These are the exact three things a bookkeeper checks, and the disclosures around them are credible enough that they will be trusted.
- **Rename `valuation_method` from 'wac' to what the code actually does (latest-window cost / FIFO-approximation), correct ADR-009's 'a real weighted average' language, and add a one-line method statement to the food-cost screen and any export. Separately, label the market prior as an explicitly non-cost management estimate, never as a book value.**
  - The method is defensible; the label is not. For a company whose only durable asset is that it does not overstate, a mislabeled valuation method found by the first accountant who looks is disproportionately expensive — it converts a fixable engineering gap into a credibility event.
- **Add a re-reconcile path so a period recomputes when its pending invoices are reviewed, and add a database-level constraint that count periods for a location are non-overlapping and adjacency-checked. Flag a gap explicitly rather than letting it surface as a mis-diagnosed 'miscount'.**
  - Today a submitted period is frozen forever and never self-corrects, so 'N waiting on invoice review' reads as temporary while being permanent. And with no contiguity constraint, a skipped month produces a wrong number whose own flag points the operator at the wrong cause.
- **Filter the variance rows to recipe-covered items, or mark uncovered rows in place and exclude them from the totals and from the sort. Never let 'no recipe mapped' render as the top row of 'where it went.'**
  - The sort is descending by dollar variance and un-mapped items carry a variance equal to their entire usage, so the most prominent row on the leak screen is systematically an artifact. An operator who chases that row once and finds nothing stops trusting the screen permanently.
- **Before opening the bookkeeper channel, run one real month end-to-end on Tacombi's own invoices and counts, and have a working restaurant CPA review the output. Treat that as a gating milestone ahead of GTM, not a parallel track.**
  - The GTM work concluded the accounting firm is the only channel this price can afford. That channel's gatekeeper is exactly the reviewer these defects are visible to, and one relationship carries many restaurants — which means one bad number does not cost one customer, it costs the whole book. Zero confirmed users after fifteen months is currently protecting the company from this; opening the channel removes that protection.

## Unknowns (founder interview script)
- What fraction of a real Tacombi month's invoice DOLLARS survive `parsePack` into a non-null base_uom? This single number bounds how much of the food-cost numerator exists at all, and it can be measured in an afternoon against invoices the founder already has — no customer required.
- On the real vendor mix (the repo names ImperialDade and Chef's Warehouse), how many lines are catch-weight — printed $/lb against a piece count? And do those invoices currently land in `needs_review` because the row-arithmetic residual fires, or do they pass silently with an understated line value? These are opposite failure modes with opposite remedies.
- Does a working restaurant bookkeeper actually want COGS-including-waste (what a periodic count physically measures), or do they want waste broken out because they book it to a separate account? The answer determines whether the missing waste log is a feature gap or a non-issue.
- When a bookkeeper reconciles a client's food cost, what do they tie it to — the bank feed, the AP aging, the P&L food line? Whichever it is, that is the number Muntin's Purchases leg must agree with, and today it agrees with none of them because of the credit-memo and line-total exclusions.
- How do multi-location operators actually attribute invoices — separate vendor accounts per location, a location code on the invoice, or a single delivery split by hand? This determines whether the org-wide Purchases bug is a query fix or a genuinely hard data problem, and nothing in either repo answers it.
- Would a CPA accept a market-index price as a disclosed management estimate inside a food-cost report they hand to a client, or is any non-cost basis a hard stop that must be withheld instead? ADR-009's engineering is strong enough to support either answer; only a practitioner can pick one.
- Do operators count on a calendar month, a 4-week period, or a 13-period year — and do they ever skip a month? The absence of a contiguity constraint only matters if real operators skip, and the period shape determines what the constraint should even enforce.
- Is 'food cost %' expected against food sales or total sales in the specific segment Muntin is selling into? Independent operators and their bookkeepers do not agree on this, and the product currently picks the less conventional one without saying so.
- When a bookkeeper sees a food-cost number they cannot tie out, do they debug it or do they stop using the tool? This is the real severity question behind every defect above, and no artifact in either repo contains a single non-founder operator's reaction to a single printed number.
