# Design & User Experience

**NOT_READY**

## Thesis
The plan's sequencing instinct is right — correctness before amplification, and deferring decorative design in M1 is defensible for a company whose product is a number you can stand behind. But the plan sells a DOCUMENT ("a dated statement of food cost… sold like an audit opinion") and across 46 queue items allocates zero hours, zero items and zero gates to that document's form. There is no `/close` route among the 63 files in `Muntin-Invoice-Decoder/apps/api/src/routes/`, no statement renderer, no print stylesheet beyond `apps/web/app/globals.css:1372`, and no template — yet Q-042's verify requires every signed close to carry a `statementUrl`. The plan's terminal day-90 proof point cannot be reached by the plan's own items. Worse, the honesty commitments are verified one layer below where they are read: Q-021 promises the uncounted exclusion is "disclosed on the close" and Q-024 promises the shortfall is "named on the close," but both verify with `pnpm --filter @muntin/api exec vitest run -t '…'` — an API unit test. Every one of those items can close green while the surface a CPA actually reads omits the disclosure entirely. Not one of 46 verify commands inspects a rendered pixel. Design is being treated as the transport layer for numbers that gates already proved, which is exactly backwards for a company whose entire product claim is legibility. The measured design substrate makes this worse, not better: `docs/handoff/bones/design-inventory.json#topInconsistencies` records 19,333 on-palette hex literals, 16,788 off-palette ones and 979 pages that redeclare the token palette inline — so a redesign that repigments tokens reaches almost none of the site, and the plan contains no item that says so. The single most important thing missing is one M2 item that makes the close statement a named, rendered, gated artifact.
