# Sell to the bookkeeper; the restaurants come free.

## Thesis
Muntin has been building an accounting-firm product for a year and aiming all 1,327 of its pages at a restaurant owner it cannot afford to acquire. Measured this session: `apps/web/app/(product)/bookkeeper/` is 20 files — a 356-line hub with five queues (review, failed sync, possible duplicates, lock-blocked, anomaly-held), bank-statement reconciliation, charge mappings, GL rules, per-location setup and a workpaper export — sitting on `apps/api/src/routes/bookkeeper.ts` (444 lines), `accountant-handoffs.ts` (909, signed IIF/CSV envelopes with a 7-day token TTL), `statement-reconciliations.ts` (432), `gl-rules.ts` (357), `charge-mappings.ts` (276) and 3,665 lines of QuickBooks/Xero/Square posting under `apps/api/src/routes/integrations/`. Even the "missing" cockpit is half-shipped: `apps/api/src/routes/cockpit.ts` serves `GET /v1/cockpit/workspaces` and `POST /v1/cockpit/switch`, wired at `apps/api/src/index.ts:301-302` with a passing test file — what does not exist is one Next.js page. None of that was built for an owner. Every one of those features is a firm's monthly labor: code it, post it, prove the period is closed, hand the client a workpaper. So the bet is not a pivot, it is a re-aim: stop selling a $19 tool to a person who signs no cheque, and sell a firm-billed practice subscription to the eight-to-ten DMV bookkeeping firms that each carry 12–25 restaurants, at a marginal acquisition cost of roughly zero. The correctness debt is not the obstacle to this — it is the wedge, because the firm is the only buyer in the market who is professionally obligated to ask what is wrong with the number, and Muntin is the only vendor that can answer with file paths and line numbers. We ship the leg that is already accounting-correct (capture → GL-code → post with lock-date respect → signed workpaper), we withhold the food-cost identity until six named defects are closed, and we publish the defect register itself as the flagship sales artifact. The determinism guarantee — `no-llm-ci.sh`, falsifiable by a stranger in under a minute — is not a nice-to-have to a firm carrying client data under an engagement letter; it is the compliance answer no competitor can give. And the Cost Index stops being a marketing asset with no revenue mechanism and becomes the substantiation a bookkeeper shows a client on the Tuesday the food line jumps nine points.

## The Company
**Positioning.** Muntin is the restaurant book for accounting firms — the AP-to-GL and food-cost engine a bookkeeping practice runs across every restaurant client at once. Not a restaurant tool that accountants may look at; a practice tool whose subject happens to be restaurants. The line: *"Your restaurant clients, closed on time, with a number you can defend."*

**Category.** Restaurant practice infrastructure. This is a real category with two working proofs of the mechanism — QuickBooks ProAdvisor Preferred Pricing (firm-billed, one itemized monthly bill) and Dext Practice (per-client-per-month, ten-client minimum, tiering with volume, 12,000+ firms) — and one direct incumbent, MarginEdge's [me] finance, launched 2023-06-27 for restaurant accountants. We do not claim white space. We claim a specific undefended slice of it: the firm's 1–3-location clients on QBO or Xero who are not on Toast, whom [me] finance's POS-tethered parent product does not reach, and whose invoices come from eight to twelve specialty distributors that no national template covers.

**Buyer.** The practice lead or partner at a restaurant-specialist bookkeeping or CPA firm. Not the owner, not the GM, not the chef. This person has a budget line for tools, buys once for the whole book, and has an engagement letter that makes "no language model reads your numbers" a procurement answer rather than a slogan.

**Business model — firm-billed, per-client, ten-client minimum.** Two components, both already declared in code:
- The practice fee: **$150/mo + $30/seat**, already in `apps/api/src/lib/stripe-tiers.ts:83` (`basePriceCents: 15000`, `seatPriceCents: 3000`, `hasCockpit: true`, `maxSeats: 50`) and already `billable: true` in `apps/web/lib/pricing-constants.ts`. This buys the cockpit, the seats, and the queues.
- The per-client rate, billed to the firm on one invoice: **$39/client/mo** for the AP + ledger + posting + workpaper tier, **$99/client/mo** for the measured-food-cost tier once it is certified. *These two figures are a proposal, not a measured market price* — what is measured is that MarginEdge posts ~$350/location and Restaurant365 ~$469–499/location for the food-cost job (public pricing pages, 2026, per `greats-restaurant-gtm.md`), and that the firm channel's own anchor, Dext, sits near $17.70/client. $39 is deliberately above the document-capture anchor and an order of magnitude below the platform anchor, because we sell restaurant fluency, not cheapness.

**The arithmetic that makes this the only channel that closes.** A ten-client firm pays $150 + (10 × $39) = **$540/mo**. Eight firms is **$4,320/mo ≈ $52k ARR from ~80 restaurants**. The same 80 restaurants at the posted $19/location founding rate is $1,520/mo — so the firm channel is **2.8× the revenue at roughly zero acquisition cost**, against a direct-to-owner motion where the GTM panel showed no human touch can ever pay for itself at a $450–600 lifetime value. The ten-client minimum is copied deliberately from Dext: it makes the firm responsible for reaching volume, excludes the single-client hobbyist who generates support load without revenue, and is free churn insurance.

**What we stop charging.** The $19/location founding rate is retired outright. It is `billable: false` in `pricing-constants.ts` with no SKU, no per-location quantity, no `trial_period_days`, and building all of that would be building billing for the channel this bet abandons. Retiring it also deletes the mid-February first-charge problem — the "three months free" term against a 2026-11-13 GA lands the first charge in the second-worst cash month of the restaurant year, and a firm on a practice subscription does not have that seasonality.

**One billing defect blocks everything.** `apps/api/src/routes/billing.ts:175` sets `line_items[0][quantity]` to `seats`, multiplying the base price by headcount — a 5-seat Accountant is charged $750 against a correct $270, and 50 seats $7,500 against $1,620. `monthlyTotalCents()` computes it correctly and checkout never calls it. No per-client quantity model is safe until that line is fixed; it is latent today only because `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` is unset.

**Ceiling, stated honestly.** 100 firms at $540/mo is ~$648k ARR from perhaps 1,200 restaurants. That is a very good one-person business and it is not, by itself, a venture outcome. The venture-scale version of this bet is the second-order one: a firm that standardizes on Muntin generates a normalized, per-vendor, per-region ledger across its whole book — which is the only path to a delivered-price dataset that the CC0 wholesale index structurally cannot contain, built from customers rather than from submitters, and therefore without touching the no-submitters clause. We do not build that in year one. We build the channel that would make it possible.

## The Storefront
**Its job changes from "traffic and consumer confidence" to one job: put a bookkeeper who is searching for a work problem in front of a firm application.** The corpus does not shrink much; its audience rotates.

**The three moves that do 90% of it.**

1. **The Hubdoc lane, which is the highest-intent commercial query in this category and currently a live liability.** `apps/web/app/(marketing)/switch/hubdoc/page.tsx:16,18,24` is written in the *present tense* about a product Xero shut down on 2026-05-08 — "Hubdoc reads with their OCR + a human-review queue offshore" — and instructs the reader to log into a dead service, in EN and ES. On a site whose entire brand is a CI-enforced fact gate, that is the exact failure the apparatus exists to prevent, and it is a 30-minute fix. Then build the evergreen cluster on the *storefront*, where the fact gate lives: "what replaced Hubdoc," "Xero Files stores documents and performs no extraction," "Dext vs AutoEntry for a restaurant book." Measured: `data/sourced-claims.json` contains **zero** occurrences of Hubdoc, Dext, MarginEdge, Xero, QuickBooks or ProAdvisor. Every competitor fact in this lane must be registered there before it reaches a page — that registration is itself the differentiator, because the entire current SERP for these queries is vendor blogspam.

2. **`/for/bookkeepers/` and `/para-contadores/` become the storefront's conversion destination, and a firm CTA rail is injected across the pages that actually carry weight.** Today exactly **4 of 1,327 pages carry a waitlist form** — measured: `/`, `/es/`, `/ledger/`, `/es/ledger/`. The firm-facing pages that do exist (`/for-your-bookkeeper`, `/para-tu-contadora`) are stranded in the *product* app behind a domain nobody reaches. Move them to the storefront and inject the rail via the existing sentinel/injector pattern (`inject-site-counts.mjs`) across **143 EN cost-index**, **400 indexable library**, and **44 indexable sheets** pages, registered in `check-idem-coverage.mjs` with a named owner and a drift note so it does not become the 22nd manual builder nobody runs. Note that `/for/restaurants/` — the page carrying the category's DefinedTerm — is one of **2 orphan pages** under `for/` with zero inbound internal links. The new hubs must be linked from nav, not just built.

3. **Fix the broadcast so the dispatch reaches the list, and add a practice lane.** `src/worker.js:8793` sends only where `source === 'cost-index'`, which is 2 live subscriptions against 613 tagged `footer` and 162 `cost-index-ingredient`. Broaden the filter, backfill the tags, and add a `source: 'bookkeeper'` lane so a firm subscribes to a *practice* edition — the same monthly market read, framed as "what to expect on your clients' food line this month." That is a firm-retention ritual that is a by-product of work already happening, which is the only kind of new ritual this company can afford.

**What happens to the 1,327 pages.**

- **The 286 cost-index pages (143 EN / 143 ES) get promoted, not pruned.** Their job changes: they stop being an operator curiosity and become *substantiation* — the thing a bookkeeper links in an email when a client asks why the food line moved. **41 of the 143 EN pages are orphans** and only 2 emit any analytics; both are mechanical fixes. These are the appreciating assets an answer engine must re-fetch.
- **The 442 library pages split.** The 175 indexable pages in the `inventory/food-cost` cluster and the 76 in `vendors` are directly on the firm thesis and get re-headed for a practice reader. The 157 in `menu-pricing` stay live and unmaintained — an operator-facing depreciating asset that still ranks.
- **The 344 glossary pages get re-based, and this is the single biggest correction.** Measured: **234 of 344 carry `retiredLineScore >= 1`**, and **96 of the 198 indexable ones do**. A retrieval pipeline ingesting Muntin's own DefinedTermSet concludes, by majority, that this is a restaurant-website-SEO company. Under this bet the glossary becomes the *practice* vocabulary — lock date, GL coding, catch weight, WAC vs FIFO, periodic vs perpetual inventory, prime cost, AP aging, credit memo — which is the term set a firm's searches actually reach.
- **The 80 sheets pages are the firm's free sample.** `weekly-prime-cost-worksheet`, `monthly-pnl-snapshot`, `recipe-cost-card`, `inventory-count-sheet`, `invoice-receiving-checklist`, `vendor-contact-sheet` are practice deliverables a bookkeeper hands a client. Every one gets a "your firm can automate this" footer pointing at `/for/bookkeepers/`. The other **18 EN sheets carrying retiredLineScore ≥ 1** go (see whatWeStop).
- **The 651 ES pages narrow rather than freeze.** Keep `/es/cost-index/` (143 pages, 1 retired-line hit across the whole root). Freeze the 221 ES library and 172 ES glossary pages at current state — a price page regenerates from source, an essay needs re-translation on every edit. Build exactly one new ES surface: `/para-contadores/`. A Spanish-speaking owner-operator with a bilingual bookkeeper is a real and unserved shape; a promise we cannot keep is not.

**The one page that decides the sale.** `/for/bookkeepers/known-limits/` — the dated, code-cited register of exactly what the food-cost number excludes and in which direction it biases, with a fix date per line. Every competitor answers "what's wrong with your number?" with silence. This is the honesty brand pointed, for the first time, at the only buyer professionally obligated to care — and it is fact-gate-safe by construction, because every line traces to a file and a line number rather than to a market claim.

## The Product
**Muntin Ledger becomes Muntin Practice: one login, every restaurant client, one review queue.**

**What ships first, because it is already built and already correct.** The firm's actual monthly labor is capture → GL-code → post → prove the period is closed → hand over a workpaper. Not one step of that depends on `Beginning + Purchases − Ending = Usage`. Measured on disk today: the five-queue bookkeeper hub (`apps/web/app/(product)/bookkeeper/page.tsx`, 356 lines) covering review, failed sync, possible duplicates, lock-blocked and anomaly-held; bank-statement reconciliation (`statement-reconciliations.ts`, 432 lines); GL rules (357) and charge mappings (276); QuickBooks (668), Xero (629), posting (955) and bulk-post (484); and `accountant-handoffs.ts` (909 lines) minting a signed, Ed25519-enveloped IIF/CSV export behind a 128-bit token with a 7-day TTL and full audit logging. The weekly accountant digest (`apps/api/src/lib/accountant-digest.ts`) already runs Mondays at 15 UTC with a written, test-enforced privacy contract that leaks no vendor names, no line items and no PDFs. This is a firm product. Nobody has told a firm.

**What gets built — a short list.**
1. **`/cockpit`** — the client list above the queues. `apps/api/src/routes/cockpit.ts` already serves `GET /workspaces` (the caller's own active memberships, most-recent first) and `POST /switch` (the 30-day `muntin_workspace` cookie, re-validated by `require-auth` on every request so a forged cookie is rejected regardless), wired at `apps/api/src/index.ts:301-302` with tests in `apps/api/tests/member-list-cockpit-routes.test.ts`. The missing artifact is **one Next.js route**. This is the smallest gap in the company between "sold in `stripe-tiers.ts`" and "exists."
2. **A `firms` entity and a firm→client edge.** Today `stripe_subscriptions.org_id TEXT PRIMARY KEY REFERENCES orgs(id)` makes the *restaurant* the billing subject and `accountant` a guest the restaurant invites — so a firm structurally cannot be a customer, only a permission. Invert it: the firm is the billing subject, each client org carries a firm-billed line, and the restaurant keeps its own data ownership and its own erasure rights (`erasure.ts` already exists and must survive the inversion untouched).
3. **Firm-billed per-client Stripe**, after fixing `billing.ts:175`.
4. **Email/forward ingest as the primary path.** All 7 committed real invoice fixtures are `application/pdf` with hand-typed filenames. The day-one copy says "Take a photo." A firm receives PDFs and email attachments; the camera path is a mobile convenience, not the funnel.

**Correctness: the register, and the release rule.** The bet's mandate is to confront this head-on, so here is the position: **we ship the ledger now and withhold the food-cost identity until six named defects close** — and we publish the register while we do it.

- **Cross-location purchases leak.** `apps/api/src/lib/inventory-purchases-store.ts` queries by `org_id` and date only; its own header says "No location filter." Sessions, counts, pars, reorder, variance and `/insights/food-cost` are all keyed on `location_key`. In a two-location org each location's Usage absorbs both locations' invoices. This is the founder's own Tacombi shape and it is exactly what the brief predicted the firm buyer catches first. **Fix first.**
- **Catch-weight meat priced at $9.85 instead of $142.60.** `apps/api/src/lib/queue.ts:1129-1132` carries `unit_price × quantity` and discards the printed `line_total`. On a catch-weight layout unit price is $/lb and quantity is pieces. The bitter part: `services/extract/reconcile.py:138,335` already computes `residual = line_total − qty × unit_price` — the engine knows which number is derived and the ledger stores the derived one. **Fix second.**
- **Credit memos and $0 promo lines dropped.** `apps/api/src/lib/verdicts-store.ts:1150-1172` filters to strictly positive quantity AND price — correct for a price median, wrong for an accounting identity — while `apps/api/src/lib/pos/food-cost.ts` states the opposite rule in its own header. Two food-cost numbers with contradictory credit-memo treatment ship in one product.
- **The zero-usage tail.** `apps/web/app/(product)/count/count-client.tsx:218-227` writes un-entered items at expected-on-hand in the default `value` mode, making their usage arithmetically zero. Headline food cost is structurally biased low, and the negative-usage checksum can never fire on a tail row because 0 is not negative.
- **`inventory-reconcile.ts:249`** — an empty `if (estimated) { }` whose comment asserts a roll-up the code does not perform.
- **The `wac` label.** `valuation_method` is stamped `'wac'` but the average excludes beginning inventory from the pool — that is latest-cost/FIFO. FIFO is fine under GAAP; calling FIFO "WAC" in a stored field is what a CPA catches in the first ten minutes, and it is the one error a company whose brand is "we do not print what we cannot stand behind" can least afford.

**How correctness is guaranteed, and why the register is the sale rather than the apology.** Three mechanisms, in order. (a) A **`db-verify` contract test** that asserts the identity foots — `Beginning + Purchases − Ending = Usage` across all items, not just the valued subset, since today the four stored legs do not foot whenever any item is unvalued. (b) **One real month end-to-end on Tacombi's own invoices and counts, reviewed by a working restaurant CPA**, before any firm sees a number. The founder has never run his own product end to end; that fact and the zero-user count are currently *protecting* the company, and opening this channel removes the protection. (c) The **known-limits register published on the storefront**, append-only, with a `fixed_in` field — the same shape as the 10,294-entry revisions ledger that has never once lapsed, because it is a by-product of work that has to happen anyway.

**And the determinism guarantee finally gets a job.** `no-llm-ci.sh` walks every tracked source file, blocks LLM SDK imports, LLM HTTP destinations and LLM-only env vars, and exits 0 — falsifiable by a stranger in under a minute, named on **zero of 1,327 storefront pages** while 93 pages make the claim it proves. To an owner that is a curiosity. To a firm partner signing an engagement letter over client financial records, it is the procurement answer, and no competitor saying "AI-powered" can give it.

**What gets cut.** The photo-first onboarding. The broadliner template seeding — the corpus contains zero Sysco/US Foods/PFG/GFS invoices, so "ten real invoices from Don's shift" produces specialty templates, and the specialty long tail (i_halper 93.2% n=73, chefs_warehouse 92.1% n=214, baldor 89.7%, pat_lafrieda 87.5%) is precisely the mix a firm's independent clients actually have and no incumbent will template. The direct-to-owner waitlist. The Tier-2 variance screen stays behind the food-cost gate — `computeVariance` unions actual and theoretical keys with `?? 0`, so an unmapped item ranks as the largest leak on a screen a bookkeeper would show a client. And **`EXTRACT_VENDOR_TRUST_ACTIVE` gets switched on**: a measured 24% → ~62% first-pass straight-through improvement over a 919-doc corpus is sitting in shadow mode, and the cold start is exactly what kills a firm's first ten uploads.

## How It Fails
**The failure mode I would actually bet on: the firm channel has a slow, quiet sales cycle with one switching window a year, and this founder has twenty hours a month.**

Twenty conversations at forty-five minutes each is fifteen hours — most of one month's entire discretionary budget, spent on a channel whose signal arrives in quarters. And firms do not change AP tooling mid-engagement. The real switching moment is January: new fiscal year, new engagement letters, new client onboarding. A bet started in August has one shot in January and then waits twelve months. Meanwhile the archive's unfabricatable half — 2,025 live-captured points since 2026-05-01 — keeps aging, 26 of 100 series are already frozen 58-77 days against a 120-day drop cliff, and this plan gives that maintenance almost none of the founder's hours. If the bet is wrong, the company loses a year *and* the compounding record.

The second failure is more specific and more likely than the plan admits: **MarginEdge's [me] finance has served restaurant accountants since 2023-06-27**, with a partner network, one login across clients, and a public accountant directory. The "Bookkeeper-mode UI — Nobody" white space asserted in `runbooks/qbo-xero-roadmap.md` was roughly three years stale on the day it was written. Our fence is that [me] finance's parent product is POS-tethered and priced at ~$350/location, which does not reach the 1-3-location independent on Clover or a spreadsheet. That fence may simply not matter to a firm that already has a vendor relationship and does not want a second one for its smaller clients.

The third, which is the one that would hurt most: **we open the channel and the number is wrong in a way we did not enumerate.** The register lists six defects. A working restaurant CPA will find a seventh — period contiguity is unenforced (`priorReconciledLines` selects any prior reconciled session with no adjacency requirement and migrations 0047/0048 carry only `CHECK (period_end >= period_start)`), a submitted period can never be re-reconciled so the "waiting on invoice review" gap is permanent rather than temporary, the denominator has no food/non-food filter even though `catalog_items.category` is populated, and an unparseable pack silently deletes an item from both sides of the identity without appearing in `coverage_total_items`. One relationship carries 12-25 restaurants, which means one bad number does not cost one customer — it costs the whole book, and the firm tells the other firms. Zero confirmed users is currently protecting this company from exactly that; this bet removes the protection deliberately.

**The early signals that would prove it wrong, in the order they arrive:**

1. **Weeks 4-8 — the door test.** Of the first ten firms Don reaches, fewer than three agree to a thirty-minute call. Restaurant-specialist bookkeepers are not hard to reach and the referral standing of a working FOH manager is real; a sub-30% meeting rate means the problem statement does not land, and no amount of cockpit polish fixes that.
2. **Weeks 4-8 — the wound test.** Of the firms that do talk, fewer than two name Hubdoc's shutdown or Xero Files' lack of extraction as an unresolved problem. The acute migration wave is three months gone. If Dext and AutoEntry have already absorbed it cleanly, the entry event is closed and the highest-intent content lane in this plan is answering a question nobody is asking anymore.
3. **Weeks 8-12 — the price test.** The first firm that gets to pricing asks for a per-client rate under $20. That collapses the channel arithmetic back into the same $450-600-lifetime-value math this bet exists to escape, and it means we are competing with Dext on document capture rather than on restaurant fluency — a fight we lose, because they have 12,000+ firms and we have zero.
4. **Weeks 10-14 — the diligence test.** A firm reads `/for/bookkeepers/known-limits/` and stops responding. The register is a bet that professional buyers reward disclosure. If the actual response is that a firm cannot put a tool with a published defect list in front of a client, then the honesty brand is a credibility asset that closes a sale, not one that starts one — and this whole plan front-loaded it.

**The kill criterion, stated in advance so it cannot be argued away later:** if by 2026-11-07 (ninety days) fewer than three firms have taken a call *and* fewer than one has agreed to run a single client through a pilot, the bet is falsified. The correct response is not another quarter of building — it is to accept that the only channel the price could afford does not want the product, and to reopen the fork this plan closed: raise the price fifteen-to-twenty-five-fold and sell the measured food-cost number directly to the 1-3-location operator who demonstrably pays $350-500 for it, funded by the same correctness work this bet already paid for.
