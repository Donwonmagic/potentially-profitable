# The Financial Model — built from the repos, committed at /home/user/potentially-profitable/docs/handoff/bones/finance-model.{md,json,py}

**Verdict: MAJOR_REVISION**

## Thesis
I built the model nobody built, and the strategy does not survive contact with its own arithmetic. The headline ceiling is wrong twice over. First, "$600/location/month, billed annually at $6,000" and "40 × $600 = $288K ARR" cannot both be true — $6,000/yr is $500/month, so the invoiced ceiling is $240,000, and the annual prepay is a 16.7% discount never named as one. Second, and larger: the 13.3 founder-hours/month that justifies the 40-location cap counts close review ONLY. Add exception-desk rule review (a signature means the founder read the rules), per-customer support and hand-invoicing, platform upkeep (306 cost-index pages, 21 manual builders, agent-PR review, keeping a deploy green that currently exits 1), and onboarding for a buyer explicitly defined as carrying 8-12 specialty vendors — and at the verdict's own 20-minute close the servable ceiling falls from 40 locations to 19.8, with zero hours left to sell the twentieth. Costs are small and knowable: $248-892/month recurring (Fly $34 effective on the founder's own June bill, Neon, Cloudflare Workers Paid, KMS, Resend, BetterStack, domains) plus $3,500-11,500/year of E&O, counsel, accounting and store fees — but the single largest line, the agent workforce that authored 491 of 584 product commits and is supposed to RUN the exception desk, appears in no repo, no budget, and no question asked of the founder in 100+ agents. Cash timing is worse than what it replaced: the verdict killed the November GA because three-months-free landed the first charge in mid-February (season 0.917), then scheduled Phase 3 at weeks 10-13 and hand-invoices on signature — first cash lands 2027-01, season 0.913, the actual worst month of the restaurant year. Under its own stated "invoice in the May-August peak" policy a November signature gets six months of signed closes free and eight months before cash, and worst cash drops from -$11,735 to -$24,262. Unit economics are excellent when they work — 79-92% gross margin, infrastructure covered by two locations forever — but that is exactly why the model is not a business plan: the residual at the service ceiling ranges from $224/year to $280,000/year on one parameter, desk_minutes_per_close, which returns zero grep hits in either repo and which the verdict proposes to measure from customer one, i.e. after the price is set. Price is the least sensitive lever in the whole model (2.5× on price moves the BASE ceiling 2.5×; 45→12 desk-minutes moves it 14×), and this company has spent fifteen months and five posted prices arguing about it. Finally, the model never makes quitting rational, and that is structural: the plan is engineered to be servable in 13-26 hours/month, which is precisely what makes it unable to become the founder's job — quitting frees ~140 hours into a business whose ceiling is a policy cap of 40 locations, converting a 90%-margin side business into a full-time job at identical revenue.

## Neglected
- **[MAJOR] The ARR headline is internally inconsistent by $48,000/yr — 20% of the stated ceiling.**
  - Evidence: phase-d-verdict.json theCompany: "$600/location/month, billed annually at $6,000" and, three sentences later, "40 × $600 = $288K ARR". 40 × $6,000 = $240,000. $6,000/yr is $500/mo — a 16.7% prepay discount never named as a discount anywhere in the verdict or in bones/dream-price-inversion.md.
  - Consequence: The one number the strategy uses to describe itself as "a very good one-person business" is overstated by a fifth. Every downstream judgement — whether it replaces the job, whether it is a venture, whether $600 is the right price — was made against the wrong figure. Either the annual price is $7,200 or the ceiling is $240K; the verdict must pick one.
- **[DISQUALIFYING] The 13.3 h/month ceiling counts close review only. Four recurring founder-hour loads are omitted, and adding them halves the servable ceiling.**
  - Evidence: Verdict: "~20 founder-minutes of close review × 40 locations = 13.3 h/month against 13-26 available." Omitted: (a) review of the exception desk's RULES — the verdict itself says the desk authors templates, pack mappings and catalog synonyms and that every rule is "inspectable" and "published to the customer in the close receipt", which a signature obliges the founder to read; (b) per-customer support, hand-invoicing and collections, since the verdict removed billing code; (c) platform upkeep — the 306 cost-index pages must "stay fresh", CLAUDE.md registers 21 manual (idem) builders (8 rendering operator-fetched data) and names inject-knit-rail.mjs as highest-churn, and the storefront deploy exits 1 (312/320); (d) onboarding for a buyer defined as carrying 8-12 specialty vendors, where docs/plans/first-try-reliability-roadmap.md:89 requires a real reference invoice A AND a held-out invoice B run through production docling per layout. My model (finance-model.md §2) computes the servable ceiling at 19.8 locations in the BASE case using the verdict's own 20-minute close, and 3.6 in the SLOW case.
  - Consequence: The 40-location cohort — the plan's entire commercial shape — is unreachable at the plan's own service assumptions unless desk minutes fall below 12. The honest ceiling to publish is ~20 locations / ~$119K, not 40 / $288K, until desk_minutes_per_close is measured.
- **[MAJOR] The plan killed a February first charge and scheduled a January one.**
  - Evidence: The verdict's stated kill reason for the 2026-11-13 GA: three months free from mid-November "lands the first charge in mid-February against Census MARTS medians of Jan 0.913 / Feb 0.917 — the second-worst cash month of the restaurant year." Its replacement puts Phase 3 ("the price call, the application page, ten locations, hand-invoiced") at weeks 10-13 = mid-Oct to early Nov 2026, invoiced on signature. At net-45 to an unknown one-person vendor, first cash lands 2027-01 — season 0.913, the actual worst month, worse than the February it rejected. finance-model.json shows first_cash = 2027-01 in all three cases.
  - Consequence: The seasonality analysis that justified killing the GA date was not applied to the replacement. Either Phase 3 slips so signature lands in the peak, or the first invoice is deliberately dated for March collection.
- **[MAJOR] The verdict's two stated cash policies contradict each other, and the one it wrote costs eight months of free service.**
  - Evidence: "$600/location/month, billed annually at $6,000, invoiced by hand, in the May-August seasonal peak" versus Phase 3 closing ten locations in weeks 10-13 (Oct-Nov 2026). Modeled both (finance-model.md §4): peak-window billing sends the first invoice 2027-05 and collects 2027-07 — six months of signed closes delivered before an invoice exists — and worst cash falls from -$11,735 to -$24,262.
  - Consequence: Six to eight months of the highest-touch service this company will ever deliver, given away, in the exact window where a pilot customer decides whether to renew. Neither policy was costed; they produce different businesses.
- **[MAJOR] The largest omitted cost line is the agent workforce, and it is also the exception desk's COGS.**
  - Evidence: 491 of 584 product commits authored by "Claude" (git log). The verdict's delivery model is "an agent-run exception desk." Neither repo records a subscription, an invoice, or an API spend: grep for ANTHROPIC_API_KEY finds only the no-LLM tripwire in verify-content.generated.ts:104 that BANS it from the read path. /about/cost publishes five per-invoice cost rows and five fixed lines and the workforce appears in none of them.
  - Consequence: The published cost receipt — the page whose whole point is honesty about cost — omits the largest operating expense. More importantly, an agent-run desk means gross margin is a function of token spend per exception, and that ratio is unmeasured. My model brackets it at $200-700/month; the founder knows the real number and nobody asked.
- **[MAJOR] The live Terms of Service forbid the product being sold, and the price raises the liability cap 26×.**
  - Evidence: apps/web/lib/legal-content.generated.ts:1243-1252: "Muntin Ledger is a record-keeping tool. It is not accounting, tax, bookkeeping, audit, or legal advice... you are responsible for verifying every figure before you rely on it" and "liability... is limited to the amount you have paid us in the 12 months immediately preceding the claim." The strategy sells "a dated, signed statement of food cost... the way an audit firm sells an opinion." docs/legal-counsel-kickoff.md records the ToS as "not yet drafted; this engagement should produce the first draft" — the live Terms are generated from repo markdown by scripts/build-legal-content.mjs, not by counsel.
  - Consequence: Either the disclaimer stays and the contract tells the customer not to rely on the thing $600 buys, or it comes out and the risk class changes. The cap moves from $228 to $6,000 per customer as a side effect of a pricing decision nobody connected to it. E&O must be bound before the first close ships, and a signed monthly financial statement may not underwrite on a standard tech-E&O form — I could not verify what that costs.
- **[MAJOR] The annual prepay is the least likely assumption in the plan and the entire cash model rests on it.**
  - Evidence: A $6,000 upfront check from a single-location independent. On the storefront's own registered bands (food 28-35%, prime 55-65%, fixed 12-14 pts) net margin is low single digits: at 3% on $1M of sales the whole year's profit is ~$30,000. The ask is 20% of a year's profit, in advance, from a vendor with zero customers. bones/operator-the-money.md records zero recorded objections ever, because there have been zero customers.
  - Consequence: If it falls back to monthly: the May-August seasonality routing disappears entirely, hand-invoicing goes from ~20 to ~240 invoices/year (≈3.3 founder-h/month, 17-25% of the whole budget, forever, growing linearly), and January churn returns with no dunning, no card on file and no auto-renew — because the verdict removed billing code. "No billing code" saves a day of build and costs ~40 founder-hours a year the moment prepay does not hold.
- **[MODERATE] Per-location is the wrong unit, and the verdict's own buyer definition says so.**
  - Evidence: The buyer is defined by vendor mix — "8-12 active specialty vendors, zero or one broadliner" — and the verdict states marginal cost "scales with distinct vendor layouts, not with invoices or customers." It then prices per location. Reconcile rates in bones/dream-price-inversion.md:77 show the tail is where the cost is: cintas 54.3%, staples 37.5%, cozzini_bros 20.8% against i_halper 93.2% — and those non-food vendors have zero coverage in the free Cost Index, so no market prior to fall back on.
  - Consequence: A one-location restaurant with 14 vendors costs more to serve than a three-location group with 9 shared vendors and pays a third as much. DMV clustering — the correct instinct — is commercially invisible because nothing in the price rewards a customer whose layouts are already covered.
- **[MODERATE] Deferred revenue is a liability with no engine behind it, and no refund policy.**
  - Evidence: Ten pilot locations at $6,000 prepaid is $60,000 of refund exposure against a worst modeled cash balance of -$24,262. The ToS says only that data is "exportable until your subscription ends"; there is no proration language, and the verdict explicitly removes the billing code that would compute one.
  - Consequence: A single pilot cancellation in month three creates a ~$4,500 refund obligation out of cash already spent, calculated by hand, against a contract that does not say how.
- **[MINOR] Six product shells are neither funded nor retired while ~460 storefront pages are retired.**
  - Evidence: apps/{api,web,ios,mobile,desktop,email-worker}. apps/ios/RELEASE.md is a full App Store Connect release runbook; apps/mobile ships an android/ target and references Google Play in strings/catalog.json. Zero customers. The verdict retires storefront pages, the audio pipeline, the bookkeeper channel, the Reference Plate, the attestation product, four waitlist forms and five prices — and says nothing about the product repo's surface count.
  - Consequence: Recurring store fees ($99/yr Apple, $25 Google Play), annual compliance cycles, and three decaying toolchains (Tauri, Capacitor, Swift) carried indefinitely. The one-in-one-out gate is applied where the cost is a page nobody reads and not where it is a bill.

## Recommendations
- [founder] **Fix the price sentence before anything else is decided on top of it. Publish ONE of: $600/mo billed monthly ($7,200/yr, ceiling $288K), or $6,000/yr prepaid ($500/mo effective, ceiling $240K) with the 16.7% prepay discount named as a discount. Restate the ceiling from the number chosen.** — The verdict's headline ARR and its stated invoice price differ by 20%. Every judgement about whether this is a good one-person business, whether it replaces the job, and whether $600 is right was made against a figure that does not exist.
- [founder] **Move the desk-minutes measurement into the Phase-0 twenty-invoice founder walk, before the price is posted — not "instrumented from customer one." Add a stopwatch and record four numbers: minutes of close review per location, minutes of rule review per location, exceptions per 100 lines, and minutes of support per customer.** — finance-model.md §5 shows the residual at the service ceiling ranging $224/yr to $280,000/yr on this one parameter. grep -rn desk_minutes returns zero hits in either repo. The walk is already scheduled for week one; the marginal cost of measuring is a stopwatch, and it converts the largest uncertainty in the business from a bet into a fact before ten customers sign at a price derived from it.
- [agent] **Republish the ceiling as ~20 locations / ~$119K until desk minutes are measured, and state the four omitted hour-loads explicitly next to it. Cap the cohort at 20, not 40.** — At the verdict's own 20-minute close plus rule review, support, upkeep and onboarding, 19.8 locations consumes the entire 20-hour budget with nothing left to sell the twentieth. Publishing 40 commits the company to a number its own service model cannot reach — the exact failure mode (a posted number the engine cannot honor) the strategy exists to end.
- [founder] **Re-time Phase 3 or the invoice. Either slip signature into the March-August window, or sign in November and date the first invoice for March collection. Do not let net-45 from a November signature land in January.** — The verdict killed the GA date because three-months-free landed the first charge in February (0.917) and then scheduled a January collection (0.913) — a worse month. The analysis was correct; it was simply not applied to the replacement.
- [external] **Get an E&O quote and a counsel scope BEFORE the specimen close ships, not before GA. Bring the carrier the actual deliverable — a signed monthly statement of food cost — and ask explicitly whether it underwrites on a tech E&O form. In the same engagement, reconcile ToS §6 ("not accounting, tax, bookkeeping, audit") with selling a signed opinion, and decide the liability cap deliberately now that fees paid are $6,000 rather than $228.** — The live Terms currently forbid reliance on the thing the price buys, and were never drafted by counsel (docs/legal-counsel-kickoff.md). A declined or repriced E&O quote changes the price, and it is cheaper to learn that from a broker in week two than from a customer in month nine.
- [founder] **Ask the founder for the agent-workforce spend and put it on /about/cost as a line item. Then instrument tokens-per-exception on the desk from close one alongside desk minutes.** — It is plausibly the largest operating expense, it is the exception desk's COGS, and it is absent from the one page whose entire purpose is publishing what things cost. A cost receipt that omits the biggest cost is the same defect class as a food-cost percentage biased low.
- [founder] **Change the unit to a base close fee per location PLUS a one-time per-novel-layout onboarding fee, waived when the layout is already covered by the cluster. Price the tail vendors (cintas 54.3%, staples 37.5%, cozzini_bros 20.8%) as the expensive items they are.** — The verdict names vendor layouts as the marginal cost driver, defines the buyer by vendor count, and then charges per location. This makes the DMV-cluster strategy commercially visible, charges the actual driver, and stops a 14-vendor single site from being served at a third the price of a 9-vendor group.
- [agent] **Write the annual-prepay fallback now: a monthly-billing plan with its clerical cost budgeted (≈3.3 founder-h/month at 20 customers), a written refund/proration policy, and a decision on whether billing code returns. Trigger it the first time a prospect asks for monthly.** — The entire cash model rests on a $6,000 upfront check being ~20% of a target customer's annual profit. Zero customers means zero recorded objections, so this assumption has never met resistance. If it fails, the seasonality routing evaporates, the clerical load quadruples, and $60,000 of prepaid pilot revenue sits as a liability with no proration engine.

## Would change my verdict
One measured number would move my verdict more than everything else combined: **actual minutes of founder close review, per location, on a real month** — recorded during the Phase-0 twenty-invoice walk that is already scheduled for week one, at a cost of a stopwatch and zero additional founder hours. Below ~15 minutes per location (with rule review folded in), the 40-location ceiling is real, the residual clears $200K, and my verdict moves to ENDORSE_WITH_CONDITIONS on the pricing arithmetic alone. Above ~35 minutes, the servable ceiling is under 10 locations, the business is a $60K side income that can never replace the job, and the honest move is to say so in public rather than publish a $288K ceiling. Second, and nearly as cheap: ask the founder what the agent workforce costs per month — a question no one in 100+ agents has asked, about the line item that is simultaneously the largest operating expense and the exception desk's cost of goods.
