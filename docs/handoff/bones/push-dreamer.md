# The Dreamer — is this the largest available idea inside a permanent 13-26 founder-hour ceiling?

## Thesis
The plan is not too small in ambition; it is too small in *mechanism*, and its own finance model already says so in a file nobody has acted on. `docs/handoff/bones/finance-model.md:108-117` puts the BASE service ceiling at 19.8 locations and $142,306 ARR at list — below the floor of the founder's own declared $250-500K/yr target — and `:335-337` shows that even $1,000/location lands at $237,176, still short. The same file's `:339-342` names the lever that actually moves the ceiling 14x: `desk_minutes_per_close`. That number appears in `data/queue.json` exactly twice, both times as a kill-criterion threshold at CP-60 and CP-90, and zero times as work: there is no queue item whose job is to reduce it. So the plan measures the variable that decides everything and schedules nothing against it — the ceiling is a policy cap (40) laid over an unmeasured cost, and the executable spine stops at three signed locations with no M4 window and no day-91 artifact anywhere in the file. Meanwhile the one asset in either repo with genuinely declining marginal cost — the cross-tenant community template pool (`infra/postgres/migrations/0013|0056|0057`, `services/extract/community_pool.py`, `engine_pkg/assemble.py:802`, `cli/publish_community_seed.py`) — is mentioned in the queue zero times, as are `/try`, `DEMO_ANONYMOUS_EXTRACT`, "template", "extraction", "standard" and "conformance". A vendor layout rule authored once removes exceptions for every customer forever; it is deterministic, so it does not touch the no-LLM promise; it is agent-shaped, so it costs almost no founder hours; and it is the only thing that makes customer forty cheaper to serve than customer one. That is the 5x version, and it is the same company: same buyer, same $600, same signed close — with the day-91+ build agenda pointed at the desk rather than at the storefront. Two further one-way doors are being taken as defaults rather than decisions: the engagement letter's no-third-party-reliance clause (`bones/board-regulatory.md:18,38`), which caps the close's worth at the labour it replaces in a strategy whose entire analogy is an audit opinion — an instrument that is valuable precisely *because* a lender relies on it; and Q-030's framing of the specimen close as one artifact rather than a monthly by-product of the two locations he closes anyway. Verdict: the plan's first ninety days are close to right and should not be re-cut. What is missing is everything after them.

## Findings
- **The plan's own finance model puts its realistic ceiling BELOW the founder's declared revenue goal, at every price the plan considers — and nothing in the queue responds to this.** — `docs/handoff/bones/finance-model.md:108-112` gives the service ceiling (locations servable with zero hours left for selling) as SLOW 3.6 loc / $26,000, BASE 19.8 loc / $142,306, FAST 51.4 loc / $370,286 at list $600. The founder's stated target is a durable $250-500K/yr. Only the FAST case clears the floor of his own goal, and `:335-337` shows $1,000/location at BASE still reaches only $237,176. `:114-117` states plainly that the 40-location ceiling survives only in the FAST case. So the strategy of record, priced and staffed as written, is a plan whose good outcome misses the founder's stated floor — and no artifact in `data/queue.json` acknowledges the gap or names the case the company is betting on.
- **`deskMinutesPerClose` is the highest-leverage number in the business, is unmeasured, and has no queue item that reduces it — only two that threaten to kill the strategy when it is too high.** — `finance-model.md:339-342`: moving desk minutes from 45 to 12 moves the BASE ceiling from $26,000 to $370,286 — a 14x swing, against a 2.5x price move producing a 2.5x swing. `finance-model.md:102-104` records that `grep -rn desk_minutes` returns zero hits in either repo. In `data/queue.json`, `deskMinutes` appears twice, both inside `checkpoints[].killIf` tests (CP-60 `> 60`, CP-90 `> 90`). There is no item of `kind: mechanism` anywhere in M1-M3 whose `doneWhen` is a reduction in close time. The plan has made the number a tripwire and never a target.
- **The only asset with declining marginal cost — the cross-tenant community template pool — appears in the plan zero times.** — Searching `data/queue.json` for `community`, `pool`, `template`, `extraction`, `/try`, `DEMO_ANONYMOUS` returns 0 hits for every term. The machinery is built and on disk: `infra/postgres/migrations/0013_community_templates.sql`, `0056_community_templates_supersession.sql`, `0057_community_template_rejections.sql`; `services/extract/community_pool.py`; the fan applied at `services/extract/engine_pkg/assemble.py:802` with `EXTRACT_COMMUNITY_POOL` default ON; and `services/extract/cli/publish_community_seed.py`, which lets the founder author a rule once and pre-fill every opted-in org's first invoice from that vendor. This is exactly the mechanism that drives desk-minutes down, it is deterministic (so it does not touch the no-LLM promise on 93 pages), and it is agent-workforce-shaped. `bones/board-scale-ambition.md:12-14` flagged it as DISQUALIFYING and the queue did not absorb it.
- **`/try` — the only acquisition surface that consumes zero founder-hours — is built, hardened, and blocked on a gate the plan's own buyer decision makes obsolete.** — `Muntin-Invoice-Decoder/docs/ux/decisions/ADR-007` records `/try` as BUILT and hardened (5MB cap, per-IP/ASN limits, $50/day budget breaker, Turnstile, replay cache, phone-photo downscale, CI-gated no-persistence), with un-gating being 'a single flag flip'. Its blocking gate is real-invoice coverage on 'the actual top-~10 broadliner layouts'. The strategy of record defines the buyer by vendor mix with zero or one broadliner, and `services/extract/VENDOR-TEMPLATE-KEY.md` already measures the corpus's specialty vendors at i_halper 93.2% (n=73), chefs_warehouse 92.1% (n=214), baldor 89.7%, pat_lafrieda 87.5%, edward_don 85.7%, fresco 83.1%, montgomery_county_dlc 79.4%. The gate is satisfiable with assets already on disk, and `/try` is a strictly stronger falsifier than Q-032's `/tools/pack-check/`, which the plan does schedule.
- **The engagement letter's no-third-party-reliance clause is a one-way door being taken as a default, and it is the sentence that sets the company's price ceiling.** — `bones/board-regulatory.md:18` and `:38` recommend that the deliverable 'may not be provided to a lender, investor, purchaser or insurer without Muntin's prior written consent', calling it 'the single highest-value sentence in the document' for liability containment. That is correct as risk management and is also the clause that caps the close's value at the bookkeeping labour it replaces. The entire strategy is built on the audit-opinion analogy — and an audit opinion is worth money precisely because a third party relies on it. Nobody in the bones or the queue asks the opposite question: what would it take to permit named-reader reliance, and what is that worth. A lender, a landlord running percentage-rent, or a broker preparing a restaurant for sale is also the acquisition mechanism the board says does not exist, because one such reader introduces many restaurants at once.
- **The specimen close is scoped as one artifact when it is available as a permanent monthly by-product of the one restaurant that will never churn.** — Q-030 in `data/queue.json` reads 'The specimen close — one complete month with the exceptions shown', `founderHours: 2`, window M2, `doneWhen: /close/specimen/ publishes one month`. The founder's seat at Tacombi is now settled as permanent. He will be closing those two locations monthly regardless — that is what Q-001 and Q-042 assume. The plan's own capacity doctrine (`dream-brief.json#constraints`) states that any new ritual must be a by-product of work that has to happen anyway; twelve dated, signed, published closes a year is the only publication in the company that satisfies that test perfectly, and it is scoped as a single deliverable.
- **There is no artifact describing days 91 and after, yet the entire value case lives there.** — `data/queue.json#capacity.windows` defines M1, M2, M3 only, ending 2026-11-06. `capacity.cuts` states 'TEN locations by day 90 becomes THREE... ten is a day-180 number', and Q-042's `doneWhen` requires exactly 3 signed locations. Searching the file for `M4`, `day 180`, `cohort two` returns 1 total hit. The strategy sentence in `strategy.verdict` asserts '~40 locations ever' — 37 locations beyond anything the executable spine describes. The plan is a rigorous ninety-day repair programme wearing a company strategy's headline number.
- **The published-negative-results asset is the strongest credential the company owns and is scheduled only as a LOW-priority M3 item.** — `cost-index/calibration.json` publishes pooledCoverage 0.767 against nominal 0.80 across 79,086 scored steps with `underCovering: 0`, and a direction-call hit rate of 0.506 against a 0.502 baseline over 34,029 calls — a company publishing that its own directional signal is at chance. `cost-index/revisions.json` carries an append-only revisions ledger. In the queue, the surface that would expose this class of honesty (`Q-033 — /cost-index/refusals/ and /cost-index/freshness/`) is `priority: LOW`, window M3, and explicitly named in `capacity.cuts` as carrying zero founder hours and free to slip. Under the plan's own doctrine that credibility is a closing asset, this is the closing asset, deprioritized.
- **A real-address fixture is a hard prerequisite to the single best public falsifier, and the two artifacts disagree about whether it is exposed.** — `services/extract/tests/golden/real_fixtures/fresco_lineitems.json` contains the literal string 'TacombiBethesda 4749 Bethesda Ave, Bethesda, MD 20814' while two sibling fixtures in the same directory are redacted. `bones/dream-brief.json#assets[1]` describes this as sitting 'in a public MIT repo'; the orchestrating brief for this validation states the product repo is PRIVATE. UNVERIFIED which is current — I cannot check repo visibility from this container. Either way the redaction is a blocker on publishing the per-vendor reconcile table, because publicity is what makes someone look, and no queue item covers it.

## Proposals
- [~0 founder hours (a stopwatch during Q-001 and Q-030, which are already scheduled); the recording schema is agent work.] **Instrument `deskMinutesPerClose` from Q-001 forward, and add one M3 queue item of `kind: mechanism` whose `doneWhen` is a measured reduction in it. Record all five founder-hour lines the board named — close review, support, onboarding, hand-invoicing, replacement selling — not just close review.** — `finance-model.md:339-342` shows this is a 14x lever against price's 2.5x, and `:102-104` shows it has never been measured. `data/queue.json` uses `deskMinutes` only as a CP-60/CP-90 kill threshold. A number that can end the strategy and has no work item pointed at it is a tripwire, not a plan. Every other proposal here is a way of moving this one number.
- [Near-zero founder hours — agent work against assets already on disk; founder time is limited to ratifying the ADR split alongside Q-019.] **Add the vendor-layout coverage engine to the queue as the named compounding asset: seed the community pool from the 919-doc corpus for the seven specialty vendors already measured at ≥79% reconcile, using `cli/seed_template.py` → `cli/publish_community_seed.py` with the held-out lift gate enforced. Split ADR-031 so it forecloses PRICE and DATA submissions (which do create an undelegatable moderation queue) while explicitly preserving the community-template contribution path, which `redactSafe` + the server-side header allowlist + migration 0057's N-distinct-org supersession already make self-moderating.** — This is the only mechanism in either repo whose marginal cost declines with customers, it is deterministic so it cannot violate the no-LLM promise, and it is the direct lever on desk-minutes. `VENDOR-TEMPLATE-KEY.md` measures the corpus at 84.1% reconcile (n=490) with per-vendor rates in enablement order. If ADR-031 is drafted broadly it closes this door permanently while it is free to leave open. The queue currently contains zero references to any of it.
- [Zero founder hours; agent work plus one flag flip and the founder's go/no-go.] **Re-scope the `/try` un-park gate from 'the top ~10 broadliner layouts' to the seven specialty vendors already at ≥79%, then flip `DEMO_ANONYMOUS_EXTRACT`. Treat it as the qualification surface `/close/apply/` (Q-040) funnels into.** — ADR-007 records `/try` as built and hardened with un-gating being a single flag flip; the strategy's own buyer definition (zero or one broadliner) makes the blocking gate satisfiable today. It is a stronger public falsifier than Q-032's pack-check and the only acquisition surface that runs while the founder is on the floor at Tacombi. CP-90's demand test has a sample size of eight conversations; this one has a sample size of everyone who lands.
- [Zero founder hours after the first publication; the redaction and the builder are agent work.] **Publish the per-vendor reconcile table as a standing public benchmark — every vendor, including cintas 54.3%, staples 37.5% and cozzini_bros 20.8% — regenerated as a by-product of the same eval that gates the pool. Redact `fresco_lineitems.json:~390` first, to match its already-redacted siblings.** — This is the category-creation artifact: it names 'specialty vendor mix' as a measurable thing and is the only claim in the company a funded competitor cannot buy, because it rests on four years of one working manager's real Mid-Atlantic invoices. Publishing where it does badly is the honesty thesis made concrete and is what a skeptical buyer or a referring CPA reaches for. Because it regenerates from an eval that must run anyway, it satisfies the plan's own rule that no new ritual may add a hand-calendared obligation.
- [Zero incremental founder hours — same counsel engagement already required before anything depending on a signature is built.] **When counsel is engaged for the signing question, ask the reliance question in the same conversation: not only 'how do we disclaim third-party reliance' but 'what entity form, E&O cover, engagement language and price would let a named lender, landlord or purchaser rely on this'. Record the answer as an ADR before the first engagement letter is signed.** — `bones/board-regulatory.md:38` calls the no-reliance clause the highest-value sentence in the document, and as liability containment it is right. It is also the sentence that fixes the close's price at the labour it replaces, in a company whose entire positioning is the audit-opinion analogy — where value comes from the reader, not the payer. The clause is a one-way door: the first cohort's letters set the precedent. Asking both questions costs the same call and the same fee, and the answer determines whether the ceiling is `finance-model.md`'s $142K or a multiple of it.
- [Minutes per month on top of work already being done; the builder and gate are agent work.] **Convert Q-030 from a one-time specimen into a standing monthly publication: `/close/specimen/<YYYY-MM>/`, one signed, dated close of his own two locations, every month, forever — with the current month's `deskMinutesPerClose` printed on it.** — He will produce this close regardless; the marginal cost is publishing it. It is the only cadence in company history that cannot lapse, because the work happens whether or not the site is updated — and `dream-brief.json#constraints` records that every machine-derived ritual has held while every hand-calendared one has slipped. Twelve dated closes a year is a compounding citation asset, a public desk-minutes trend line, and the single artifact CP-90 says the buyer either asks to see or does not.
- [~1 founder hour of decision, written up by an agent.] **Write the missing page: days 91 through the ceiling. One ADR that states which finance-model case the company is betting on (SLOW/BASE/FAST), what desk-minutes number that case requires, how locations 4 through 40 are acquired, and — separately from the public pilot cap of 10 — keeps 40 as an internal planning number that is never published.** — `capacity.windows` ends 2026-11-06 and Q-042 tops out at three signed locations, while `strategy.verdict` asserts '~40 locations ever'. Thirty-seven locations of the value case have no plan. Separately, `board-scale-ambition.md:21-23` is right that publishing 'forty ever' is unbreakable for a company whose only asset is that its statements hold; ten is honest, creates the same scarcity, and leaves the ceiling open at no credibility cost.
- [Zero founder hours to test (one question added to the Q-031 conversations); agent work only if the answer is yes.] **SPECULATIVE, lower confidence, cheap to test: publish `/close/standard/` — a versioned, machine-checkable definition of what a defensible monthly close is (which legs must foot, what must be labeled estimated, what must be withheld) plus a conformance checker anyone can run against their own numbers. The $600 signed close becomes its reference implementation.** — In the audit analogy the entity with returns to scale is not the auditor, it is the standard — and this company's single most developed capability is turning a rule into a gate with mutation-tested teeth (`scripts/audit-gate-teeth.mjs`, `check-honesty-debt.mjs`, `check-src-sentinel.mjs`). The content already exists: Q-025's append-only code-cited limits register is the standard's exceptions clause and Q-030's specimen is its worked example. A standard costs no founder-hours to distribute, is cited rather than sold, and makes a bookkeeper who adopts it a channel that consumes no capacity — which is a different shape from the bookkeeper CHANNEL that ADR-014 deferred and the bookkeeper PLATFORM that phase-D killed FATAL. I flag this as the least-grounded proposal here: no evidence in either repo suggests anyone would adopt it, and it should be tested by whether the two CPA reviewers in Q-031 ask for the rules unprompted, not by building it first.
