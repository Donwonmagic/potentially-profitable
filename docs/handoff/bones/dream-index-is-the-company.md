# The Index IS the company — Michelin, inverted. The free, CC0, per-ingredient wholesale reference is the product, the brand and the moat; Muntin Ledger is downstream depth. Revenue comes from analysis, service and expertise sold OVER data that stays free forever — which means the first dollar needs no billing code, no Stripe SKU, no launch, and no correctness work on the food-cost identity.

## Thesis
Muntin has spent fifteen months building a product nobody has used and a publication nobody has cited, and only one of those two is nearly finished. The index is already an instrument: 100 ingredients, 82 with a published dollar-denominated wholesale reference (`cost-index/feed.json`), 79,086 conformal steps scored at 0.767 coverage against 0.80 nominal with zero under-covering items, a direction call published honestly at 0.506 against a 0.502 baseline, 42 written structural reasons why an ingredient cannot be priced, 18 flagged `insufficient`, and a 10,294-entry revisions ledger (`cost-index/calibration.json`, `data/cost-index.json#coverage.gaps`). Nothing else in restaurants publishes that, and the reason is not difficulty — it is that no vendor has an incentive to publish its own error rate. Michelin's insight was that independence was the revenue mechanism, not a tax on it: a guide that shaded its recommendations produced fewer miles and therefore fewer tires. Muntin's version is exact — a reference that operators believe makes them ask "am I paying above this?", and that question is unanswerable without their own invoice data, which is what Ledger is. So stop treating the index as marketing for a SaaS that has no users, no billing and a headline number biased low, and treat it as the business. This bet's decisive structural advantage over every other path in the register: it removes billing (`pricing-constants.ts` `billable:false`, no $19 SKU, `billing.ts:174-175` seat multiplication), the bookkeeper-channel schema block (`stripe_subscriptions.org_id`), and the Purchases-leg correctness debt from the critical path to revenue simultaneously — because an invoice from a one-person analysis desk requires none of them. What the index is missing is not rigor, it is the four rungs of citation capture: a single quotable number, a definition anyone can check, a cadence that never breaks, and permanence a fact-checker accepts. Muntin is strong on two and structurally broken on the other two — the headline is a percentage of a basket its own weights file labels "INTERNAL JUDGMENT… NOT a sourced fact," and the edition spine holds four editions ending 2026-07-06 against a first-Tuesday promise printed on 83 pages. Fix those four rungs and the company is a benchmark publisher with a free instrument, an inexhaustible editorial line, and three saleable labour products; leave them broken and it is a very well-audited hobby.

## The Company
POSITIONING. Muntin is a benchmark publisher, not a SaaS company. The formal instrument is the Muntin Restaurant Cost Index; the quotable object is the Muntin Reference Plate — a frozen, stipulated recipe priced in dollars from already-published wholesale references. The claim is deliberately narrow, because the wide one is occupied: BLS PPI, USDA ERS Food Price Outlook and the National Restaurant Association already own "food prices." The vacuum is per-ingredient, restaurant-framed, machine-readable, CC0, free, and — the differentiator no incumbent offers — it says when it does not know. 37 of 100 ingredients openly withheld is the pitch, not the gap.

CATEGORY. "The reference for what a restaurant's ingredients cost." Own the number, not the noun. We abandon the category campaign: 691 files carry DefinedTerm markup, `/for/restaurants/` carries the category term and has 0 inbound internal links in `data/link-graph.json`, and 234 of 344 glossary pages carry retired-line web-design vocabulary — so the term set we publish argues we are a restaurant-website-SEO company. A model fixes a definition by corroboration across independent domains, not by one domain asserting it. Attribution rides the possessive: "Muntin's reference plate," the way "the Economist's Big Mac Index" works.

BUYER. Not the operator, and not at $19. The Case-Shiller/Morningstar rule governs: we charge only for things that become MORE valuable the more widely the free number is quoted, and never for the number, access to it, earlier sight of it, or influence over it. That admits parties with budget and no ability to move a print: multi-unit restaurant groups, restaurant-specialist accounting firms, food-service lenders and insurers, franchisors, trade-press desks, culinary programs, and software vendors who need a market reference to say "you're paying above market." It forecloses distributors and manufacturers permanently — that is the LIBOR line and the strongest sentence on the site.

BUSINESS MODEL — three labour products over free data, in order of how soon they can invoice.
(1) The Muntin Reference Desk — a monthly written interpretive read plus a subscriber-named ingredient watchlist plus direct access to the analyst. Hypothesis price $250–500/month or $2,500–5,000/year. This is Morningstar's shape: the classifier (our lock/cushion/float/withhold postures — 50 flat, 18 easing, 11 structural, 3 emerging, 18 insufficient) is free and everywhere; the interpretation is sold to a different customer.
(2) Custom basket engagements — a basket weighted to one group's actual purchase mix, built once and published privately monthly. Hypothesis price $2,500–7,500 setup plus $250–750/month. This is the honest paid version of the thing the frozen public weights refuse to be.
(3) The certified feed — SLA'd hosted API, versioned schema, historical bulk delivery, a support contact. Hypothesis price $1,000–5,000/year. The bits stay CC0 and the free bulk archive ships alongside; what is sold is uptime, stability and a human who answers.
EVERY PRICE ABOVE IS AN UNVALIDATED HYPOTHESIS. No number here is measured; the first ten conversations set them, and a price we cannot stand behind is exactly what this brand cannot print.

WHAT THIS MAKES OF LEDGER. Downstream depth, not the business. It answers "am I paying above the reference?" against an operator's own invoices — the Michelin loop closed. Its posted $19 comes off the site now (it is `billable:false`, has no SKU, and seat multiplication would bill a 5-seat Accountant $750 against a correct $270), and paid GA on 2026-11-13 comes off the calendar, which also removes the mid-February first-charge problem entirely.

CONSTITUTIONAL ADR, founder-signed, first month. FORECLOSED: advertising, sponsorship, paid placement, affiliate or referral commission, lead-sale to distributors, award-licensing to the reviewed, submitted prices from any market participant including operators, paywalling or embargoing a reading, and selling exclusivity or early sight. AVAILABLE: interpretation, custom analysis, SLA'd delivery, support, education, and the product. Plus a Consumer Reports-style No Commercial Use clause — the data is free to reuse, but no vendor may cite a reading in advertising or imply endorsement. It costs nothing today because no vendor is citing us, which is precisely why it is credible: a promise made before there was money to refuse.

VENTURE SHAPE, honestly stated. The labour products are a good one-person business, not a venture. The venture is what they buy: if every piece of restaurant software that wants to say "you're paying above market" must reach for a market reference, and the only free, per-ingredient, restaurant-framed, machine-readable, CC0 one is Muntin's, then Muntin becomes the settlement layer for food cost and Ledger is the adjacent transaction — Zestimate to agent leads. That is the only path here where a free artifact scales past the founder's hours. It is a hypothesis, and rung one is the test.

## The Product
WHAT GETS BUILT — the Reference Plate, and it is the whole bet's rung one.

Today's headline is "−0.5% for a weighted basket of 16 staples against its baseline window" at 50.5% agreement, over weights `data/cost-basket-weights.json` itself calls "INTERNAL JUDGMENT (label illustrative), NOT a sourced fact," and whose `basket.asOf` reads 2026-06-01 because two contributors are frozen. No editor prints that. Replace it as the QUOTABLE number (keep it as the analytical one — never re-weight, never re-anchor) with a stipulated plate: a frozen, `_version`-stamped recipe of ingredient × quantity, priced as Σ(qty × published wholesale reference). The references already exist and are dollar-denominated: chicken-breast $1.22/lb (usda-ams-national), russet-potato $24.50/sack and onion $24.50/sack (usda-ams-baltimore), romaine-lettuce $26.00/carton and tomato $21.50/carton (usda-ams-atlanta), cheddar $1.52/lb and butter $1.60/lb (usda-lmr), ribeye $13.43/lb (usda-lmr), eggs $1.03/dozen. The sentence this produces is the one a journalist writes: "the wholesale ingredients for Muntin's reference plate cost $X.XX in August, up N cents from July."

THE HARD DETAIL, AND IT IS WHAT MAKES IT HONEST. References come per lb, per carton, per sack, per dozen. Converting a sack price to a plate needs a pack weight, and a pack weight is a judgement. So the pack weights go INTO the stipulation — published in `data/reference-plate.json` alongside the recipe, frozen at the same version, stated in the definition. A stipulation cannot be wrong, only more or less useful; an estimate can be wrong. That distinction is why the plate is HARDER to attack than the current composite, not softer. The plate never claims to be a menu price, a food-cost benchmark, or an average of what anyone pays. It says: these stated quantities, at these published wholesale references, on this date.

WITHHOLDING IS INHERITED. If any component's series is withheld or stale, the plate publishes WITHHELD with the reason rather than substituting a proxy. That is the product. A quietly aged print is the thing that ends the company.

BUILD: `data/reference-plate.json` (frozen recipe + pack weights + `_doc` carrying the never-re-specify discipline, modeled on the frozen-weights pattern); `scripts/build-reference-plate.mjs` (Σ via the parity-locked conversion, coverage/withholding rules inherited from the basket, emits `cost-index/reference-plate.json` + `.csv`, renders `/cost-index/plate/` EN+ES, and an embed card); the plate value frozen into every `data/cost-index-editions.json` entry so the series is self-comparable forever; `scripts/check-reference-plate.mjs` with `--check`, wired into `check-all` in the same commit, and the builder wired into `build.command`. Founder decides the recipe and the pack weights, once, in thirty minutes, forever.

CORRECTNESS IS GUARANTEED BY MAKING THE PROSE STOP CITING MOVING DATA. The 72 live falsehoods on `blog/cost-index-2026-07/index.html` were all true on publication day; the cause is static prose annotated with `<!-- src: -->` pointers at LIVE files, a parallel convention operating outside the fact gate. The fix is structural and matches how the weights were already handled: repoint all 165 annotations in that post at the FROZEN edition snapshot in `data/cost-index-editions.json`, and write `scripts/check-src-sentinel.mjs` — which the founder named "rung 0" ten days ago and which does not exist on disk, while the page's own citation drawer at line 852 tells a reader those files are "re-checked in CI." That drawer is a false claim about a gate, which for this brand is categorically worse than a stale number. Then ship `/cost-index/corrections/` from an append-only `data/cost-index-corrections.json` whose every entry names the gate now preventing recurrence — Michelin publishes demotions on schedule, unsigned, without apology; Consumer Reports published the retest that reversed its own verdict. Note the gap this fills: `data/cost-revisions.json` logs 10,294 SOURCE revisions, other people's numbers changing. Nothing logs ours.

WHAT GETS CUT. No forecast, ever — not "a week ahead of your costs," not an outlook number, not a direction verb. The band is HOW FAR, never WHICH WAY, and the July dispatch already asserted a downward lean the live artifact contradicts. No 101st ingredient while 26 series are frozen. No LLM anywhere near the index's commentary, driver text or ingredient prose — `no-llm-ci.sh` is the most falsifiable claim either repo owns and it is named on zero of 1,327 storefront pages while 93 make the claim it proves; put it on `/press/`, `/cost-index/methodology/` and the plate page. And no operator-contributed price lane, ever: an operator is a market participant, and a caveated no-submitters clause is a weaker instrument than an absolute one. LIBOR died of architecture, not of a bad print.

MUNTIN LEDGER, under this bet: frozen as a paid product, kept alive as free depth for the handful of people the index sends. Its three arithmetic biases (Purchases missing credit memos, $0 promo lines and unparseable packs; the default count mode zeroing usage for un-entered items; `inventory-purchases-store.ts:19-20` carrying no location predicate against per-location counts) stay off the critical path to revenue, because nothing in the index touches them. When there is money and a validated buyer, they get paid down properly with a CPA in the room and one real month run on Tacombi's own invoices — which is founder-blocked work that cannot be bought with agent hours anyway.

## How It Fails
THE FAILURE I WOULD BET ON: the Reference Desk finds no buyer, and this becomes a beautifully-maintained public good with no revenue. The mechanism is precise and it is the $19 arithmetic reappearing one level up. The parties with budget and no ability to move a print — groups, lenders, insurers, firms, trade press — already get "food prices are up" free from BLS, USDA ERS and the National Restaurant Association, and none of them has a decision that a per-ingredient wholesale reference changes. Interpretation is a nice-to-have for a watcher and a must-have only for a buyer, and the buyer is the operator, who cannot pay institutional prices. If that is true, then the honest monetization set collapses to consulting hours sold by a man who already works full-time as a front-of-house manager, and this bet has built the world's best-audited food price index for an audience of zero. Every price in this plan is an unvalidated hypothesis and I have marked them as such; that marking is also the admission that the revenue half of this bet rests on nothing measured.

THE SECOND FAILURE: cadence breaks anyway. Automating the fetch fixes the data, not the edition. The edition still needs a human signature, and the gap between an automated data ritual and a published editorial ritual is exactly where every hand-calendared obligation in this company has died — the warrant canary at 89 days unsigned, the transparency report 37 days late, the first-Tuesday dispatch missed on 2026-08-04 with a 38-day gate already reding to say so. Coursework starts. 20 hours a month is the optimistic figure. If the September edition ships on novelty and October slips, rung three is unreachable and every other rung is decoration on a decaying record.

THE THIRD FAILURE: the plate reads as invention, not stipulation. The Big Mac survived that charge because the Economist was already the Economist and framed it self-deprecatingly. Muntin has no institutional cover, and the plate requires stipulated pack weights on top of stipulated quantities — two layers of judgement over sourced references. A hostile trade editor writes "a website nobody has heard of made up a plate and priced it," and the very fact gate that makes everything else credible cannot defend a claim about the world.

AND THE HONEST COST OF CHOOSING THIS: the quarter goes to distribution while the product's headline number stays biased low three ways, Ledger stays unlaunched, and the bookkeeper channel stays structurally closed. If the Desk works, that is fine and the product becomes the funded depth tier. If it does not, the company has spent its best quarter amplifying an asset with no buyer, the live-capture chain is 188 days old instead of 98, and there are still zero users.
