# Radical narrowing — one number, one surface, one job

## Thesis
Muntin is not short of assets; it is short of a single point of contact with a human being, and it has 1,327 pages of surface area preventing it from building one. The company's own inventories say this out loud: of 492 library pages, exactly 3 cluster as cost-intelligence (`content-intent.json`); 135 of 344 glossary pages carry a retired-line score ≥3; 78 of 126 orphans are indexable event pages with zero inbound links; 162 pages are measured dead weight. A one-person company cannot defend 1,327 promises, and the ledger proves it — ~53 obligation-hours a month against 13–26 available, ~10% actually paid, and nothing ever retired. So amputate. Keep the one number that is already built, already deterministic, already CI-checked, and already dated three days ago: the **posture** — Lock / Cushion / Float / Won't-call — computed for 100 ingredients in `data/cost-lockfloat.json` (counts today: lock 37, cushion 19, float 7, withhold 37), a *risk* read that natively satisfies the "how far, never which way" non-negotiable. Retire the weighted-basket headline, whose own file marks its weights "INTERNAL JUDGMENT (label illustrative), NOT a sourced fact" — a percentage of an abstraction no editor will print. Keep the one surface those postures live on: `/cost-index/<ingredient>/`, 94 EN pages with an average of 57.9 inbound internal links and zero orphans. Keep the one job an operator actually has on a Tuesday: *can I commit to a price on this, and am I paying above the market?* Free at the market level (the index), private at the invoice level (Ledger). Everything else — the glossary, the library, the sheets, the learn tree, the audio pipeline, the food-cost identity, two pricing tiers, and 651 pages of Spanish prose — is severed. The company that survives is roughly 120 English pages plus a Spanish price mirror that regenerates from the same source at zero translation cost. That company is defensible by one person, provably correct on every claim it still makes, and pointed at exactly one query.

## The Company
**Positioning:** Muntin publishes the Lock List — the monthly record of which restaurant ingredients are priceable enough to commit to, and which it refuses to call. Not "restaurant cost intelligence" (a phrase that appears in 2 of 1,327 HTML files and has never earned a citation). Not a category campaign; the branded-index panel is right that category creation is a sales-enablement mechanism for companies with working demand capture, and Muntin has 4 waitlist forms across 1,327 pages.

**The headline sentence, monthly:** "Muntin's Lock List, August 2026: 37 of 100 restaurant ingredients are priceable enough to commit to this month. 37 we won't call." That is a count, not a percentage of a construct — it needs no basket weights, no methodology gloss, and it survives paraphrase because attribution rides the possessive ("Muntin's Lock List"). It is a serial: it changes every month and the archive is append-only.

**Buyer, in order of who pays first:**
1. *A reader of the free number* who wants Don's written interpretation. **The Lock List (paid), $49/month or $490/year.** Zero billing code — a Stripe payment link. Permitted by the independence contract, which forecloses only placement and influence, never analysis. The writing is the same writing the free monthly dispatch already requires, so this **retires** an obligation rather than adding one — the only kind of new ritual the capacity math allows.
2. *An operator with invoices.* **Muntin Ledger, $29/location/month, $290/year, ONE SKU.** No seats. No Team tier. No Accountant tier. This deletes the `billing.ts:174-175` seat-multiplication defect (which would bill a 5-seat Accountant $750 against a correct $270) by deleting the tiers that made multiplication necessary, and it creates the annual plan that does not exist today — which is the only thing that stops the first charge landing in mid-February, the second-worst cash month on the repo's own Census MARTS seasonality.
3. *Don's own DMV vendor network.* **Cost reviews, priced per engagement.** Labour, doesn't scale, and it is the fastest validated dollar available. It also produces the only thing money cannot buy: more real invoices.

**Why $29 and not $79–149:** the operator-money panel is right that $19 is a self-imposed floor and that the real barrier is TCO, not price — a monthly count costs $100–150 of floor labour. But it prices a *measured food-cost number*, and this bet deletes that number because it is not correct. We do not charge a premium for a computation we just decided not to perform. $29 with zero manual input is a defensible price for the only tool in the category that costs the operator no monthly labour at all.

**The bookkeeper channel is deferred, not killed.** The GTM panel's arithmetic is sound and the channel is structurally closed anyway (`stripe_subscriptions.org_id` makes the restaurant the billing subject; `accountant` is a guest permission; the Accountant tier ships `hasCockpit: true` against a `/cockpit` route that does not exist). We do not open a channel to a buyer whose first question is "does this include the other location's invoices?" while `inventory-purchases-store.ts:19-20` documents that it does not.

**The venture claim, stated honestly:** the labour business does not scale and I am not pretending it does. The venture bet is that `/cost-index/<ingredient>/` becomes the page an answer engine returns for "ribeye wholesale price, August 2026" — the Case-Shiller architecture, where the free number's ubiquity *is* the product and money only ever attaches to things that get more valuable the more widely the number is quoted. That is a decade bet. Narrowing is the only way one employed person survives long enough to place it.

## The Storefront
**muntin.digital stops being a company website with an index on it and becomes the index, with a company footer.**

**What survives — ~120 EN routes:**
- **94 ingredient pages** (`/cost-index/<slug>/`). Measured: zero orphans, 57.9 average inbound internal links, 81 of 94 already carry a subscribe form. Each one absorbs three things it does not have today: (a) the **yield fact** folded from `/library/ingredient-yields/<slug>/` — 90 of 133 yield slugs match a cost-index slug exactly; (b) the **co-occurrence events** already governed by ADR-011, which lets us kill 78 standalone orphan event pages; (c) its **own freshness, printed as a fact** — "last measured 2026-08-04" or "frozen since 2026-06-01, reason: no maintained free source."
- **The spine, 6 pages:** `/cost-index/`, `/cost-index/methodology/`, `/cost-index/sources/`, `/cost-index/weekly/`, `/cost-index/basket/` (demoted, relabeled internal-judgment), and one genuinely new page — **`/cost-index/track-record/`**, the calibration record as a destination rather than a stat strip: 79,086 scored conformal steps at 0.767 realized coverage against 0.800 nominal with **zero under-covering items**, and the direction-call hit rate of 0.506 against a 0.502 baseline over 34,029 calls, published because it is unflattering. This is the page a fact-checker asks for before an editor permits a citation to a site they have never heard of.
- **5 dispatch editions** (`/blog/cost-index-*`), the of-record chain. Slugs final-forever.
- **1 product page** (`/ledger/`), **1 home**, and ~14 chrome/trust routes (about, methods, claims, receipts, trust, security, status, changelog, privacy, terms, accessibility, cookies, 404, sign-in).
- **2 tools** of 16 — Cost Pulse and Vendor Benchmark — because they are the only two that consume the one number.
- **~101 Spanish price pages** and nothing else in Spanish.

**What the storefront's job becomes:** make one number reachable and believable. Three mechanisms, all currently one line short:
- **The broadcast filter.** `worker.js:8793` sends only where `source === 'cost-index'`. Live values are 613 `footer`, 162 `cost-index-ingredient`, 2 `cost-index`. 775 people signed up for a monthly email and 773 of them have never received one. Widening that filter is the single largest distribution act available to the company and it costs one line.
- **The determinism falsifier.** `no-llm-ci.sh` is named on **zero of 1,327 pages** while 93 pages make the claim it proves. It goes on `/cost-index/methodology/` and `/ledger/` with the exact command a stranger can run.
- **Analytics.** 274 of 1,327 pages emit any, and **0 of 94 ingredient pages do**. Every surviving page gets it before anything is deleted.

**The equity we destroy, counted honestly:** 419 pages carry retired-line vocabulary and 1,066 pages are indexable. We are deleting roughly 1,100 routes without knowing what they earn, because the company never measured. The mitigation is not confidence, it is sequencing: 301 everything to its strongest surviving counterpart (90 yield pages → ingredient pages; 46 cost-clustered glossary terms → ingredient pages and methodology; 78 event pages → their ingredient), and cut the *unmeasurable* categories first — the 162 measured dead-weight pages (142 already frozen and out of the index, 14 noindex-and-unlinked, 6 admin harnesses) and the 103 indexable pages scoring retiredLineScore ≥3, which the company's own positioning gate already says must be frozen or justified. Those need no traffic data to justify. Everything else waits 30 days for analytics.

**The trust equity we destroy is real and I am not minimizing it.** 80 operator sheets and 50 learn pages are the gift economy the storefront's positioning is built on. The counter-evidence is that the gift was never connected to anything: 4 of 1,327 pages carry a waitlist form, 775 subscribe forms feed a filter that skips 773 of them, and after fifteen months there are zero confirmed users. Generosity that reaches no one is not trust equity; it is unpaid maintenance.

## The Product
**Muntin Ledger becomes the private half of the Lock List: your invoice line, against the published index read, with the posture that item carried when you bought it.** Nothing else.

**What gets cut — the largest thing the company has ever shipped.** The entire count/inventory feature (Tier 0→3, PR #222): `/count`, `/reorder`, `/insights/food-cost`, `/insights/variance`, `inventory-reconcile.ts`, `inventory-theoretical-store`, `inventory-pars-store`, and the food-cost percentage itself. This is not a cost-saving move; it is a correctness move, and the evidence is the company's own:
- `line_item_observations` was engineered for **price intelligence** — it drops credit memos, drops $0 promo lines, drops unparseable pack tokens, and stores `unit_price × quantity` rather than the printed `line_total`. It was then reused unchanged as the **Purchases leg** of `Beginning + Purchases − Ending = Usage`. Every one of those omissions is *correct* for a per-unit price read and *fatal* for a total-spend read. The narrowing is simply: return the table to the job it was built for.
- The default count mode writes un-entered items at expected-on-hand, making their usage arithmetically **zero** — headline food cost is structurally biased low.
- `inventory-purchases-store.ts:19-20` documents that the roll-up has **no location predicate** while counts are per-location — so in Don's own two-location shape, each location's usage includes the other's invoices.
- `inventory-reconcile.ts:249` is an empty `if (estimated) {}` block whose comment claims a behaviour the code does not perform.

Fixing all of that requires a CPA review, one real month on Tacombi's data, and a location-attribution product decision — founder-blocked work in a company with 13–26 discretionary hours a month. Deleting it takes agent sessions.

**What the cut buys, immediately:**
- **The manual input disappears.** CLAUDE.md states the count is "the only manual input." Kill the count and Ledger has none: upload an invoice, get an answer. That erases the $100–150/month floor-labour TCO the operator panel names as the *actual* barrier — which price cuts never touched.
- **The `needs_review` wall stops being fatal.** Today `needs_review` blocks `/post` to the ledger, so a low-confidence read means an uploaded invoice can never post. With no ledger to post to, an unread line renders as "we could not read this line" instead of gating the feature. Determinism's ~75% novel-invoice ceiling stops being an existential cap.
- **The determinism constraint flips from handicap to fit.** A food-cost identity needs *total coverage* — the one thing a deterministic engine cannot promise. A price check needs *per-line certainty* — the one thing it is excellent at (i_halper 93.2% n=73, chefs_warehouse 92.1% n=214, baldor 89.7%). Narrow to what the engine is actually good at.
- **The POS denominator problem vanishes.** No net sales, no partial-window denominator, no food-cost % printed against 12 of 30 settled days.

**How correctness is guaranteed on what remains.** Every claim Ledger makes is a *per-line* claim, and a per-line claim is checkable by the operator holding the paper. The product prints: "Invoice #, line 4: ribeye, $X/lb. Muntin's published read for that week: $Y/lb. That ingredient was a **Lock** in July." Three facts, each traceable — the first to the operator's own PDF, the second to a CC0 published file with a DOI, the third to a CI-checked deterministic classifier with a published coverage record. No aggregation, no estimate shadowing a measurement, no identity that can be biased low.

**Also cut:** the Team and Accountant tiers, the `hasCockpit: true` entitlement with no route, and the seat-quantity checkout path. One SKU, one price, per location, annual available. Switch on `EXTRACT_VENDOR_TRUST_ACTIVE` — the measured 24% → ~62% first-pass improvement on the 919-doc corpus that is sitting in shadow mode solving the exact cold start that would kill a first user. And redact `services/extract/tests/golden/real_fixtures/fresco_lineitems.json:390`, which carries a real Tacombi address, invoice numbers, Net-45 terms and negotiated unit prices in a public MIT repo, before publishing anything that makes a stranger look.

## How It Fails
**The failure mode I would actually bet on: the cut half-lands, and the company ends up strictly worse.** Nothing has ever been retired in this company's history. This plan requires ~15 agent sessions and roughly 20 founder-hours of irreversible judgement, against a realized maintenance payment rate of ~10% and a deploy that is red right now (312 of 320) on five builders absent from the build chain — one of which, `build-ingredient-state-record`, needs the founder's Mac and API keys and cannot be cleared from a container at all. If 400 pages get deleted and 700 get kept, and the gates get half-retired, Muntin has surrendered the SEO surface *and* kept the obligation. That is the modal outcome for a company with this closure record, and it is worse than doing nothing.

**The second failure mode: we cut traffic we could not see.** 1,066 pages are indexable and only 274 emit any analytics. I am proposing to delete roughly 1,100 routes on the argument that a per-ingredient price page is a stronger citation target than 492 explanatory essays. That is a bet on answer-engine behaviour, not a measured fact, and this company has never measured referral traffic at all. If the library and glossary are in fact the entire source of whatever organic traffic exists, day 90 leaves 120 pages, no traffic, one number nobody has cited, and a product that does less than it did in July.

**The third failure mode, and the one nobody wants to say: the Lock List may be honest and unwanted.** "37 of 100 ingredients are priceable this month" is a claim about *Muntin's own confidence*, not about the world. An editor wants "beef is up 11%." Direction has been deliberately and permanently foreclosed — the 2026-07 rigor audit killed every direction claim and the trend hit rate is 0.506 against a 0.502 baseline. It is entirely possible that the only number this company can honestly publish is a number nobody wants to quote, and that the honesty which makes it defensible is exactly what makes it unquotable.

**Dated signals that would prove this wrong, in the order they arrive:**
- **Day 30 — the cut list.** Analytics live on all 1,327 pages. If more than 60% of sessions land on library or glossary routes queued for deletion, the cut list is wrong and moves 3 onward must be re-scoped against measurement. Cut the provably-dead 260 regardless; pause the rest.
- **Day 45 — the audience.** The corrected dispatch goes to 775 subscribers. If the open rate is under 10%, or the list is largely undeliverable, then the "largest reachable audience the company owns" is not an audience, and the storefront has no distribution to narrow toward. That would mean the traffic problem is upstream of the surface problem and this whole bet is aimed at the wrong layer.
- **Day 60 — the revenue thesis.** The paid Lock List link is live. If zero people pay $49 in thirty days from 775 subscribers plus whatever organic traffic exists, then "sell analysis over free data" — the only revenue path in this bet that needs no product, no billing code and no launch — is dead, and the fallback is a product we just deliberately made smaller.
- **Day 75 — the private half.** Don's 20-invoice walk. If more than a handful of the 20 unit prices cannot be matched to a published index read, then Ledger has nothing to say and the "one job" has only its free half. That is the finding that would send me back to the food-cost identity despite everything above.
- **Continuous — the capacity guard.** If the cut consumes more than 25 founder-hours across the 90 days, stop and freeze wherever it stands. The arithmetic that motivated the bet (53 obligation-hours against 13–26 available) also condemns it: an amputation that costs more than it saves in the first quarter will not finish, and an unfinished amputation is the worst state of all.
