# Scale, Ambition & the Shape of the Company

**Verdict: MAJOR_REVISION**

## Thesis
The $288K ceiling is not a fact about this asset base — it is a consequence of one chosen delivery model, and the verdict presents it as though it were arithmetic. Market size never binds: there were 412,498 independent US restaurants at end-2025 (external, secondary), so even a low-single-digit specialty-vendor share is tens of thousands of locations. What binds is that the close is hand-delivered. Worse, the ceiling and the capacity limit arrive at the same instant: the verdict's 13.3 h/month counts close review only (20 min x 40 locations) and nothing else. Add support, onboarding, hand-invoicing, and replacement selling at even 20% churn and steady state is ~23 h/month against a 13-26 band — so at exactly the point the business stops growing, it has consumed the entire founder budget and has zero surplus hours to fund an escape. That is the trap mechanism, and it is structural, not attitudinal. Meanwhile the one asset in either repo with genuine returns to scale is never named: the cross-tenant community template pool (migrations 0013/0056/0057, services/extract/community_pool.py, engine flag EXTRACT_COMMUNITY_POOL default-ON at engine_pkg/assemble.py:802, per-org opt-in default-off). Six repair slices already landed — row-invariant column_v2, held-out lift gate, supersession, operator-correction retirement — and the sole remaining gate is real-invoice layout coverage. A rule authored once serves every customer thereafter; forty customers ever starves the only mechanism whose marginal cost declines. The verdict's own buyer decision makes that gate cheap and it does not notice: the roadmap wanted "the top ~10 BROADLINER layouts," but the chosen buyer has zero broadliners, and VENDOR-TEMPLATE-KEY.md:213-224 already measures the corpus's top seven specialty vendors at 79-93% reconcile. The same re-scoping un-parks /try — a stranger dropping their own invoice into the deterministic engine, already built, Turnstile'd, budget-broken, no-persistence-gated (ADR-007) — which is a strictly stronger public falsifier and acquisition surface than the /tools/pack-check/ the verdict proposes building. On the two branches: (a) a deliberate $250-300K, ~23 h/month service business is a genuinely excellent outcome at roughly $1,000 per founder-hour and I would endorse it if the founder says that is what he wants; (b) a larger company is available and its wedge is not the close but the specialty-vendor extraction layer nobody serves, with the close as its first application. The verdict is wrong only in presenting (a) as the honest ceiling rather than as a choice, in hard-capping at forty by public commitment in the one company that cannot break a public commitment, and in taking several branch-asymmetric kills without labelling them as such.

## Neglected
- **[DISQUALIFYING] The founder-hour ceiling counts close review only; adding the rest of hand-delivery puts steady state at the top of the budget, leaving zero surplus to ever escape the model**
  - Evidence: The verdict's own figure is '≈20 founder-minutes of close review × 40 locations = 13.3 h/month against 13-26 available'. That excludes per-customer support, onboarding a new customer's vendor layouts, hand-invoicing, and replacement selling. Modelled at 20 customers (2 loc avg): close 13.3 + support (20 min/cust) 6.7 + invoicing 0.8 + replacement sales at 20% churn 1.0 + onboarding 1.3 = ~23.2 h/month. The 13 h/month floor of the founder's own band is crossed at ~24 locations (~12 customers), less than a third of the way to the ceiling. Nothing named `desk_minutes`, `deskMinutes` or `close_minutes` exists anywhere in either repo — the one number the verdict says decides everything is not instrumented.
  - Consequence: The plan reaches its revenue ceiling and its capacity ceiling simultaneously. There is never a month in which the business generates spare founder-hours, which is the only resource that could fund productization. The small version is therefore a trap by construction, not by temperament.
- **[DISQUALIFYING] The only asset with declining marginal cost — the cross-tenant community template pool — is never mentioned, and a 40-customer cap starves it**
  - Evidence: infra/postgres/migrations/0013_community_templates.sql (the pool, deliberately the one table without RLS because it is global by construction), 0056 supersession, 0057 distinct-org rejection counter; services/extract/community_pool.py; the fan applied at engine_pkg/assemble.py:802 with EXTRACT_COMMUNITY_POOL default=True; per-org opt-in default OFF (docs/community-templates.md, 'Default posture: Off'). docs/plans/first-try-reliability-roadmap.md logs six landed repair slices including the row-invariant column_v2 that fixed the fatal 'pooled fingerprint identifies a CELL not a COLUMN' defect, plus a held-out lift gate and operator-correction retirement. services/extract/cli/publish_community_seed.py lets the founder author rules once and pre-fill every opted-in org's FIRST invoice from that vendor.
  - Consequence: The verdict's own claim that 'marginal cost scales with distinct vendor layouts, not with invoices or customers' is true only if this pool is live and populated. As written the plan neither activates it nor reaches the customer count at which pooled contribution compounds — so the economics it asserts are the economics of a mechanism it leaves dark.
- **[MAJOR] The verdict's buyer choice makes the parked /try demo cheap to un-park, and the verdict instead proposes building a weaker falsifier**
  - Evidence: docs/plans/first-try-reliability-roadmap.md: the one remaining un-park gate is '(a) real-invoice coverage… (i) the actual top-~10 broadliner layouts'. The verdict's buyer is defined by 'zero or one broadliner' and the corpus contains zero Sysco/US Foods/PFG. services/extract/VENDOR-TEMPLATE-KEY.md:213-224 already measures i_halper 93.2%, chefs_warehouse 92.1%, baldor 89.7%, pat_lafrieda 87.5%, edward_don 85.7%, fresco 83.1%, montgomery_county_dlc 79.4% on the 919-doc corpus. ADR-007 records /try as BUILT and hardened (5MB cap, per-IP/ASN limits, $50/day budget breaker, Turnstile, replay cache, phone-photo downscale, CI-gated no-persistence), un-gating being 'a single flag flip'.
  - Consequence: The verdict proposes /tools/pack-check/ as 'the one falsifier a stranger can actually execute' while a stronger one — the stranger's OWN invoice, read by the same deterministic engine — sits behind a gate its own buyer decision just made satisfiable. It also gives up the only acquisition surface that works without founder hours, in a plan whose scarcest resource is founder hours.
- **[MAJOR] $600/location is set against a misread competitive anchor, and realized discounting collapses the ceiling**
  - Evidence: Search results as of Aug 2026 (UNVERIFIED, secondary aggregator pages) report MarginEdge at $350/location/month flat with unlimited invoice processing and bill pay included, $250 one-time setup, and $660-$1,650/month for two-to-five locations with consolidated daily P&L. Muntin at $600/location quotes a 3-location buyer $1,800/month against roughly $990. The '$350-500 demonstrated willingness to pay' the verdict leans on is willingness to pay for the incumbent's full bundle, not evidence of $600 for a narrower monthly deliverable.
  - Consequence: Multi-location buyers — the ones who make 40 locations reachable at 20 customers — see the worst relative price, so the mix skews to single-location buyers and the location count per customer falls. Every 25 points of realized discount removes ~$72K from a $288K ceiling, and the ceiling is already the plan's entire upside.
- **[MAJOR] The plan converts a capacity estimate into a public commitment, in the one company whose sole asset is that its statements hold**
  - Evidence: The verdict specifies 'Cohort hard-capped at 10 for the pilot and ~40 ever' alongside a storefront that replaces five posted prices with 'Pricing set at the close of the pilot' — i.e. the cap sits in the same public register as the price. The doctrine adopted wholesale is that credibility is the only asset the company has.
  - Consequence: A soft internal ceiling is reversible; a published 'forty ever' is not, for this company specifically. If the pilot succeeds and demand exceeds 40, growth requires publicly breaking a stated limit — the exact category of act the brand is built to make impossible. The trap then closes through brand rather than through economics.
- **[MAJOR] The verdict's own seasonal-invoicing rule cannot be satisfied by its own 13-week sequence**
  - Evidence: It kills the 2026-11-13 GA partly because three-months-free lands the first charge against Census MARTS medians of Jan 0.913 / Feb 0.917, then adopts 'billed annually at $6,000, invoiced by hand, in the May-August seasonal peak'. Phase 3 (the price call, the application page, ten locations) is weeks 10-13, which from 2026-08-07 is roughly 2026-10-16 to 2026-11-06.
  - Consequence: The first invoice either goes out in November — the timing it just rejected — or slips to May 2027, nine further months at zero revenue for a founder already fifteen months in. Neither branch survives this: (a) needs first dollar to justify continuing, (b) needs it to fund anything.
- **[MAJOR] Several kills are branch-asymmetric and are taken without labelling which branch they serve**
  - Evidence: Killed: the bookkeeper CHANNEL (the only acquisition path with multiplication — one firm introduces many restaurants); the Solo/Team/Accountant tiers (re-introducing tiers later means re-pricing existing customers, the one move a trust-brand cannot make cheaply); the operator-submitter lane, foreclosed by ADR on the ground that it creates 'a permanent, undelegatable founder moderation queue'. But the community pool's contribute path needs no moderation queue: docs/community-templates.md and apps/api/src/lib/community-templates.ts show redactSafe + a ~15-header server-side allowlist + a re-walk assertion, and migration 0057 retires a bad rule on N distinct-org corrections automatically.
  - Consequence: At 40 customers all three kills are correct. At 400 the first removes the channel, the second forecloses volume pricing, and the third — if drafted broadly — takes the community pool's supply side down with it by association. The verdict gives the founder no way to see which decisions are cheap to revisit and which are one-way doors.
- **[MAJOR] The promise that 'every rule is inspectable, replayable, and published to the customer' is falsified by the code path that would serve it**
  - Evidence: apps/api/src/lib/templates-store.ts:180-181 — defaultTemplatesStore returns NeonTemplatesStore whenever DATABASE_URL is set; NeonTemplatesStore.listForOrg / getByIdForOrg / retire all `throw new Error("… not yet wired (needs @neondatabase/serverless install)")` at lines 144, 152, 175 — yet apps/api/package.json:19 already declares "@neondatabase/serverless": "1.1.0". apps/api/src/routes/templates.ts:179,198,227 call it on every request.
  - Consequence: In production with a database configured, the customer-facing 'show me the rules that read my invoice' surface is a 500, not a stub. The exception desk's central honesty guarantee — the thing the $600 price is justified by — has no working delivery surface, and this is a latent production error rather than an unbuilt feature.

## Recommendations
- [founder] **Settle the scale branch in one written page BEFORE Phase 1, and put it in an ADR: either 'Muntin is a deliberate ~$250-300K one-person service business' or 'the close is the wedge into a specialty-vendor extraction layer'. Every recommendation below and roughly a third of the verdict's kills change sign on this one sentence.** — The orchestrator introduced 'venture-scale' into an earlier context and the judge may have optimised against an invented constraint. This is the only fact no agent can supply, it costs thirty minutes, and it is currently blocking a correct reading of the entire plan.
- [founder] **Do not publish the 40-location cap. Publish the pilot cap of 10, which is an operational fact; keep 40 as an internal planning ceiling recorded in the ADR, not on the storefront.** — A published 'forty ever' is unbreakable for a company whose only asset is that its statements hold. Ten is honest, creates the same scarcity, and leaves the growth question open at no credibility cost.
- [agent] **Instrument all five founder-hour lines from customer one, not just desk_minutes_per_close: close review, support, onboarding, invoicing, replacement selling. None of them exist in either repo today (grep for desk_minutes/deskMinutes/close_minutes returns nothing).** — The verdict makes desk_minutes_per_close 'the one number that decides whether it is more than that' while measuring one of the five terms that actually consume the budget. On the four-term model the founder's own floor is crossed at ~24 locations, not 40.
- [agent] **Re-scope the /try un-park gate from 'the actual top-~10 broadliner layouts' to the seven specialty vendors already measured ≥79% reconcile, seed the community pool from the 919-doc corpus via cli/seed_template.py → cli/publish_community_seed.py with the held-out lift gate enforced, then flip DEMO_ANONYMOUS_EXTRACT. Treat this as the single highest-leverage agent-workforce task in either repo.** — The verdict's own buyer definition (zero or one broadliner) makes the blocking gate satisfiable with assets already on disk. It serves both branches: at 40 customers /try is the qualification instrument and the strongest public falsifier available; at 400 the seeded pool is the returns-to-scale engine. It also costs almost no founder hours — the demo is already hardened per ADR-007.
- [agent] **Split the operator-submitter ADR in two: forbid PRICE and DATA submissions (which genuinely create an undelegatable moderation queue), and explicitly preserve the community-template CONTRIBUTION path, naming redactSafe, the ~15-header allowlist, and migration 0057's N-distinct-org supersession as the reason it needs no moderation.** — The stated reason for the kill does not apply to the contribution path, and a broadly drafted ADR would permanently close the only supply mechanism that scales past the founder — the exact door that is free to leave open today and expensive to reopen.
- [founder] **Re-shape the price so multi-location buyers are not quoted the worst relative number: a per-CUSTOMER close fee with declining location adders, benchmarked explicitly against the incumbent's own 2-5 location bundle rather than against its single-location sticker. Verify the incumbent's current posted pricing directly before the price call.** — At $600 × locations a three-location buyer sees roughly 1.8× the incumbent for a narrower deliverable, which selects against exactly the customers who make 40 locations reachable at 20 accounts. The competitive figures I have are secondary-source and must be confirmed first-hand before a price is posted by a company that publishes nothing it cannot stand behind.
- [founder] **Bill monthly in arrears against a delivered close rather than annually in a season. Drop 'invoiced in the May-August peak' entirely.** — Phase 3 lands mid-October to early November, so the seasonal rule forces either the November timing the verdict itself killed or a nine-month slip to May 2027. Monthly-in-arrears after a delivered close is also how an audit opinion is actually billed, which is the analogy the whole positioning rests on.
- [agent] **Fill in or fail-fast the NeonTemplatesStore bodies (apps/api/src/lib/templates-store.ts:144/152/175) before any customer sees a close receipt, and keep two accounting firms as named referral partners with a written fee in Phase 2 rather than killing the bookkeeper relationship outright.** — The rule-inspection surface is a production 500 whenever DATABASE_URL is set, which falsifies the exact promise the $600 price is justified by. And deferring the bookkeeper CHANNEL while retaining firms as referral sources costs nothing and preserves the only non-serial acquisition path, which is the difference between a reversible and a one-way kill.

## Would change my verdict
The single fact is the founder's own written answer to: "do I want a ~$250-300K, ~23-hour-a-month service business I could run for a decade, or a company?" It costs thirty minutes and no research, and it is the fact the judge assumed rather than obtained. If the answer is the former, my verdict moves to ENDORSE_WITH_CONDITIONS — the plan is close to right, and $288K at roughly $1,000 per founder-hour is an outcome most people never reach; the conditions reduce to the honest hour model, the unpublished cap, and the billing-timing fix. If the answer is the latter, the spine must change: the close becomes the first application of a specialty-vendor extraction layer rather than the company, the community pool and /try move from unmentioned to Phase 1, and the tier/channel/submitter kills must be re-taken with that branch named. The cheap second fact, already scheduled as Phase 0, is the measured minutes-per-close from the twenty-invoice founder walk — if a close costs 90 minutes rather than 20, the honest ceiling is roughly a dozen locations and both branches are re-planned regardless of ambition.
