# Accessibility, Performance & the Craft Floor — both repos

## Thesis
A company whose product withholds numbers it cannot prove currently publishes an unqualified accessibility opinion its own tooling disproves, and guards both of its accessibility workflows so they can never fail. That is not a compliance gap; it is the honesty contract breaking on the one surface where the company grades itself. `accessibility.html` says "Text meets WCAG AA standards for color contrast" while `--stone-2:#9AA0AB` renders at 2.45:1 on cream in 58 places including every form placeholder — and `check-stone-2-usage.mjs` has counted them since 2026-07-28 as a non-blocking report. So the craft floor is not a checklist to satisfy; it is an instrument the company must be willing to read out loud. My position: retire the compliance statement entirely and replace it with a generated, dated, **qualified** conformance report in the exact register of the closed month — verdict first, then coverage, then typed exceptions with file:line and measured ratio, then schedules — carrying the command a stranger runs to reproduce it. A qualified opinion is the most authoritative sentence an auditor writes; an unqualified false one is the least. Everything else follows from making the instrument real. The two contrast gates today test 41 hand-curated pairs from a hardcoded table and never open an HTML file; of 328 gates in `check-all.mjs`, zero parse rendered markup for accessibility. The fix is not more gates, it is *derived* gates that compute the cross product rather than read a list a human maintained — because a curated list is exactly how `stone` got tested against three surfaces but never against `cream-2`, where it fails at 4.17:1. On performance the numbers are not close: 200MB of `brand/` PNGs shipping to the edge, a 6MB portrait as an `<img>` fallback nobody's browser will ever request, 116KB gzip of CSS on every route, and 15.4MB of duplicated inline `<style>` — 20% of the site's HTML bytes. And the gate that should have caught the images is `WARN_ONLY`, skips `brand/`, and reports success on zero subjects found. Two things here are already frontier-grade and must be protected rather than redesigned: the product's five a11y gates all ship `--self-test`, which is the only mechanism in either repo that proves a gate can fail; and `ConfidenceChip`'s withheld state — hollow ring, dotted border, aria "no value read — not guessed" — is better than anything in the published pattern literature. The same component then prints `${pct}%` from zero calibration data, which is the one falsifiable-and-false number the company ships.

## Proposal
THE SURFACE: `/floor/` — the company's own qualified opinion, generated.

Delete `accessibility.html` and `es/accessibility.html`. In their place, `scripts/build-floor.mjs` writes `/floor/index.html` from the gate output, on every deploy, in the audit register the CPA already reads and the closed month will use:

**Opinion** (first, above everything, no preamble). One of three sentences, chosen by the gate, never by a person: `Unqualified — every check passed on 2026-08-07.` / `Qualified — every check passed except for the 4 exceptions below.` / `Withheld — coverage fell below the threshold to state an opinion.` Set in Fraunces at the page's largest size, on a hairline-ruled band. Today it reads *Qualified*, and that is the point.

**Basis.** Coverage, in the risk-coverage grammar the frontier uses for abstention, because it is measured and therefore sayable: `1,327 routable pages · 1,333 HTML files scanned · 61 checks run · 4,912 contrast pairs computed`. Not a score. Not a badge. No percentage anywhere on the page.

**Exceptions.** One row per typed defect, tabular, `lining-nums tabular-nums`, ratio right-aligned and decimal-aligned. Columns: mark · what · measured · required · where · since. Marks are a closed legend printed at the foot of the table, Eurostat-style, and they are the *same glyph set the product uses* — `e` estimated, `⊘` withheld, `∑` footed, `✓` traced. A contrast exception reads: `⊘  --stone-2 as text  2.45:1  4.50:1  assets/site-core.css:1510 (+57)  2026-07-28`. Every row carries a repair path in one line, per the AVA finding — a bare defect list reads as incompetence, a defect with a named fix reads as a work queue.

**Schedules.** The command. `node scripts/check-floor.mjs` — printed as text a stranger copies, next to `bash scripts/no-llm-ci.sh` in the product repo. Both claims carry their falsifier inside the claim rather than beside it. This is where `no-llm-ci.sh` finally appears on a page.

THE REGISTER, not a redesign. `/floor/` is append-only on its exception history: an exception that closes moves to a `Cleared` section with its close date. That converts each individual failure from an admission into an instance of a routine, which is the whole mechanism that defuses the corrections-trust penalty. A register that only grows is an indictment; a register with a visible burn-down is a company working.

THE MECHANISM BEHIND IT: `check-floor.mjs`, three derived sub-gates, each with `--self-test` ported from `check-motion-safe.mjs:142`.

1. **Contrast by construction.** Parse every `--token: #hex` out of every theme block in every stylesheet, plus every `<style>` block across all 1,333 HTML files, and compute the *cross product the CSS actually composes* — not a curated pair list. This is the fix for the specific failure mode that produced today's state: `stone` was tested against three surfaces and never against `cream-2`. Thresholds 4.5 / 3.0 / 3.0 (normal text / large text / non-text and focus indicators).

2. **Weight budget, per route class, tied to a real reader** — an owner in a walk-in on one bar of LTE, not a lab. Reading route: ≤55KB gzip CSS, ≤120KB HTML, ≤35KB JS. Index route: ≤55/180/35. Product route: ≤90KB JS. Any asset over 250KB fails outright. Today every reading route is at ~116KB CSS and 94 of them carry 146KB of `listen.js` — this gate is red on day one and ships UNWIRED with a dated entry and a burn-down, exactly as `check-gate-coverage.mjs:31-34` requires.

3. **Focus and motion discipline**, ported from the product's two existing gates, which already work. Every `outline:none` must have a `:focus-visible` in the same selector family (73 vs 145 today, unproven). Every `transform`/`opacity` transition must sit under or beside a reduced-motion branch.

THE PIGMENT WORK, and it is deletion not re-skinning.

`--stone-2` is **deleted**, not corrected. It exists to be "fainter than stone" and there is no legible role for body text at 2.45:1. Its 58 uses split three ways: placeholders and metadata → `--stone` (4.51:1 on cream, passes); pure separators (`.breadcrumb-sep`, `·` bullets) → `aria-hidden="true"` plus a border colour, which carries no text-contrast obligation; anything that survives neither test was never meant to be read. `--gold` and `--line-input` get one OKLCH-derived step darker each.

The neutral ramp is then re-derived in `oklch()` with hex fallbacks — not because OKLCH looks different (it will look nearly identical) but because an even-L ramp makes contrast *arithmetic instead of eyeballing*, which is the only kind of colour decision that survives 13–26 hours a month. There is exactly one token file and the gate hash-locks it, which already works via `check-tokens-sync.mjs`.

THE INVERSION — the one real aesthetic risk, and it is a typographic one.

Every stylesheet in this repo treats explanatory text as the faintest thing on the page: 58 uses of the lowest-contrast token for labels, notes, sources, and captions. **In a product whose thesis is withholding, the reason a number is missing is the most important text on the screen.** So invert the hierarchy: a withheld or estimated cell keeps its tabular width, its baseline, its border and its full-strength ink; the *number's absence* is what recedes, not its explanation. The held cell fills with `⊘ no ending count — enter it →` at 4.5:1 minimum and the same optical size as the figures around it. Nothing pulses, nothing greys out, nothing collapses. A hole reads as failure; a held position at full contrast reads as a decision, and it is the same component in the product's `ConfidenceChip` and on `/floor/`'s exception table and eventually on the closed month. One CSS component, three surfaces, one legend.

That inversion is the risk: it makes the ugliest information the most legible thing on the page, which is the opposite of every SaaS instinct. It is also the only design position consistent with what this company sells.

TYPE AND NUMBER. Add `lining-nums` alongside `tabular-nums` everywhere — 0 occurrences today in either repo, one find-and-replace, gated. Right-align every numeric column. Keep Fraunces as the display face (it is the one thing on the site that escapes the Inter monoculture the research names as the AI tell) and retire the four static Fraunces cuts plus Alfa Slab One, Bebas Neue, Cormorant Garamond, Cormorant SC and Noto Serif — 9 of 16 faces, none load-bearing.

SPANISH, under a 40-customer strategy. The storefront's 651 ES pages served a discovery objective ADR-032 already released; freeze them at the funnel per that ADR and spend nothing more. The product's ES is the one that matters — a Spanish-first cook counting a walk-in at 11pm is the actual reader, and 54 `useT()` call sites already exist. So: one gate, `check-copy-translated.mjs`, that fails when an ES leaf value is byte-identical to its EN value. Today's shape-parity test passes on copied English; this one cannot. That is the whole bilingual investment, and it is measured in hours not weeks.

## Risk
The honest failure mode is that `/floor/` reads as incompetence rather than rigor. Nyhan's finding is that corrections increase belief accuracy while *decreasing* trust in the corrector, and a CPA landing on a page headed "Qualified" with a live table of the company's own defects has been handed a reason to say no. Three things have to be true or the surface backfires: the exception list must visibly shrink (a register that only grows is an indictment), every row must carry a repair path (a bare defect reads as a limitation; a defect with a named fix reads as a queue), and the opinion must be able to reach "Unqualified" within a quarter or the honesty reads as resignation. If the burn-down stalls at 13–26 hours a month, the correct move is to narrow the gate's scope until the opinion is truthfully unqualified over a smaller declared surface — a qualified opinion over everything is worse than an unqualified one over a stated subset, which is exactly the coverage-over-accuracy logic the product itself runs on.

Second risk, more mundane and more likely: the derived contrast gate will find far more than 58 violations on first run — the cross product across 1,333 files and every theme block is thousands of pairs, and I have measured the token layer but not the full composed cascade. It cannot be wired until the backlog burns down, so it ships UNWIRED with a dated entry, and an unwired gate is how `check-css-drift.mjs` (505 issues since 2026-07-28) already became permanent furniture. The discipline that prevents a repeat is that `/floor/` publishes the unwired gate's output whether or not it is wired — the page is the pressure the registry never applied.

Third: I verified everything above by reading files and computing ratios, in a container with no browser and no screen reader. Nothing here is a device walk. The 4.17:1 and 2.45:1 figures are WCAG 2.x arithmetic on declared token values, which is sound; whether a given rule actually composes against that background in a real render is a claim only a browser can settle, and `/floor/` must say so in its own Basis section rather than imply a walk that never happened.
