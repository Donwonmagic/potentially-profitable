# The open institution — credibility as the entire moat

## Thesis
Muntin should stop trying to be a software company with an honesty policy and become a price institution whose product is its own record. The material already exists and is measured: `cost-index/calibration.json` publishes 79,086 scored conformal steps at 0.767 coverage against a 0.80 nominal target with zero under-covering items, and a direction-call hit rate of 0.506 against a 0.502 baseline over 34,029 calls — a publisher stating in machine-readable form that its own directional signal is at chance. `data/cost-index-health.json` reports `highEligible: 0` and `overstated: 0` across all 100 ingredients, and gives every single one a written `toHigh` string naming the one thing that would have to be true for it to claim more. `cost-index/lockfloat.json` refuses a posture on 37 of 100 ingredients. `data/cost-index.json#coverage.gaps` carries 42 ingredients that are honestly absent, each with a written structural reason. `cost-index/revisions.json` holds 10,294 corrections to previously-published prices, append-only. No competitor selling a "proprietary price database" can publish any of this, because publishing it destroys their pitch — which is precisely what makes it a moat and not a virtue. The refusals are the story; the readings are the product of it. This bet says: build the reading room, not the funnel. Make the institution's record — corrections, calibration, refusals, freshness, decisions — the primary surface of muntin.digital, and let the 1,327 pages of explanatory prose collapse into a supporting layer. Then answer the funding question the way every durable independent authority has: never take money from anyone the index reports on, and take it instead from the people who rely on it — readers who pay for membership, third parties who pay for a certified as-of statement about what the index said on a given day, and members who use Ledger as an instrument of the institution rather than as a $19 SKU. This does not violate the independence contract; it is the only shape the contract permits, and nobody has ever written it down.

## The Company
POSITIONING. "The Muntin Cost Index — the price record for restaurant food inputs, published in the open, with its own error rate." Not "cost intelligence." Not a category campaign. The category noun is a losing fight: `data/content-intent.json` classifies 200 of 1,327 pages as `topicCluster: retired-web-SEO`, and the DefinedTermSet a retrieval pipeline ingests still argues, by majority, that this is a restaurant-website-SEO company. You cannot win a definition by asserting it. You can win a reading by being the only party that publishes one with its band, its revision count and its miss rate attached.

CATEGORY. Reference-rate publisher, not SaaS. The comparables are SOFR, Case-Shiller and Consumer Reports, not MarginEdge. The constitutional sentence already on `cost-index/methodology/index.html` — "no market participant submits a price to us… no vendor feed, no sponsorship, no pay-to-be-listed" — is the exact architectural difference between SOFR and LIBOR, and it is currently buried in body copy on a page reachable from almost nothing.

THE BUYER — three, in strict order of who is allowed to pay:
1. READERS. Operators and restaurant-specialist bookkeeping firms who read the index and want the institution's attention.
2. RELYERS. Anyone who needs to name a neutral, dated, citable reading inside a document they are signing — a group negotiating a 12-month produce contract with an escalator, a franchisor setting a food-cost target, a landlord with percentage rent, a lender or insurer, a researcher, a journalist, a lawyer in a dispute. They pay for certainty about what was published on a date, not for the number.
3. MEMBERS' OWN DATA. Ledger, given to members, never sold as a SKU.
NOBODY ELSE MAY EVER PAY. Written into an ADR with both lists named: FORECLOSED — advertising, sponsorship, paid placement, affiliate or referral commission, lead-sale to distributors, award-licensing to the reviewed, any paid data tier that shows a reading earlier or in bulk. AVAILABLE — reader membership, certification of the record, service and interpretation over data that stays CC0. ADR-020 made the free data line "the company for the next year" and used the words market, competitor, revenue and customer exactly zero times. This ADR is the missing half.

BUSINESS MODEL AND PRICING. Three lines, all reader-side.

(a) MEMBERSHIP — $390/year, operator, one location. This sits inside the band the brief establishes as demonstrated: independents pay $350–500/location for a measured food-cost number. It is 17x the currently-posted $228/year, and the arithmetic is the same arithmetic: 1pp of food cost is roughly a third of annual profit at a 3% net margin, so $390 needs about 12 basis points of movement to pay for itself. The product already computes that dollar figure — `cost-watch-impact.ts` carries a materiality floor of $25/week, 3.3x the annual membership — and `/ledger` prints no dollar claim at all. Membership buys: the hand-written monthly read, a basket re-weighted to the member's own menu computed from the free index, Ledger, and standing to put a question into the next edition. It does NOT buy earlier data, exclusive data, bulk data or a private reading — asymmetry is exclusivity and exclusivity is the thing CC0 forbids in spirit.

(b) FIRM MEMBERSHIP — $290/month, up to 25 client restaurants, the firm as the billing subject. This is the only price arithmetic that closes the channel the brief identifies as the only affordable one, and it sidesteps the schema blocker entirely: `stripe_subscriptions.org_id` makes the restaurant the billing subject and `accountant` is a guest permission, so a firm cannot be a SaaS customer — but a firm can absolutely be a MEMBER of an institution, because a membership is a subscription to a publication, not a per-tenant entitlement. No `firms` entity, no client-org edge, no fourth `priceIdForTier` branch required on day one. It also fixes the bug in place: `billing.ts:174-175` multiplies base price by seat count, which would bill a 5-seat Accountant $750 against a correct $270. A flat firm membership has no seat multiplier to get wrong.

(c) ATTESTATION — $250 per certified as-of statement; $2,400/year for unlimited within one organization. This is the genuinely new lane and it is the one that makes "the open institution" a business rather than a posture. CC0 means anyone may reference a reading for free; CC0 does not give anyone the ability to prove what the reading was. Muntin already froze it: `data/cost-index-editions.json` holds one append-only frozen snapshot per `asOf`, `cost-index/week-<asOf>.json`/`.csv` publishes a CC0 per-week dataset, and 10,294 revisions in `cost-index/revisions.json` are exactly why "what did it say that day" is a question with an answer nobody else can give. An attestation is a signed, permanently-addressed, PDF/A statement: the reading, the band, the confidence tier, the number of revisions since publication, the content hash of the frozen edition, and the methodology version in force. It is notarization over free data. It is money from the party relying on the index, never from a party the index reports on. It is the single cleanest fit between what the company has already built and something someone can be billed for.

HOW MONEY IS ACTUALLY MADE. Membership is the base and it is slow. Attestation is the wedge because it needs no launch, no billing tier, no GA date and no working food-cost number — the first one can be produced by hand from artifacts that already exist on disk. 40 operator memberships at $390 plus 6 firm memberships at $3,480 plus 30 attestations at $250 is $44,880/year. That is not a venture outcome and I will not pretend it is; it is the proof-of-mechanism year. The venture case, if it exists, is that a referenced index compounds: every contract that names the Muntin Cost Index makes the next one cheaper to sign, and a reference rate that survives ten years of unbroken publication is an acquirable, unbuildable asset — the Wirecutter lesson stripped of the affiliate model that Muntin's own methodology page forbids.

WHAT THIS RESOLVES. Is the index the product or the moat? Product. Is $19 wrong by an order of magnitude? Yes, and the fix is not a price increase on the same SKU — it is a different object. Who is the buyer? The reader, and the firm is a reader too. And the no-submitters clause stays absolute: no operator-contributed price lane, ever, because the moment an operator submits a price the strongest sentence on the site needs a caveat, and a caveated architectural claim is a weaker instrument than an absolute one.

## The Storefront
muntin.digital becomes THE RECORD — a public reading room whose front matter is what the institution does not know.

THE NEW SPINE (six generated surfaces, all in `build.command`, none hand-maintained, none capable of lapsing):
- `/cost-index/corrections/` — append-only, five fields per entry: what was published, what is true, when it broke, why, and WHICH GATE NOW PREVENTS RECURRENCE. The fifth field is the whole asset. Note the gap it fills: `cost-index/revisions.json` (10,294 entries) logs SOURCE revisions — someone else's number moving. Nothing on disk logs Muntin's own published errors, and that is the ledger that buys trust.
- `/cost-index/calibration/` — rendered from `cost-index/calibration.json`. Headline: "We aim for 80% band coverage. We measured 76.7% across 79,086 steps. The worst-covered item ran 73.1%. Zero items under-covered their own stated band." Second panel: "Our direction calls are right 50.6% of the time against a 50.2% baseline over 34,029 calls. That is why we stopped making them." A competitor saying "AI-powered, 99% accurate" structurally cannot publish this page.
- `/cost-index/refusals/` — the flagship. 42 honestly-absent ingredients from `data/cost-index.json#coverage.gaps`, each with its written reason already on disk ("LMR feed is volume-only; cut prices are PDF-only"; "a daily quote needs a paid reporter network — Urner Barry tier"); 18 ingredients flagged `insufficient`; 37 of 100 where `lockfloat.json` withholds a posture; and — the piece nobody has noticed — all 100 `toHigh` strings from `data/cost-index-health.json`, each naming the single binding blocker to a higher confidence tier. That is 100 pre-computed installments of a serial editorial line, framed as reporting on where public U.S. food-price data structurally does not exist. It must never read as "look how honest we are." It shows the 47 skillets; it does not write about them.
- `/cost-index/freshness/` — per-series vintage from `data/cost-index-health.json`, with the frozen ones named. Today that page would say: 18 of 100 series carry no observation newer than 2026-06-01, 67 days stale as of 2026-08-07, and two of them (shrimp, vegetable-oil) are basket contributors, which is why `basket.asOf` reads 2026-06-01 while 82 other series read 2026-08-04. Publishing that is not a confession; it is the odometer.
- `/cost-index/attest/` — the product page for the certified as-of statement, with one worked example against a real frozen edition from `data/cost-index-editions.json`.
- `/decisions/` — the ADR series rendered from markdown with status shown, EXCLUDING every PROPOSAL-status ADR and the entire `docs/` tree. A company that publishes its decision to postpone its own revenue by a year reads as serious in a way no marketing page can.

THE 1,327 PAGES. `data/content-intent.json` does the triage and I am adopting its verdicts wholesale rather than re-litigating them: keep 1,135 / elevate 82 / merge 60 / freeze 36 / retire 14. The 82 `elevate` pages are almost entirely cost-index (18 EN + 36 ES) plus blog (3+3) and library (11+8) — those become the reading room. The 306 cost-index pages and 20 `/open/` explore surfaces are the appreciating assets: an answer engine must re-fetch them because the number moves. The ~460 pages of explanatory prose are depreciating in the category AI eats, and the answer is not "prune to 800" — it is CHANGE WHAT A PAGE IS. Each of the 143 EN ingredient pages gains, generated: a "revised N times" chip linking into the revisions ledger, its `toHigh` blocker printed in plain language, its lock/cushion/float/withhold posture, its vintage, and the embed snippet. `scripts/build-cost-index-embed.mjs:45` reads `EMBED_SLUGS = ['ribeye']` — one of 82. Widening that array is a one-line change that turns the index into an object in other people's glove boxes, which is worth more than any on-site edit because it reaches people who never search.

THE RETURN ADDRESS, FIRST IN WALL-CLOCK ORDER. 775 pages carry a subscribe form and `src/worker.js:8793` mails only `source === 'cost-index'`, of which there are 2. 4 pages of 1,327 carry a waitlist form. `data/link-graph.json` counts 501 dead product-CTA anchors across 402 pages pointing at `ledger.muntin.digital`. And 1,053 of 1,327 pages emit no analytics at all, so nobody knows what traffic exists. Every amplification move in this plan pours into a funnel with four mouths, a mail filter that skips 775 of 777 signups, and no instrumentation. Fix the filter, resolve or retire the hostname, put ONE membership seam on the 306 cost-index pages, and put analytics on the record layer — before shipping a single new page.

WHAT THE STOREFRONT'S JOB BECOMES. Not lead-gen. The reading room's job is to make a specific sentence true: "if you are going to name a food-price reference in a document, this is the one whose error rate you can read." Traffic follows from being the only such page on the open web; confidence follows from the corrections ledger existing before anyone needed it to.

## The Product
Muntin Ledger stops being a $19 SaaS product and becomes THE MEMBER'S INSTRUMENT — the thing that answers, against your own invoices, the question the free index makes you ask. It is never sold separately, never priced per seat, never given a GA countdown. That single reframing dissolves three of the company's hardest blockers at once: the price arithmetic that forecloses every acquisition channel, the `stripe_subscriptions.org_id` schema that makes a bookkeeping firm structurally unable to be a customer, and the `billing.ts:174-175` seat multiplication that would bill a 5-seat Accountant $750 against a correct $270. A membership has no seats to multiply.

WHAT GETS BUILT — the provenance receipt, which is the index's grammar applied to one restaurant. Every number Ledger prints ships with what it measured, what it estimated, and WHAT IT EXCLUDED AND WHY. Concretely: N invoices in the window, M line items parsed, K excluded with a reason code, coverage percentage, and the valuation source per line (the ladder already exists: per-line override → WAC → prior-count carry → Cost-Index market prior → null). This is not a feature; it is the only form in which this company is permitted to print a food-cost number at all.

WHAT GETS CUT. The $19 SKU and the entire posted-but-not-billable pricing surface. The `hasCockpit: true` entitlement with no `/cockpit` route. The 2026-11-13 paid-GA date and the "three months free" term that lands the first charge in mid-February, the second-worst cash month of the restaurant year — under a membership model the term is an annual one that starts when the member joins. The Spanish 24-hour reply and 24-hour phone-callback SLA at `copy.es.ts:2521,2532,2538`, published by a one-person company with no artifact establishing that the founder speaks conversational Spanish; an institution does not publish an uncosted promise.

HOW CORRECTNESS IS GUARANTEED — and this is where the bet is most exposed, so it gets the most machinery. The headline number is biased low three separate ways, and under an institution brand a knowingly-biased number is unshippable. The honest interim is the institution's own grammar: WITHHOLD THE PERCENTAGE, PUBLISH THE LEDGER AND THE EXCLUSION LIST. Specifically:

1. `line_item_observations` was engineered for price intelligence and reused unchanged as the Purchases leg. It drops credit memos, drops $0 promo lines, drops unparseable pack tokens, and stores `unit_price × quantity` rather than the printed `line_total`. Until it is re-derived from the printed totals, Ledger does not print a food-cost percentage — it prints the purchase ledger, the excluded-line count, and the reason codes. That is a shippable, correct, differentiated product today.
2. The default count mode writes un-entered items at expected-on-hand, making their usage arithmetically zero. Flip the default: un-entered is UNCOUNTED, and an uncounted item is excluded from the identity with its dollar exposure named. The identity `Beginning + Purchases − Ending = Usage` was designed as an honesty checksum; a default that silently satisfies it is the one thing it must not do.
3. `inventory-purchases-store.ts:19-20` documents that the Purchases roll-up has no location predicate while counts are per-location. In Don's own two-location shape each location's usage includes the other's invoices. This is a hard gate: the food-cost surface is disabled for any org with more than one location until the predicate lands.
4. `inventory-reconcile.ts:249` is an empty `if (estimated) {}` block whose comment claims behavior the code does not perform. Delete the comment or write the code; a comment that lies is the same class of defect as a page that lies.
5. A POS window with 12 of 30 days settled prints against a partial denominator. Withhold below a stated settlement-coverage floor and print the floor.

THE COLD START, WHICH IS SOLVED AND SWITCHED OFF. `EXTRACT_VENDOR_TRUST_ACTIVE` holds a measured 24% → ~62% first-pass straight-through improvement across a 919-document corpus, sitting in shadow mode. Turn it on. Under determinism it is the only lever that exists — `no-llm-ci.sh` caps novel-invoice first-try accuracy and that ceiling is not negotiable, because "no language model reads your numbers" is falsifiable by a stranger in under a minute and is currently named on ZERO of 1,327 storefront pages while 93 pages make the claim it proves.

THE SEED CORPUS. Lead with the specialty long tail the corpus actually contains — I. Halper 93.2% (n=73), Chefs' Warehouse 92.1% (n=214), Baldor 89.7%, Pat LaFrieda 87.5%, down to Cozzini Bros 20.8% — not the broadliners the corpus contains none of. Publishing that per-vendor table INCLUDING Cozzini at 20.8% is the honesty thesis made concrete and is the single most persuasive page the product could own. HARD PREREQUISITE, non-negotiable and first: `services/extract/tests/golden/real_fixtures/fresco_lineitems.json:390` carries a real Tacombi address, invoice numbers, Net-45 terms and negotiated unit prices in a public MIT repo while two sibling fixtures are properly redacted. Publicity is what makes someone look. Redact it before any of this ships.

## How It Fails
"The most likely death is not that the institution is disbelieved. It is that nobody ever needs it.

Credibility is a CLOSING asset, not a demand asset — the brief says this plainly and this bet has to survive it.
