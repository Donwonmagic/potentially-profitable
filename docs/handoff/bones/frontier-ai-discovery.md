# Discovery in the Agent Era — how anything gets found in 2026, and what a ~40-customer publisher should do about it

## Thesis
Discovery in 2026 has split into two separate machines, and Muntin has been optimizing the wrong one. The first machine is volume: ten blue links, still real but decaying — Pew's tracking of 68,000 queries found clicks fall from 15% to 8% when an AI summary appears and only 1% of AI Overviews produce a click on a cited source, while Cloudflare's crawl-to-refer ratios sit in the hundreds-to-thousands of fetches per referred visitor. That machine is dead to a company that needs forty customers and has declared traffic a non-objective. The second machine is citation: at answer time a model runs a fan-out of sub-queries against a live index, scores passages rather than domains, and names two to eight sources. Citation is where credibility lives, and it runs on different physics — reported data shows AI Overview overlap with the organic top ten collapsing from 76% to 38% in a year and roughly 47% of citations coming from below position five, meaning a tiny site with the right passage can be named next to Restaurant365. What earns that naming is consistently reported as the same three things: a specific number, a date, and an attributable source in a self-contained passage — which is, by accident, exactly the honesty contract Muntin already enforces at CI. The `llms.txt` investment is the clearest miss: Ahrefs' 137,000-domain server-log study found 97% of published llms.txt files received no requests at all, no major model vendor has committed to reading the format, and the bots that do fetch it are coding agents and SEO audit tools, not answer engines. Meanwhile the decaying dated price series is a genuine structural advantage, because "what is broccoli wholesale right now" cannot be answered from parametric memory — a year, a price constraint, or a comparison reportedly triggers live retrieval close to 100% of the time, so a model that wants that answer must fetch, and if it fetches it must cite. But the advantage is only real if the pages are in the retrieval index and the passage is liftable in one piece, and the payoff is being *named*, not being clicked. The single largest unforced error on the site is that `no-llm-ci.sh` — the one thing on the property a stranger could falsify in thirty seconds — appears on zero of 1,327 pages, while roughly fourteen pages teach restaurants how to do SEO for a services line the company retired.

## Findings
### Publish llms.txt and llms-full.txt as an AI-citation surface
- **Who:** Anthropic, Stripe, Vercel, Cursor, Mintlify, Supabase, LangGraph — and Muntin (45KB /llms.txt, 681KB /llms-full.txt, 376KB /feed-llm.json, all verified on disk)
- **Why:** It does not work as an answer-engine surface. Ahrefs' study of 137,000 domains reports 97% of published llms.txt files received zero requests in May 2026; of ~38,000 valid files only ~1,100 saw any traffic and 96% of that was bots. Reported: no major vendor (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Meta, Mistral) has publicly committed to reading it in production, and Google's Gary Illyes said in July 2025 that Google does not support it and does not plan to. Crucially, AI bots never requested the file on domains where it did not exist — they are not probing for it. The adopters above are all developer-documentation sites, which is the one context where it demonstrably gets read.
- **Confidence:** well-established

### Treat llms.txt as a machine-readable docs surface for CODING agents, not for answer engines
- **Who:** Ahrefs' log analysis; the pattern visible across the adopter list
- **Why:** The bots that actually fetched llms.txt were reportedly led by Claude-Code and GPTBot, alongside SEO audit tools (21% of requests), unidentified bots (14%), Googlebot-class crawlers (13%) and tech-profiling tools like BuiltWith (11%). That is a developer-tooling audience. The format has a real, narrow job — letting an agent working IN a codebase find your API docs — and Muntin has no API and no developer audience, so the 726KB of corpus is serving a population that does not exist for this company.
- **Confidence:** reported

### Measure the crawl-to-refer ratio rather than sessions, and accept that referral volume is structurally gone
- **Who:** Cloudflare Radar; Pew Research; Chartbeat; Digital Content Next
- **Why:** Pew's 68,000-query study reportedly found click rates of 8% with an AI summary vs 15% without (a ~47% relative drop) and only 1% of AI Overviews producing a click on a cited source. Cloudflare Radar figures reported for May 2026 put Anthropic's crawler at ~10,300 HTML fetches per referred visitor, GPTBot at ~904:1, Perplexity at ~193:1. Chartbeat reportedly measured a 33% decline in Google search referrals across 2,500+ news sites in 2025. The direction is corroborated from several independent directions and is not seriously disputed; the exact ratios are vendor-reported and I could not fetch Cloudflare's blog to verify them.
- **Confidence:** well-established

### Optimize the PASSAGE, not the domain — citation is decided at retrieval time by chunk-level scoring
- **Who:** Reported across AI-Overview citation trackers and the RAG-pipeline literature
- **Why:** Reported data shows AI Overview citations overlapping the organic top ten falling from 76% to 38% within a year, with ~47% of citations coming from pages ranking below position five and domain-authority correlation dropping to r≈0.18. The mechanism is that the engine chunks a page and scores each chunk against a fanned-out sub-query, so what wins is a self-contained span that answers one question with a number, a date, and an attributable source. This is the single most important structural finding for a small publisher: authority is no longer the gate, extractability is.
- **Confidence:** reported

### Add statistics, direct quotes and inline source citations to the passages you want lifted
- **Who:** Aggarwal et al., 'GEO: Generative Engine Optimization', Princeton/Georgia Tech, KDD 2024
- **Why:** This is the only controlled experiment in the field with a real methodology. It reported visibility lifts of roughly +28% for adding quotations, +26% for adding statistics and +25% for citing sources, with keyword stuffing and fluff showing no benefit. Caveat that matters: it was run on GEO-Bench against research-grade generative engines in 2024, not against production ChatGPT/AI Mode in 2026, and essentially every GEO vendor now cites it as proof of practices it never tested. Treat it as strong evidence about what the class of system rewards, not as a validated 2026 playbook.
- **Confidence:** well-established

### Add JSON-LD schema to lift AI citations
- **Who:** Nearly every AEO/GEO vendor; Muntin already ships it heavily (195 pages carry Dataset JSON-LD, plus 435 FAQPage, 236 HowTo, 575 Article, 1,259 DefinedTerm blocks — counted on disk)
- **Why:** The direct-lift claim does not survive testing. A tracked cohort of 1,885 pages adding JSON-LD reportedly showed +2.4% AI Mode and +2.2% ChatGPT citation change — statistically indistinguishable from zero. Schema on cited pages is correlation with other quality signals, not cause. It still earns its keep for entity resolution, for Google Dataset Search, and as a stable machine contract for non-LLM consumers — but it is not a citation lever, and further schema investment has no evidenced return.
- **Confidence:** reported

### Publish primary research as a NAMED, MEASURABLE BENCHMARK rather than as a narrative report
- **Who:** Kevin Indig / Growth Memo, analyzing Gauge citation data (July 2026)
- **Why:** The reported sample found primary research was only 2.7% of AI-cited pages but earned ~3.3x the citation density (11.3 citations/page vs 3.4) — and that nearly all the advantage concentrated in one format: a benchmark that directly answers a 'which is best' comparison. Data buried inside a story does not get picked up. This is the most actionable finding for Muntin: the Cost Index is currently published as a market read (a narrative), when the citable object is a dated, named comparison table.
- **Confidence:** reported

### Rely on freshness and numeric specificity to force live retrieval instead of parametric recall
- **Who:** DataForSEO's fan-out research; search-augmented-LLM literature
- **Why:** Reported: prompts containing a year, a price constraint, or an X-vs-Y comparison triggered live search ~100% of the time across tested models, and DATE and NUMBER are reported as the entity types most predictive of ChatGPT citation. Fan-out breadth varies sharply by model tier (reportedly ~1 query for a fast default vs ~8.5 for a thinking model). Mechanistically this is sound and does not depend on trusting a vendor: a decaying dated series is definitionally unanswerable from training data, so the model must fetch, and a fetched source is a cited source.
- **Confidence:** well-established

### Earn third-party mentions on the small set of domains that actually feed the answer pipeline
- **Who:** 5W's AI Platform Citation Source Index 2026; multiple citation-concentration studies
- **Why:** Reported synthesis across ~680M citations finds roughly 15 domains absorbing ~68% of AI answers, with Reddit the #1 source across every major engine (~40% frequency), plus Wikipedia, YouTube, LinkedIn and arXiv. Roughly 90% of third-party brand mentions reportedly originate in listicles, comparison pages and review roundups. The uncomfortable implication: the answer pipeline runs mostly through sources you do not own, so 1,327 owned pages is the wrong shape of investment relative to being named once in the right comparison.
- **Confidence:** reported

### Audit crawler policy as a commercial decision, because the defaults changed underneath you
- **Who:** Cloudflare (Content Signals Policy, Pay Per Crawl); the RSL/Really Simple Licensing group (Reddit, Fastly, news publishers)
- **Why:** Cloudflare now extends robots.txt with search / ai-input / ai-train signals and reportedly defaults ai-train to no, began blocking AI crawlers by default in July 2025, and per reporting will from 15 September 2026 block 'mixed-use' AI crawlers from ad-carrying pages absent payment. For a publisher whose goal is to be BLOCKED this is a win; for one whose goal is to be CITED it is a live risk that an infrastructure default silently removes you from the retrieval index. Muntin's robots.txt (verified on disk) carries explicit per-agent Allow stanzas for GPTBot, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai, Claude-Web, PerplexityBot and Google-Extended — the posture is correct and deliberate, but it is only half the stack; the Cloudflare-side toggles are the other half and are not visible in the repo.
- **Confidence:** well-established

### Value AI referral traffic on conversion, not volume
- **Who:** Adobe Digital Insights Q1 2026; Semrush/Seer/Ahrefs measurements
- **Why:** Reported: AI-assistant referrals converted 42% better than non-AI traffic in March 2026, a reversal from converting 38% worse in March 2025 — but AI referrals remain roughly 1% of total traffic for most sites. For a company needing ~40 customers ever, a channel that is tiny and high-intent is precisely the right shape, and a channel that is large and low-intent is worthless. This inverts the normal publisher conclusion.
- **Confidence:** reported

### Ship an MCP server or ChatGPT app as a distribution channel
- **Who:** OpenAI's Apps SDK / plugin directory (became a shared ChatGPT+Codex plugin directory 9 July 2026)
- **Why:** Reported as real but curated and gated: ChatGPT accepts only remote HTTPS MCP endpoints (never stdio), the directory is editorially curated, and uncurated servers require the user to paste a URL manually. It is a channel for a hosted API, not an organic discovery surface. For Muntin this is a plausible future home for the Cost Index JSON endpoints, but it is not discovery and it is not free — it is infrastructure with an ongoing maintenance cost against a 13–26 h/month budget.
- **Confidence:** reported

### Make the falsifiable proof visible on the page where the claim is made
- **Who:** Nobody in the AEO literature says this; it follows from what the citation research reports
- **Why:** Verified on disk: `no-llm-ci` appears in zero of 1,327 HTML files, while the 'no language model reads your numbers' claim is made on ~93 pages. Every reported citation-selection factor — attributable, verifiable, named, checkable — describes a claim paired with its proof. An unfalsifiable promise is exactly the class of statement a risk-minimizing retrieval system declines to lift. The single highest-leverage discovery change available is naming the gate, in one sentence with the command, next to the claim.
- **Confidence:** inference

## For Muntin
- THE llms.txt VERDICT: cargo cult, not harmful — but it is a maintenance liability, not an asset. 726KB across /llms.txt + /llms-full.txt + /feed-llm.json, built by scripts/build-llms-txt.mjs and scripts/build-llms-full.mjs, serving a bot population the Ahrefs data says does not arrive. It is not actively harmful (no evidence of a penalty, and it costs nothing at the edge), so DELETING it is not the call — FREEZING it is, per the site's own disposition doctrine. RETIRE: stop rebuilding llms-full.txt on every deploy and stop counting it as a discovery investment. Keep the small /llms.txt as a cheap, honest map. The 681KB corpus is a duplicate of content the crawlers already fetch as HTML.
- THE RE-FETCH ADVANTAGE IS REAL, AND IT IS THE COMPANY'S BEST DISCOVERY ASSET — but it is an asset in CITATION, not in traffic. A dated, decaying, sourced price series is structurally unanswerable from parametric memory; reported fan-out research says a query carrying a year or a price constraint triggers live search essentially always. 195 Cost Index pages already ship Dataset JSON-LD with temporalCoverage, dateModified, variableMeasured, license CC0 and DataDownload distributions pointing at series.json/.csv (verified in cost-index/broccoli/index.html and siblings). That is close to a best-in-class citable-dataset implementation and should be PROTECTED, not expanded.
- THE ONE CHANGE WITH THE BEST EVIDENCE BEHIND IT: reformat the Cost Index read as a NAMED BENCHMARK, not a narrative dispatch. The Growth Memo/Gauge data says primary research wins citations almost exclusively when packaged as a measurable 'which is best' comparison. Muntin has the raw material — 81 ingredients, a dated range, a position-in-range, a spread — and publishes it as prose ('the basket reads -5.0% vs baseline'). A dated table titled something a person would type, with the number, the unit, the date and the source in one liftable span, is the format the evidence rewards. This costs editorial framing, not new data.
- WHAT DOES NOT TRANSFER: everything volume-shaped. AI referral is ~1% of traffic; a company needing ~40 customers should not spend one founder-hour on click share. ADR-032 already released traffic as an objective and the research fully vindicates that call — do not let 'discovery' smuggle traffic back in under a new acronym.
- WHAT DOES NOT TRANSFER: more schema. The 1,885-page test found JSON-LD adding ~2% (indistinguishable from zero) to citations on already-cited pages. Muntin ships 435 FAQPage, 236 HowTo, 423 SpeakableSpecification and 1,259 DefinedTerm blocks. That is done. Any further inject-*-schema.mjs work has no evidenced return — RETIRE the impulse to add more, and treat the existing injectors as frozen infrastructure.
- THE UNFORCED ERROR: no-llm-ci.sh is named on 0 of 1,327 pages while ~93 pages make the claim it proves (verified by grep). Every reported citation-selection factor rewards a claim welded to a checkable proof. One sentence — the gate's name, what it asserts, and the command a stranger runs — placed next to the privacy claim, is the cheapest credibility move available and is the only place where the honesty contract and the citation research point at the identical action.
- THE OFF-THESIS CONTENT IS A DISCOVERY LIABILITY, NOT JUST A POSITIONING ONE. Verified on disk: 8 blog posts and 6 library articles teach restaurants SEO/AI-search tactics (blog/how-to-appear-in-ai-search-restaurant-2026/, blog/may-2026-wave-publishing-for-citation/, library/restaurant-schema-markup-guide/, library/how-to-get-cited-in-google-ai-overviews-restaurant/, and others). These are the retired web-design line wearing an AEO costume. Worse, they are the pages most likely to be retrieved when a model is asked about AI search — so the company's entity gets resolved as 'restaurant SEO advice' rather than 'restaurant cost intelligence.' RETIRE: freeze all 14 to noindex,follow under the existing check-positioning-drift discipline. Entity coherence is a reported citation factor, and this content actively degrades it.
- CRAWLER POLICY IS HALF-DONE AND THE MISSING HALF IS INVISIBLE TO THE REPO. robots.txt correctly Allows GPTBot/ClaudeBot/anthropic-ai/Claude-Web/PerplexityBot/Google-Extended (verified). But Cloudflare's own defaults now block AI crawlers and its Content Signals default ai-train to no — a dashboard toggle can silently remove Muntin from the retrieval index without any repo change. ADD ONE QUEUE ITEM: a check that fetches the live robots.txt and Content-Signals response from the deployed edge and diffs it against the committed file, so an infrastructure default cannot quietly undo the stated posture. Anything else is asserting a policy nobody has verified is in force.
- DISCOVERY AT THIS SCALE IS FOR CREDIBILITY, NOT VOLUME — and that changes the metric. The right measure is not sessions and not even referrals; it is: does asking a model 'what does broccoli cost wholesale right now' or 'is there a citable restaurant food-cost index' produce the name Muntin? That is a small, cheap, repeatable eval (a fixed prompt set, run monthly, logged) and it is the only discovery instrument worth a founder-hour. It also fits the audit-opinion positioning exactly: the buyer is a bookkeeper or CPA checking whether you are real, and being named by a model is the modern version of being in the reference shelf.
- WHAT TO RETIRE, EXPLICITLY, TO PAY FOR THE ABOVE: (1) llms-full.txt rebuilds on every deploy; (2) any further schema injector work; (3) the 14 SEO/AEO-advice pages, frozen; (4) the ambition to have a discovery strategy at all beyond the two moves that survive the evidence — a citable benchmark format for the Cost Index, and the falsifiable-proof line. Everything else in the AEO/GEO literature is unfalsifiable vendor advice, and a company whose brand is refusing to publish numbers it cannot stand behind should not be buying it.
