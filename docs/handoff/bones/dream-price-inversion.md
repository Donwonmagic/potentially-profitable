# Price inversion — sell a signed monthly close at ~$600/location, serve ~40 locations extremely well, and make correctness the product rather than a deferred debt.

## Thesis
Muntin has been priced as a scanner and built as an auditor, and the mismatch is the reason nothing has ever converted. At $19/location a customer is worth $450–600 over two years, which the GTM panel correctly shows forecloses every human-touch channel and leaves exactly one — the bookkeeping firm — which the data model then structurally forbids (`stripe_subscriptions.org_id` makes the restaurant the billing subject; `accountant` is a guest permission). Invert the price and that entire trap dissolves: at $600/location/month the founder can afford to talk to the check-signer himself, so the channel problem is solved by not needing a channel, and the firm-billing rebuild — plus the `/cockpit` route sold on the Accountant tier and never built — never has to happen. The price also matches what the market already pays: MarginEdge is $350/location flat and Restaurant365 is reported at $435–635, and both prices exist because a human keys the invoice in 24–48 hours. Muntin reconciles the hardest invoice mix in the category — 919 real documents, zero broadliners, i_halper 93.2%, chefs_warehouse 92.1%, baldor 89.7% — deterministically, in seconds, with no language model anywhere in the read path, a claim `no-llm-ci.sh` lets a stranger falsify in thirty seconds. What Muntin sells is not software and not data: it is **a closed month** — a signed statement of food cost where the four legs of `Beginning + Purchases − Ending = Usage` foot on screen, every estimate is labeled, and any number that cannot be stood behind is withheld rather than printed. That deliverable is unsellable at $19 and only barely sellable at $600, because it costs real exception labor — which is precisely why it is defensible: the exception desk is run by the agent workforce that already writes 80–84% of both repos, under a protocol whose output is a **rule, never a number**, so the determinism guarantee survives intact and every resolution compounds into a vendor template. Correctness stops being a backlog item and becomes the product: the Purchases leg that drops credit memos, $0 promo lines and unparseable packs; the default count mode that zeroes the un-counted tail's usage; the org-wide purchases roll-up feeding per-location screens; the empty `if (estimated) {}` at `inventory-reconcile.ts:249` — at $19 these are technical debt, at $600 they are the thing being bought. And the count, the $100–150/month of floor labor that is the real total cost of ownership, is not something to perform for the customer; it is something to **shrink**, using the already-shipped Tier-2 theoretical usage, pars and market-prior engines to derive most of the sheet and ask the operator to verify only the divergent rows. Half the labor, twice the price, a number that foots — that is the whole company.

## The Company
**Category:** not "AP automation," not "cost intelligence." Muntin sells **the monthly close for independent restaurants** — the way an audit firm sells an opinion, not the way a SaaS sells a dashboard. The unit of value is a dated, signed statement, not a login.

**Positioning line:** "Your month closes on the second Tuesday. Every legs foots or we withhold the number." Three artifacts already point at Tuesday and nothing joins them — the prime-cost sheet's "prior week is closed," the cost-watch cron `0 15 * * 2`, the first-Tuesday dispatch. Joining them makes the promise calendar-shaped rather than clock-shaped, which is the only kind of premium promise one employed person can keep. MarginEdge's public promise is a 24–48h rolling turnaround (3–5 days for the first four weeks); a fixed monthly close date is a promise they structurally cannot make and Muntin can.

**Buyer:** the specialty-vendor operator, defined by vendor mix rather than seat count — 1–3 locations, 8–12 active vendors, zero or one broadliner. This is not a guess: the 919-document corpus contains zero Sysco/US Foods/PFG invoices; its top ten vendors are I. Halper Paper, Chefs' Warehouse, Baldor, Pat LaFrieda, Edward Don, Fresco, Montgomery County DLC, Cintas, Staples, Cozzini Bros. Champion is the bookkeeper; check-signer is the owner. At $7,200/year of ARR per location, three founder-hours to close a customer is trivially affordable — the exact acquisition motion $19 made impossible.

**Price:** one tier. **$600/location/month, billed annually at $6,000 (2 months free), one SKU, quantity = locations, no seats, no invoice quota.** Break-even for a $1M-revenue restaurant is 0.72 points of food cost against a line worth ~$10,000/point/year — a far harder sell than the 7-basis-point arithmetic at $19, and honestly so: the price is paid out of *replaced labor plus the number's existence*, not out of savings alone. The comparables are the evidence: independents demonstrably pay $350–500/location for a measured food-cost number produced with a human in the loop. Muntin at $600 includes the close, includes the exception desk, and removes ~80% of the count labor the incumbents still charge the customer for.

**Billing mechanics — this bet is also the cheapest billing fix available.** Delete the $19 founding rate (`pricing-constants.ts` already flags it `billable: false` — no SKU, no `priceIdForTier` branch, no `STRIPE_PRICE_*`, no `trial_period_days`), delete Solo/Team/Accountant. One price, quantity = locations, which sidesteps the `billing.ts:174-175` seat-multiplication defect entirely instead of fixing it. Annual billing lands the charge in the seasonal peak: the repo's own Census MARTS medians are Jan 0.913 / Feb 0.917 against May 1.062, and the current "three months free from 2026-11-13" plan lands the first charge in mid-February, the second-worst cash month of the restaurant year. Never dun in January.

**Cohort cap:** 10 locations for the pilot, hard-capped at ~40. That ceiling is honest arithmetic, not modesty: at ~20 founder-minutes of close review per location per month, 40 locations is 13.3 hours/month against 13–26 available. **$600 × 40 = $288K ARR is the one-founder ceiling**, and the venture question is entirely whether `desk_minutes_per_close` falls as covered layouts rise — a number this company can measure from customer one and publish.

**What is never sold:** per-invoice pricing (variable cost is ~half a cent; flat-rate is the one thing LLM-COGS competitors structurally cannot copy) and percentage-of-savings (`fair-price-gap.js` states on the record that wholesale data alone cannot prove overpayment, so the product cannot bill on a saving it cannot measure). Both foreclosed in an ADR so no future session reproposes them.

**Adjacency the independence contract permits:** the methodology page forecloses placement and influence only. A paid cost review — Don's own interpretation, over data that stays free and CC0 — is fully sellable and is the natural second SKU once the close is proven.

## The Storefront
muntin.digital stops being a traffic machine and becomes **the public audit file for a paid close** — the thing a bookkeeper or CPA reads for forty minutes before recommending you, and the thing an answer engine cites. At $19 you needed volume, so you needed 1,327 pages. At $600 you need roughly 400 qualified readers, ever.

**The new center — three pages that do not exist today.** `/close/` (what a Muntin close guarantees, what it withholds, what it costs). `/close/specimen/2026-09/` — **one real month, in full, forever**: Tacombi's own September, four legs printed and footing, dollar-coverage stated, every estimate labeled, every exception receipt visible, every withheld number named with its reason, signed by Don. Nobody in this category publishes a complete month with the exceptions shown; it is the negative-results instinct applied to the product, and it doubles as the first time the founder has ever run his own product end to end. And `/close/apply/` — an application, not a waitlist, asking the five qualifying questions (vendor count, broadliner share, POS, who keeps your books, do you count today).

**The index's role is resolved, and it is a third answer.** The Cost Index is neither the product (Bloomberg) nor the marketing (Michelin) — it is the **published basis**, the rate the close is priced off. `cost-index-valuation.ts` already values un-invoiced inventory from a fail-closed, 30-day-stale-guarded snapshot of that index; the 306 cost-index pages (`surfaceClass: cost-index`, 145 of them ES) are the public half of a paid deliverable's audit trail. This preserves CC0 and the no-submitters clause perfectly: you never sell the number, you sell the close that cites it. It also finally gives the **95 orphaned cost-index pages** a functional reason to be linked — each specimen close links to the ingredient page for every line it valued at market prior, which is the first inbound-link mechanism the index has ever had that isn't decorative.

**What happens to 1,327 pages.** Keep and elevate the 354-page core (306 cost-index + 26 trust + 16 tool + `/ledger/` + `/about/`). Add ~6 close pages. Freeze or retire the rest of the prose corpus in stages: 162 dead-weight pages retired outright (142 frozen retired-line, 14 noindex-with-no-inbound, 6 admin harnesses), the 200-page retired-web-SEO cluster frozen, the 234 of 344 glossary pages carrying `retiredLineScore ≥ 1` frozen with their EN↔ES parity obligation released. Target: ~700 maintained pages, of which ~360 are appreciating data surfaces and ~340 are library prose that is kept but frozen — read-only, no dated cites to re-verify, no audio, no parity ratchet.

**Forms.** 4 waitlist forms become 1 application on `/close/apply/`. The 775 subscribe forms shrink to the 164 on cost-index surfaces — and `src/worker.js:8793` gets its one-line fix so `footer` and `cost-index-ingredient` sources are mailable, because 83 pages promise a monthly dispatch and today it reaches two pages' worth of subscribers. The 464 subscribe boxes on library and glossary pages come out; under this bet list growth is not a metric.

**Analytics.** 274 of 1,327 pages emit any today. Do not fix that site-wide — instrument the ~50 pages a qualified buyer actually touches (`/close/*`, `/methods/`, `/receipts/`, `/claims/`, `/security/`, `/system/`, `/ledger/`, the 16 tools, the top cost-index entries) and stop pretending to measure the other 1,277. Under a 40-customer bet, "which pages did the person who applied read" is the only funnel question worth answering.

**The cheapest high-value move on the whole site:** `no-llm-ci.sh` is named on **zero of 1,327 pages** while 93 pages make the claim it proves. Put it on `/methods/`, `/security/` and `/close/` with the command a skeptic can run. A falsifiable claim is worth more to a $600 buyer than every DefinedTerm block on the site — and 685 pages carry DefinedTerm markup while `/for/restaurants/`, the page holding the category term, has 0 inbound internal links.

**Traffic still happens, but as a by-product.** The 306 cost-index pages are the appreciating asset an answer engine must re-fetch because the numbers change; the ~460 pages of explanatory prose are depreciating in exactly the category AI eats. Freezing the prose and defending the data cadence is the same decision as the pricing bet: stop paying for volume, pay for the thing that cannot be summarized away.

## The Product
**Muntin Ledger becomes a close engine with an exception desk.** Three things ship to the cohort and everything else is hidden or cut.

1. **The ledger** (the Decoder — read, file, flag). Already correct, already deterministic, now bundled rather than sold. It is the free-standing proof that runs before anyone counts anything.
2. **The close** (count → identity → signed statement). The paid deliverable. Monthly, dated, and the only thing the price attaches to.
3. **The receipt.** Every estimate, every exception, every withheld number, and every rule change published to the customer as part of the close. This is the artifact that justifies $600 and it is a document, not a screen.

**The exception desk — and the honest edge of the determinism claim.** The engine reconciles 84–87% straight-through on the vendor-trust pool; the ~13–16% that doesn't reconcile is gated to review and never auto-posts. That residual is exactly what MarginEdge pays "actual people with prior restaurant experience" to key at 24–48 hours, and it is what sets the $350 floor. Muntin's desk is agent-run under a protocol with one hard constraint: **the desk produces rules, never numbers.** An agent session may author or amend a vendor template, a pack-parse mapping, a catalog synonym — all of which are inspectable, replayable and published to the customer in the receipt. It may never write a quantity, a price or a total. `no-llm-ci.sh` stays green because the product's read path is unchanged; the desk is disclosed on `/close/` in plain language rather than hidden behind a clever reading of the claim. Every resolution compounds: this is the same mechanism `EXTRACT_VENDOR_TRUST_ACTIVE` already measures at 24% → ~62% first-pass straight-through, currently sitting in shadow mode.

**Correctness, guaranteed and enforced — the seven things that must be true before anyone is charged.**
- **Re-derive the Purchases leg as a new consumer of `line_item_observations`.** Carry `line_total` (today `queue.ts:1129-1132` stores `unit_price × quantity`, which values a catch-weight meat line at $9.85 against a printed $142.60 — and `reconcile.py` already computes that residual per row). Stop filtering out negative lines: a credit memo must reduce Purchases. Stop filtering out $0 lines: free goods are on the shelf. Stop dropping `base_uom IS NULL` items from the VALUE leg. Write the seam down as an ADR so the next reuse re-derives.
- **Kill the default value-count tail fill.** `count-client.tsx:218-227` writes un-entered rows at expected-on-hand, making their usage arithmetically zero and biasing the headline low by whatever the tail actually consumed — and the negative-usage checksum can never fire on a row whose usage is 0. Uncounted becomes withheld and surfaced as its own line.
- **Location predicate or no picker.** `inventory-purchases-store.ts:19-20` documents the absence; counts, pars, variance and food-cost are all keyed on `location_key`. In a two-location org each location's usage absorbs both locations' invoices — which is Don's own Tacombi shape, which is why nobody caught it.
- **Make the legs foot on screen.** Print Beginning, Purchases, Ending and Usage. Today the three totals roll up all items while Usage sums only doubly-valued ones, so `B + P − E ≠ Usage` whenever anything is unvalued, invisible only because Beginning is never displayed.
- **Ship a dollar-coverage metric.** What fraction of the period's invoiced dollars landed in Purchases. Today coverage is item counts, which structurally cannot detect a dollar gap.
- **Withhold, not dock.** The percentage is withheld — not shown at 48px with a footnote — in three named states: the reconciliation flag is set, POS sales coverage is incomplete (a 12-of-30-day settled window currently prints against 40% of its denominator), or valued spend falls below a stated share.
- **Fix the two label defects.** `valuation_method: 'wac'` is a latest-window / FIFO-style valuation, not weighted-average cost, and ADR-009 calls it "a real weighted average" — the first accountant who looks finds this in ten minutes, and it is the one error a company built on "we do not print what we cannot stand behind" can least afford. And `inventory-reconcile.ts:249` is an empty `if (estimated) {}` block whose comment claims a roll-up the code does not perform; per-item `estimated` is computed, returned, never persisted and never displayed, so a period valued entirely at market prior shows "0 estimated."

**The count is the product opportunity, and the move is to shrink it, not perform it.** A monthly count is ~$100–150 of floor labor (arithmetic on BLS QCEW Montgomery County NAICS 7225, $626 average weekly wage Q4 2025) — $1,200–1,800/year against any subscription price, which is why cutting price changes nothing. Nobody can send a person. But three engines already ship that let the sheet be *derived*: Tier-2 theoretical usage from recipes, Tier-3 pars with days-of-cover, and the fail-closed Cost-Index market prior. The premium count is therefore: **"count these 14 things — we derived the rest, here is which ones we are least confident about, and every derived line is labeled and docks confidence."** If that converts a half-shift sweep into ~20 minutes, Muntin's total cost of ownership is *lower* than MarginEdge's at nearly twice the sticker, because MarginEdge's customer still does a full count.

**What gets cut from the product surface.** The 16 `/insights/*` routes reduce to food-cost and variance for the cohort. `/bookkeeper/` (five queues, rules, statement reconciliation, workpaper export) is built and unmarketed — it becomes the **desk's own tool**, not a customer surface, until a firm actually buys. The Accountant tier and its `hasCockpit: true` entitlement are deleted rather than built; no `/cockpit` route is ever written. `/reorder` and `/learned-yields` stay dark. Recipes stay, because Tier-2 theoretical is what shortens the count.

## How It Fails
**The failure I would actually bet on: the exception desk does not shrink, and the ceiling is real.** The entire venture argument rests on `desk_minutes_per_close` falling as covered layouts rise. The corpus already hints the tail is hostile — cintas 54.3%, staples 37.5%, cozzini_bros 20.8% — and those are exactly the non-food vendors for which the free Cost Index covers zero of its 100 ingredients, so there is no market prior to fall back on either. If desk minutes stay flat, the desk *is* the business, founder review capacity is the binding constraint, and the honest terminal shape is ~40 locations at $600 = ~$288K ARR: a very good one-person business that is not a venture and should be described as one rather than dressed up.

**The failure that kills it fastest: the CPA says no to the market prior.** ASC 330 does not permit a public wholesale median as a book basis. The short count — derive most of the sheet, ask the operator to verify only the divergent rows — is what makes a $600 close deliverable at one-person scale and what makes Muntin's total cost of ownership lower than MarginEdge's at nearly twice the price. If a practitioner says any non-cost basis is a hard stop, that entire premise collapses back to a full physical count, the customer still pays $100–150/month of floor labor, the TCO argument inverts, and $600 becomes indefensible against a $350 incumbent that includes human keying.

**The failure nobody wants to name: Don cannot close ten customers.** There are zero recorded outbound conversations in fifteen months. A $7,200/year purchase is a considered sale requiring a champion and a check-signer, and Don signs no cheque at his own restaurant — he has never been on the buying side of the transaction he is now designing around. The price inversion's central claim is that $600 buys back the direct channel; that claim is worthless if the founder does not make the calls, and no agent session can make them for him.

**The subtler failure: correctness becomes a permanent excuse.** Every move in the first six weeks is a defect fix. This company has a documented pattern of shipping mechanism instead of shipping — gates went ~90 → 320 in 33 days, prior audits close at 26%, and zero closures ever came from working a list. A bet that makes correctness existential is a bet that can be spent entirely on correctness. If the specimen close has not been published by day 60, the bet has already failed and become another quarter of building for nobody.

**Early signals that would prove it wrong, in order of arrival:**
- **Day ~45, from the specimen close:** if fewer than ~70% of Tacombi's invoice *dollars* survive into a valued Purchases leg, the number cannot be made correct at any price this quarter and the pilot must not open. This is measurable in one afternoon against invoices the founder already has.
- **Day ~45:** if the September close requires more than ~2 hours of exception-desk work for one location's month, the unit economics are a service business with no software margin. The correct response is to cut the cohort to five and raise the price further — not to scale.
- **Day ~50:** if the count still takes a half-shift after Tier-2 derivation, the "half the labor" half of the pitch is gone and only "twice the price" remains.
- **Day ~60, from the CPA:** a refusal of the market prior as any basis at all kills the short count. A refusal of the *method* (periodic identity, physical count, disclosed estimates) would be worse and would mean the paid tier is unsellable, but that outcome is unlikely — the method is orthodox; the labels were the problem.
- **Day ~90, from applications:** if fewer than 3 of the first ~10 qualified applicants convert at $600, the $350–500 comparable does not transfer to a tool with no human keying the invoice — meaning buyers were paying for the human, not the number, and the premium bet is wrong in the opposite direction from the one it was designed to correct.

**The thing I would refuse to do to save it:** ship the close before the legs foot. A flattering food-cost percentage that does not tie to what a bookkeeper calls food purchases is the single fastest way to destroy the only asset this company has, and at $600 with a signature on it, one bad number does not cost one customer — it ends the brand that everything else is built on.
