# How the best actually build with AI — the operating frontier (agent-built code quality, verification, isolation, orchestration) and what transfers to a 13–26 h/month solo operator

## Thesis
The frontier in mid-2026 has moved decisively from generation to verification, and the winning teams are the ones who converted judgement into machinery rather than into more agents. Three independent lines of evidence agree on the shape of the problem: agentic PRs fail overwhelmingly for socio-technical and workflow reasons rather than raw code incorrectness; a 20,574-session study found ~91% of visible agent failures still required explicit human correction; and the review bottleneck is now measured in pickup-time multiples rather than defect rates. The architectural response multiple independent teams converged on is a generator/checker/deterministic-gate triad, where the gate is code and therefore cannot be wrong, and where a flaky gate is an emergency because engineers stop trusting a gate the first week it reds for an unrelated reason. On isolation, git worktrees became the default primitive for parallel agents in 2026 — with the loudly-stated caveat that worktrees isolate files but not ports, databases, caches or test state, which is precisely the class of corruption Muntin hit today. On orchestration the honest 2026 verdict is a retreat from ambition: peer-collaboration/GroupChat patterns lost production ground, Anthropic's own ~15x token premium for multi-agent stands, single-agent systems match or beat multi-agent at equal token budgets, and the surviving pattern is Cognition's single-writer principle — many agents may contribute intelligence, only one may contribute actions. The productivity evidence is far weaker than the discourse: METR's RCT found experienced developers 19% SLOWER with AI while believing they were 20% faster, and METR's own 2026 follow-up had to change design because developers refused to work without AI, leaving the newer favourable numbers explicitly self-described as weak evidence. Quality claims are similarly contested — GitClear's vendor telemetry reports record duplication and collapsing refactoring, while a peer-reviewed study of 151 repositories with self-admitted GenAI usage found no general increase in churn. What is genuinely established about agent code is subtler and worse for a solo operator than the churn headline: agents resolve tasks up to 13.1% less successfully when building on prior agent code, agent maintenance skews superficial and additive, and agents comply poorly with constructive instructions they were explicitly given. The solo-founder literature is the least trustworthy corner of this space — dominated by content-farm figures with no denominators — with Pieter Levels the one durably public case, and his lesson is anti-sophistication: vanilla PHP and SQLite, no framework, agents managed rather than orchestrated. For someone with a permanent 13–26 h/month the frontier is therefore not more agents; it is fewer writers, harder gates, and an explicit written decision about which paths a human still reads.

## Findings
### Tier review by blast radius, not by authorship or guilt — spend scarce human attention only where being wrong is costly, and let deterministic gates plus AI reviewers own the rest. Explicitly: for a solo project with no users, letting AI review almost all of it is called a defensible 2026 position.
- **Who:** Addy Osmani (Google Chrome), 'Agentic Code Review' (O'Reilly Radar / addyosmani.com, 2026)
- **Why:** It converts an unbounded obligation ('read every line') into a bounded, written policy. The alternative in practice is not careful review — it is unacknowledged non-review, which is what the industry actually did: PRs merged with zero review rose sharply once volume outpaced attention.
- **Confidence:** well-established

### The generator → fresh-context checker → deterministic gate triad, where the gate is ordinary code with no model call in it, plus deterministic replay so a red gate always means a real regression. Checkout-free, LLM-free CI gates (e.g. Agent-Gate) are the purest expression.
- **Who:** Convergent pattern across ~9 independently-reported teams; Codacy, Optimum Partners ('adversarial gating'), the Agent-Gate GitHub Action, and 'Reason Less, Verify More' (arXiv 2607.07405)
- **Why:** Named explicitly: 'the gate is the part that can never be wrong, because it is code, not a guess.' And the failure mode is named too — 'without replay, engineers stop trusting the gate the first week it fails for a reason unrelated to their code.' A permanently red gate is worse than no gate.
- **Confidence:** well-established

### The single-writer principle: run many agents if you like, but let only ONE contribute actions/writes; the others contribute intelligence (review, analysis, proposals) that flows back into the single writer's context. Peer-collaboration / GroupChat topologies lost production ground in 2026.
- **Who:** Cognition, 'Multi-Agents: What's Actually Working' (2026), following their 2025 'Don't Build Multi-Agents'
- **Why:** Cognition's stated diagnosis is that decision-making becomes too dispersed and cross-agent context-passing is unsolved. This is exactly the failure Muntin hit today: six concurrent agents in ONE working tree with six writers and no shared context, corrupting their own measurement.
- **Confidence:** well-established

### Git worktrees as the isolation primitive — one agent, one worktree, one branch, one PR, always branched from the stable base and never from another agent's branch — PLUS separate runtime isolation (ports, databases, caches, secrets, test state), because worktrees only isolate the filesystem.
- **Who:** Widely documented 2026 practice (Augment Code, MindStudio, Zylos, Penligent, Developers Digest); JetBrains shipped first-class worktree support in 2026.1, VS Code in July 2025; Docker Sandboxes / Sysbox / devcontainers for the runtime half
- **Why:** The distinction is the whole lesson: 'Git worktrees stop one task from trampling another task's files, but are not enough to stop one task's runtime from trampling another task's ports, databases, caches, secrets, or test state.' A shared build output or a shared JSON manifest is exactly such shared runtime state.
- **Confidence:** well-established

### Agentic PRs fail for workflow and social reasons far more than for wrong code. Across ~33k agent-authored PRs from five agents, 71.48% merged overall (Codex 82.59%, Cursor 65.22%, Claude Code 59.04% on n=271, Devin 53.76%). Merge-blocking patterns: no reviewer engagement ~38%, duplication ~23%, CI/test failure ~17%. Docs/CI/build tasks merge best; performance and bug-fix tasks worst. Unmerged PRs touch more files.
- **Who:** 'Where Do AI Coding Agents Fail? An Empirical Study of Failed Agentic Pull Requests in GitHub' (arXiv 2601.15195, MSR 2026 Mining Challenge)
- **Why:** It gives a task-selection policy grounded in data rather than vibes: delegate mechanical/structural work, keep human framing on performance and bug-fix work, and cap agent change size. The Claude Code cell is n=271 — too small to rank agents on, but the cross-agent pattern is consistent.
- **Confidence:** reported

### Design the loop around cheap human correction, not around autonomy. In 20,574 real coding-agent sessions across 1,639 repos, ~90.5% of misalignment episodes cost effort and trust rather than causing irreversible damage — but ~91.5% of visible resolutions still required an explicit user correction. Misalignment persists across adjacent sessions.
- **Who:** 'How Coding Agents Fail Their Users' (arXiv 2605.29442)
- **Why:** Correction is the dominant cost, not catastrophe. That inverts the design goal for a capacity-constrained operator: the highest-value engineering is making correction cheap and making it stick across sessions — which is what a session-start hook and machine-readable rules do, and what a prose CLAUDE.md loading in 1 of 108 sessions does not.
- **Confidence:** reported

### Treat multi-agent fan-out as an expensive last resort. Anthropic has measured roughly a 15x token premium for multi-agent versus chat; 2026 studies report single-agent systems matching or beating multi-agent at equal token budget; a 180-configuration study identifies a capability ceiling where coordination goes net-negative once single-agent performance is already high; additional agents show clear diminishing marginal returns.
- **Who:** Anthropic's multi-agent research (~15x figure); 2026 topology/scaling studies summarized across FlowHunt, Glean, and the token-economics literature
- **Why:** Fan-out is the most seductive form of overhead-disguised-as-sophistication for a solo operator, because it feels like hiring. The evidence says it buys coordination cost unless the subtasks are genuinely independent AND write-isolated.
- **Confidence:** reported

### Mutation testing (and property-based invariants) as the trust signal for agent-written tests, because agents optimise for coverage and coverage is now free. A documented LLM-generated suite hit 100% line and branch coverage with a 4% mutation score. Practitioner thresholds circulating in 2026: ~70% mutation score on critical paths, ~50% standard, ~30% experimental; the highest-leverage habit is a manual sub-one-minute mutation check.
- **Who:** Practitioner writing throughout 2026 (Prateek Singh, 'Your AI-Generated Tests are Lying to You'; Nimble Approach; twocents.software)
- **Why:** It is the only cheap answer to 'the tests pass, so what?' — the specific way agent work looks verified without being verified. The thresholds are unsourced practitioner convention, not research; the 100%-coverage/4%-mutation example is the load-bearing part.
- **Confidence:** reported

### Agent code is measurably harder for the NEXT agent to work on — task resolve rates drop up to 13.1% when building on agent-authored code versus human-authored code — and classical maintainability metrics do not explain the gap. The signals are behavioural: changed input validation and error handling, and downstream code size.
- **Who:** 'Is Agent Code Less Maintainable Than Human Code?' (arXiv 2606.21804); 'To What Extent Does Agent-generated Code Require Maintenance?' (arXiv 2605.06464)
- **Why:** This is the most important finding for an 80–84% agent-authored codebase with a permanent part-time maintainer, because it compounds silently: today's agent output is tomorrow's agent input. It also explains why the standard remedy (a linter, a complexity metric) does not detect the problem.
- **Confidence:** reported

### Enforce module boundaries BEFORE agents start building rather than refactoring them in afterward; keep agent surface area narrow and let structure do the constraining. Related: lazy-load tool definitions rather than loading every tool into every session, and treat context rot (coherence degradation well before the nominal window fills) as an architectural constraint, not a model deficiency.
- **Who:** 2026 context-engineering practice literature (Chroma's context-rot research is the most-cited empirical anchor); post-mortem-derived guidance on tool-definition bloat
- **Why:** Boundaries are the only constraint that survives an agent forgetting your instructions, which the logging study suggests happens often — agents failed to comply with explicit constructive instructions ~67% of the time in that domain. Structure beats instruction.
- **Confidence:** reported

### Deep scepticism toward every productivity number in this space. METR's RCT found experienced OSS developers 19% SLOWER with early-2025 AI while estimating afterward that they were 20% faster (n=16 developers, 246 tasks). METR's 2026 follow-up changed design because developers increasingly refused to participate without AI, and METR itself labels the newer, more favourable read as only very weak evidence. DORA 2025 reports ~90% adoption with throughput up an estimated 2–18% and stability declining. GitClear's vendor telemetry reports record duplication and collapsing refactoring, while a peer-reviewed study of 151 self-admitted-GenAI repos found no general increase in churn.
- **Who:** METR (arXiv 2507.09089 and the 2026-02-24 design-change note); DORA / Google Cloud State of AI-assisted Software Development 2025; GitClear 2026 Maintainability Gap vs 'Self-Admitted GenAI Usage in Open-Source Software' (arXiv 2507.10422)
- **Why:** The measurement instruments disagree, the vendor sources have an interest, and the one clean RCT found a perception gap of ~39 percentage points between felt and actual speed. Any Muntin plan that budgets founder-hours against an assumed agent speedup is budgeting against a contested number.
- **Confidence:** well-established

### Verified in this repo, not on the web: /home/user/potentially-profitable contains NO worktree tooling. `grep -rl worktree scripts/ .claude/` returns exactly one incidental hit in scripts/test-article-graphics.mjs; .claude/hooks/ contains only session-start.sh. There is no per-agent isolation mechanism of any kind.
- **Who:** Direct inspection, 2026-08-07
- **Why:** The frontier's cheapest, highest-consensus practice — one agent, one worktree — is entirely absent, while the failure it prevents already occurred today. This is the largest gap between Muntin's practice and documented 2026 practice, and it is roughly an hour of work.
- **Confidence:** verified

## For Muntin
- TRANSFERS, AND IS THE SINGLE HIGHEST-LEVERAGE CHANGE: one agent = one git worktree = one branch = one PR, with a single writer. Muntin already suffered the exact corruption this prevents (six concurrent agents, one tree) and has zero worktree tooling (verified: only hit is scripts/test-article-graphics.mjs). Add the runtime half too — the shared artifacts here are not ports and databases but data/*.json manifests and sentinel-rewriting builders (build-contract-facts.mjs, inject-site-counts.mjs, inject-knit-rail.mjs), which are precisely 'one task's runtime trampling another's state.' Cost: ~1 founder-hour. Retires: spawning parallel agents in the working tree at all.
- TRANSFERS: the red deploy (320 of 328) is not a backlog item, it is a trust emergency, and every hour spent elsewhere is spent on a system whose gates no longer mean anything. The frontier's own stated failure mode is 'engineers stop trusting the gate the first week it fails for a reason unrelated to their code.' Muntin has run that experiment for far longer than a week, with a 26% audit close rate as the result. Nothing else on this list works until check-all and the deploy are green.
- TRANSFERS: write the blast-radius tier list down as data, not prose. Osmani's dial belongs in docs/contracts/ next to rules.json — TIER-HUMAN (the fact gate, data/sourced-claims.json, pricing constants, anything touching PII or the no-llm claim, any published number) which Don reads personally, no exceptions; TIER-GATE (everything with a passing check-*.mjs) which the machine owns and Don never reads; TIER-AGENT (prose, figures, ES mirrors) which an adversarial reviewer agent owns. Today the policy is implicit and therefore is 'read nothing, trust nothing.' Retires: the guilt-driven ambition to review everything, and the pretence that the 5 rules with no detector are enforced.
- TRANSFERS, AND IS THE BEST-FIT FRONTIER PRACTICE MUNTIN ALREADY HALF-OWNS: mutation-test the GATES, not the code. Muntin's load-bearing artifact is not the site, it is the 136 check-* scripts; if a gate's parser is wrong the gate is decorative. scripts/test-article-graphics.mjs is already the correct precedent — extend that pattern to check-fabrications.mjs, check-cost-index-editors-note.mjs and check-queue.mjs's --done verifier. A gate with no test is a 100%-coverage/4%-mutation-score artifact wearing a badge.
- TRANSFERS: task-select by the merge-rate evidence. Documentation, CI and build-update tasks merge best; performance and bug-fix tasks merge worst; unmerged PRs touch more files. For Muntin that means agents own the 419 retired-vocabulary pages, the taxonomy migration, the 547 broken ledger.muntin.digital CTAs and the surface-disposition execution — all bulk, structural, gate-checkable work. Don keeps the honesty-contract judgement calls and the withheld-number decisions, exactly the class agents fail at.
- TRANSFERS, AS DIFFERENTIATION RATHER THAN OPERATIONS: publishing the gate is a genuinely uncontested position. The frontier converged on deterministic gates as an INTERNAL trust mechanism; nobody publishes theirs. Muntin already has no-llm-ci.sh — a claim a stranger can falsify in 30 seconds — named on zero of 1,327 pages while 93 pages make the claim it proves. 'Here is the script that proves it, run it yourself' is the storefront's strongest available proof of the technical competence the founder says the site lacks, and it costs nothing to build because it already exists.
- DOES NOT TRANSFER — multi-agent orchestration as an architecture. ~15x token premium, single-agent parity at equal budget, clear diminishing returns, and a documented capability ceiling where coordination goes net-negative. Anthropic's own 2026 report puts fully-delegable tasks at 0–20%. For 13–26 h/month, orchestration frameworks are the purest overhead disguised as sophistication: they add a coordination surface that must itself be maintained by the person who has no hours.
- DOES NOT TRANSFER — eval engineering as a separate discipline with its own harness and LLM judges. Muntin's 136 deterministic check-* scripts ARE its evals, and they are better than an LLM-judge suite because they cannot hallucinate a pass. Building an eval layer on top would mean maintaining two verification systems while one is already red. Narrow exception worth one hour: a fixture-based test that the fact gate rejects a known fabricated number.
- DOES NOT TRANSFER — memory systems, RAG over the repo, or a knowledge layer. The problem they solve (CLAUDE.md loading in 1 of 108 sessions) is already solved here by .claude/hooks/session-start.sh plus contract-as-data, which is more robust than retrieval because it is unconditional. Adding memory infrastructure would add context-rot surface to a system whose measured problem is instructions not surviving, not facts not being findable.
- DOES NOT TRANSFER — heavy spec-driven development. ThoughtWorks' Technology Radar placed SDD in 'Assess' and warned specifically about bias toward heavy up-front specification and big-bang releases. The vendor claims for it (GitHub's 'order of magnitude fewer regenerate cycles', AWS Kiro's '40 hours to under 8') carry no denominators and come from the tool owners. At 16 h/month in the M1 window, writing specs would consume the budget the specs exist to protect. Muntin's queue items already carry doneWhen + a verify command, which is the useful 10% of SDD at 2% of the cost.
- DOES NOT TRANSFER — purchased AI code-review tooling (Greptile, CodeRabbit, Qodo). Those relieve a human-review bottleneck across a team; Muntin has no team and no review queue, and benchmark catch rates in the 28–36% range mean they would not replace the gates anyway. Greptile's widely-cited '27.6% of merged PRs are AI-generated' has Greptile's own customer base as its denominator and must not be quoted as an industry figure.
- READ THE SOLO-FOUNDER LITERATURE AS MARKETING. Searching it returns content-farm figures with no denominators and, in at least one case, an implausible revenue claim ($401M from a $20k zero-employee telehealth launch) presented alongside named-founder anecdotes I could not corroborate. Do not cite any of it. The one durably public case is Pieter Levels (~$3M+/yr, self-reported publicly over years), and his transferable lesson is anti-sophistication: vanilla PHP and SQLite, no framework, agents on a VPS being managed rather than orchestrated, revenue charged from day one. That is the closest published analogue to what Muntin is attempting, and it argues for fewer moving parts, not more.
- EVERY PROPOSAL ABOVE RETIRES SOMETHING, PER THE STANDING RULE: worktree isolation retires unisolated parallel spawning; the tier list retires the pretence of universal review and the 5 undetected rules' claim to enforcement; gate-testing retires untested gates; publishing the gate retires 93 pages of unproved claims. And the largest available retirement is already measured and unclaimed — 92 of 393 scripts are invoked by no runner (data/system-graph.json). A meta-gate in the exact style of check-gate-coverage.mjs, requiring every script to be invoked or registered-with-a-reason, would retire roughly a quarter of the automation surface. That is the cheapest real reduction in maintenance load available to a 13–26 h/month operator, and the only item here that gives hours back rather than spending them.
