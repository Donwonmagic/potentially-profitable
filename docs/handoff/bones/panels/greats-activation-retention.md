# Activation, Habit and the Cold Start

## Thesis
Every company that beat a cold start did it by moving the cost of the first configuration off the user and onto the company — Mint rented Yodlee's bank coverage so day-one value did not depend on Mint's own user base, Notion staffed a template gallery so nobody faced a blank page, Superhuman put a human on a 30-minute call with every new user. Muntin's cold start is not unsolved; it is solved three separate ways and all three are switched off. `services/extract/VENDOR-TEMPLATE-KEY.md` documents a fully-wired, migrated, tested vendor-header-trust path measured on a 919-document real corpus at 24% → ~62% first-pass straight-through, held in SHADOW by a single env var (`EXTRACT_VENDOR_TRUST_ACTIVE`); `cli/seed_template.py` can seed a vendor from one reference invoice plus a sidecar; the community pool is opt-in-off with its line-item supply side structurally severed. That is the whole game, and it is one founder decision and a shadow-agreement report away. The activation threshold that matters is not uploads — it is invoices-until-first-auto-confirm, because `needs_review` blocks `/post`, so an operator whose vendors never auto-confirm is doing manual data entry with extra steps, and no habit survives that. On habit: refuse Duolingo's streak wholesale — a restaurant's invoice flow is exogenous (deliveries arrive on the distributor's calendar, not the operator's), so a streak would punish an operator for Sysco's route; take instead Duolingo's forgiveness valve and MyFitnessPal's real lesson, which was never gamification but the barcode scanner — the chore got shorter, not more fun. The honest weekly return loop is already built and dark: `apps/api/src/lib/cost-watch-digest.ts` is pure, materiality-floored, fail-closed, forecast-free, reports its own suppression count, and runs on a Monday cron with default-off subscriptions — it is trustworthy precisely because it was engineered to say nothing most weeks. The highest-leverage move is therefore to build nothing: read the shadow-agreement rate the flag was built to produce, flip it per-vendor, and instrument first-auto-confirm as the company's activation KPI.

## Mechanisms
### Slack
**What:** Slack chose a single usage-depth number — 2,000 messages exchanged by a team — as the definition of 'has really tried Slack,' derived by looking backward at which teams stuck and which churned. Stewart Butterfield stated the framing publicly: any team that has exchanged 2,000 messages in its history has tried Slack. Every onboarding and product decision was then oriented at getting teams there faster. The widely-repeated '93% of teams past 2,000 messages converted' figure comes from secondary write-ups, not a Slack primary source — UNVERIFIED.

**Why it worked:** It converted an unanswerable question ('is our onboarding good?') into a scalar anyone could optimize, and critically it measured DEPTH OF USE rather than signup or login. 2,000 messages is a proxy for the product having become the team's system of record — the point past which leaving costs more than staying. A signup metric would have optimized the top of the funnel; a depth metric optimized the moment of switching cost.

**For Muntin:** Muntin's analog cannot be uploads, because `needs_review` blocks `/post` (`apps/api/src/lib/extractions-store.ts`) — an operator can upload twenty invoices and have zero become food cost. The switching-cost moment is when the ledger holds purchase history the operator did not type. Proposed threshold: 3 auto-confirmed invoices, posted, spanning ≥2 distinct vendors, within 21 days of first upload. Two vendors guards against a single seeded vendor manufacturing a false success; 21 days is roughly one delivery cycle per vendor on most broadliner accounts. The leading indicator to instrument first is simpler and is already named in the repo's own audit findings: invoices-until-first-auto-confirm, per vendor.

### Mint
**What:** Two separate cold starts solved two ways. (a) Data: Mint contracted Yodlee for account aggregation rather than building bank connections, so day-one coverage was independent of Mint's own user count. (b) Audience: Aaron Patzer launched the MintLife blog roughly nine months before the product shipped, building a subscriber list against a product that did not exist; by launch MintLife reportedly out-trafficked competitors' entire sites and the beta was oversubscribed. Specific subscriber counts in circulation (e.g. ~20,000) are secondary-source — UNVERIFIED.

**Why it worked:** Patzer refused to let a network effect be a prerequisite for the first user's value. Coverage was bought, not accumulated. And the content asset compounded on a different clock than the product, so launch day landed on an audience rather than on silence.

**For Muntin:** This is Muntin's exact situation, half-done and inverted. The content asset is over-built (1,368 pages, 100-ingredient CC0 index, 171-term glossary) and structurally disconnected — 4 of 1,368 pages carry a waitlist form, and the monthly broadcast filter at `src/worker.js:8793` sends only where `source === 'cost-index'`, which is 2 of 777 signups. The data-coverage half maps precisely onto `cli/seed_template.py` (runbooks/seed-templates.md, 'Path C'): the company seeds broadliner templates from reference invoices BEFORE any customer arrives, exactly as Mint rented coverage. Don has real Tacombi invoices from real distributors. Muntin does not need Yodlee; it needs a seeding sprint.

### Notion
**What:** Notion's blank page was its own cold start. The answer was a curated template gallery — first staffed internally, later community-contributed and creator-monetized. A new user's first act became 'pick one' rather than 'build a system from nothing.'

**Why it worked:** It moved first-configuration cost from the user to the company, then to enthusiasts, so the cost fell over time while coverage grew. Second-order: each template is a public, indexable, shareable artifact — the cold-start solver was simultaneously an SEO surface and a distribution unit. One asset paid three ways.

**For Muntin:** Muntin's cold-start solver is literally a template gallery — a seeded vendor-template library. Seed the broadliners an American independent actually buys from (Sysco, US Foods, PFG, Baldor, Chefs' Warehouse, Restaurant Depot, regional produce and protein houses), then publish the coverage list as a public page: 'these vendors read clean on your first invoice; these do not yet.' That page is honest (it publishes its own gaps, this company's signature move — the Cost Index already refuses a posture on 37 of 100 ingredients), it is the highest-intent SEO surface the product could own ('does it read Sysco invoices'), and it is a waitlist-capture surface on a site that currently captures almost nowhere.

### Superhuman
**What:** Every new user got a 1:1 video onboarding — reported at roughly 30 minutes — in which a specialist configured the app to that person's workflow, imported mail, taught shortcuts, and drove them to inbox zero on the call. Rahul Vohra personally ran the first several hundred. The waitlist was a consequence of onboarding capacity, not a marketing device. Reported staffing figures (~20 specialists at peak; ~60–70 needed for venture-scale) are secondary summaries of podcast remarks — UNVERIFIED.

**Why it worked:** It eliminated activation risk by brute force at exactly the stage when a startup has more founder-hours than users. It also generated the diagnostic corpus — where do real people stall — that made later automation correct rather than guessed. Doing it manually first is what made automating it right.

**For Muntin:** Muntin has zero confirmed non-founder users after ~15 months and, per its own audit, a founder who has never run the product end-to-end. That combination makes concierge onboarding not a growth tactic but the only available source of truth. Adapted: Don personally onboards the first 20 operators — in their restaurant, with their invoice stack, at the pass, on a slow afternoon — and each session ends with that operator's top 2 vendors seeded via `cli/seed_template.py`. This is the one recommendation on this panel an agent session cannot execute, and saying so is the point.

### MyFitnessPal
**What:** Food logging is genuinely tedious, and the durable answer was not points or badges — it was the barcode scanner, a large pre-built food database, and 'copy yesterday's meal.' The chore got measurably shorter.

**Why it worked:** Habit formation is far more sensitive to friction than to reward. A reward makes a user want to do a 6-minute task; removing 4 minutes makes them actually do it. The pre-built database was also a supply-side cold-start solve of the Mint variety — the company pre-loaded the world's foods so a first log did not require authoring a food.

**For Muntin:** Directly applicable to the monthly count, and it argues AGAINST gamifying it. `apps/web/app/(product)/count/` already has the right instincts (auto-built sheet from purchase history, value-count 80/20, offline-safe save). Next friction cuts, in order: (1) pre-fill each line with last count's quantity as a ghost default so the operator edits deltas instead of authoring numbers; (2) sort the sheet by station in physical walk order (`inventory-stations-store` exists and is not yet driving order); (3) a keypad-only / voice-capture mode so counting is one thumb, not a form. Target a stated, measured 'counted in N minutes' and publish the real N — including when it is bad.

### Duolingo
**What:** The streak: an accumulating asset the user built, visible and losable — plus the Streak Freeze, a designed forgiveness valve letting a lapse be recovered instead of becoming a quit. Escalating, character-voiced push notifications drive the daily return; freezes are also sold, which monetizes the anxiety the streak creates.

**Why it worked:** Loss aversion on a self-authored asset is one of the strongest retention forces in consumer software, and the freeze is the load-bearing half — without a recovery path a single missed day converts a committed user into a churned one. It works for Duolingo specifically because the target behavior is ENDOGENOUS: the user fully controls whether they practice today.

**For Muntin:** The streak half does NOT transfer and must be refused — an operator's invoice flow is exogenous, so a streak punishes them for their distributor's schedule, and a truth-telling company cannot ship a metric whose denominator it knows the user does not control. What transfers honestly: (a) the accumulating asset made visible — seeded vendor coverage, the depth of your own price history, the length of your unbroken reconciliation chain, shown as something you built (the substrate exists: the append-only chain, `chain-head`, the audit ledger); and (b) the forgiveness valve — a missed monthly count must be met with a plain, recoverable-period message and the steps, not silence. That is the half almost nobody copies.

### Calendly
**What:** The unit of value and the unit of distribution are the same object: the scheduling link. Every time the product works for a user, a non-user encounters it in a context where it is already delivering value to someone they are dealing with.

**Why it worked:** Distribution rides on normal use rather than an asking-for-a-favor referral step. The recipient sees competence demonstrated, not claimed, and meets the product at the moment it is solving a problem they also have.

**For Muntin:** Muntin's only true viral loop is the bookkeeper handoff, already half-built: `apps/api/src/routes/accountant-handoffs.ts`, `/for-your-bookkeeper`, `/para-tu-contadora`, `/bookkeeper/*`, and the accountant digest cron. A bookkeeper serves many restaurants; a clean, well-coded Muntin export lands on their desk as a demonstration. Give the export a quiet, non-salesy provenance line linking to a bookkeeper-specific page (not the consumer landing page), and instrument `handoff_claimed` — already in the funnel allowlist. This is the one loop where private data creates exposure without ever leaving the operator's control.

### Figma
**What:** The file URL was simultaneously the activation unit and the viral unit. Multiplayer meant the second person in a file was already using Figma before deciding to adopt it; sharing was a normal work act, not an evangelism act.

**Why it worked:** It collapsed 'try it' and 'share it' into one gesture and made the shared artifact live rather than a static export — so the recipient's incentive to open it was the work itself, and once open they were inside the product.

**For Muntin:** Does NOT transfer to the invoice product — invoices are private, the whole posture is org-scoped RLS, and multiplayer over a restaurant's AP data fights the brand. It transfers to the Cost Index: the shareable live artifact is a per-ingredient embeddable card, which the audit found exists once out of 82 possible. An embeddable, CC0, honestly-sourced card ('beef 80/20 wholesale, last 12 months, withheld-reading disclosure intact') is a live object other people's pages will host, and each is a backlink and a permanent distribution surface. Highest-ceiling storefront move here, and pure agent work.

### Robinhood / Monzo waitlists — as a cautionary contrast
**What:** Both ran pre-launch waitlists with position-visible queues and referral-based line-jumping, converting scarcity into signups.

**Why it worked:** It works when the product genuinely has a capacity constraint, because the queue is then true information. It is a manufactured lie when there is no constraint, and users increasingly recognize it.

**For Muntin:** Partially, with a hard boundary. Muntin's waitlist can honestly become capacity-constrained IF the constraint is real — Superhuman's was, because onboarding was human-run. So 'we onboard N operators a month because Don does it personally and seeds your vendors' is TRUE and is the only version Muntin may ship. A position counter, a referral ladder, or a fabricated 'N ahead of you' is not available at any price: a manufactured number on a site whose brand is a CI-enforced fact gate, trivially falsifiable. Take the mechanism, refuse the theatre.

## Recommendations
- **Read the vendor-trust shadow-agreement rate and flip `EXTRACT_VENDOR_TRUST_ACTIVE=1` per-vendor, highest-reconcile vendors first. This is the single highest-leverage activation change available today and it requires building nothing.**
  - `services/extract/VENDOR-TEMPLATE-KEY.md` states the path is fully wired and tested — the store (`vendor_profile_store.py`), the gate (`vendor_trust_gate.py`), the deriver (`role_map.py`), migrations 0046/0047, the read side in `orchestrate.py`, the construction in `main.py`. Its own real-corpus measurement (919 documents, 746 evaluated, `cli/vendor_flywheel.py` at header gate 0.7) is 24% → ~62% first-pass straight-through with 87% reconcile among newly-covered docs; three vendors account for 219 of the 265 newly-safe documents. The change is a TRUST re-key — auto-confirm counts confirmations per (org, vendor, header-signature) instead of per (org, vendor, layout_hash) — and reconciliation stays the final gate, so downside is bounded. Today `main.py:120` activates only on the literal string '1', so this measured unlock is off in production. Cold start IS this: `drift.py` returns NEW_LAYOUT for any brand-new vendor, NEW_LAYOUT is a HARD block reason, so a first-touch invoice from an unseen vendor can never auto-confirm. These figures are the repo's own; I did not re-run the corpus.
  - Agent-executable: An agent session builds everything except the flip: (1) run `cli/vendor_trust_shadow.py` against whatever shadow telemetry exists and produce the agreement-rate report the flag was designed to gate on — if no invoices flow yet, run `cli/vendor_flywheel.py` against the existing 919-doc corpus and present the per-vendor table as the decision input; (2) write a per-vendor rollout ladder with an explicit one-env-var rollback; (3) add a `vendor_trust_qualified` counter to the funnel allowlist so the flip's effect is readable within a day; (4) write the ADR. The flip itself is a founder call — it changes what earns auto-confirm trust on real money and should carry a human name.
- **Instrument 'invoices until first auto-confirm' as the company's activation KPI, and adopt the threshold: 3 auto-confirmed invoices, posted, across ≥2 distinct vendors, within 21 days of first upload.**
  - Slack's 2,000 messages worked because it measured depth of use at the point of switching cost, not signups. Muntin's switching cost arrives when the ledger holds purchase history the operator did not type. `needs_review` blocks `/post`, so upload count is a vanity metric here — an operator can upload and still have no food cost. Two distinct vendors guards against a single seeded vendor manufacturing a false success. 21 days is roughly one delivery cycle per vendor for most broadliner accounts. The leading indicator — invoices-until-first-auto-confirm, per vendor — is the number that governs whether anyone stays, and it is also the direct readout on whether the vendor-trust flip worked.
  - Agent-executable: Fully agent-executable. The sink exists: `apps/api/src/routes/funnel.ts` with allowlisted event names, allowlisted non-PII field names, `check-funnel-no-pii.mjs` gating it and a `FUNNEL_EMIT=off` kill switch. Add `activation.first_auto_confirm`, `activation.first_post`, `activation.threshold_reached` with allowlisted fields `invoices_before_auto_confirm`, `distinct_vendors`, `days_since_first_upload`, `vendor_seeded` — no vendor names, no amounts, so the PII gate stays green. Then an `/ops` panel reading it. One session. Dependency the audit surfaced: `PLAUSIBLE_INGEST_URL` may be unset, so verify the sink actually lands before trusting the dashboard.
- **Run a vendor-template seeding sprint against the real broadliners, then publish the coverage list — including what is NOT covered — as a public page.**
  - This is Mint-renting-Yodlee and Notion's template gallery in one move, and the machinery exists: `cli/seed_template.py` with `--catalog-match` drops the promotion threshold to a single confirmation per scalar field, so one reference invoice plus a ground-truth sidecar fully seeds a vendor (runbooks/seed-templates.md). Don has real invoices from real distributors at Tacombi. Coverage bought up-front means a new operator's first invoice reads clean instead of landing in `needs_review` — which is the entire cold start. The public page does triple duty exactly as Notion's gallery did: highest-intent SEO the product can own ('does it read Chefs' Warehouse invoices'), a waitlist-capture surface on a site with 4 capture points across 1,368 pages, and an honest gap disclosure in the same register as the Cost Index refusing a posture on 37 of 100 ingredients.
  - Agent-executable: Agents build the whole apparatus: a seeding wrapper CLI taking a PDF and producing the docling JSON plus a sidecar scaffold, the sidecar validator, a coverage manifest (`data/vendor-coverage.json`), the public page generator under the standard fact-gate discipline, the waitlist form on it, and a CI gate that fails if the published coverage list drifts from the seeded template store. What agents cannot supply is the reference invoices — one photo or PDF per vendor from Don's stack, minutes of founder time per vendor, not hours.
- **Ship the honest weekly return loop: turn on the cost-watch digest in-product, and build its public sibling — per-ingredient watch subscriptions on the Cost Index — to replace the broken monthly broadcast.**
  - The founder-vision doc is right that monthly is a weak habit loop, and the answer it gestures at is already built to a high standard. `apps/api/src/lib/cost-watch-digest.ts` is pure, ranks one action per item (buy-now / reprice / ask-vendor / ride / watch), applies a materiality floor, fails closed on a stale snapshot, adds no number and no market dollar level, makes no forecast, and always reports its `suppressed` count. It runs on a Monday cron (`0 15 * * 1`) with subscriptions DEFAULT-OFF. That property set is exactly what makes an alert trustworthy rather than noise: opt-in, user-set materiality floor, silent most weeks, never claims cause, never predicts. The public sibling is the honest daily-or-weekly reason to return that the data can actually support — some series (usda-lmr) carry daily points, so a watch on 'beef 80/20' has real weekly signal, while a monthly BLS series must state its own cadence and stay quiet. Meanwhile the current email path is broken at the seam: 162 ingredient pages promise an email `src/worker.js:8793` cannot send, because the subscribe source is `cost-index-ingredient` and the filter accepts only `cost-index`.
  - Agent-executable: Fully agent-executable and mostly one-line-adjacent. (1) Align the subscribe source with the broadcast filter — the audit puts this at 15 minutes and it is the difference between having an audience and not knowing whether you have one. (2) Add a per-ingredient watch form to all ~162 ingredient pages via an existing injector, storing the ingredient key on the subscription. (3) Build the public watch mailer as a sibling of the cost-watch digest, reusing its honesty invariants verbatim — materiality floor, suppression count, no cause, no forecast — plus an explicit 'nothing crossed your floor' quiet-week send at most monthly, so silence is legible rather than ominous. (4) Wire a gate that fails if a watch email can be composed containing a number absent from the frozen edition snapshot.
- **Reorder the onboarding checklist so the paywall step never precedes the first value moment, and add first-auto-confirm to it.**
  - `apps/api/src/routes/onboarding.ts` fixes step order as passkey → recovery_phrase → subscription → first_extraction → first_posting, and its own comment claims the order 'encodes the natural setup sequence.' It does not: step 3 is a paywall placed ahead of step 4, the first time the product does the thing it exists to do. Every activation exemplar on this panel — Slack, Figma, Notion, Mint — put value strictly before commitment. While in there: the checklist has no step for the moment that actually predicts retention (a vendor auto-confirming without review), so the progress bar can read 100% while the product has never once worked unattended for that operator.
  - Agent-executable: Fully agent-executable in one session: reorder `OnboardingStep`, add a `first_auto_confirm` step and its probe, add EN and ES copy keys in `lib/copy.ts` / `copy.es.ts` (the shape-parity test enforces both), run `check-copy-grade.mjs` (Flesch-Kincaid ≤ 7) and `check-verboten-phrases.mjs`, update the `/today` FirstRunStrip consumer, prettier, typecheck.
- **Design activation and habit around the restaurant seasonal calendar explicitly: recruit in the trough, ask for the count in the trough, go quiet in the crush.**
  - Purchasing, cash and attention in an independent restaurant follow a predictable and brutal calendar — the holiday crush and the January cliff, patio season, back-to-school, month-end and the week the P&L lands. An activation ask arriving during Friday dinner service is not a weak ask, it is an insult, and it teaches the operator that the product does not know their world — precisely the empathy the founder-vision doc names as the site's axis. Concretely: the count is the only manual input in the whole system (`Beginning + Purchases − Ending = Usage`), so ask for it on the calendar's terms — Sunday or Monday morning in the last week of the period, never a Friday, never December 20–31. And an operator who misses a December count should meet a recoverable-period message, not silence and not a broken streak.
  - Agent-executable: Agent-executable, but it needs one founder input agents cannot invent: the actual calendar. Don writes down in fifteen minutes the weeks his restaurants are slammed, the days and hours that are dead, and when his P&L lands. An agent then encodes it as a data file with stated provenance ('operator-supplied, one two-location brand in the DMV — not a claim about all restaurants') and threads it through the count-due nudge, the `notification-prefs` defaults (`burnAnomaly` currently defaults to push — check it against the calendar), the push dispatcher's send windows, and the digest cron. Honesty discipline: this is one operator's calendar, and no copy may imply it is an industry dataset.
- **Make the count shorter rather than more fun — ghost defaults from last period, station-ordered walk sequence, and publish the measured minutes-to-count.**
  - MyFitnessPal's durable answer to a tedious log was the barcode scanner, not badges. The count already has the right bones — auto-built sheet from purchase history, the value-count 80/20, offline-safe save, and a stations store (`inventory-stations-store`) not yet driving sheet order. Ghost defaults turn authoring into editing, the single largest friction cut available. Publishing the real measured time is the Muntin move: it converts a UX claim into a falsifiable number, the same mechanic that makes `no-llm-ci.sh` credible.
  - Agent-executable: Fully agent-executable. Station ordering is a query change plus the picker. Ghost defaults read the prior period snapshot (`inventory-period-store`) and MUST render visibly as un-entered with an explicit accept — a pre-filled number the operator scrolls past is a fabricated count that flows into food cost — and `inventory-reconcile.ts` must treat un-accepted ghosts as missing. Instrument session duration client-side, emit as an allowlisted funnel field, publish the median only once there is a real N.
- **Concierge-onboard the first 20 operators personally, in their restaurants, and let onboarding capacity be the honest reason the waitlist exists.**
  - Superhuman's founder ran the first several hundred onboardings himself, and the reason it was correct was not growth-hacking — manual onboarding is the only way to learn where real people stall before you automate. Muntin has zero confirmed non-founder users and, per its own audit, a founder who has never run the product end-to-end. Every other recommendation here is a guess until someone watches a stranger try. It also makes the waitlist honest: 'we take N a month because Don seeds your vendors and sits with you' is a true capacity constraint, and it is the only version of Robinhood-style scarcity this brand may ship.
  - Agent-executable: The visits are founder-only and should not be dressed up as anything else — this is the item that costs hours and it is the highest-information hour in the company. Agents make each hour count: a one-page onboarding script in order, with the pass-side seeding steps; a structured stall-log the founder fills on his phone; a per-visit runbook wrapping `cli/seed_template.py`; and a synthesis pass every five visits turning the stall-log into ranked product work. Also agent-executable and strictly prerequisite: the funnel has four mouths across 1,368 pages and every product CTA points at `ledger.muntin.digital`, which neither repo routes — resolve the hostname first or the twenty operators have nowhere to go.

## Antipatterns
- Do NOT ship a Duolingo-style streak on invoice uploads or any delivery-driven behavior. The behavior is exogenous — deliveries arrive on the distributor's calendar — so a streak punishes the operator for Sysco's route. A company whose brand is 'we withhold a number when we don't know' cannot ship a metric whose denominator it knows the user does not control.
- Do NOT copy Duolingo's escalating, emotionally-charged, character-voiced notification ladder. Guilt-shaped nudges at a restaurant owner about their food cost are punching at someone's livelihood. The honest version is a materiality-floored alert that stays silent by default — which `cost-watch-digest.ts` already implements.
- Do NOT monetize anxiety the product creates (Duolingo sells streak freezes). Selling relief from a worry you manufactured is structurally incompatible with a methodology page that forecloses sponsorship and paid placement.
- Do NOT build Strava-style peer leaderboards or 'restaurants like yours pay X.' Muntin has no peer dataset — it has a public wholesale index. Comparison against the MARKET is honest and already shipped (Vendor Benchmark); comparison against unnamed peers requires inventing a cohort, exactly the fabrication pattern `check-fabrications.mjs` was written to block.
- Do NOT add social proof the company does not have — no 'N restaurants trust Muntin,' no counters, no placeholder testimonials, no 'join 500 operators.' There are zero confirmed non-founder users. One such line on a site whose differentiator is a CI-enforced fact gate would be discovered and would discredit the honesty machinery wholesale.
- Do NOT run a Robinhood/Monzo referral-ladder waitlist with position counters and line-jumping. Scarcity is available only where the constraint is real (founder-run concierge onboarding capacity). A displayed queue position no capacity limit backs is a manufactured number.
- Do NOT gamify financial surfaces with points, badges, XP or confetti over food cost. Vohra's own distinction applies: game DESIGN (clear goals, fast feedback, legible progress) transfers; gamification (extrinsic reward bolted on) reads as unserious on a screen where someone is deciding whether they can make payroll.
- Do NOT pre-fill count quantities silently. A ghost default the operator scrolls past becomes a fabricated inventory number flowing into Beginning + Purchases − Ending = Usage and out into a published food-cost percentage. Ghosts must render as un-entered, require explicit acceptance, and be treated as missing by the reconciliation checksum — otherwise a convenience feature becomes an invention engine.
- Do NOT solve cold start by loosening the review gate — no lowered confidence thresholds, no widening `auto_confirm_safe` past the measured 0.7 header knee, no dropping reconciliation as the final gate. The VENDOR-TEMPLATE-KEY sweep is explicit that documents admitted below 0.7 reconcile at only ~70%. Shipping wrong dollar amounts to make activation look better is the one failure this brand cannot absorb.
- Do NOT hold three cold-start shortcuts at 80%. Vendor-trust is in shadow; the community pool's line-item supply side is structurally severed (the pooled fingerprint folds the row's SKU, so a pooled line-item rule matches almost never) and is opt-in-off; the anonymous `/try` read is parked behind `DEMO_ANONYMOUS_EXTRACT`. Three parked mechanisms deliver exactly as much activation as zero. Pick the one with a measurement behind it and finish it.
- Do NOT let any alert imply causation or forecast. The events surface is co-occurrence-only by ADR-011 and the drivers layer is 'association, not a measured cause.' A subject line reading 'beef is rising because of X' breaches the honesty constraint even when the correlation is real — and an alert that overreaches once is never trusted again, which destroys the very habit loop it was built to create.
- Do NOT treat notification volume as a growth lever. `apps/api/src/routes/push.ts` was deliberately built so the push payload carries no business data and the detail is dereferenced only after authentication; that restraint is an asset. Adding frequency to hit an engagement number would spend a privacy posture the company took real engineering effort to earn.
