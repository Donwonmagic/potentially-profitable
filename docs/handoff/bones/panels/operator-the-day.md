# The Operator's Day — when would this person ever open Muntin?

## Thesis
Muntin has been designed for a person who does not exist in either repo's evidence: an owner who photographs paper invoices at the back door and reviews them at 10pm. The only real operator this company has ever observed is Don himself, and his own 919-document corpus says something different — every committed real fixture is `application/pdf`, two of them still carry hand-typed filenames in an `MM.DD.YY` convention, and the corpus lives in a folder on a Mac. His invoices are already digital, already downloaded, already renamed by a human; the friction was never OCR, it was the twenty seconds of filing and the fact that nobody ever reads the pile. Worse, his vendor set — Chef's Warehouse, I. Halper, Fresco, Pat LaFrieda, Cintas, Cozzini Bros, the Montgomery County liquor board — contains none of the national broadliners a new customer's first invoice most likely is, and the product repo already wrote that warning down and then built the whole demo strategy on top of it anyway. He is also not the buyer: he is a salaried front-of-house manager at a two-location brand, signs no check, owns no P&L, and would not be the person who purchases software for that restaurant. So the company's single sample is biased on capture format, on vendor mix, on technical fluency, and on purchasing authority simultaneously. The one thing the repos do get right, and get right three separate times without noticing, is the week's real hinge: the storefront's own prime-cost sheet says "Tuesday morning works for most operators — prior week is closed," the cost-watch digest fires Tuesday, and the Cost Index publishes the first Tuesday — while the product's front door says "Three things tonight." There is a habit moment available here, and it is a Tuesday-morning email, not a nightly app session.

## Implications
- **Make an inbound email address, not a camera, the first thing a new account is handed — and make the activation success metric "a vendor's mail auto-forwards" rather than "an invoice was uploaded."**
  - The founder's own 919-document corpus is PDF-native (7/7 fixtures `application/pdf`, hand-renamed `MM.DD.YY`), the email worker that resolves `inb_*@invoices.muntin.digital` is already built and tested, and the only capture design that survives a busy week is one that requires no weekly act. The repo itself names the alternative's failure mode: "I forgot to forward last week's invoices."
- **Reorder the onboarding checklist so `first_extraction` is step 1 and passkey / recovery phrase / plan come after. Let a magic-link session carry an operator all the way to a read invoice.**
  - `onboarding.ts` fixes the order as passkey → recovery phrase → plan → first extraction, so the first value is behind two security ceremonies and a paywall step. `auth.ts` already supports magic-link and password sessions, so nothing forces this. A back-of-house manager on a phone at 9am will not enrol a passkey to find out whether the thing works.
- **Pick Tuesday morning as the company's single habit moment and land exactly one artifact there — a short email that reconciles last week's invoices against the Cost Index — instead of a nightly app session.**
  - Three Tuesday artifacts already exist independently (the prime-cost sheet's "Tuesday morning works for most operators — prior week is closed," the `0 15 * * 2` cost-watch digest, the first-Tuesday Cost Index dispatch) and nothing joins them. Tuesday is also the day a real operator has: prior week closed, payroll ahead, the produce reorder pending — all three named on the founder's own sheet.
- **Retire "Three things tonight" as the product's organizing frame, or write down the evidence for it. `/today` should be a summary you can read in an email, not a destination you must open.**
  - The 10pm audience is an inheritance from INTELLIGENCE-SPEC.md, not an observation; it contradicts the founder's own Tuesday-morning cadence copy; and it commits the product to a daily-open habit that a zero-user company has no evidence anyone will form. A nightly surface is the single most expensive habit to ask for and the last one to earn.
- **Fix the day-clock: make crons DST-aware and Eastern-anchored, and stop describing 08:00 UTC as "immediately before the typical North American breakfast hour." Re-time the "While you slept" window to the delivery window (roughly 5am–noon local) rather than a rolling 24 hours.**
  - `wrangler.toml` anchors the whole schedule to CST for a Silver Spring founder; 08:00 UTC is 4am ET; and "X invoices arrived overnight" is false for a business whose invoices arrive with the morning truck. These are small edits that make the product's clock match a restaurant's.
- **Treat `/bookkeeper` as a primary surface with its own onboarding, not a $150 tier — and give the accountant digest a reply-to-act path rather than a manual `hello@` inbox.**
  - `accountant` is already a first-class role, the bookkeeper console already ships queue/rules/charge-mappings/statements/workpaper-export, the weekly accountant digest already runs, and the final onboarding step is "Post to your accounting tool." The product already closes an accounting loop; the GTM panel independently found the bookkeeper is the only channel this price can afford. Both facts point at the same person, and today the runbook's own opt-out flow is a human reading email.
- **Make non-food, route-delivered vendors a named category with a recurring-charge check — linen, blade exchange, smallwares, office, and control-state liquor — instead of treating every document as a food invoice.**
  - Cintas, Cozzini Bros, Edward Don, Staples and Montgomery County DLC together are a large minority of the founder's own corpus, they arrive on fixed routes with a driver and a signature, they carry exactly the disputable recurring lines the repo already fixtures (`LOST CHARGE`, `MAT EXCHANGE`), and they are the worst-reconciling vendors in the measured table (54.3% and 20.8%). This is also the one invoice class where an operator has a live, weekly, unhandled dispute — the strongest available trigger.
- **Before any of the above, run the founder's own 919 PDFs through the product's real activation path — email-forward and ZIP, not the Mac CLIs — and time every step. Redact `fresco_lineitems.json` first.**
  - He has never run the product end to end; his corpus is exactly the shape `/v1/inbox/zip-batch` was written for; and this is the only way to convert the single strongest asset in the company (four years of real Mid-Atlantic distributor invoices) into evidence about the product rather than about the engine. The audit's upside lens flags that the same fixture file still carries real Tacombi address, invoice numbers, Net-45 terms and negotiated prices.

## Unknowns (founder interview script)
- At Tacombi, who physically receives the vendor invoice — Don, the kitchen manager, a shift lead — and does a copy ever leave the building for corporate AP? If corporate handles it, Don's 919-PDF folder is a personal archive, not a workflow, and the product has never observed an actual invoice-to-books path at all.
- Of a real independent's weekly invoices, what fraction arrive as (a) paper with the delivery, (b) emailed PDF, (c) a vendor-portal download, (d) a driver's handheld with a tablet signature? Ask five operators to count last week's pile by channel. The photo-vs-email product decision turns entirely on this and no repo artifact answers it.
- Who typed "Bethesda Invoice 11.06.23.pdf"? Whoever renames and files those PDFs is the real user of this product, and nobody has interviewed them — including Don about his own habit. Ask him: how long does that take you per week, and what would you stop doing if it went away?
- When in the week does an independent operator actually sit down with invoices — and is it a scheduled act or only triggered by something going wrong? Ask for the last three times they opened the invoice pile and what caused each.
- What triggered the last vendor charge you disputed, and how did you notice? If the honest answer is "the bookkeeper caught it at month-end" rather than "I saw it at the back door," the receiving-checklist moment is fiction and the bookkeeper moment is the product.
- What day does your P&L come back from your accountant, and what do you do in the 48 hours after? That is a candidate habit moment nobody in either repo has considered, and it is the moment a bad number actually hurts.
- Would your bookkeeper accept invoices from a tool you set up, or do they insist on their own intake (Dext, a shared Drive folder, a shoebox at month-end)? If the bookkeeper's intake wins, Muntin should feed it rather than replace it.
- Have you ever set up an auto-forward rule for a vendor's emails? This is the single riskiest assumption in the recommended activation path — it is one step, but it is a step in an email client, and an operator who cannot do it has no path at all.
- Would you rather open an app or get an email on Tuesday morning? Ask it that plainly, to five operators, and let the answer decide whether `/today` is a page or a newsletter.
- Which of your vendors are national broadliners? If a typical independent's first invoice really is Sysco/US Foods/PFG, the founder's corpus buys almost nothing for cold-start accuracy, and the demo cannot be un-parked on the strength of it.
- Did the Cost Index reading ever change a price you paid or a conversation you had with a vendor rep — on any actual shift? The storefront's own strategy doc named this the biggest unaudited assumption in the company and nobody has run it. One vendor conversation settles it.
