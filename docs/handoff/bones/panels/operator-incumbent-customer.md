# The Incumbent's Customer — who is served, who is abandoned

## Thesis
The incumbents in restaurant AP are not sorted by feature — they are sorted by whether a human keys the invoice. MarginEdge publicly describes its pipeline as AI plus "actual people with prior restaurant experience," turning invoices around in 24–48 hours (3–5 days for the first four weeks); that human step is why the price is $350 per location per month and why Restaurant365 sits at $435–635. Every one of those companies is optimized for the invoice mix where that labor amortizes: broadliners, EDI feeds, high line-count Sysco and US Foods deliveries. But the founder's own 919-document corpus — the most reliable customer evidence either repo contains — has **zero broadliner invoices in it**. The top ten vendors by volume are I. Halper Paper, Chefs' Warehouse, Baldor, Pat LaFrieda, Edward Don, Fresco, the Montgomery County Department of Liquor Control, Cintas, Staples, and Cozzini Bros. That is the abandoned customer, described precisely: an operator whose invoice dollars run through eight to twelve regional specialty distributors, a county liquor monopoly, a linen service and a knife-sharpening route — a mix no national vendor will ever build templates for, and that costs a human-keyed platform more to process than a small operator will pay. Muntin already reconciles that mix at 84–87% straight-through, deterministically, in seconds, which is the one thing on this company's balance sheet that money cannot buy quickly. The strategic error is not the product; it is that the indexed `/vs/marginedge` page, fact-checked 2026-05-21, still calls the tool "vertical-agnostic" and tells the highest-intent visitor the company will ever get that MarginEdge wins on inventory and recipe costing — both of which shipped months ago. The beachhead is not "small restaurants." It is **the specialty-vendor operator in a single metro, reached through the bookkeeper who already keys those invoices by hand.**

## Unknowns (founder interview script)
- Ask a bookkeeper who serves restaurants: how many of your clients' invoices come from vendors your capture tool has never seen a template for, and what do you personally do with those — key them, batch them, or bill the client extra? This is the single question that confirms or kills the specialty-vendor thesis.
- Ask three operators who left MarginEdge or R365: what actually made you leave — price, the 24–48 hour lag, the implementation, or the fact that nobody on staff kept doing the counts? If the answer is 'nobody kept doing the counts,' the abandoned segment is an activation problem, not a pricing one, and Muntin inherits it.
- Ask a Toast-using operator: were you quoted xtraCHEF, at what price, and did you buy it? Toast publishes nothing, and the entire threat assessment turns on whether the add-on is $149, $349, or effectively free to existing subscribers.
- Ask an operator with a specialty-vendor mix: what do you pay today, in total, for anything that touches invoices — including the bookkeeper's hours? That number, not a SaaS list price, is the real budget line Muntin is competing for.
- Ask a bookkeeping firm principal: would you pay per client for a restaurant-specific tool on top of Dext, or would you switch entirely? Additive means a small per-client price and a long sales cycle; replacement means a much higher price and a much harder sale.
- Ask an operator directly: when a case price moves 12 cents, do you want to be told, and would you actually do anything about it? The pre-post anomaly intercept is ranked as the #1 moat in the roadmap and has never been tested against a person who has to act on the alert during service.
- Ask a restaurant CPA: is the food-cost number this product prints — Beginning + Purchases − Ending = Usage, with the market-prior fallback for uncounted items — one you would put in front of a client, and where would you refuse it? The audit's completeness critic flagged that nobody has checked whether the number the product exists to print is accounting-correct.
- Ask any of them: who told you about the last back-office tool you bought? If the answer is consistently 'my bookkeeper' or 'another owner,' the channel arithmetic holds; if it is 'my POS rep,' the price forecloses the only channel that works.
- Confirm from Search Console and Plausible whether the `/vs/*` and `/switch/*` pages get any traffic at all before investing another hour in them — the audit found 79% of the site emits no analytics, so their value is currently assumed, not measured.
