# The Buying Decision — what triggers it, what kills it, who signs

## Thesis
There is no buying decision at Muntin today, and the reason is structural rather than commercial: `PUBLIC_LAUNCH` defaults to `pre`, `LAUNCH_DATE_ISO` is `null` by deliberate decision (ADR-020), and the one posted price a customer has actually been offered — $19/location founding — is marked `billable: false` in the company's own registry. Every surface the company calls a funnel terminates in a waitlist, so what needs designing is not a close but the first purchasable moment. When that moment is built, the code says unambiguously who signs: `stripe_subscriptions.org_id TEXT PRIMARY KEY REFERENCES orgs(id)` makes the **restaurant** the billing subject, `owner` is the only role that cannot be invited, and `accountant` is a guest the restaurant invites — so a bookkeeping firm structurally cannot be a customer, only a permission. That collides head-on with the price arithmetic the GTM panel established, and the collision, not the price, is the actual blocker on the only affordable channel. The channel is also not empty: MarginEdge launched **[me] finance on 2023-06-27**, an AP-automation product built specifically for restaurant accountants and bookkeepers, with a partner network, one login across all clients, and a public accountant directory — which means the "Bookkeeper-mode UI — **Nobody**" white space asserted in `runbooks/qbo-xero-roadmap.md` (2026-05-25) was roughly three years stale on the day it was written. Meanwhile the thing that would kill the sale at the moment of decision is not price, contract, or setup — it is the cold start: with no template the engine falls to `position_heuristic` (0.55) and `generic_harvest` (0.35), low-confidence reads land in `needs_review`, and `needs_review` blocks `/post`, so a first upload becomes manual entry with extra steps. The one instrument built to defuse that objection *before* anyone commits — `/try`, an anonymous read of the visitor's own invoice, fully built and hardened — is switched off behind `DEMO_ANONYMOUS_EXTRACT` pending real-invoice coverage the founder can supply from his own shift. And the mechanism designed to solve the cold start permanently, the community template pool, is opt-in OFF by default and empty, so it requires customers to produce the thing that produces customers. The honest summary: the product's buying decision is blocked by three one-person acts (name the ICP, seed ten real broadliner layouts, make a firm an account) and by zero unsolved technology.

- **[well-established]** Price is not the objection, and treating it as one misreads the buyer. A single-location restaurant commonly pays $500–$1,200/month for outsourced bookkeeping, and a small group $500–$2,500 (figures from bookkeeping-vendor publications — directionally consistent across several independent sources, but vendor-published and therefore not audit-grade). Against that line item a $19–25 tool is 2–4%, invisible on a card statement. The scarce resource is the owner's attention and the bookkeeper's trust, not his dollars. The corollary is the actual channel economics: a firm charging per client is selling **labor**, so every keying hour Muntin removes is margin the firm keeps — which is exactly why Dext prices Practice at $17.70–$19.20 per client per month with a ten-client minimum and a one-year auto-renewing commitment, and why MarginEdge pitches accountants on 'scaling without adding labor costs.' Muntin at $19 is at parity with Dext, not undercutting it, and 5x under [me] finance.
  - Basis: WebSearch across paperchase.ac, getexact.com, lexstartbk.com (restaurant bookkeeping fees); help.dext.com + aggregator pricing pages for Practice plans; marginedge.com/for-accountants
- **[verified]** Switching cost in this category is data death, not contract — and Muntin has deliberately built none of it. MarginEdge's monthly plans carry no contract; the lock is that after cancellation you keep access for 12 months and then **permanently lose the account**, so leaving means losing your invoice history. Dext Practice auto-renews annually with a one-year minimum, which means the reachable moment for a bookkeeper is a **renewal date**, not a mood. R365 adds implementation quoted in the thousands as sunk cost. Muntin's posted promises run the opposite way — free CSV at every tier, no per-export fee, 'we do not lock you in' — which is a genuine conversion asset and an equally genuine retention liability. The only ethical lock-in available to Muntin is accrued measurement: per-(org, vendor, layout) learned templates, six-month price baselines that make the anomaly flags work, the hash-chained audit trail, and period-over-period count history. All four take three to six months of tenure to become valuable, which means retention depends entirely on surviving the cold start that month one is most likely to fail.

## Implications
- **Name the buyer in one sentence and delete the contradiction in the same commit: **the independent restaurant operator with 1–3 locations, on QBO or Xero, not on Toast, whose books are kept by a part-time bookkeeper or an outside restaurant-specialist firm.** Rewrite `vs/marginedge` lines 62 and 103–104 (EN + ES), and record the ICP as an ADR so no future session re-opens it.**
  - 'Vertical-agnostic — a coffee shop and a hardware store' is shipped copy on the comparison page a buyer reads at the exact moment of evaluation, and it argues against the only asset Muntin has that Dext does not: restaurant fluency. Two ICPs is the same failure the audit found in ADR-020 — a strategic act with no customer in it.
- **Make a **firm** a first-class account before building any more bookkeeper features. A `firms` entity, a client-org membership edge, one Stripe subscription billed to the firm with per-client quantity, a ten-client minimum, and a client switcher across orgs. Fix the `billing.ts:174-175` seat multiplication first — a per-client quantity model on top of that bug bills a 20-client firm ~$3,000/month.**
  - `stripe_subscriptions.org_id` is a primary key on the restaurant org and `owner` is not invitable, so today a bookkeeping firm cannot be a customer at any price — it can only be a guest of 20 separate restaurant accounts. Every feature in the T2 bookkeeper tier is already built (queues, rules, statements, workpaper export, lock-date, charge mappings) and none of it is sellable to the party it was built for. The commercial container is the gap, not the features.
- **Clear the `/try` gate with ten real invoices from Don's own shift, seed header glyph-variants per column as the roadmap specifies, then flip `DEMO_ANONYMOUS_EXTRACT=on`. Treat this as the highest-priority item in either repo.**
  - The cold start is what kills the sale at the moment of decision, and `/try` is the only artifact that answers it before anyone commits — a stranger drops their own Sysco invoice and watches it read. It is fully built. The one blocker is real-invoice coverage of the top broadliner layouts, which the founder handles weekly at Tacombi and which no agent can supply. Every other GTM investment is downstream of this flag.
- **Turn the community template pool ON by default for new orgs (with a visible, reversible opt-out), and seed it from the top ten broadliner layouts before the first customer arrives — the same corpus that clears the `/try` gate.**
  - Opt-in OFF plus zero users means the mechanism designed to eliminate the cold start can never accumulate the contributions that eliminate it. The pool trades only in redacted column geometry with a PII-shape assertion in CI, and a pooled hit already renders as confirm-me at 0.80 rather than a confirmed read — so the honesty cost of defaulting it on is small and the cold-start benefit is the whole product.
- **Add four fields to the waitlist form and backfill by asking the existing list: role (owner / manager / bookkeeper / accountant), number of locations, accounting system, and who does the books. Then segment the ~777 existing signups and route bookkeepers to a different sequence than owners.**
  - The company is choosing between two incompatible go-to-market motions and cannot count how many people on its own list belong to each. Four fields converts an undifferentiated list into the only primary market evidence the company has ever had, at the cost of one afternoon.
- **Resolve the hostname split so the storefront links the bookkeeper argument: either route `ledger.muntin.digital` to `apps/web`, or rewrite all 547 storefront CTAs to `app.muntin.digital`. Then publish the bookkeeper lane — Hubdoc/Xero-Files replacement, Dext-renewal comparison, restaurant-fluency-vs-horizontal — on the **storefront**, where the traffic is, and link it into the product pages.**
  - Every bookkeeper-facing asset lives on a hostname the 1,368-page content machine never mentions, and only 10 storefront pages contain the word 'bookkeeper' at all. Until that seam closes, no content strategy aimed at the channel the price can afford can possibly land.
- **Publish and defend one trigger the company can actually own: **the vendor price hike found too late**, answered by the free Cost Index. Rank the content lanes as (1) the bookkeeper's tool changing under them — Hubdoc's aftermath and Dext renewal dates, (2) a price move discovered at month-end, (3) opening a second location. Stop planning around POS migration and delivery-platform statements, which the product does not serve.**
  - Software gets bought after an event. Of the reachable events, exactly one has a free asset already built that IS the answer, and it is the asset the company has spent fifteen months on. Dext's one-year auto-renew also means the bookkeeper trigger is a dated decision point, not a mood — content aimed at it should be evergreen and always-on, not campaign-shaped.
- **Write the support answer before the first firm asks: state the response window in writing, cap the free beta at a number of orgs one employed person can carry, and remove the Team tier's 'phone support (Tuesday SMS group + a real number)' promise or bound it explicitly.**
  - A firm evaluating Muntin for 20 clients asks who answers at close week, and a one-person company that promises a real phone number will either break the promise or break the founder. This is the failure the audit already measured on a different axis — 53 recurring founder-hours per month against a stated capacity of 13–26 — and support load is the one obligation that arrives on the customer's schedule rather than the founder's.

## Unknowns (founder interview script)
- Does a restaurant-specialist bookkeeping firm in the DMV actually keep its clients on separate Dext/QBO subscriptions, or does it bill the tool through to the client as a pass-through line? This decides whether the firm entity needs its own Stripe subscription with per-client quantity or a firm-billed reseller spread — and it is a schema decision that is expensive to reverse.
- What is the real number of restaurant clients per bookkeeper at a firm of this shape, and how many keying hours per client per month go into invoices specifically? The entire 'we sell the firm its own margin back' argument rests on that hour count, and no public source gives it. Ask three firms and take the range.
- When does the firm's current Dext or [me] finance contract renew, and has anyone in the DMV actually evaluated [me] finance? If MarginEdge's accountant product has already swept the local restaurant-specialist firms, the channel is contested rather than open and the wedge has to be something other than 'nobody serves bookkeepers.'
- Would a firm pay anything at all for a tool it did not choose but its client did — and inversely, would a chef-owner sign a subscription his bookkeeper picked without reading it? The answer decides whether the first sale is bottom-up (owner signs, invites bookkeeper) or top-down (firm signs, enrolls clients), and the product can only be built for one of them first.
- What does a real operator do in the first ten minutes after an upload lands in needs_review with 'Layout changed'? Don has never run the product end to end. One shift of his own invoices through the live product — not the engine — would settle the single largest unknown in the company, and no agent can do it.
- Which ten vendor layouts actually cover the majority of a DMV independent's invoice volume? The roadmap assumes 'top ~10 broadliners' from power-law reasoning; Don can name the real ten from his own AP stack in five minutes, and that list is simultaneously the /try gate, the pool seed, and the cold-start fix.
- Is 'no language model reads your invoices' a reason a bookkeeper switches, or a curiosity they nod at? It is the company's most defensible and most falsifiable claim, and nobody has tested whether it moves a purchase or only earns respect. If it doesn't move a purchase, the wedge is restaurant fluency plus the free Cost Index instead — a different storefront.
- What does the bookkeeper ask about first: Xero posting, lock-date respect, or the audit chain? Xero is 'wired but not live,' and if it is the first question, the honest answer ends the meeting. Three conversations would tell you whether to finish Xero before the firm entity or after.
