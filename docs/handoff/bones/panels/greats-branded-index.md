# The Branded Index as an Institution

## Thesis
An index becomes cited when it makes a working journalist's sentence easier to write than not writing it — and everything else is downstream of that. Across every exemplar, the citation-capture stack is the same four rungs in the same order: a single number that needs no explanation, a definition anyone can check, a cadence that never breaks, and permanence a fact-checker will accept — and no amount of money buys a shortcut past rung one (Wikipedia explicitly rejects paid placement and primary data repositories as notability evidence). Muntin is unusually strong on rungs two and four and structurally broken on rungs one and three: the headline is "−0.5% against baseline for a weighted basket of 16 staples" whose weights its own file marks "INTERNAL JUDGMENT (label illustrative), NOT a sourced fact" — a percentage of an abstraction that no editor will print — and the edition spine holds four editions, last dated 2026-07-06, while `/cost-index/weekly/` publicly promises "monthly on the first Tuesday since August 2026." The fix for rung one is the Big Mac mechanic, and it is available here without touching the honesty contract: a *stipulated* reference plate priced in dollars from the already-published wholesale references, because a stated recipe is a definition that cannot be wrong, while a weighted composite is an empirical claim that can. On monetization, the methodology page forecloses exactly two things — influence and placement ("no submitters, no paid placements, no pay-to-be-listed") — which kills the Levels.fyi/Glassdoor counterparty model dead in food (the counterparty is the distributor) but leaves the Morningstar and Zillow models fully intact: sell depth and sell the adjacent transaction, never the print. The "no submitters" clause is not boilerplate; it is the precise architectural difference between SOFR and LIBOR, and it is the strongest sentence on the site, currently buried in a subsection. Muntin's real citation vacuum is not "food prices" — BLS, USDA ERS and the National Restaurant Association already own that — it is *per-ingredient, restaurant-framed, machine-readable, CC0, and free*, where a reporter writing about the price of a chicken sandwich currently has to choose between a raw USDA AMS report and a vendor's press release. The nearest-term unlock is embarrassingly cheap: the DataCite record already exists with `"doi": null`, Zenodo mints versioned DOIs free with a concept DOI that always resolves to latest, and the embeddable card is built and shipping to exactly one ingredient out of eighty-two.

## Mechanisms
### The Economist — Big Mac Index
**What:** Introduced September 1986 by Pam Woodall as a semi-serious illustration of purchasing-power parity, and published continuously since. It prices one physically identical, publicly-specified good across countries and reports the raw local price, the dollar price, and the implied PPP. The unit of citation is the *price of a defined thing*, not a percentage move of an abstract composite. It is now taught in economics textbooks (verified).

**Why it worked:** Three structural properties compound. (1) A dollar price is quotable in one sentence with no baseline to explain — the reader already knows what a burger costs. (2) The basket is a STIPULATION, not an empirical claim: 'one Big Mac' cannot be wrong, only more or less useful, which permanently immunizes it from methodological attack in a way a weighted composite never is. (3) The recipe never changes, so every year is self-comparable — the comparability IS the asset, and it accrues only by never re-specifying. Its self-deprecating framing was a fourth defense: by never claiming rigor it never had rigor to lose, and academic use followed anyway.

**For Muntin:** Directly, and it is the single highest-value move on this panel. Muntin's headline today is `−0.5%, the weighted basket of 16 restaurant staples, against its baseline window` with `agreement 51%` — a percentage of an abstraction, over weights that `data/cost-basket-weights.json` itself labels internal judgment and not a sourced fact. Replace it as the *quotable* number (keep it as the analytical one) with a stipulated **reference plate**: a frozen, versioned recipe in ounces — e.g. 6 oz chicken breast, 8 oz russet potato, 2 oz romaine, 1 oz vegetable oil, 0.5 oz cheddar — priced as Σ(qty × published wholesale reference) from the 81–82 series already shipping. That is arithmetic over sourced data with a stipulated definition on top, so it clears the fact gate more cleanly than the current illustrative weights do. And it produces the sentence an editor will actually print: 'the wholesale ingredients for Muntin's reference plate cost $X.XX this month, up N cents from last.'

### CBOE — the VIX
**What:** The formal name is the Cboe Volatility Index. Nobody uses it. The number travels through press and television as 'the fear index' — a nickname that names what the number MEANS to the reader rather than what it measures or who computes it. The number itself is a single scalar on an intuitive scale.

**Why it worked:** A journalist needs a phrase their reader already understands, and will reach for the phrase that requires no gloss. An index whose common name describes the *reader's situation* gets used; one whose name describes the *publisher's method* gets paraphrased away, and a paraphrase carries no attribution. The nickname was not assigned by marketing — it emerged — but an index that supplies a plain-English handle for the thing being measured makes that emergence far likelier.

**For Muntin:** Applies to the naming question directly. 'The Muntin Cost Index' names the publisher and the method; nothing in it tells a reader what they are looking at. Keep it as the formal/institutional name (it is correct for the DataCite record, the methodology page, and the license line), but supply a plain handle for the NUMBER itself that describes the reader's world — the reference plate above is the natural carrier. Attribution then rides on the possessive rather than the noun ('Muntin's reference plate'), which is exactly how 'the Economist's Big Mac Index' works and is stronger citation capture than putting the brand in the noun phrase, because a possessive survives paraphrase and a compound noun does not.

### S&P Dow Jones Indices — S&P/Cotality Case-Shiller Home Price Indices
**What:** The headline index level is free and republished everywhere; Case-Shiller itself is a licensed trademark. S&P DJI's disclosed revenue lines are asset-linked fees on funds tracking its indices, royalties on exchange-traded derivatives, licensing fees for OTC derivatives and structured products, and data/custom-index subscriptions (verified). CME lists futures on the ten-metro composite. Nobody pays to read the number; everybody pays to build an instrument on it.

**Why it worked:** The free number's ubiquity IS the product. Every additional republication raises the value of the license, so independence and revenue point in the same direction — there is no configuration in which shading a print earns more than it costs. That alignment, not governance, is what makes the print trustworthy.

**For Muntin:** The architecture transfers even though the exchange does not. Muntin has no derivative market and will not have one. What transfers is the *rule*: the number stays free and CC0 forever, and money is only ever charged for something that becomes MORE valuable the more widely the number is quoted. That rule cleanly permits a paid interpretation layer, paid depth in Muntin Ledger, and paid advisory work; it cleanly forbids paywalling a reading, charging for API access to the index, or licensing exclusivity. Write that rule down as an ADR so it survives a cash-poor quarter.

### Morningstar — the star rating
**What:** A free, universally-republished 1-to-5 classifier over funds became the shorthand every fund advertiser and journalist used. Revenue comes from research, screeners, analyst coverage and institutional data feeds sold ON TOP of the rating — never access to the rating, and never the ability to influence one.

**Why it worked:** The free artifact is a CLASSIFIER, not a datum: it compresses a decision into one token. Compression is what makes a thing quotable, and it simultaneously creates demand for the underlying detail from exactly the people who care most — which is the cleanest possible qualification funnel. Because the paid tier sells work adjacent to the rating rather than access to it, no buyer can pay to move a star, and the classifier stays credible.

**For Muntin:** Muntin already has this and has not noticed. The four postures — lock / cushion / float / withhold, currently 37 / 19 / 7 / 37 across 100 ingredients — are a star rating for restaurant ingredients, and nothing in food has one. 'Ribeye is a LOCK this month; tomato is WITHHELD' is a compressed, quotable, decision-shaped token. The audit's §4 read is the right monetization: a paid monthly written interpretation over the free data (needs zero billing code and zero new product), and depth inside Ledger. Elevate the posture vocabulary from an internal analytic to the public-facing shorthand, with a permanent explainer page each posture links to.

### Zillow — the Zestimate
**What:** A free automated valuation on essentially every US home, plus a public accuracy page reporting the nationwide median error rate split by on-market vs off-market. (The specific figures commonly cited — roughly 1.9% on-market and 7.5% off-market — are UNVERIFIED here; I did not fetch Zillow's own page. The established mechanism is the *practice* of publishing your own error rate, which is what matters.) Money comes from the adjacent marketplace — agent leads — never from the estimate.

**Why it worked:** Publishing your own error rate converts a criticizable guess into a measured instrument: the obvious attack ('it's often wrong') is pre-answered, quantified, and thereby defanged. Covering every home made Zillow the default destination for a question nobody else had claimed. And because the money sits in the adjacent transaction, the estimate never has to be sold, so it can stay free, universal, and unconflicted.

**For Muntin:** Muntin already runs the harder version — an 80% nominal band that realized 77% across 79,086 scored reads, published raw and un-tuned, 'misses included' — but it is a stat strip on `/cost-index/`, not a destination. That is the difference between having evidence and having a citation. Give it a permanent named URL (`/cost-index/track-record/`) with the full history, the named misses, the calibration chart, and a fixed citation string. It is the single artifact that converts 'trust us' into 'check us,' and it is the page a fact-checker will ask for before an editor permits a citation to a site they have never heard of.

### LIBOR → SOFR (the failure case)
**What:** LIBOR was submitter-fed: panel banks self-reported rates they could not always evidence with transactions. After the manipulation scandal, the FCA announced in July 2017 it would stop compelling submissions after 2021; the remaining USD settings ceased publication on a representative basis after 2023-06-30 (verified). Its replacement, SOFR, is computed by the New York Fed from actual overnight repo transactions.

**Why it worked:** As a death: the cause was not a single bad print. It was that the ARCHITECTURE admitted a self-interested party into the number. Once that was demonstrated, no governance layer, no oversight committee and no methodology revision could restore it — a benchmark cannot outlive proof that someone with a position could move it. The successor won on architecture alone: transactions, not opinions.

**For Muntin:** Muntin's methodology already names this exact risk and forecloses it — 'No submitters… so no one the index reports on can shade the number; the submitter-capture risk that an assessed, contributor-fed benchmark must manage is structurally absent here.' That sentence is the most valuable prose on the site and it sits inside a subsection. Two moves: (1) promote it to a headline claim with its own anchor and citation line; (2) constitutionalize it as an ADR, because the single most tempting future revenue idea — letting distributors or operators contribute delivered prices to enrich coverage — is precisely the LIBOR architecture, and it will look reasonable on the day cash is short.

### Levels.fyi / Glassdoor
**What:** Free, granular, crowd-sourced compensation data assembled an audience of exactly the people employers pay to reach. Monetization runs through the counterparty: employer data offerings, recruiting, and negotiation services. (Third-party revenue figures exist but are UNVERIFIED and not load-bearing here.)

**Why it worked:** Free data with high personal stakes attracts a self-selecting, high-intent audience, and that audience's counterparty has budget. The integrity condition is that the counterparty buys ACCESS to the audience and to aggregates — never the ability to change a number about themselves.

**For Muntin:** **This one does not transfer, and saying so is the point.** In food the counterparty to a restaurant operator is the distributor and the manufacturer — and taking their money is exactly what `/cost-index/methodology/` forecloses: 'No vendor feed, no sponsorship, no pay-to-be-listed.' Any advisor who proposes 'get Sysco/US Foods/PFG to sponsor the index' is proposing the one trade that ends the company's differentiation. What DOES transfer is the shape: find a party with budget and no ability to influence a print. In this market that is restaurant groups, the bookkeepers and accountants who serve independents, and lenders/insurers underwriting restaurants — none of whom appear anywhere in the index's data and none of whom can be favored by a reading.

### Wikipedia (as citation infrastructure, not a marketing target)
**What:** Notability requires significant coverage in reliable, INDEPENDENT SECONDARY sources; industry directories and primary data repositories explicitly do not count, and paid media placements are explicitly not usable to build a notability case (verified). So the citation stack is order-forced: a journalist cites you first → the encyclopedia entry becomes possible → downstream writers and LLM answer engines inherit the citation from the encyclopedia and the press.

**Why it worked:** It is a ratchet, and each rung makes the next one cheaper. Critically, the ordering cannot be bought — which is why patient, cadence-driven indices (Big Mac, Case-Shiller) win it and marketing budgets do not. It also means all the leverage sits at rung one.

**For Muntin:** Muntin should not attempt a Wikipedia article — it would fail notability today and a self-authored attempt is a COI violation that becomes a story. It should engineer rung one exclusively: be trivially quotable by a working food journalist on deadline. The asset nobody else in this space offers is the license — CC0 means a reporter can republish the number and the chart with NO permission request, no legal review and no embargo. For a journalist on a two-hour deadline that is not a nice-to-have, it is the reason to use you instead of a source who requires an email.

### DataCite / Zenodo (the permanence layer)
**What:** DataCite DOIs make a dataset a first-class citable object with versioning. Zenodo — operated by CERN, free of charge — mints a DOI on every GitHub release plus a CONCEPT DOI that always resolves to the latest version, so a citer can point at 'this edition' or 'this index, always current' (verified).

**Why it worked:** A DOI is a promise of permanence. It answers the exact question a fact-checker asks before allowing a citation to an unfamiliar website — 'will this URL resolve in ten years, and will the number it shows still be the number we cited?' — and the versioned/concept split answers both halves at once. It is also the format academic and institutional citers already know how to consume.

**For Muntin:** Muntin is one form away. `cost-index/menu-pricing/study/datacite.json` is a complete, correct DataCite 4.x record sitting with `"doi": null` and an ORCID marked `TO-FILL`, with an honest header forbidding fabrication of either. Zenodo requires no membership fee, its GitHub-release hook is fully automatable, and an ORCID is free and takes ten minutes. Do it for the INDEX itself and not only the menu-pricing study: a concept DOI for 'the Muntin Restaurant Cost Index' plus a versioned DOI per monthly edition, wired to the existing append-only editions spine which already freezes exactly the right object.

## Recommendations
- **Ship a stipulated, dollar-denominated **reference plate** as the quotable headline number, alongside (not replacing) the existing 16-staple basket read.**
  - The Big Mac mechanic, and the one change that most raises citation probability. Today's headline — '−0.5%, the weighted basket of 16 restaurant staples, against its baseline window,' at 51% agreement — is a percentage of an abstraction over weights that `data/cost-basket-weights.json` itself calls 'INTERNAL JUDGMENT (label illustrative), NOT a sourced fact.' No editor prints that. A stated recipe priced in dollars from the already-published per-ingredient wholesale references is a definition plus arithmetic — it is HARDER to attack than the current composite, not softer, and it produces a one-sentence quote with a dollar sign in it. It also survives the weights file's own hard constraint ('never a national price level, never what restaurants pay') because a stipulated plate makes no claim about what anyone pays; it says what these exact quantities cost at these published references.
  - Agent-executable: Fully agent-executable. (1) `data/reference-plate.json` — a FROZEN, `_version`-stamped recipe (ingredient slug × quantity × unit) modeled on the existing frozen-weights pattern, with the same never-re-specify discipline in its `_doc`. (2) `scripts/build-reference-plate.mjs` — computes Σ via the parity-locked unit conversion, inherits the basket's coverage/withholding rules (if any component is withheld, the plate reports withheld-with-reason rather than substituting), emits `cost-index/reference-plate.json` + `.csv` and renders `/cost-index/plate/`. (3) Freeze the plate value into each `data/cost-index-editions.json` entry so the series is comparable and citable. (4) `scripts/check-reference-plate.mjs` in `--check` mode wired into `check-all` in the same commit, per the gate-coverage rule. Founder decides the recipe itself — that is a judgment call about what a representative plate is, and it must be frozen forever, so it is a five-minute decision he should personally own.
- **Repair the cadence before adding anything, and put an agent session on the monthly edition draft by Routine.**
  - This is the top killer on this panel and it is currently firing. Four editions exist in the spine, last dated 2026-07-06; `/cost-index/weekly/` publicly states the dispatch runs 'monthly on the first Tuesday since August 2026'; 2026-08-04 passed with no edition. Separately, 26 of 100 series are frozen 58–77 days. Every exemplar that survives — Big Mac since 1986, Case-Shiller, VIX — survives on unbroken cadence, and the reason is not discipline theater: a citer's first check is 'is this still being published,' and a visible gap answers no. Muntin also has the worst version of this failure, a *published promise* of a cadence it is not meeting, on the surface whose entire value is reliability. The existing 38-day-lag gate is doing its job and is being ignored, which per the audit is the company's structural pattern (26% audit close rate; zero closures from anyone working a list).
  - Agent-executable: Agent-executable to ~90%. Create a Routine firing on the 1st of each month into a fresh session with a standing prompt: run `build-cost-index-dispatch.mjs`, read the frozen edition snapshot and the driver catalog, and produce a complete draft edition on a branch with the fact-gate and all five cost-index gates green — never publishing. The founder's 2026-07-09 call reserves publication to a human and should stand; drafting is the labor, judgment is the founder's. Separately, an agent can triage the 26 frozen series and open a scoped issue naming which are recoverable from public sources versus which should be reclassified as withheld — reclassifying a stale series to 'withheld' is on-brand and is better than a stale print.
- **Mint the DOI. Concept DOI for the index, versioned DOI per edition, via Zenodo; register a free ORCID.**
  - The permanence rung, and it is one form away. The DataCite record is already written, correct, and honestly refusing to fabricate what it lacks (`"doi": null`, ORCID `TO-FILL`). Zenodo is free, CERN-operated, and its concept-DOI-plus-per-release model maps exactly onto the append-only editions spine that already exists. A DOI is what lets an institutional citer — a restaurant association's research desk, a trade-press fact-checker, a graduate student — cite Muntin without an internal argument about whether a website counts. It also gives AI answer engines a canonical, versioned identifier rather than a URL that may have changed.
  - Agent-executable: Mostly agent-executable, with two founder actions of ten minutes each: register an ORCID (personal identity, cannot be delegated) and authorize the Zenodo↔GitHub connection (an OAuth grant). Agents then handle: extending `datacite.json` generation to cover the index itself and each edition; a GitHub Actions step that cuts a tagged release per published edition so Zenodo mints automatically; back-filling the DOI into `cost-index/index.json`, `feed.json`, `methodology.json`, the per-edition Dataset JSON-LD across the 85 pages that already carry it, the 'Cite this edition' blocks, and `llms.txt`/`llms-full.txt`. Never fabricate a DOI in the interim — the file's own header already forbids it and it is correct.
- **Build `/cost-index/track-record/` — a permanent, named, linkable accuracy page — and `/press/` — a journalist-facing kit.**
  - Zillow's mechanic plus rung one of the citation ratchet. Muntin already publishes its own error rate raw and un-tuned ('80% nominal band, 77% realized across 79,086 scored reads, misses included'), which is rarer and braver than what most commercial indices do — and it is a stat strip, not a destination a fact-checker can be pointed at. Meanwhile there is no press surface anywhere on 1,368 pages. A working food journalist on deadline needs six things: the number in one sentence, the definition, a chart they can publish, the license, a citation string, and a human who answers.   - LIBOR's lesson is that an index dies from architecture, not from a bad print — and Muntin's methodology already contains the exact right sentence, buried. Two things follow. - **Keep the one-person disclosure on the methodology page, even though the positioning pivot wants it hidden everywhere else.**
  - A genuine and unresolved conflict in the repo. `founder-vision.md` records a decisive pivot — 'I don't want to give me a face… I worry that if people know it's just me, they'll discount the product' — while `/cost-index/methodology/` states the index 'is built by an independent one-person operation.' Different surfaces, different audiences, both honest.
  - Agent-executable: Founder decision, agent-implementable. - **Aim the citation claim at the vacuum that actually exists — per-ingredient and restaurant-framed — not at 'food prices,' which is already occupied.**
  - Verified this session: the National Restaurant Association publishes wholesale food price indicators, and USDA ERS's Food Price Outlook is the canonical forecast citation. Muntin will not displace either and should not try — a claim to be 'the' food price index invites an unwinnable comparison and, worse, would be an overclaim. A reporter writing about why a chicken sandwich costs more cannot cite BLS PPI for boneless chicken breast; their options today are a raw USDA AMS report or a vendor's press release. That is the sentence Muntin should own, and 37 of 100 ingredients being openly WITHHELD is part of the pitch, not a gap in it.
  - Agent-executable: Fully agent-executable: it is a positioning-and-copy pass across `/cost-index/`, `/cost-index/methodology/`, the press page, `llms.txt`, `llms-full.txt` and `feed-llm.json`, plus the JSON-LD `description` fields. The specific line worth engineering into every one of those surfaces is the differentiator no incumbent offers: the index says when it does not know. ## Antipatterns
- **Do not add a forecast, outlook, or predicted-price number to make the index more quotable.** This is the fastest possible route to press pickup and it is foreclosed by `/cost-index/methodology/` ('measured levels, measured directions… never a price forecast'). It is also already causing damage: the 2026-08-07 audit found the July dispatch asserting 'a faint downward lean / −0.145' while the live outlook artifact reads 'building / +0.055' — an inverted published claim on a page whose own cite drawer invites a stranger to check it. A forecast the index cannot stand behind is the credibility event that ends the whole thing.
- **Do not take distributor, manufacturer, or vendor sponsorship, and do not accept submitted delivered prices to enrich coverage.** The Levels.fyi / Glassdoor counterparty model does not transfer, because in food the counterparty is the party the index reports on. Submitted prices are literally the LIBOR architecture, and they will look reasonable on the day coverage gaps are frustrating and cash is short. The methodology's 'No submitters' clause is worth more than any revenue it forecloses.
- **Do not re-weight the basket, re-specify the plate, or re-anchor the baseline to make a number more interesting.** `data/cost-basket-weights.json` already warns against exactly this ('never re-weight to whatever data is densest, or the index becomes uncomparable across quarters'). Comparability is the only asset a young index accrues by waiting, and it is destroyed retroactively — one re-weight invalidates every prior citation. The Big Mac survives on forty years of the same burger.
- **Do not publish a national 'what restaurants pay' price level, and do not let the reference plate be presented as a menu price, a food-cost benchmark, or an average.** The weights file forbids the first explicitly; the second is the obvious way the plate idea gets corrupted. The plate must always read as 'these stated quantities at these published wholesale references,' never 'what a plate costs' — the stipulation is what makes it honest and what makes it unattackable.
- **Do not pursue a Wikipedia article, hire anyone to write one, or place sponsored content to build a notability case.** Wikipedia explicitly rejects paid placements and primary data repositories as notability evidence, and a self-authored or commissioned attempt is a COI violation that becomes its own bad story. The ratchet only turns from rung one; there is no purchasable shortcut.
- **Do not put an LLM anywhere near the index's commentary, driver text, or ingredient prose.** The `no-llm-ci.sh` guarantee in the product repo is the strongest single differentiator in either codebase precisely because it is falsifiable in thirty seconds. An LLM writing the market read would not violate that gate's letter and would gut its spirit the moment a journalist asked 'who writes the analysis' — and it would put fabrication risk directly upstream of the one surface where a fabrication is unsurvivable.
- **Do not fix a stale series by relaxing the freshness rule or carrying a last-good value forward silently.** 26 of 100 series are frozen 58–77 days and the landing page currently renders '−0.5%… as of 2026-08-04' while `data/cost-index.json`'s basket object carries `asOf: 2026-06-01` and the page's own fine print concedes '3 of 16 staples are holding last-good since 2026-06-01' — worth verifying and reconciling before that number is promoted to a citable headline. The brand-correct move for a stale series is to publish WITHHELD with the reason. Withholding is the product; a quietly aged print is the thing that ends it.
- **Do not launch the press page, the DOI, or the reference plate before the cadence is repaired.** Rung one of the citation ratchet is 'is this still being published.' Inviting journalists to a site that publicly promises a first-Tuesday monthly edition and last published 2026-07-06 spends the first impression on the weakest fact. Sequence is: cadence, then headline number, then permanence, then press.
- **Do not claim to be 'the restaurant food price index' or imply the category is unoccupied.** BLS PPI, USDA ERS Food Price Outlook and the National Restaurant Association's wholesale-food indicators already exist and are already cited. Overclaiming the category invites a comparison Muntin loses on institutional weight and wins on nothing — and it would be the rare marketing line the site's own fact gate could not catch, because it is a claim about the world rather than a number.
