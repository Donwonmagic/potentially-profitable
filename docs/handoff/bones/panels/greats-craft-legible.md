# Making Craft Legible — how SaaS converts quality into a purchasing signal

## Thesis
Muntin does not have a craft problem; it has a craft-transmission problem, and the two require opposite remedies. Every surface where craft is genuinely world-class here is one a stranger cannot reach: the product's empty states and error copy are Superhuman-grade ("A muntin holds many small panes in one window. Yours has none yet. That is exactly how it starts."; "The link is stale or we moved it without telling you. Both are our problem.") and live inside a product zero non-founder humans have ever used; the claims registry with its ✅/⏳/🔴 tiers is the most institution-projecting document in either repo and sits in a private `docs/` folder; and `no-llm-ci.sh` — the one claim in this category that is falsifiable by a stranger in thirty seconds — is named on **zero of 1,368 storefront pages** while 93 of those pages make the claim it proves. Meanwhile the surfaces a stranger *does* reach are transmitting the opposite signal: `/changelog/` is linked from 629 pages, has not moved since June 2026, and its own history is dominated by a retired web-design business; `404.html` recommends "Services & pricing." So the highest-return work in this panel is not producing new craft — it is **relocating existing craft to reachable surfaces and pairing every claim with its falsifier**. The second insight is about scale posture: institutions are recognized by their *obligations*, not their headcount — a company that dates its documents, versions its methodology, corrects itself in public, and never misses a stated cadence reads institutional at any size, which is exactly how Panic and 37signals projected weight without a team claim. That cuts both ways, and it is why the founder's fear of the solo discount is currently being realized by his own artifacts: **a missed obligation reads worse than an absent one** — it reads as a company that shrank. The binding constraint is therefore capacity, not ambition: retire every cadence one person plus agents cannot hold, and make an agent, not the founder, satisfy the ones that remain.

## Mechanisms
### Linear
**What:** Published a public changelog with product-launch fidelity — and, per Linear's own "Build in Public" page in the Linear Method, started publishing it *weekly before they had a significant user base*, explicitly because it reminded the team what happened each week and forced constant shipping. Each entry is written like a small release: what changed, why it matters, often with a visual.

**Why it worked:** It converts invisible engineering into a dated, cumulative, externally-legible record. A prospect who cannot inspect your code reads velocity and consistency as institutional health — a proxy signal that is cheap to read and expensive to fake. It is also self-enforcing: the artifact creates the cadence rather than reporting on one, which is why it worked pre-users.

**For Muntin:** Directly, and it is Muntin's single largest negative-value surface today. `/changelog/` is already linked from 629 pages — the distribution is built. But it holds 16 entries ending at "June 2026," mentions "studio" (the retired services line) 12 times against "Cost Index" twice, and so currently broadcasts *decay* at 629-page scale. Adapt the frequency: monthly, not weekly, because a broken cadence is worse than none at 13–26 founder-hours/month. Generate the draft from merged PRs and ADRs; the founder writes one paragraph of judgment.

### Stripe
**What:** Made documentation the primary marketing surface, organized not around feature coverage but around the shortest path to the reader's own first working result — a copy-pasteable request that returns a real object on their machine, in their language, in minutes.

**Why it worked:** The docs *were* the trial. The moment of value happened before any sales conversation, and the reader evangelized because the docs made them look competent to their own team. Crucially the mechanism was not comprehensiveness — it was **reproducibility**: the reader proved the claim themselves rather than being told it.

**For Muntin:** Muntin's Stripe-docs analogue is **not** the glossary (171 terms, definitional, an SEO asset) and only partly the methodology page (64KB, 18 sections — excellent, but written as a defense). The true analogue already exists as one buried section of that page: **"Reproduce a number yourself."** Combined with `/claims.json`, `sources.json`, per-ingredient `series.csv`, and the CC0 snapshots, Muntin can offer the exact Stripe moment — "copy this, and get our published number on your own machine from the government's data." No funded competitor can offer it, because their numbers are proprietary. Promote it from a section to a product.

### Linear
**What:** Published the Linear Method as a separate, named body of principles and practices — a manifesto about how software should be built — rather than only shipping a product that embodied it.

**Why it worked:** An opinion is a filter. It pre-qualifies buyers, converts the product's *constraints* into evidence of intent rather than gaps, and gives evangelists something to argue with colleagues about that is not a feature list. It works only because the product visibly obeys the method; a manifesto the product contradicts is a liability.

**For Muntin:** Yes — with a specific adaptation and a live warning. The warning: Muntin **already tried this and retired it** (`/method/`, the method-manifesto plus Workshop-Kit pages, retired 2026-07-28 and 301'd to `/cost-index/`). It failed because it was about the retired web-design line and because nothing enforced it. The adaptation that makes it work here: publish the opinion as a **numbered, versioned specification with a conformance checker**, not prose. `cost-index/methodology.json` is already versioned and 129 gates already implement the rules — so the method and the enforcement can be the same object. Each numbered rule links to the gate that enforces it and to a page that would fail it.

### Superhuman
**What:** Measured product-market fit with Sean Ellis's survey — "How would you feel if you could no longer use the product?" with a 40% "very disappointed" benchmark — then segmented on that cohort and rebuilt for them. Per First Round Review, the score moved 22% → 32% → 46% → 56% → 58% over roughly eighteen months of systematic iteration.

**Why it worked:** It made a felt, qualitative property legible and therefore optimizable. It also reframed roadmap arguments from taste debates into a search with a scoreboard, which is what let a small team behave like a disciplined institution.

**For Muntin:** The **instrument** transfers and should be pre-committed now; the **timing** does not. Muntin has zero confirmed users and the founder has never run the product end-to-end, so there is nobody to survey. What transfers today: write the measurement into the launch plan before launch, so the first cohort is instrumented from day one rather than retrofitted — and publish the commitment to measure it. Publishing "here is the number we will hold ourselves to, and we will print it whether or not we clear it" is itself an institutional signal, and it costs nothing before there are users.

### Superhuman
**What:** Ran a one-to-one, human-led concierge onboarding for every new user — a deliberately non-scalable ritual, roughly a half-hour of a real person's time per customer.

**Why it worked:** A costly signal. The expense *is* the message: a company that spends thirty minutes on you before you pay is asserting confidence in retention. It doubled as the qualitative research engine that fed the PMF work.

**For Muntin:** **This one does not transfer, and copying it would be the most expensive mistake available.** The founder's total capacity is 13–26 hours/month against ~53 hours/month of existing obligations — the audit measured roughly 3 of 31 maintenance-hours actually paid in the last 30 days. Twenty concierge onboardings would consume the entire annual discretionary budget and starve every trust artifact that is already overdue. Muntin's substitute for the costly signal is a *different* costly thing an agent workforce can produce endlessly and a funded competitor will not match — see the reproduction path and the published-refusals mechanisms.

### Panic
**What:** Published "The Panic Report" — a voluntary annual year-in-review from a private company that owes no one a public accounting, describing what shipped, what sold, what failed, and what is next. The practice runs back at least to a 2010 Year In Review and continues in the named Report form (2014, 2015).

**Why it worked:** Institutions are recognized by the obligations they voluntarily accept and reliably honor, not by headcount. Publishing an accounting nobody requires, on a stated cadence, in a measured register, reads as scale and confidence — it is the posture of an entity that expects to still be here next year. It never once requires stating how many people work there.

**For Muntin:** This is the precise answer to the founder's "don't discount me for being one person" fear, and Muntin has already built every *form* of it — a transparency report, a warrant canary, a revisions ledger, a versioned methodology, a changelog. The problem is that the audit found all of them overdue (canary 89 days, Q2 transparency 37 days past its own published date, changelog 40+ days). **An overdue obligation is a stronger negative signal than a missing one**, because it reads as retrenchment. The fix is subtraction plus automation: retire the cadences one person cannot hold, and make an agent session produce the drafts for the ones that stay.

### Basecamp / 37signals
**What:** A small team published books (Getting Real, Rework) and a named methodology (Shape Up) in a distinct house voice with editorial standing — writing as a publisher, not as a startup with a blog.

**Why it worked:** Publishing like an institution *is* institutional standing. A small team's opinions travel infinitely further than its headcount, and the register of the writing — declarative, edited, willing to be wrong in public — does the work that a team-size line cannot do honestly.

**For Muntin:** Muntin already has the honest construction for this and should stop treating it as a bookkeeping convention: library articles ship under **The Muntin Desk** with JSON-LD naming Don underneath. That is exactly the honest institutional byline — an editorial desk that exists, with a real human accountable, and no implied staff. Extend the register (dated, versioned, corrected in public) rather than the volume; the corpus is already 1,368 pages and more content is not the constraint.

### Ramp / Mercury
**What:** Financial-grade interface design as a trust proxy — tabular numerals, restrained neutral ramps, hairline depth, near-zero decoration, dense information rendered legibly — in categories where the buyer cannot audit the underlying rigor.

**Why it worked:** When the thing being sold is competence with money, visual restraint is a cheap-to-read proxy for expensive-to-verify discipline, because sloppiness in that register is disqualifying. It is a signal that works precisely because it is easy to fail.

**For Muntin:** Largely already achieved and the internal record is stale about it: the audit found the "two design languages" fork the founder-vision doc describes as open is in fact **closed** — the token spine is byte-identical and hash-locked across both repos. The remaining transfer is not more design work, it is *removing* the surfaces still rendering the retired register: `404.html` (53KB, recommending four retired web-design articles and "Services & pricing") and `/system/` ("The system behind the studio") — a full craft-legibility page built for the dead line, whose mechanism is right and whose subject is wrong.

### Stripe
**What:** Versioned the API, published a changelog of breaking changes, and documented a deprecation policy — so "we will not break your integration" stopped being a promise and became an inspectable mechanism with a version string.

**Why it worked:** It converts a trust claim into an artifact a skeptic can check without asking anyone. The buyer stops evaluating your sincerity and starts evaluating your record, which is a much easier sale for a company with no brand legacy.

**For Muntin:** Muntin is unusually far along and unusually quiet about it: `methodology.json` is already versioned with a change log, `revisions.json` is already an append-only ledger of ~10,294 corrections, and `calibration.json` publishes the index's own at-chance direction result. The missing piece is the **stated policy alongside the data** — what triggers a revision, what triggers a retraction, how a citer is notified — and a single reachable page that presents the three together as a record rather than as three JSON endpoints in `llms.txt`.

## Recommendations
- **Pair every trust claim on the storefront with a link to the artifact that falsifies it — starting with the no-LLM gate — and make the pairing a CI gate. Ship a `/verify/` page whose whole content is a thirty-second recipe a skeptic can run: clone the public repo, run `bash scripts/no-llm-ci.sh`, watch it exit 0; then add an `import openai` line and watch the build fail.**
  - This is the highest trust-per-unit-effort action available to Muntin, and it is currently at zero. 93 storefront pages make the language-model claim; `no-llm-ci.sh` is named on **0 of 1,368** pages; the entire site carries four GitHub links. The claim as currently stated is indistinguishable from every competitor's marketing assertion — the gate is what makes it a different *kind* of claim, and the gate is invisible. A buyer cannot inspect your code, but they can run one command, and the willingness to publish that command is itself the signal. It also survives the honesty constraint trivially, because it is the constraint.
  - Agent-executable: Fully agent-executable. (1) Build `/verify/index.html` (+ ES mirror) with the copy-paste recipe and the expected pass/fail output. (2) Write an injector that adds a "Check this yourself" drawer — reusing the existing `<details class="cite">` pattern — to the 93 pages carrying the claim, linking the exact file and line. (3) Write `scripts/check-claim-falsifier.mjs`: any page asserting no-LLM / determinism / CC0 must carry a link to its named artifact; register it in `check-all` per the repo's own gate-coverage rule. No founder time except approving the copy.
- **Delete the retired-line changelog history and restart `/changelog/` as the company's monthly public shipping record, agent-drafted from merged PRs and ADRs, with a 45-day freshness gate. Do it before there are users, not after.**
  - 629 pages already link here — the distribution is built and is currently pointed at a decay signal: 16 entries ending June 2026, "studio" 12× against "Cost Index" 2×, and `/methods/` telling readers this is where changes appear. This is the founder's feared solo-discount being manufactured by his own artifact. Linear's own Build-in-Public page settles the "but we have no users" objection directly: they published weekly *before* a significant user base, because the changelog creates the cadence rather than reporting on it. Monthly rather than weekly is the necessary adaptation — the audit measured roughly 3 of 31 maintenance-hours actually paid, so a weekly obligation would break, and a broken cadence is worse than none.
  - Agent-executable: Agent-executable end to end except one paragraph. Build `scripts/build-changelog.mjs` reading `git log` + merged PR titles + new ADR files since the last dated entry, grouped into shipped/fixed/published sections; add `scripts/check-changelog-fresh.mjs` at 45 days wired into `check-all` (the repo's existing pattern — the red *is* the reminder, exactly like `check-cost-index-dispatch-fresh.mjs`). Founder writes the one-paragraph lede per month; the agent writes the rest.
- **Publish the claims registry. Convert `docs/marketing-claims.md` into a public `/claims-registry/` carrying the ✅ verified tier and — this is the whole point — the ⏳ fenced tier: the claims Muntin does *not* yet make, why, and exactly what would make each sayable. Withhold only rows naming an unremediated security exposure.**
  - This is the most institution-projecting artifact in either repo and it is invisible. A table that says "we do not say 'files auto-delete' because the reaper is off by default; we will say it when it is armed and verified" is a signal no competitor will reproduce, because their marketing and legal functions exist to prevent exactly that publication. It answers the founder's scale worry without a headcount claim — only an entity with institutional confidence publishes its own fenced claims. It is also the cheapest possible expression of the brand: the company that will not print a number it cannot stand behind, printing the list of numbers it will not print.
  - Agent-executable: Agent-executable, with one founder decision. Agent: build `/claims-registry/` (+ ES) from the markdown; extend `check-claims.mjs` so every headline claim on any storefront or product surface must resolve to a registry row, closing the gap that let the July dispatch's cite drawer invite a verification it now fails. Founder decision (30 min): which 🔴 rows stay private. Default recommendation — publish the tense/dead-CTA rows, withhold anything naming an open security gap until closed.
- **Promote "Reproduce a number yourself" from a section of the methodology page to the Cost Index's front door: a generated per-ingredient reproduction block — source URL, report identifier, the arithmetic, the expected published number — for all 100 ingredients.**
  - This is Muntin's Stripe moment, and the answer to the panel's question about the real docs analogue: it is neither the glossary (definitional, SEO-shaped) nor the methodology page as currently written (a defense document, and per the founder's own emotional pivot the site should stop defending itself). Stripe's docs converted readers into evangelists because the reader produced the result themselves. Muntin can offer the same and no funded competitor can, because reproduction requires that your inputs be public — MarginEdge, Restaurant365 and Toast structurally cannot publish a recipe that reconstructs their numbers. It reframes the honesty machinery from an argument into an invitation, which is precisely the register shift the founder asked for.
  - Agent-executable: Fully agent-executable and high-volume, which is the point. Build `scripts/build-reproduce-pages.mjs` generating `/cost-index/reproduce/` plus a per-ingredient block on each of the 101 ingredient pages, sourced from `cost-index/sources.json` (already maps every reading to its government series by report identifier). Gate it: a reproduction block whose stated arithmetic does not yield the published number fails CI. Also fixes the embed problem in passing — 1 embed card exists across 101 ingredient dirs; generate the other 100 in the same builder.
- **Publish the method as a numbered, versioned specification whose every rule links to the CI gate that enforces it — not as a manifesto. Include the refusals as first-class content: the 37 of 100 ingredients where Muntin declines a posture, the `seasonalUseful:false` findings, the published at-chance direction result.**
  - Muntin genuinely has the strong opinion this panel asks about, and Linear proves a published opinion is a buyer filter rather than an academic exercise. But Muntin already shipped a method-manifesto and retired it on 2026-07-28 — so the pattern has failed here once, for two reasons worth not repeating: it described a retired business, and nothing enforced it. The version that works is the one where the method and the enforcement are the same object, which Muntin uniquely can build because `methodology.json` is already versioned and 129 gates already implement the rules. The refusals are the load-bearing part: a competitor will publish a method, and none will publish the list of questions their method refuses to answer.
  - Agent-executable: Agent-executable. Generate the spec page from `methodology.json` + a new rule↔gate mapping file, so each numbered rule renders with its enforcing script, its current pass state, and an example page that would fail it. Generate the refusals section directly from `data/cost-index.json` coverage fields rather than hand-writing it, so it stays true as coverage changes. Founder writes the preamble stating the opinion in his own words — one sitting.
- **Rebuild `404.html` and retire or repoint `/system/`. Both currently render the retired web-design company to live traffic.**
  - The 404 is a high-traffic craft surface every serious software company treats as owned real estate, and Muntin's is 53KB recommending "How much does a custom restaurant website cost?", "Wix vs. custom for restaurants", and "Services & pricing." A prospect who lands there — including from any of the 301s created by the July retirement — is told, on the exact surface where craft is cheapest to demonstrate, that this is a website agency. `/system/` is the same error at higher stakes: it is a genuinely well-built "how this was made" page, the right mechanism entirely, built for the dead line. The mechanism should be kept and re-aimed at the Cost Index and the Ledger.
  - Agent-executable: Trivially agent-executable, hours not days. Rebuild the 404 against the funnel: a search box (pagefind is already configured in `pagefind.yml`), the Cost Index, the tools, the waitlist. Rewrite `/system/` as "how the Cost Index is built" reusing its existing structure and craft. Extend `check-positioning-drift.mjs`'s root list to cover `404.html` and `/system/` — this is the fourth instance of the repo's own documented "a scanner is only as good as its root list" lesson.
- **Move the product's best copy onto a public surface: extend `/ledger/demo/` to walk the day-one empty state, a `needs_review` hold, and a failed read — showing the product refusing to post a number it is not sure of.**
  - The single most wasted asset in either repo. `copy.ts` is 4,408 lines (3,518 ES), gated to Flesch-Kincaid ≤ 7 with a banned-phrase checker, and contains writing at Linear/Superhuman grade — "A muntin holds many small panes in one window. Yours has none yet. That is exactly how it starts."; "The link is stale or we moved it without telling you. Both are our problem." Zero non-founder humans have ever seen any of it, and the founder has never run the product end to end. Meanwhile the honesty promise is currently *argued* in prose across the storefront, which the founder explicitly said to stop doing. A demo where the product visibly holds a number back is that promise delivered as an experience rather than a defense — and `needs_review` blocking `/post` is a real shipped behavior, not a mock-up, so it survives the fact gate.
  - Agent-executable: Agent-executable; `/ledger/demo/` already exists at 186KB and is already re-skinned to the app token language. Add three walked states sourced from the real `copy.ts` strings so the demo cannot drift from the product. One founder task this cannot substitute for, and it is the most valuable hour in the audit: run the product end to end once on a real Tacombi invoice and become the first user — no agent can do that, and everything above is calibrated on writing he has not yet watched work.
- **Reframe the 645 Spanish pages from an unadvertised maintenance cost into a stated institutional commitment, with a public parity receipt — and resist the temptation to cut them.**
  - Everything is on the table per the founder, so this needs saying plainly: EN↔ES parity across the corpus is the clearest example of the high-effort-signal-a-funded-competitor-will-not-match that this panel was asked to find. Restaurant back-of-house in the DMV is heavily Spanish-speaking; a funded competitor will localize the *app* because that is where revenue is, and will not translate a 171-term glossary and 40 library articles because the ROI never pencils for a content team on salary. For an agent workforce it is near-marginal-cost. Today it is invisible — a gate obligation (`check-locale-parity.mjs`) rather than a claim. Stated as a commitment with a live parity number, it reads as institutional reach, which is exactly the register the founder wants, and it is entirely honest because the pages exist.
  - Agent-executable: Fully agent-executable. Generate a parity receipt page from `check-locale-parity.mjs`'s own output (live EN/ES counts, what is covered, what is not — the honest gaps included), surface a small bilingual affordance on EN pages, and add it to the `/verify/` family. Warning to respect: the Cost Index dispatch is EN-only today and `*_es` driver fields exist but are unrendered — the receipt must state that gap rather than paper over it, or it breaches the constraint it is meant to demonstrate.

## Antipatterns
- Do NOT copy Superhuman's concierge onboarding. It is a per-user founder-hour cost and Muntin's founder has 13–26 hours/month against ~53 hours/month of standing obligations, of which the audit measured roughly 3 of 31 maintenance-hours actually paid last month. Twenty onboardings would consume the entire discretionary year and starve the trust artifacts — canary, transparency report, changelog, dispatch — that are already overdue. Adopt the PMF *instrument*; refuse the ritual.
- Do NOT copy Linear's weekly changelog cadence, only its existence. At current capacity a weekly obligation will break, and a broken public cadence is a stronger negative signal than no cadence — it reads as a company that shrank, which is precisely the discount the founder is trying to avoid. Monthly, agent-drafted, gate-reminded. The same logic retires rather than rescues any obligation that cannot be met: retiring the warrant canary honestly is better than a canary 89 days unsigned.
- Do NOT build a Stripe Press or an Increment. A magazine is a prestige play funded by headcount and margin, and Muntin's problem is emphatically not a shortage of content — it has 1,368 pages, 40 library articles, a 171-term glossary, and 645 Spanish pages, with four waitlist forms across all of it. Adding a publication would deepen the exact imbalance the audit found: distribution machinery built and disconnected.
- Do NOT stage an Apple-style launch narrative for a product with zero confirmed users that the founder has never run end to end. Product narrative works when the artifact exists and the claims are already true; here it would require asserting an experience nobody has had, which is the overclaim trap and would breach the fact gate the moment any specific was added.
- Do NOT reach for Figma's multiplayer-as-demo or Arc's novelty-as-positioning. Both depend on a live product with multiple simultaneous humans in it. The honest Muntin equivalent is the *refusal* demo — the product visibly declining to post a number it is not sure of — which is a real shipped behavior (`needs_review` blocks `/post`) rather than a staged one.
- Do NOT ship any social-proof surface: no logo wall, no testimonials, no case study, no 'trusted by N restaurants', no usage counter, no 'join X operators' on the waitlist. There are zero confirmed users. Fabricating one would be caught trivially and would detonate the entire differentiator; even an implied 'select customers' framing crosses the line. Substitute costly signals that are true today — the reproduction path, the published refusals, the falsifiable gate.
- Do NOT construct a masthead, role titles, or 'our engineers / our analysts / our data team' language, even as generic marketing register. The founder-vision doc already draws this line correctly and it must hold under the capability-projection pressure this panel creates: declining to state headcount is honest, implying a staff is not, and the 'Muntin Desk' byline with Don named in JSON-LD is already the correct honest construction. Anything that names or numbers people who do not exist is out of bounds at any price.
- Do NOT publish the internal audit's raw severity counts as marketing ('235 findings, 118 critical, 26% close rate'). Radical-transparency-as-content is a real mechanism but this is the wrong artifact for it — it reads as self-flagellation rather than confidence, it invites a reading of institutional weakness that is the opposite of the goal, and the audit's own header forbids citing it publicly without independent sourcing. Publish the *forward-looking* registry (what we do not yet claim and what would make it sayable), not the backward-looking defect list.
- Do NOT resurrect the retired `/method/` manifesto's form. A prose manifesto with nothing enforcing it already failed here once and was 301'd away on 2026-07-28. If the method is republished it must be a numbered spec each of whose rules names the gate that enforces it — otherwise it is an assertion competing with every competitor's assertion, on the one axis where Muntin has an actual structural advantage.
