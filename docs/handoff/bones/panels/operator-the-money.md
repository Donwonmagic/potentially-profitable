# The Money — restaurant P&L reality and what a cost tool is actually worth

## Thesis
Muntin is priced as if the constraint were the customer's wallet, and it is not. The value arithmetic is overwhelming and revenue-invariant: one percentage point of food cost equals one percent of sales, which at a 3% net margin is a third of the entire year's profit at any revenue level — against $228/year for the $19 founding rate, the tool needs to move food cost by roughly seven basis points to pay for itself. The company already computes that dollar figure in `cost-watch-impact.ts`, sets its own materiality floor at $25/week (5.7x the annual subscription), and then prints none of it: the `/ledger` landing page makes no dollar claim anywhere, so the buyer cannot see the 44x he is being offered. The real barrier is not $19; it is total cost of ownership — a monthly count that costs $100-150 of floor labor, and an engine that reads a novel invoice at ~75% first-try, so early customers pay in correction time at a multiple of the sticker. That means cutting price does nothing and raising it costs almost nothing: independents demonstrably pay $350-500/location for a measured food-cost number, so $19 is a self-imposed floor, not a market ceiling. Two timing facts nobody has priced: restaurant sales bottom in January and February (computed from the repo's own Census MARTS data), and a 2026-11-13 GA plus a "three months free" founding term lands the very first charge in mid-February — the second-worst cash month of the year, with no annual plan in existence. Meanwhile the largest and angriest controllable cost line, the 15-30% delivery rake, appears in zero lines of product code even though the storefront publishes the honest margin walk. And the number the paid tier exists to print is currently biased low, because the Purchases leg excludes freight, surcharges, tax and un-canonicalized SKUs. Price is the least broken thing here; the missing pieces are a visible dollar, a correct dollar, and a calendar.

## Portrait
- **[well-established]** The honest P&L shape, from the storefront's own registered claims: food and beverage 28-35% of food sales (QSR/fast-casual ~25-30%, casual ~30-34%, fine dining ~34-40%); prime cost (food + beverage + labor) 55-65% of sales, full-service 60-65%, ~60% a common target; occupancy and other fixed costs another 12-14 points on a typical DMV independent; what is left is low single digits. The industry-wide corroboration is that 42% of operators reported their restaurant was not profitable in 2025, up from 29% in 2024, while 71% plan to raise menu prices in 2026 — near-universal price increases and a rising share still underwater.
  - Basis: data/sourced-claims.json: food_cost_target_band_2026 (Restaurant365, verified 2026-06-18), prime_cost_healthy_band (Restaurant365, verified 2026-07-23), nra_2026_operators_unprofitable_2025 (NRA 2026 State of the Restaurant Industry), popmenu_2026_operators_raising_prices (Popmenu, n=328, fielded Jan 2026). The 12-14 points of fixed cost is Don's own figure in library/third-party-delivery-economics/, labeled illustrative there.
- **[inference]** The value arithmetic, computed and revenue-invariant. One percentage point of food cost = 1% of sales. Against a 3% net margin that is 33% of the year's profit; against 5%, 20% — and both ratios hold at ANY revenue, because numerator and denominator both scale with sales. In absolute dollars: $5,000/yr at $500K revenue, $10,000 at $1M, $15,000 at $1.5M. Against $228/yr ($19/mo) the ratios are 22x / 44x / 66x. Break-even is a food-cost cut of 0.152% / 0.076% / 0.051% of annual food spend — at $1M revenue, $228 out of a $300,000 food bill, or roughly seven basis points. Even a 1% RELATIVE cut in food spend (not a point of sales) returns 6.6x-19.7x the price.
  - Basis: Arithmetic computed by me on the two registered bands above (food cost 30% of sales as midpoint; net margin 3-5% as the residual after prime 60-63% + fixed 12-14). Net-margin band is the weakest input — it is a residual and a rule of thumb, not a sourced statistic.
- **[verified]** The company computes the dollar value of a caught price hike, sets its floor at 5.7x the annual price, and shows none of it. `apps/api/src/lib/cost-watch-digest.ts` sets DEFAULT_MATERIALITY_FLOOR_CENTS = 2500 — a price hike must add $25/week ($1,300/yr) to the operator's own measured spend before it is even worth an email. `cost-watch-impact.ts` computes that figure honestly (incremental, not gross: windowSpend x delta/(1+delta), spend-weighted off the operator's own reviewed purchases) and its own header states: 'The email never prints it.' The `/ledger` landing page contains no dollar claim of any kind — it sells mechanism (read, file, flag) and never once tells the reader what a flag is worth. So the product's minimum-worth-mentioning event is worth 5.7 years of subscription, and the buyer is never told.
  - Basis: Read directly: apps/api/src/lib/cost-watch-digest.ts:213, apps/api/src/lib/cost-watch-impact.ts (full file), potentially-profitable/ledger/index.html.
- **[verified]** Restaurant cash is sharply seasonal and the trough is January-February — computed from a public-domain dataset already vendored in the repo. Census MARTS food services and drinking places (NAICS 722), median NSA/SA ratio 2022-2025: Jan 0.913, Feb 0.917, Mar 1.043, Apr 1.012, May 1.062, Jun 1.024, Jul 1.028, Aug 1.035, Sep 0.980, Oct 1.005, Nov 0.952, Dec 1.012. That is roughly 15 points peak-to-trough at the NATIONAL AGGREGATE, which understates a single restaurant because aggregation smooths idiosyncratic swings. January is also the low on a per-calendar-day basis (0.905), so it is a real demand trough and not a short-month artifact.
  - Basis: I computed the monthly medians myself from data/marts-sales.json (138 months, Census MARTS via FRED, public domain, fetched 2026-07-24), excluding 2020-2021. The file's own _doc labels it a demand backdrop, never a forecast.
- **[inference]** The launch calendar puts the first charge in the worst month of the restaurant year, and there is no annual plan to route around it. GA is locked at 2026-11-13; the founding offer is 'three months free, then $19 a month per location.' Three months from mid-November is mid-February — the second-lowest month in the seasonal series above, immediately after the lowest. `apps/api/src/lib/stripe-tiers.ts` states plainly: 'Yearly tiers are NOT in Phase 1A scope,' and no `trial_period_days` is ever set anywhere in billing, so the three-months-free term is not even implemented.
  - Basis: runbooks/launch-plan-2026-11-13.md; data/sourced-claims.json ledger_founding_offer_2026; apps/api/src/lib/stripe-tiers.ts header and TIERS map; apps/web/lib/pricing-constants.ts founding entry ('no trial_period_days is ever set'). The 'worst month' claim rests on my computation above.
- **[inference]** Total cost of ownership is 6-10x the sticker, and none of that excess is price. The count is the only manual input to real food cost (ADR-006), and even with the value-count 80/20 default it is a physical sweep by station; at the Montgomery County QCEW average weekly wage of $626 for NAICS 7225 (Q4 2025), a two-person half-shift count runs roughly $100-150 of floor labor per month — $1,200-1,800/yr against a $228 subscription. On top of that, the extraction engine reads a NOVEL invoice at ~75% first-try; a learned per-vendor template lifts that same vendor to ~95%+, and blended first-try F1 across the install base is projected at ~83% / ~89% / ~92% at 10 / 50 / 200 covered layouts. Early customers therefore pay in correction time. This is the reason a $6 price cut would change nothing.
  - Basis: docs/ux/decisions/ADR-006-count-entry-ux.md; data/qcew-wages.json (BLS QCEW, Montgomery County MD, 1,730 establishments, 27,083 avg employment, $626 avg weekly wage Q4 2025); docs/plans/first-try-reliability-roadmap.md §1 (75% / 95% / blended 83-89-92 anchors). The $100-150 labor figure is my arithmetic on the QCEW average, not a measured count time.
- **[verified]** The posted price has no consistent unit of value, and one posted limit is enforced nowhere. Solo ($25) and Team ($60) are declared `per_account` in `pricing-constants.ts`; the founding rate ($19) is `per_location`. No tier caps locations anywhere in the code — `grep` for a location cap returns nothing. So a three-location founding member pays $57/month while the list Solo tier is $25/month with unlimited locations: the 'founding discount' becomes a penalty at two locations and up. Separately, `copy.ts` posts 'Solo. $25/month. Up to 150 invoices/month' and no invoice quota exists in `ingest.ts` or anywhere else — a fourth posted-price-versus-engine drift alongside the three `pricing-constants.ts` already documents.
  - Basis: apps/web/lib/pricing-constants.ts POSTED_PRICES; apps/api/src/lib/stripe-tiers.ts TIERS (maxSeats only, no location or document cap); apps/web/lib/copy.ts tierSoloBody; grep for quota/location-cap across apps/api/src returned no enforcement.
- **[verified]** Muntin's own unit economics permit flat-rate pricing and forbid per-invoice pricing — this is the one durable cost advantage and per-invoice billing would throw it away. Published variable cost per invoice sums to about half a cent (vendor match ~$0.002, storage <$0.001, record ~$0.001, workers ~$0.0005, KMS ~$0.0003); fixed compute is the always-warm docling pool at ~$34/month effective plus a near-zero Neon bill. Gross margin at $19/location is essentially 100%, and infra breaks even at roughly two or three paying locations. The real marginal cost is founder-hours per VENDOR LAYOUT — a community template requires a real reference invoice A and a held-out real invoice B run through production docling, never hand-typed. Costs therefore scale with distinct broadliner layouts, not with invoices or customers, which is a genuine economy of scale and an argument for concentrating early customers on the same distributors (a DMV cluster) rather than scattering.
  - Basis: apps/web/app/(marketing)/about/cost/page.tsx (cost rows + founder-bill notes: Fly $200/yr commit + overage, ~$34/mo effective; Neon $0.84 for a partial month); docs/plans/first-try-reliability-roadmap.md 'Per-template cost (realistic)'; runbooks/proposals/differentiation/competitor-pain-points.md §A ('competitors built per-document pricing because their unit costs are LLM-call-per-document').
- **[verified]** The delivery rake is the largest angry controllable line, the storefront already owns the honest math, and the product is completely blind to it. Registered claims: DoorDash Marketplace Basic 15% / Plus 25% / Premier 30%; Uber Eats 30% Uber-delivered, 15% self-delivered, 7-10% pickup (effective 2026-03-11); Grubhub marketing 5-20% plus 3.05% + $0.30 processing plus 10% delivery. Don's own margin walk on a real $42 average ticket: the restaurant keeps $7.73 (18.4%) through Marketplace Plus versus $12.15 (28.9%) direct even after eating an $8.50 last-mile fee — a $4.42 per-order swing, worth $4,243/yr on 2,400 platform orders at a 40% direct-conversion rate. A restaurant running $150K of platform gross pays $45,000/yr in commission — roughly 200x the annual price of Muntin. A grep for doordash / ubereats / grubhub across `apps/`, `services/` and `packages/` returns ZERO files: the product cannot read a delivery statement, so the wedge exists only in content.
  - Basis: data/sourced-claims.json doordash_marketplace_tiers, uber_eats_published_rates, grubhub_published_rates (all verified 2026-05-14); library/third-party-delivery-economics/ margin walk; blog/30-days-after-leaving-doordash-restaurant-case-study/. The zero-hit grep across the product repo is mine.
- **[verified]** The food-cost number the paid tier exists to print is currently biased LOW, which is the worst possible direction for a product sold on 'it does not guess.' `inventory-purchases-store.ts` computes Purchases as SUM(round(unit_price_cents * quantity)) over non-provisional observations WHERE pc.catalog_item_id IS NOT NULL AND lio.base_uom IS NOT NULL. Freight, fuel surcharge, delivery fees, sales tax and every SKU not yet canonicalized fall outside the sum. That understated Purchases flows straight into Beginning + Purchases − Ending = Usage and into food cost %, while `inventory-reconcile.ts` reports coverage as item counts (total / counted / estimated), which structurally cannot detect a dollar gap. An operator can be shown a flattering food-cost percentage that will not tie to what their bookkeeper calls food purchases.
  - Basis: apps/api/src/lib/inventory-purchases-store.ts inWindow (SQL read directly); apps/api/src/lib/inventory-reconcile.ts coverage shape. First surfaced by the audit's completeness critic; I re-read the SQL and confirm the WHERE clause and the omitted line types.

## Implications
- **Print the dollar. Surface `hikeImpactPerWeekCents` as an annualized figure on the flag, in the digest, and on the `/ledger` page ('this one line is running $X/week above its own history — $Y a year'), and put a running 'caught this year' total in the app. Add one honest sentence to the pricing page: the product does not bother the operator about anything smaller than $25/week, and the price is $19/month.**
  - The company computes the value in cost-watch-impact.ts, sets its floor at 5.7x the annual price, and its own header says the email never prints it. The landing page makes zero dollar claims. A buyer cannot act on a 44x value ratio he is never shown — and this is the cheapest change on the list, because the number already exists and is already gated for honesty.
- **Re-time the money calendar. Change the founding term from 'three months free' to free-until-a-date that lands the first charge in the seasonal peak (May-August, not February), and ship an annual plan billed in the peak before GA rather than deferring it to Phase 2. Never dun or auto-renew in January.**
  - Sales bottom in January (0.913) and February (0.917) against a May peak of 1.062 — computed from the repo's own Census MARTS data. A 2026-11-13 launch plus three months free lands the very first invoice in mid-February, and stripe-tiers.ts says yearly tiers are out of scope, so there is no instrument to route around it. Nobody has considered restaurant seasonality anywhere in either repo.
- **Fix the price UNIT before GA. Choose one basis and enforce it in code: either make every tier per-location with a location cap on Solo and Team, or drop the per-location founding rate. Then either enforce the posted 150-invoice Solo cap or delete it from copy.**
  - Solo and Team are per_account with no location cap anywhere in the code, while the founding rate is per_location — so a three-location founding member pays $57/month against a $25 list tier with unlimited locations, and the discount becomes a penalty at two locations. The 150-invoice cap is posted in copy.ts and enforced nowhere. pricing-constants.ts already documents three posted-versus-billable drifts; these are the fourth and fifth.
- **Split the price and raise the top. Keep a $19-25 Decoder tier (read, file, flag, export — no count required) as the acquisition product, and price the measured-food-cost tier against MarginEdge rather than against a browser extension: $79-149 per location. Do not lower any price.**
  - Independents demonstrably pay $350-500/location for a measured food-cost number (MarginEdge, Restaurant365), so $19 is a self-imposed floor rather than a market ceiling — and the value arithmetic supports far more. Meanwhile the real cost of the count tier to the customer is $1,200-1,800/yr of floor labor, so the subscription price is not what is stopping anyone; a higher price on the tier that does the work is more credible, not less.
- **Foreclose per-invoice and percentage-of-savings pricing in writing, as an ADR, so no future session proposes either.**
  - Variable cost is about half a cent per invoice against LLM competitors whose COGS is a model call per document — flat-rate is the one thing competitors structurally cannot copy, and per-invoice pricing would discard it. Percentage-of-savings is foreclosed by the honesty contract itself: fair-price-gap.js states on the record that wholesale data alone cannot prove overpayment, so the product cannot measure a saving it caused and therefore cannot bill on one.
- **Fix the Purchases denominator and ship a DOLLAR-coverage metric before charging for food cost: report what fraction of the period's invoiced dollars actually landed in Purchases, include freight/surcharge/tax lines or state their exclusion on the surface, and withhold the food-cost % below a coverage threshold.**
  - The number the paid tier exists to print is currently biased low — freight, fuel surcharge, tax and un-canonicalized SKUs are outside the SQL sum, and coverage is reported as item counts that cannot detect a dollar gap. A flattering food-cost percentage that does not tie to the bookkeeper's is the single fastest way to destroy the 'it does not guess' brand, and it is the one defect that gets worse the moment someone pays.
- **Sell into the bookkeeper's invoice, not a new budget line on the restaurant's P&L. Frame and price the Accountant tier around hours removed from a monthly bookkeeping bill, and make the client-side subscription firm-billed at a discount rather than free-riding on a flat firm fee.**
  - Back-office cost software is the first discretionary line in an independent's stack — POS/payments, payroll and the bookkeeper are all non-negotiable, so Muntin displaces nothing and must create its own budget. At $19 it does not need a new line: one hour a month off a bookkeeping bill covers it several times over at any plausible rate. This also fixes the flat-firm-fee problem the GTM panel identified, where the cheapest channel monetizes worst.
- **Decide the delivery-rake question explicitly: either build a delivery-statement reader (DoorDash/Uber Eats/Grubhub payout statements are structured documents the deterministic engine could read) or state plainly on the storefront that Muntin reads vendor invoices and does not touch platform statements.**
  - The rake is 15-30% of gross on the affected channel — roughly 200x the annual price of the product on a restaurant with $150K of platform volume — and it is the cost line operators are angriest about. The storefront already publishes the honest $42 margin walk and a delisting playbook, but a grep across the entire product repo returns zero references to any delivery platform. Content is promising a competence the product does not have, which is the exact failure mode this company's brand cannot survive.

## Unknowns (founder interview script)
- Tacombi's own numbers, which Don has and nobody has written down: actual food cost % at each location, invoices received per month, count of distinct vendors, and how many minutes a count actually takes with the 80/20 value-count. Every TCO estimate in this portrait is arithmetic on a public wage average; one shift settles it.
- What Don's own bookkeeper or accountant charges per month, how many of those hours are invoice keying, and whether that person would say out loud that a tool like this removes an hour. This is the whole budget theory for the $19 price and it rests on nothing.
- Ask five DMV operators: what was the last software subscription you cancelled, what did it cost, and in what month did you cancel it? This tests the January-February churn hypothesis directly and costs five conversations. If cancellations do not cluster in the trough, the calendar recommendation is wrong.
- Take one real Muntin food-cost output to a real restaurant bookkeeper and ask whether it ties to what they call food purchases. If freight and surcharges are the gap, they will say so in thirty seconds — and that answer decides whether the count tier is sellable at any price.
- Get one independent operator's itemized monthly software statement: POS, payments processing rate, payroll, scheduling, reservations, delivery commissions, accounting. Nobody has ever seen the real stack this product must fit inside, so 'where would Muntin's line item rank' is currently a guess.
- What share of revenue actually flows through delivery platforms for a target operator, and which commission tier are they on? The registered rates are public; the exposure is not. This decides whether the rake is a wedge or a distraction.
- Would an operator pay MORE for the measured food-cost number than for the invoice ledger — and by how much? Ask the question with two prices on a page. This is the only way to test whether the $350-500 ceiling is reachable from a self-serve motion or only from a sales-led one.
- Is the operator already in a group purchasing organization? GPOs claim 10-30% savings on food and supply costs, which is an order of magnitude larger than anything Muntin measures. If the answer is usually yes, Muntin's honest pitch changes from 'save money' to 'verify what you were promised.'
- Does an operator experience a monthly physical count as normal work or as a new burden? The count is the manual input the entire food-cost tier depends on, and if operators will not do it monthly, the paid tier's core number never gets produced regardless of price.
- What did the last operator who said no say no to — the price, the count, the trust, or the fact that they already have a spreadsheet that works? Zero confirmed users means zero recorded objections, and the objection is worth more than any of the arithmetic above.
