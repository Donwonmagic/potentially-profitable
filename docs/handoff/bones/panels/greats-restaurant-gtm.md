# How Software Actually Reaches Independent Restaurants — channel, buying behavior, and willingness to pay

## Thesis
Muntin's posted price sets its channel, and almost nobody has noticed. At $19–25/month per location, a customer is worth roughly $450–600 over two years, and Toast — the company that actually knows what it costs to acquire an independent restaurant — spends thousands to tens of thousands per location using field and inside sales (aggregator figures of ~$10K and ~$19K/location circulate; treat the exact number as UNVERIFIED, but the order of magnitude is not in doubt). That arithmetic forecloses every human-touch acquisition path Muntin could imagine, and it does so permanently: no amount of craft makes a $19 subscription pay for a salesperson, a POS dealer's residual, or an association sponsorship. What remains is the small set of channels where one relationship delivers many restaurants — and in this vertical there is exactly one that is proven, cheap, and already half-built inside Muntin's own repo: the bookkeeper. QuickBooks' ProAdvisor Preferred Pricing (firm-billed, 30% ongoing discount, one itemized monthly bill) and Dext's Practice plans (per-client-per-month, ten-client minimum, tiering down with volume, 12,000+ firms) are the two working proofs that accounting firms will carry a back-office tool into dozens of small businesses at near-zero vendor cost. Muntin has an Accountant tier priced at $150 + $30/seat, an `accountant` membership role, a `hasCockpit: true` entitlement flag, and a shipping weekly accountant digest — and no `/cockpit` route anywhere in `apps/web`, meaning the entitlement is sold and the surface is missing. Two live dislocations point the same direction: Hubdoc's 8 May 2026 shutdown left Xero's replacement (Xero Files) storing documents with no OCR and no extraction at all, which is a *bookkeeper's* emergency, not an owner's; and Toast's AI Invoice Scanning is a beta enhancement to **Toast Retail's receiving workflow**, not a bundled AP product shipped to ~120K restaurants — a correction Muntin's own fact gate should force on `runbooks/qbo-xero-roadmap.md` before that number ever reaches a public page. The credible path to 100 paying restaurants is therefore not 100 sales; it is six to eight restaurant-specialist bookkeeping firms, each carrying 12–25 clients, reached through a Hubdoc-replacement content lane the storefront machine is already optimized to win and closed by the one act no agent session can perform — Don picking up the phone.

## Mechanisms
### Intuit / QuickBooks Online Accountant (ProAdvisor Preferred Pricing)
**What:** Accounting firms enroll clients under the firm's own account and choose one of two billing modes: firm-billed (the firm is invoiced monthly for every client subscription, at an ongoing discount — currently 30% off QBO products — and decides what to charge the client), or client-billed (a time-limited direct discount passed through). The firm gets one itemized monthly bill listing all firm-billed clients. Intuit also runs free certification and a searchable ProAdvisor directory on top of it.

**Why it worked:** It converts the accountant from a recommender into a reseller with a durable spread. The firm's economics improve every time they migrate another client, so the firm — not Intuit — pays the acquisition and onboarding cost, and does it enthusiastically. The single itemized bill is the load-bearing detail: it makes 30 client subscriptions one line item in the firm's own accounts payable instead of 30 credit-card decisions by 30 restaurant owners, each of which is a churn event waiting to happen. Certification adds a switching cost measured in the accountant's own trained hours.

**For Muntin:** Directly, and it is the single highest-leverage pricing change available. Muntin's Accountant tier ($150 flat + $30/seat, `hasCockpit: true` in `apps/api/src/lib/stripe-tiers.ts`) is celebrated in `runbooks/proposals/differentiation/competitor-pain-points.md` §M as an un-copyable inverted moat — but flat-firm-unlimited means a firm with 40 restaurant clients pays $3.75/restaurant/month, so the cheapest channel is also the one that monetizes worst, and there is no path from firm-brought client to direct paying customer. Restructure to: flat firm fee buys the cockpit and the seats; each client org carries its own firm-billed, discounted subscription. Muntin keeps the honest 'no per-document, no per-vendor fee' stance and gains a revenue line that grows with the channel.

### Dext (formerly Receipt Bank)
**What:** Two practice plans (Essentials / Advanced) sold per-client-per-month with a ten-client minimum, price per client falling as the firm adds clients — roughly $17.70 and $19.20 per client per month at the entry tier (UNVERIFIED beyond current public pricing pages). Dedicated partner support, training, certification, and practice-management tooling sit on top. Public claim: 12,000+ accounting firms.

**Why it worked:** The ten-client minimum is the mechanism, not a detail — it makes the firm, not the vendor, responsible for reaching volume, and it structurally excludes the single-client hobbyist who generates support load without revenue. Volume tiering means the firm's margin per client improves as it standardizes on the tool, so the firm actively pushes remaining clients onto it. Dext therefore acquires small businesses at a marginal cost near zero and reaches them at a scale no direct-to-SMB funnel would fund.

**For Muntin:** This is Muntin's exact price band, which is more important than it looks: at ~$17.70/client Dext is the anchor a restaurant bookkeeper already pays, so Muntin at $19 is at parity, not undercutting. That means the sale is won on restaurant fluency (NRA-aligned chart of accounts seeded in `migrations/0022_org_categories.sql`, canonical vendor resolution, statement reconciliation) and on the things `qbo-xero-roadmap.md` §2 marks as literal white space — bookkeeper-mode dashboard, lock-date respect, pre-post anomaly intercept — not on price. Copy the ten-client minimum. It is free churn insurance and free qualification.

### 7shifts
**What:** A free 'Comp' plan for single-location restaurants up to 30 employees covering scheduling, time clock, team chat and hiring, with paid plans starting around $34.99/location/month and a 14-day no-card trial on top. Self-serve throughout, restaurant-specific vocabulary throughout. Public claim: 55,000+ restaurants.

**Why it worked:** It is the existence proof that bottom-up self-serve *does* work in independent restaurants, under a specific condition: the free tier had to be genuinely sufficient for the smallest operator, and the upgrade trigger had to be an event the restaurant experiences on its own (a second location, the 31st employee, payroll). 7shifts never had to detect the moment or sell into it — the restaurant's own growth generated the upgrade. Crucially, scheduling is used daily by many people, so the tool embeds in the staff's habit, not the owner's.

**For Muntin:** Partially, and the differences matter more than the similarity. Invoice extraction is used weekly by one person, so it never gets 7shifts' habit embedding — which argues against copying the free tier naively. What does transfer is the upgrade-trigger design: Muntin's natural event-driven trigger is the second location (the founding price is already per-location) and the arrival of a bookkeeper. Design the free/paid line so that the restaurant's own change of state produces the upgrade, and Muntin never has to detect or sell it.

### Square
**What:** Give the point-of-sale software away at zero price, including hardware at near-cost, and monetize the payment flow that necessarily passes through it. No sales call, no contract, no per-seat decision — the restaurant's willingness to pay is never tested because the price never appears as a line item.

**Why it worked:** It removed the buying decision entirely. An independent operator does not evaluate free; they try it. The revenue arrives as a percentage of money that was already moving, which the operator experiences as a cost of accepting cards rather than a software subscription — so it survives the annual card-statement audit that kills $19 subscriptions. It also let Square acquire restaurants at a cost per acquisition that a field sales force could never match.

**For Muntin:** The structure does not transfer — Muntin touches no money flow and has explicitly ruled out entering payments (`competitor-pain-points.md` §4.3, correctly: money-transmitter licensing and AML are wrong for this stage). But the *principle* transfers exactly and is already half-executed: the Cost Index, the five live free tools, and the 171-term glossary are Muntin's zero-price surface. Today they convert nothing — the audit reproduced that exactly 4 of 1,368 pages carry a waitlist form. Square's lesson is that the free surface has to sit directly on the monetized one, not adjacent to it.

### Choco
**What:** A free ordering app for restaurants, monetized by subscription fees paid by the food distributors and suppliers on the other side of the order. Public claims: 21,000+ distributors, 100,000+ buyers; $111M raised (UNVERIFIED beyond company/press sources).

**Why it worked:** It found the side of the two-sided market with budget, procurement, and a measurable ROI story. Distributors have sales reps, order-error costs, and revenue per account large enough to justify software spend; restaurants have none of that. Inverting who pays let Choco give restaurants a genuinely free product and still build a venture-scale business.

**For Muntin:** **It does not transfer, and this must be stated plainly.** A distributor paying Muntin while Muntin publishes a CC0 wholesale price index that grades those same distributors would detonate the honesty brand in a single news cycle — and the Cost Index methodology page has already explicitly foreclosed sponsorship and paid placement. This is the exemplar the prompt asked about: a growth mechanism that requires a conflict Muntin cannot accept at any price. Name it, foreclose it in writing, and treat the foreclosure itself as a public differentiator against every vendor-funded 'benchmark' in the category.

### Toast (sales motion, and the abandoned dealer channel)
**What:** Two parallel acquisition engines — geographically deployed field reps who walk into restaurants, and a remote inside-sales org — reflecting that the buyer is a non-desk worker unreachable by ordinary digital funnels. Toast also once ran a POS reseller/dealer channel and pulled back from it around 2020, reportedly while the channel was its top-performing source (UNVERIFIED; single trade-press source). Reported CAC figures circulate at ~$10K and ~$19K per location (UNVERIFIED, aggregator-derived).

**Why it worked:** Field sales is the only mechanism that reliably reaches an owner who is on the line at 7pm and asleep at 11. It works because Toast's ARPU — POS subscription plus payment processing plus lending plus payroll — can amortize a four-or-five-figure CAC over many years and many products. The dealer retreat is equally instructive: dealers require hardware margin and recurring residuals, which only a high-ARPU product can fund.

**For Muntin:** As a *foreclosure*, which is more valuable than as a tactic. Muntin at $19–25/location can spend roughly nothing to acquire a restaurant, which permanently rules out field sales, inside sales, POS dealers, and paid acquisition. This is not a limitation to work around — it is the strategy statement, and it should be written into an ADR so no future session proposes a sales hire or a reseller program. The only arithmetically available channels are: content that costs agent-hours instead of dollars, the accountant channel where one relationship carries many restaurants, and Don's own DMV network.

### MarginEdge and Restaurant365 (the price ceiling and the fence it builds)
**What:** MarginEdge: ~$350/location/month flat, with unlimited invoice processing, and a POS requirement (Toast/Square); Toast customers additionally need Toast's ~$50/month Restaurant Management Suite for the API. Restaurant365: roughly $469–$499/location/month for the entry plan, billed annually or quarterly, plus separate implementation typically quoted in the $2,000–$10,000 range, with multi-year contracts standard. (Prices from current public pricing pages and reviews, 2026.)

**Why it worked:** These are infrastructure purchases with a real ROI story for a multi-unit operator, and the price and contract length are self-selecting: they filter to operators with a controller, a budget line, and enough units to amortize implementation. The POS requirement is a deliberate qualification gate, not an oversight — it guarantees the data quality the product's promises depend on.

**For Muntin:** It defines Muntin's undefended segment with unusual precision, exactly as `qbo-xero-roadmap.md` §3 argues: the 1–3 location operator on QBO or Xero, with a part-time bookkeeper, who will not buy a $350–500/location platform and whose POS is a Clover or a Lightspeed or a spreadsheet. It also sets the honest ceiling on willingness to pay: independents *will* pay $350+/location when the product returns measured food cost — so $19–25 is not the market's limit, it is Muntin's chosen entry point. Price the second product (inventory, food cost, pre-post anomaly intercept) against MarginEdge, not against a browser extension.

### Xero / Hubdoc (a vacuum, correctly located)
**What:** Xero shut Hubdoc down on 8 May 2026. The replacement, Xero Files, stores documents inside Xero but performs no data extraction — no OCR, no line-item reading, no supplier-portal auto-fetch. Every invoice that Hubdoc used to key at header level now has to be entered by hand or routed to a paid third party; Dext and AutoEntry absorbed most of the displaced volume.

**Why it worked:** A bundled free capability disappearing is the single most reliable trigger for software evaluation in this vertical, because it forces a decision on a fixed date instead of waiting for the owner to feel enough pain. But the person who feels it is the **bookkeeper**, not the owner — the bookkeeper is the one keying the invoices, and the bookkeeper decides the replacement for every client at once.

**For Muntin:** Two distinct actions, one urgent. (1) `apps/web/app/(marketing)/switch/hubdoc/page.tsx` is written in the present tense about a product that no longer exists — 'Hubdoc reads with their OCR…' — and its migration steps instruct the reader to log into a dead service. On a site whose entire brand is a fact gate, that page is a live liability and it is a 30-minute fix. (2) The acute migration window is largely closed three months on, but 'Hubdoc alternative / what replaced Hubdoc / Xero Files no OCR' is now a permanent evergreen commercial-intent query lane whose current results are vendor blogspam. That is precisely what a 1,368-page editorial machine with a fact gate is built to win — and the searcher is the bookkeeper.

### Toast AI Invoice Scanning (the segment fence, corrected)
**What:** Toast's AI Invoice Scanning is in beta as an enhancement to **Toast Retail**, pulling invoice data into Toast's *receiving* workflow so product quantities move into inventory with little manual entry, with catching cost changes before product hits the shelf as a stated benefit. It is tied to Toast's POS and inventory stack. Toast's US location count is reported variously (one source cites ~80K against ~1M US restaurants); the repo's '~120K restaurants' figure is an inference and is not independently sourced.

**Why it worked:** For Toast, bundling invoice capture into receiving is a retention and attach-rate play, not a standalone product — it makes the POS subscription harder to leave and closes an inventory data gap. It works because the invoice is already arriving at a Toast-managed back door.

**For Muntin:** **Not an extinction event — a fence, and it points at Muntin's segment.** Toast's version serves the receiving/inventory job for Toast customers; it does not serve the bookkeeping job (GL-correct coding, lock-date respect, vendor canonicalization, multi-client portability) and it does not exist for the Square/Clover/Lightspeed/spreadsheet majority. MarginEdge has the same POS fence. Toast will also, at its own expense, teach 100,000+ operators that invoice scanning is a normal thing to expect — which raises the bar for why a *standalone* tool exists, and the only durable answer is a job Toast structurally cannot do: the accountant's, across many restaurants and many POS systems at once. Separately and immediately: the '~120K' figure must be marked unsourced in `runbooks/qbo-xero-roadmap.md` before Muntin's own fact gate ever meets it on a public page.

## Antipatterns
- **Do not take money from food distributors, in any form** — no sponsorship, no listing fee, no data-licensing deal, no 'preferred distributor' placement. Choco's inversion (free for restaurants, distributors pay) is a genuinely great mechanism and it is unavailable to Muntin at any price: a distributor paying the company that publishes a CC0 index grading distributors is a conflict the honesty brand cannot survive, and the Cost Index methodology page has already foreclosed sponsorship in writing. Treat the foreclosure as a public asset, not a cost.
- **Do not repeat '~120K restaurants' or 'Toast is bundling invoice scanning' as fact.** The figure in `runbooks/qbo-xero-roadmap.md` §1(5) is an inference; the verified reality is a beta enhancement to Toast Retail's receiving workflow. Under Muntin's own fact gate this number cannot reach a public page without independent sourcing — and an internal strategy doc that carries an unsourced number will eventually leak into copy, which is exactly the propagation path the audit documented.
- **Do not leave present-tense claims about dead competitors on the site.** `/switch/hubdoc` currently describes Hubdoc's OCR and offshore review queue in the present tense and routes readers into a shut-down service. `check-competitor-claims.mjs` is explicitly informational and never blocks CI — which means the one gate that would have caught this was designed not to. For a company whose brand is enforced honesty, a stale competitor claim is worse than a missing page.
- **Do not build a POS reseller or dealer channel.** Dealers require hardware margin and recurring residuals that only a high-ARPU product can fund; Toast reportedly retreated from its own dealer channel around 2020 (UNVERIFIED, single trade-press source) despite its performance. At $19–25/location there is no residual to pay. Write the foreclosure into an ADR so no future session proposes it.
- **Do not pursue a restaurant-association endorsed-provider deal that touches the Cost Index.** Association partnerships typically want sponsorship dollars or a revenue share, and the Cost Index has already foreclosed both. An association relationship may be viable for the *product* alone, but it must never be allowed to attach to the Index, and the separation has to be explicit before any conversation starts — not negotiated afterward.
- **Do not copy 7shifts' free tier naively.** Their freemium works because scheduling is a daily, many-user habit that embeds in staff behavior; invoice extraction is weekly and single-user, so a free tier buys dormant accounts instead of embedded ones. If a free tier ships at all, it must have an upgrade trigger generated by the restaurant's own change of state (a second location, a bookkeeper joining), not by a usage cap Muntin has to detect and sell into.
- **Do not keep celebrating flat-firm-unlimited Accountant pricing as an un-copyable moat.** It is un-copyable because it is bad: a 40-client firm pays $3.75 per restaurant per month, and the channel that acquires most cheaply becomes the one that monetizes worst, with no route from firm-brought client to direct customer. The honest, defensible version keeps the no-per-document / no-per-vendor / no-per-export guarantees and adds a firm-billed per-client rate — which is what both working proofs in this channel actually do.
- **Do not compete on an accuracy percentage, and do not answer Toast's 'AI' with better 'AI' copy.** `check-verboten-phrases.mjs` already bans 'AI-powered' and '99% accurate' in product copy, and `no-llm-ci.sh` makes the real claim falsifiable by a stranger in thirty seconds. The differentiator is the honesty of the uncertainty — named reason families, a held-back number instead of a guessed one — not a number in the same shape as the competitor's marketing.
- **Do not treat the Hubdoc shutdown as an acquisition event still in progress.** It closed on a fixed date three months ago and Dext and AutoEntry absorbed most of the displaced volume; a campaign written as if operators are mid-migration would be both late and, on this site, dishonest. The durable asset is the evergreen search lane and the fact that the person searching it is a bookkeeper — a channel entry point, not a land grab.
