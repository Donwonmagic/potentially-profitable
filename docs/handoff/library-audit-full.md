# THE MUNTIN LIBRARY AUDIT — SYNTHESIS (39 articles, every line read)

## 1. THE STATE OF THE LIBRARY

**Funnel-fit composition (the headline finding):**
- **Cost-intelligence (on-funnel): 10 of 39 (26%)** — commission-free-online-ordering, keep-plate-cost-honest, how-to-tell-if-a-restaurant-tool-is-safe, ingredient-yields, privacy-forward-bookkeeping, restaurant-menu-engineering, how-to-raise-menu-prices, what-beef-prices-mean, service-charge-vs-tipping, third-party-delivery-comparison.
- **Retired-website-era (off-funnel): 22 of 39 (56%)** — the whole website/SEO/GBP/brand/menu-template neighborhood.
- **Adjacent (right buyer, wrong moment): 7 of 39 (18%)** — reviews ×2, loyalty-ROI, restaurant-app-decision, legal-essentials, third-party-delivery-economics, ai-mode-reservation-strategy.

**The blunt verdict:** The library does **not** serve the cost-intelligence company Muntin now is. A clear **majority (56%) is retired-website-era content** — the exact "restaurant website pricing" audience the retention strategy names as the wrong on-ramp — and the genuine cost spine is barely a quarter of the shelf. Muntin is a cost company whose reference desk still reads like a web studio.

**Quality distribution (the good news):** Craft is not the problem. **30 of 39 (77%) score ≥4**; 12 are 5/5. Only 3 score below 3 — and all three are the retired menu-design/brand marketing hubs (menu-design-themes 2, menu-design-cuisines 1.5, when-to-rebuild 2). The library is a **well-built library pointed at the wrong reader.** That's actually the ideal problem to have: aim, not rebuild.

**Verdicts:** keep 13, upgrade 14, rewrite-onto-funnel 3, retire-redirect 9. So **23% of the library should be 301'd**, and only ~13 pieces are genuine keepers, of which just 5 are pure on-funnel cost content.

## 2. THE FUNNEL PROBLEM

The library's SEO on-ramp is pulling traffic the funnel can't convert. Concretely, the wrong-audience magnets:

**What it costs the retention strategy:** the library is supposed to be the SEO on-ramp that *feeds the tools and the Brief*. Instead it recruits reputation-, ranking-, and website-minded searchers the cost funnel then has to re-qualify — and mostly can't. The retention doc's own thesis (return visits come from the right first visit) is undercut: these arrivals bounce because the site's product (cost intelligence) has nothing to do with why they came. On an honesty brand, a stapled-on non-sequitur CTA reads as exactly what it is.

And the internal-link graph **compounds** the leak: every website piece's "further reading / companion kit" points at *other* website pieces, so the rare cost-curious arrival gets escorted deeper into the retired neighborhood, not toward the funnel.

# PRESSURE-TEST: The Muntin Library Audit

## Opening: the audit is good craft aimed at the wrong altitude

The audit's *reads* are almost all correct (I spot-verified the load-bearing ones and they hold). Its *plan* is where it fails a one-person cost company. The core error: it treats a retired-era library as **a renovation project** when the company's own retention strategy ranks the library **#5 of 5** ("evergreen SEO on-ramp + transparency proof… **do NOT depth-farm**") and puts *staleness repair* at punch-list item **#0**. The audit proposes ~14 upgrades + 3 full rewrites + 9 redirects + merges. That is a multi-month editorial program, doubled by EN/ES, for the single lowest-leverage surface on the strategic board. Most of my pressure-test is pulling the plan back down to the altitude the strategy actually assigns it.

**First concrete catch:** the audit claims "39 articles, every line read," but the library has **40 article directories**. `can-chatgpt-write-your-restaurant-website/` is never named in any bucket. A retired-era, off-funnel piece was missed by a "comprehensive every-line" pass — which is itself evidence that a 39-item hand-audit is already at the edge of what's sustainable to *maintain*, let alone renovate.

## (1) SEO-equity risk: the retire-vs-rewrite framing is too coarse

The audit's fatal simplification is treating "301 redirect" as equity-preserving. **A 301 preserves ranking, backlink, and AI-Overview equity *only* when the target is a close topical match.** Point a page at an unrelated target and Google treats it as a soft-404, drops the redirect from the index, and you lose the equity anyway — the worst of both worlds, because you also lose the live URL that AI Overviews and LLM citations (you ship `llms.txt` + `feed-llm.json`) currently point at.

Re-sort the audit's 9 redirects by what actually happens:

- **Rewrite-in-place, never redirect (high equity + latent cost spine already inside):** `best-restaurant-website-platform` (5/5, **104 in-body cost mentions** — the per-order-commission / owned-channel argument *is* a margin argument), `toast-vs-square-vs-clover` (POS total-cost-of-ownership = cost), the delivery pieces. The audit already lands here for these three — correct. Keep the slug, keep the URL, re-lead the cost angle. This is the equity-safe move and it doubles as the "bridge" answer in (2).
- **Merge-redirect only because the target is topically adjacent (the *safe* redirect):** `wix-vs-custom` → `best-restaurant-website-platform`. Same intent cluster, so the equity mostly transfers. Defensible. But port its two good figures first, as the audit says.
- **The genuinely risky redirects the audit waves through:** `how-to-read-google-search-console` + `technical-seo-checklist` → `restaurant-local-seo` (GSC-diagnostics and site-audit intent ≠ local-pack intent — different queries, likely independent backlinks; this is a soft-404 that *dumps* equity, not consolidates it), and `brand-system` + `photo-spec` → `/tools/` or "a /library/ hub" (topically distant → soft-404). For these, **freeze-in-place beats redirect.** Freezing costs nothing and keeps whatever authority/citation standing they hold.
- **Don't 301 — 410/deindex (the dead-product case):** the `menu-design-*` children. These aren't equity you want to preserve; they carry a *live fabrication* and 48+ dead-end pages. 301'ing 48 thin templated pages onto one hub is read as mass soft-404 anyway. A clean `410 Gone` removes them from the index without pretending to pass equity, and is the honest signal. Point the two *hubs* at `menu-engineering` (nearest topic); 410 the children.

**Bottom line for (1):** the audit's own instinct (rewrite the high-equity website/POS/delivery pages in place, freeze the flagships) is right; its error is the middle tier, where it reaches for 301-to-unrelated as if that banks equity. It doesn't. The correct three-way sort is: **rewrite-in-place (equity + cost spine) / freeze (equity, no honest cost angle) / 410 (dead product, no equity worth saving).** "301 everything retired" is the one move that can actively destroy ranking.

## (2) Is "wrong audience" right? Overstated — the 56% conflates two very different jobs

The "wrong audience / leak" thesis is **half right and materially overstated.** It lumps two distinct populations:

- **Genuine leak (different job-to-be-done):** pure discovery/reputation/design intent — GBP setup, GSC, schema, reviews, brand system, photo spec, menu-design. A searcher here wants *visibility or a look*, not margin. The cost funnel can't convert them and shouldn't try. Freeze or remove. The audit is right about these.
- **Bridge, not leak (same buyer, cost-adjacent decision):** website-platform, POS, online-ordering, third-party-delivery. An operator choosing between Toast's per-order online-ordering fee and an owned channel **is making a margin decision** — that's why `best-platform` naturally accumulated 104 cost mentions with no one forcing them. A "restaurant website pricing" searcher spending $2.5k–$25k is *by definition* cost-conscious; they're one lead paragraph away from "here's the per-order commission math that dwarfs the build cost." That's the on-ramp working, not leaking.

So the headline "56% off-funnel" is inflated by folding commercial-intent, cost-adjacent pages into the discovery bucket. The real split is closer to: ~25% pure cost spine, ~20% cost-*adjacent* bridge (rewrite the lead, keep), ~35% genuine discovery leak (freeze/remove), the rest trust/legal/adjacent.

Second challenge to the framing: **"leak" assumes conversion is the library's only job.** The strategy explicitly assigns it two *non-conversion* jobs — evergreen SEO on-ramp and transparency/authority proof. Ranking and getting AI-cited for real operator questions builds domain authority and citation standing that compound *even when the visitor never buys*. On an honesty brand, that standing is an asset. "Wrong audience" over-indexes on immediate funnel-fit and undercounts authority equity.
