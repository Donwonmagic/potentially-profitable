# Muntin retention (return-visitor) strategy — grounded

Feeds: `founder-vision.md`, `site-reinvention-blueprint.md`. Grounded in the real product
+ a return-visitor investigation and adversarial pressure-test.

## The one deep truth (resolves the founder's doubt)

**Honesty and frequency are in direct conflict, because honest cost data is mostly boring.**
Most periods, most of an operator's ingredients don't move enough to matter — and the
honesty contract forbids manufacturing drama. So a *frequent, bare Cost Index* cannot be the
habit: a personalized "your basket this week" that's honestly "basically flat" most weeks
*trains the operator that it rarely matters* — the correct lesson, and the death of the ritual.
The founder was right. The resolution is not more frequency; it is **richer value at the
operator's real cadence + event-driven reflex.**

## The real return cadence (not weekly)

The operator works 55+ hrs on the floor; they do not develop a Tuesday-morning market-check
reflex. Their real decisions recur **monthly** (P&L close, menu/food-cost review) with
**event-driven spikes** (the week romaine doubles, they come running). Publish of-record on
whatever cadence is honest, but **retention lives at monthly + spikes**, not weekly.

## The retention architecture (corrected, ranked, honest)

1. **The monthly Brief** (the expanded publication — the index as ONE desk, wrapped in *The
   Move / The Method / On the Horizon / From the Floor*). This is what makes the monthly
   appointment worth opening *even in a quiet cost month* — the sections carry value the bare
   index can't. Emailed monthly, captured inline at the point of value, personalized to the
   operator's saved basket where honest. Matches the real monthly decision.
2. **Event-spike alerts — the real return engine for this audience.** "A thing *you* buy moved
   enough to matter." A reflex to a real-world event, more defensible + more honest than any
   calendar ritual. **Guard hard:** threshold-gated (a cents-per-plate / persisted-N-periods
   floor), magnitude-honest, and *sometimes reassuring* ("this barely moved your plate — no
   action needed"). An alert that sometimes says "nothing to do" is the honesty proof; one that
   always implies action is the Dealer dark pattern.
3. **Free tools — the everyday first-visit win + the honest bridge.** High Ability / no signup;
   win the first visit, and the *save* ("save this, we'll tell you when it moves") is the honest
   investment that personalizes the Brief + the alerts.
4. **The Ledger — the long-term retention CORE (under-ranked by the raw strategy).** An
   operator's *own logged invoices* are a more trusted, more actionable return reason than any
   public index; the price-hike-against-your-own-history flag is the least-manufacturable trigger
   the company owns. The public Brief is top-of-funnel that *feeds* the Ledger habit. The export
   guarantee is cheap and is the brand's proof-of-honesty.
5. **Library + citable methodology/changelog** — evergreen SEO on-ramp + transparency proof.
   Link where genuinely relevant; do NOT depth-farm.

## The habit loop (honest, monthly)

Internal trigger (already exists): *"am I getting squeezed — are costs moving under me?"* — fires
episodically (before the order, at re-pricing, at P&L close, when an invoice looks wrong).
External trigger: **one** value-first email per edition (subject = the real headline number),
default **monthly digest + opt-in instant spike-alerts**, power-users may upgrade to weekly.
Action: one tap, answer above the fold, the read *ends* (finite is a feature). Reward: the
genuinely-new intel (it's just the news — no slot-machine vocabulary needed). Investment: save
your basket / plate-costs / (in Ledger) your invoices → next visit is pre-personalized.

## Do-first punch-list (ordered for a ONE-PERSON company)

0. **Fix staleness + the cadence contradiction FIRST.** The site sells "monthly"
   (`cost-index/index.html`), "four notes a quarter" (`index.html` footer), and "every week"
   (dispatch) simultaneously, and it *opens on a 10-week-old anchor* — which literally teaches
   people not to return. Pick ONE honest cadence everywhere; make opening on a stale anchor
   structurally impossible (live number or a stated "why it's held"). Pure trust-debt repair,
   near-zero risk, precondition for everything.
1. **One email — default MONTHLY digest + event-spike alerts** (not weekly). Capture inline at
   the point of value. Weekly only as an explicit power-user upgrade.
2. **Event-driven "a thing you buy moved" alert** — materiality floor, honest "no action needed".
3. **Tools = first-visit win + honest save-and-watch bridge.**
4. **Ledger = the switching-cost cockpit + a visible export guarantee.**
5. **Library/changelog = SEO on-ramp + transparency.**
6. **Monthly "From the floor — Don" note — ONLY if Don will genuinely sustain it** (it's the
   advocacy/personality converter; also the first thing to go stale in a solo shop).

## Cut (cargo-culted from consumer scale; cutting them *strengthens* the honesty moat)

- Reward-of-the-**Tribe** framing ("others on the floor see this too") — there is no tribe yet;
  keep `elevatedWeeks` as market *fact*, drop the social dressing (brushes the fabrication gate).
- The incrementing **publish-streak counter** → "publishing since [date]" (survives a skipped
  week; an integer streak pressures Don to ship weak editions — anti-honesty).
- **Countdown-to-next-edition** anticipation styling → a plain dateline ("Edition of Jul 14").
  Anticipation you design for is FOMO in a cardigan.
- **"Continue where you left off"** returner-homepage — wrong memory horizon for a monthly
  visitor (feels uncanny, not seen). Recognize a *monthly* returner instead ("what moved on your
  basket since you last looked — 5 weeks ago").

## The two risks to respect

- **Retention theater:** a beautiful honest machine a busy operator subscribes to and never opens.
  Measure return-that-drives-action (tool re-runs, Ledger logins, basket edits), not open rate.
- **One-person maintenance decay:** every mechanic is maintenance forever, on top of a full-time
  FOH job. **Ruthless scope reduction is itself the retention strategy** — half-built machinery
  decays into the staleness that's the actual killer. Build the few that matter; kill the rest.

## Design spine (validated): the window

The site = clear panes into your cost world, each pane its own register (Market read / Workbench
tools / Reference-desk library / Cockpit Ledger / the human Window). The honesty brand demands
the glass be *clean*: a stale anchor, a contradicting cadence, a bounced capture are smudges that
break the one thing the whole strategy runs on — trust that what you see is current and yours.
