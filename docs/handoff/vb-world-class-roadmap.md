# Vendor Benchmark — world-class elevation roadmap

_Founder directive (2026-07-10): make VB rival the biggest tech/SaaS companies in power, UX, cutting-edge feel, and the largest segments they compete in — WITHIN the honest / on-device / no-backend / private-prices moat. Produced by a 10-segment competitive-teardown + product-council synthesizer (workflow wf_25cc0642-463). This is the resume-here plan; check off items as they ship._

## Thesis

Vendor Benchmark is already an A-at-rest, unusually honest single-item microscope — the bones (compute-as-you-type, conformal forecast bands, calibrated withhold, a real on-device journal, a four-material design system) are genuinely above the free-tool field. "World-class within the moat" therefore does NOT mean adding a backend, an account, or a cloud dashboard; it means making the moat FELT rather than asserted, and turning a static-render calculator into a living instrument. The through-line has three verbs: (1) INSTRUMENT the promise — make privacy/provenance kinetic and verifiable (a live "carrying your prices: 0" counter, a nutrition-label strip, drill-to-source, portable exports) so honesty is watched working every session, the one thing the data-monetizing incumbents structurally cannot copy; (2) ACTIVATE the memory and the data already sitting dead on the device — wire the loaded-but-unread ADR-012 context, mine the on-device 3-yr series for seasonality, and turn the journal from an overwriting log into a history spine that greets the returning operator with what moved while they were away; (3) SHIP ONLY WHAT THE SESSION NEEDS, to a thumb, offline — shard the 360KB history seed to ~6KB, hit the WCAG/mobile floor, install as an offline PWA, and hand the operator a shareable, exportable artifact they own. Everything stays static HTML + vanilla JS, dual-locale, on-device, honesty-gated. The sequence front-loads four foundations (mobile floor, seed sharding, motion-epoch gate, journal history spine) because each is cheap, independently shippable, and unblocks a whole family of later moves.

## Ranked roadmap

### #1 — Mobile floor: 16px inputs + a --vb-touch:44px target token  `[high/S · mobile]`

- **What:** Bump .vb-input/.vb-select to font-size:16px (matching the site's own site-core.css form baseline, which VB overrode DOWN to 15px), add a --vb-touch:44px token and apply min-height to .vb-remove/.vb-add/.vb-example/.vb-clear/.vb-btn/.vb-jchip and the <summary> toggles, give the destructive row-× a padded 44x44 hit area (22px glyph), add -webkit-text-size-adjust:100%. Pure CSS in index.html + byte-identical es/ mirror.
- **Why world-class:** Kills iOS zoom-on-focus (the #1 'not built for phones' tell) and clears the WCAG 2.5.5 / Apple HIG 44px floor across every control — Square/Toast fat-target discipline. The kitchen phone is VB's largest and most-defining segment.
- **Honesty/moat:** Pure token/CSS change; no data, network, privacy, or honesty surface touched.
- **Sequence:** First: zero dependencies, one afternoon, and it is the single cheapest win in the largest segment. Nothing else needs to land before it.

### #2 — Shard the 360KB history seed per item + immutable /data cache rule + intent-gated prefetch  `[high/M · performance]`

- **What:** Extend build-cost-index-history-seed.mjs to emit one tiny file per item (data/ci-history/<item>.js, ~6KB gz vs 360KB monolith); in loadSeeds() load only the picked item's shard, monolith as 404 fallback. Add '/data/*.js -> Cache-Control: immutable' to _headers (the existing ?v= query makes this safe). Stop calling loadSeeds() unconditionally at init; prefetch the one shard the moment an item is picked or the first price field is focused.
- **Why world-class:** Vercel/Next 'ship the route's data, not the dataset': transfer 360KB->6KB (98%) and, on a cheap phone, main-thread parse 1.6MB->25KB — an INP/TBT win, not just bandwidth. A bounce visit downloads 0KB of history instead of 360KB.
- **Honesty/moat:** Static build-time file generation; shards are public USDA wholesale data, same-origin <script> like today, no fetch. Operator prices never involved. Identical numbers, split by item.
- **Sequence:** Second foundation: unblocks the PWA (offline precache is only sane once payload is small) and the predictive-prefetch UX, and makes every on-device analysis move (context/seasonality) cheap. Independent of the other three foundations.

### #3 — Render-epoch motion gate + a unified motion-token system  `[high/M · visual-motion]`

- **What:** Promote motion to --vb-* tokens (--vb-ease, --vb-ease-out, --vb-t-1/2/3) and route the existing combobox motion through them. Then add the gate: in render(), compute an identity signature from market.key/item + tone + thin + sign(gapPts), keep lastSig at module scope, derive identityChanged vs sameItemRecompute, and pass an 'animate' flag to chart/number renderers — animate on identity change / no-result->result / loading->resolved; SNAP on keystroke recompute.
- **Why world-class:** The Linear principle: one curve, purposeful, never re-firing. This is the safety rail that lets the signature-motion moves exist on a tool that re-renders on every keystroke without becoming nauseating — the reason those moves haven't been built.
- **Honesty/moat:** Pure tokens + ~30 lines of guard logic; no data, no network, no fabricated values.
- **Sequence:** Third foundation: a hard prerequisite for the signature-motion 'wow' (rank 12) and it also gives a11y focus-management the same 'only act on meaningful change, never on keystroke' discipline. Ship before any draw-on/tween.

### #4 — Journal history spine: a per-item ring, not overwrite (+ fix the cold-load trend bug)  `[high/M · personalization]`

- **What:** Change the journal schema from map[k]={...} overwrite to a capped newest-first ring per key (checks:[{at,gapPts,yourPct,marketPct,lastCents,firstDate,lastDate}], cap ~12), mirroring the bus's dishCostHistory ring. 'Since your last check' now reads the true prior check from storage instead of the fragile in-memory reopenBaseline var (which is lost on refresh, silently breaking the flagship memory feature on cold load). - **Why world-class:** Memory that keeps only the last value isn't memory. The trajectory is what makes VB feel like it accumulates into YOURS — the Linear activity-trail / Datadog metric-history feeling — and it hardens the trend note against refresh loss.
- **Honesty/moat:** On-device localStorage; ~38KB well under the 200KB budget; every stored number is the operator's own previously-computed value. Back-compatible reader.
- **Sequence:** Fourth foundation: unblocks the entire personalization tier (while-you-were-away re-read, pinned Your Items, vendor memory) and fixes an existing silent bug. Independent of the other three foundations; ship in parallel.

### #5 — Wire the loaded-but-dead ADR-012 context line + on-device seasonality baseline  `[high/M · analysis-power]`

## Rejected (big-SaaS patterns that need a backend/login or erode the moat)

- **Account-based cross-device sync (Linear/Arc continuity across your phone, walk-in tablet, and laptop with auto-merge).**
  - Why rejected: Requires a server + login, and it forces the operator's literal invoice prices and vendor names to leave the browser — a direct breach of the on-device/no-backend moat and the /security/ published promise.
  - Honest on-device alternative: The rank-13 Export/Import book file (Blob + FileReader, carry your book deliberately, zero upload) plus the URL-fragment share for a single benchmark. Arguably a stronger trust story than the SaaS default: the operator chooses when their data moves and can watch it never touch a server.

- **Server-rendered per-link OG unfurl images (Slack/iMessage rich previews that render each shared benchmark as a custom card).**
  - Why rejected: A per-link dynamic preview image must be generated by a backend; a static site cannot mint one per URL without the server the moat forbids.
  - Honest on-device alternative: One strong STATIC OG card for the tool route (already present) so pasted links preview well but generically, plus client-side document.title/summary from the decoded state so the tab and any client-rendered preview read meaningfully. State plainly that a per-link image would need a backend — don't fake it.

- **A crowd-sourced vendor price database / benchmark against what OTHER restaurants pay (the 'community data' network-effect play).**
  - Why rejected: Aggregating other operators' prices requires collecting and hosting them on a server — the exact opposite of the privacy moat, and it would make the honesty contract unenforceable (unverifiable, non-public inputs).
  - Honest on-device alternative: Keep the public-domain USDA wholesale series as the shared, verifiable yardstick (rank-5 context + seasonality), and make the operator's OWN journal the only accumulating dataset (rank-4/9/11). The benchmark is against a sourced public reference, not against unverifiable strangers.

- **Server-side usage/price analytics and cloud email/SMS delivery of the vendor brief (dashboards of what operators benchmark; 'email this to your rep' from our backend).**
  - Why rejected: Both would send the operator's prices or fine-grained usage through a server, eroding the 'requests carrying your prices: 0' guarantee the whole trust story rests on.
  - Honest on-device alternative: Bucketed, price-free Plausible events only (honoring the event-cardinality gate), the rank-7 live '0' counter proving no price token ever rides an outbound request, and navigator.share (rank-14) handing the brief to the OS share sheet — the operator's own text, their channel, never our server.

- **Real-time multiplayer collaboration (Figma-style presence, co-owners editing the same benchmark live with cursors).**
  - Why rejected: Live shared editing needs a realtime server and identity — backend + login, and shared server-held state means prices leave the device.
  - Honest on-device alternative: The rank-13 shareable link (recipient opens and interrogates the exact benchmark, account-free) plus same-origin cross-tab liveness via the storage event / MuntinContext.subscribe — 'one connected instrument' across the operator's own tabs with zero network.

- **Runtime cloud TTS / AI narration and server-side PDF generation for the brief.**
  - Why rejected: Runtime calls to a TTS or PDF service are network round-trips that could carry the operator's numbers and add a backend dependency the tool otherwise never has.
  - Honest on-device alternative: The existing pre-rendered audio pipeline (EN+ES, built offline and fact-gated) for narration, and client-side print/Blob for the brief and CSV (rank-13) — the document goes to the operator's own disk/printer, never a server.
