The invoice arrives.

A produce invoice lands the way invoices always do — five lines, one total. One of these five lines is hiding a raise, and the $115.80 total won’t tell you which. Skip to the flag →

Read.

You snap or upload the invoice; templates and rules pull the lines — vendor, date, item, quantity, price. No language model reads your invoices — and you don’t have to take that on faith: a published build gate fails any release that adds a language-model library. The AI policy →

File.

Every line lands filed and searchable — by vendor, by item, by date — and the whole record exports to a clean CSV whenever you ask. This is the price spreadsheet you never had to build; it filed itself.

Flag.

The June 26 romaine line lands at $29.45 — up $5.23 a case against the trailing median of your prior reads (+21.6%) — and gets the flag.

How the flag decides — thresholds, market context, and the first weeks

The check runs against your own history, nothing else, and it fires only when a move clears both of Ledger’s thresholds: at least 8% and at least $5.00 over your trailing median.

$5.23 ÷ 24 heads is about 22¢ a head — every dish romaine touches re-costs the same day. And because romaine is in the public Cost Index basket, the flag arrives with market context: my guy, or the market? Outside the basket — spirits, wine, graded cuts — a raise still files and re-costs, but isn’t market-checked. And an item with no earlier reads yet gets no flag at all: with nothing of yours to compare against, Ledger withholds rather than guesses — so the first weeks look like this: invoices file and the history builds, and the flags start arriving once your own reads can back them.

Ledger runs this check on every line, the day the invoice lands — $19 a month per location is less than four of these caught cases. Opens November 13, 2026 — 19 weeks out. The founding terms →
