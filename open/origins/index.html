Where America's food comes from

A country-first walk through the origin geography of 155 tracked US food imports — about $62.9B a year in customs value, attributed across 57 supplier countries. Pick a country to see the foods it sends; pick a food to see its origin mix. Every figure is share of tracked import value, a co-occurrence in the record — never volume, and never a claim about why.

    The division of labor

    54 vs 22

    Mexico is #1 source for 54 foods — overwhelmingly the produce aisle (fruiting 15, greens 8, fruit 7, roots, alliums, citrus). Canada is #1 for 22 — the protein case & cold-store (beef, shellfish, meat, potatoes, mushrooms). Recolor by category and the two blocks physically separate.

    Half the foods, under half the dollars

    76 / 42%

    Mexico + Canada are the #1 source for 76 of 153 foods — a clean half of the shelf — yet only 42% of attributed import dollars. The money's majority is high-value protein & specialty spread thin down the tail: Chile, Australia, Ecuador, Peru, India, Norway, Vietnam. Breadth of the aisle vs value of the case.

    Concentration is the norm

    132 / 153

    Only 21 of 153 foods are diversified. 105 are concentrated and 27 are outright single-source (≥90% one country). The default state of an American import ingredient is a lopsided bet on one country.

    The 100% bars

    7

    Seven foods have a #1 source at literally 100% — Raspberry, Yellow squash, Poblano, Serrano & Nopales (Mexico); Red & Russet potato (Canada). The "top-3" framing hides that they're one-country foods. The Concentration lens is what exposes it.

    Data-honesty artifact

    155 → 153

    155 foods carry an import figure but only 153 carry named sources; 143 list exactly three, nine list two, one lists a single source. A food with no source rail is drawn empty, never padded to look complete.

    What "attributed $" means

    $62.9B

    Each food's customs value is split across its named sources by their share, then summed per country. It mixes price and quantity (quantity is unpublished at this level) and is never a delivered or retail price. A supply-diversity fact, not a risk read.

    The far-flung single bets

    The heaviest concentration isn't next-door. These are the foods with ≥80% of tracked value from one supplier an ocean away — mid-sized, so invisible on a dollar-ranked list, yet the most lopsided bets on the shelf.

    Dot position = the single supplier's share of that food's tracked import value. Read left (80%) to right (100%). Concentration label is straight from the record's import_source_concentration, so the fragility read never depends on color.

    Source & method. US Census Bureau, International Trade (imports by HS code) — a US-Government work, public domain. The per-country aggregate (attributed value, dominance counts, origin shares) is computed by Muntin and published under CC BY 4.0, attribution Muntin Cost Index (muntin.digital).

    Schema. The download is newline-delimited JSON (one food per line) with fields import_top_sources[] (country + share_pct), import_source_hhi, import_source_concentration, and us_import_value_usd. Shares are the source-country mix of tracked import value; HHI ranges 0.09–1.0 (median 0.46).

    Honesty. Import figures are nominal customs value — they mix price and quantity (quantity is unpublished at this level) and are never a delivered or retail price. Origin shares are a supply-diversity fact and a co-occurrence in the trade record, never a risk forecast or a claim about why a food comes from where it does.
