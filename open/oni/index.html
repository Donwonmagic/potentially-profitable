The Zero Horizon

Seventy-six years of the Oceanic Niño Index — the observed sea-surface anomaly that names each season El Niño (warm), La Niña (cool), or neutral. The horizon line is zero: warm fills up, cool fills down. One climate gauge, read straight from the record — never a forecast.

The signature view

The zero-horizon ribbon

Every one of the 917 overlapping three-month seasons since DJF 1950, as a diverging area around a hard zero line. Rust fills upward for El Niño, teal fills downward for La Niña, and the guide lines mark the ±0.5°C thresholds. The pale ribbon is the full record; the saturated runs are the 42 official episodes. Nearly half the record — 45.6% — sits in the quiet neutral band, so ENSO reads as episodic punctuation, not a constant seesaw.

Sonification maps anomaly to pitch (warm = higher, cool = lower) as a play-through; a moving caret and the readout track it, and every value is also in the episode and seasonal tables below. Audio is an enhancement, never the only channel.

ONI subtracts the trend. Raw sea-surface temperature (gold) drifts up across the decades — about 26.7°C in the 1950s to ~27.1°C lately — yet the anomaly ribbon stays centered on zero because ONI is measured against a moving 30-year base period. This index tracks the wobble, not the warming.

Scattered peaks, one heartbeat

El Niño and La Niña can begin in any season. Watch where the big ones peak. Below are three of the strongest warm events; a tick marks the season each one topped out. Every tick lands in late autumn or winter.

Now fold those scattered peaks onto a single dial. The clock below places all 76 years of every season around a ring — and the same winter seasons that caught the peaks are exactly where the ring bulges widest.

The non-obvious form

The phase clock

One spoke per overlapping season, DJF around to NDJ. Each spoke's length is the spread (standard deviation) of all 76 years of anomalies in that season. The dial bulges through NDJ / DJF / OND (std 1.03–1.09) and pinches to its narrowest waist at AMJ / MJJ (std ≈ 0.56 — a tight spread, not zero; the radius is short, the anomalies still vary). This is the seasonal phase-lock made into one glanceable shape — ENSO's heartbeat, wide in winter, narrow in spring.

Winter seasons carry roughly twice the spread of spring seasons. Select a season to read its spread.

The asymmetry & the browser

Twin span-bars: the sprinter and the marathoner

El Niño episodes stack above the zero line, La Niña below; bar length is duration in seasons. La Niña bars run longer and, above all, repeat — five distinct multi-year events, the 1998–2001 and 2021–2023 dips re-intensifying across three to four consecutive winters, a double-dip pattern El Niño doesn't make in this record. El Niño's longest runs (2014–16, 1986–88) can span three calendar years too, but each holds a single peak and fades rather than re-deepening winter after winter. And El Niño owns the sharper ceiling. Warm events spike higher; cool events last longer and repeat. Select any bar or table row to walk through that episode.

What the record earns

Five honest reads

Phase-lock is real and stark

32 of 42 official episodes peak in OND/NDJ/DJF, 34 of 42 in Oct–Mar. Seasonal spread climbs from ~0.56 in spring to ~1.09 in NDJ. The dataset has a spring waist and a winter bulge.

Sprinter vs marathoner

La Niña reaches 28 and 32 seasons and stages 5 multi-year events; El Niño's longest single run is 19 and, though it can span three calendar years, it doesn't double-dip across winters. But El Niño owns the extremes: +2.75 ceiling vs −2.03 floor.

Detrended by design

Raw SST drifts up across decades, but the anomaly stays centered on zero — ONI is measured against a moving 30-year base. A regime index that removes the warming trend, not a climate-warming line. Flip the detrend toggle above to see it.

A balanced seesaw

Over 76 years the record splits 26.9% El Niño / 27.5% La Niña / 45.6% neutral — remarkably symmetric time-in-state, even though the intensity distribution is not.

The live tail (observed)

The record ends climbing out of a La Niña trough — SON 2025 −0.51 up through MAM 2026 +0.51 to AMJ 2026 +0.98. An emerging warm signal in the observed record, described as observed — never extrapolated.

Provenance & access

Dataset. Oceanic Niño Index (ONI) — the observed three-month running sea-surface temperature anomaly in the Niño 3.4 region, the standard gauge for El Niño / La Niña. CC0 · public domain Published by the NOAA Climate Prediction Center. US-government public-domain data, freely redistributable.

Columns used. SEAS (overlapping 3-month season), YR, TOTAL (raw SST °C), ANOM (the ONI anomaly °C; ≥+0.5 El Niño, ≤−0.5 La Niña). Full record parsed: 917 seasons, DJF 1950 – AMJ 2026. Episodes computed as the standard 5-or-more consecutive overlapping seasons past threshold.
