The Import Shelf

Walk through 16 years of US food imports — 155 ingredients, about $72B a year. Every food shows a real import-value trend, a 12-month seasonal fingerprint, and its top origin countries. Every figure is import value (nominal customs dollars), never volume.

Key findings

Counter-seasonal fill

114 / 155

items see an import peak in Dec–Mar — the shelf fills exactly when the local harvest is bare. Imports mirror the domestic calendar; they don't parallel it.

A two-country spine

82 & 70

Mexico is a source for 82 foods, Canada for 70. No third country tops 16. And 28 foods draw ≥90% from a single country (18 Mexico, 7 Canada).

Dollars vs. drama

flat at the top

The biggest-value imports (ground beef, avocado, shrimp, banana) run nearly flat year-round; the wild seasonality sits in low-value produce like cherries (amplitude 3.3).

Compounded exposure

corr +0.29

The most seasonal foods also tend to be the most origin-concentrated — two risks that co-occur in the record. Shown as association, never cause.

The record shows its gaps

82 blanks

82 of 155 foods carry no import-reliance figure and 2 no concentration class. Gaps are shown as gaps, never padded.

The fingerprint wall

Each glyph is one food's 12-month seasonal fingerprint — the area rises above the dashed 1.0 baseline in the months imports surge. Reorder by peak month and the winter wave sweeps across the grid. Select any glyph to open its walk-through.

Value × seasonality

Where money lives versus where seasonality lives. Horizontal: annual import value (log scale). Vertical: seasonal amplitude (peak index minus trough). Base-load staples sit low-right; spiky produce sits high. Every dot is keyboard-reachable.

Amplitude is the spread of the seasonal index (max − min), a descriptive shape measure, not a volatility forecast.

A few culinary names share one customs (HS) line and appear as byte-identical alias rows — salmon fillet / salmon skin-on fillet, shrimp shell-on / head-on / P&D, beef tenderloin / striploin, onion / red onion, red / russet potato, and lemon / lime. They are listed under both cook's names for findability, so the 155-food count and the ~$72B aggregate include these aliases; they are not 155 distinct HS lines. Where a paired figure differs (e.g. lemon's reliance vs lime's), the proxy is only trustworthy for one of the names.
