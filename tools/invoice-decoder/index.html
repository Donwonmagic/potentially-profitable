    What this is, and what it isn't.

    Your invoices stay on your device.

    Pick a photo, PDF, or CSV. The reader runs in your browser via WebAssembly. Image bytes never travel. Your network panel will stay empty until you press Save — and even then, what travels is encrypted ciphertext, not the contents.

    We don't sell, train, or pool your data.

    Saved invoices are encrypted with a key only you can derive from your passphrase. No benchmark dataset. No ML training set. No vendor pool. Not retroactively if a future opt-in arrives.

    Doesn't sync to QuickBooks or Restaurant365.

    Instead, exports a one-click CSV your accountant imports in the four most common formats: QuickBooks Online, QuickBooks Desktop, Xero, and ContPaqi/Aspel. Plus a generic ledger CSV for everything else. Your data never leaves your device.

    Open DevTools → Network. It stays empty.

    Until you press Save, no requests carry your image or your line items. After Save, exactly one POST goes out — and its body is base64-encoded ciphertext. Search the body for any of your dish names. You won't find them.

    Five other channels — each opt-in, each disclosed.

    Beyond image bytes and saved invoices, five surfaces exist that can move data off-page when you choose to use them. Every one is opt-in, none is required, and each gets its own verdict in the Privacy Self-Check.

    • Voice query (audio). The mic FAB sends audio to your platform's speech service (Apple on iOS / Safari, Google on Chrome / Android). Muntin servers never see audio. Don't tap the mic, don't speak.
    • Distributor portal bookmarklet. A piece of operator-installed JavaScript runs on your authenticated session at sysco.com / usfoods.com / gfs.com and hands rows back via URL hash. Data crosses origins inside your own browser; Muntin servers never see the portal payload.
    • PWA share-target intake. When you forward a file from the OS share sheet, the file arrives in this page's service-worker cache and is read same-origin. Same posture as a manual drag-and-drop.
    • OS clipboard writes. Reconciliation notes, order pads, and comparison notes write to your OS clipboard when you tap "Copy". Operator-initiated; the OS clipboard is outside Muntin's control.
    • Native share sheet. "Share this catch" opens the OS share sheet with a 1080×1350 PNG. SKU names are redacted by default. The receiving app — Messages, Slack, Mail — is your choice.

    Verify it yourself: open DevTools → Network 2-minute walk-through
    1. Open Chrome / Firefox / Safari DevTools. Right-click anywhere on this page → Inspect. (On iPhone or Android, plug into a desktop browser and use remote inspection — phone DevTools is too cramped to read.)
    2. Switch to the Network tab and check "Preserve log." That keeps the request list visible across page actions instead of clearing.
    3. Pick a photo, then read it. Watch the Network tab as the OCR runs. The only requests are the Tesseract WASM + language packs (cached after the first load) — and zero requests carrying your image bytes. The image lives in a <canvas> in this tab.
    4. Save to your Workshop. Now the Network tab shows ONE POST to /api/workbench/save. Click it → Payload. The body contains an envelope.ct field of base64 bytes — that's the AES-GCM ciphertext. Search the body for any of your dish names. You won't find them.

    If at any step you see something that looks like your invoice contents leaving the page, tell us — that would be a bug worth fixing immediately.
