Enter what you paid for an item on each invoice — with the date. Vendor Benchmark reads the wholesale market’s move over your exact window and sets it beside yours, so a price jump stops being a mystery: you see whether the market moved, or your vendor did. It runs on your device and keeps your prices there.

Check an item

Add two or more dated invoices for the same item. The more dates you give it, the sharper the read against the market — and it updates as you type.

How Vendor Benchmark reads it.

It puts a date on every price. Each purchase carries the invoice date, so the tool compares a real window — your second-week-of-May delivery against last week’s — not a vague “before and after.”

It reads the market over your window. For a matched item, it pulls the wholesale reference on your first date and your last date from the live Cost Index and measures the market’s move over the exact same span. Your move minus the market’s move is the part that’s on your vendor.

It stays honest about wholesale. The Cost Index is a wholesale reference; a delivered price runs higher, so the tool leads with the rate of change, never “you should pay $X.” When the market history behind an item is thin, it says so and withholds the verdict rather than accusing a vendor on noise.

It uses your own baseline, too. Alongside the market read, it flags a hike against the trailing median of what you’ve paid — the same rule Muntin Ledger uses, so the two never disagree.

It stays on your device. Your dated prices are kept in this browser, never sent anywhere. Peer comparison against other operators is a separate, opt-in feature inside Ledger — never built from what you type here.
