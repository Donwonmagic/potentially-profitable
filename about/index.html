Why a restaurant person

A studio built out of the dining room, not into it.

I'm Don. I manage two DMV restaurants — The Irish Inn at Glen Echo, and Tacombi in Bethesda, where I'm front-of-house manager five days a week and lead the area marketing, outreach, and local design work. Two very different rooms, same week. One is a 1931 dining landmark on the Potomac; the other is a fast-paced taquería that opens for brunch and closes after the bar crowd leaves. I split my time between them, which means I'm not guessing what restaurant owners need from a website. I'm living it.

I started Muntin Digital because I kept watching small business owners juggle four vendors who never talked to each other — a designer, a developer, a social person, and a hosting company — and nothing matched. The website said one thing, the Instagram said another, and whoever printed the menus hadn't been given the logo file. So I built a studio that does all of it from one set of hands: brand, custom site, social, and the day-to-day maintenance that keeps it from drifting.

The advantage isn't theoretical. I answer the phone during a rush and hear the three questions customers keep asking — so those three questions go on the homepage. I watch a four-top walk in because the website told them we had a patio and parking — so every restaurant I build leads with that. I've redesigned a menu at 4pm because we 86'd the branzino at lunch — so the sites I build are edited from a phone, not a ticket queue. The operator's view is the product.

I work in English and Spanish. A lot of the kitchens I've been in run in Spanish, and a lot of the owners and staff I want to build for do too — so the whole studio, not just a translated landing page, is available in both languages. Esta página en español →

— Don

Credentials

Certified for the parts that can't be winged.

Food safety, allergen handling, and alcohol service aren't design opinions — they're enforced standards. Holding the credentials means every site, menu, and campaign I build already respects what your jurisdiction requires.

  • SS

    ServSafe Food Protection Manager

    The manager-level certification for food safety systems — the standard most jurisdictions require on-site during service.

  • SA

    ServSafe Allergens

    Formal training on identifying, communicating, and preventing cross-contact for the nine major allergens.

  • FH

    ServSafe Food Handler

    The foundational food-handler certification: temperature control, hygiene, and cross-contamination discipline.

  • RAM

    RAM Alcohol Awareness

    Responsible Alcohol Management training — intoxication signs, ID verification, and statutory liability for servers.

  • MC

    MC ABS ALERT

    Montgomery County's Alcohol Beverage Services certification, required for alcohol service in the county.

Sourcing & corrections

Where the library’s claims come from, and how to flag one that’s wrong.

Operations claims (food cost ranges, prime cost bands, reply-time norms) are sourced from the National Restaurant Association annual reports, my ServSafe / RAM / MC ABS coursework, and 14 years of operating-floor experience listed in the timeline above. Web-engineering claims (page-speed thresholds, schema requirements, mobile usability rules) are sourced from Google Web.dev research, Lighthouse documentation, and the schema.org spec; all benchmarks are dated in the tool footers and reviewed quarterly.

Corrections are taken seriously. If you find a factual claim that’s wrong, an outdated benchmark, or a tool result that doesn’t match the citation, email don@muntin.digital with the URL and the specific line. I correct in-place with a dated note, not silently.
