How Lighthouse scores performance ↗

Field note from the kitchen

The Lighthouse score is one number. Your reservation funnel is six. I've watched a site with a 92 PSI score lose more bookings than a 67 — because the 92 site loaded fast and then stuck the reservation widget below three full screens of carousel. Speed buys you the right to ask for the booking. It doesn't make the asking land. Run the audit, fix what's red — but don't stop reading at the score.

Frequently asked

What is Core Web Vitals?

Core Web Vitals is Google's three performance scores for a real page load on a phone — one for loading speed (LCP), one for layout stability (CLS), and one for responsiveness (INP). You'll see them as colored bars in any audit report.

The 90-second explainer

Core Web Vitals, in 90 seconds.

The three speed scores Google grades your site on — and your guest feels. · 73s

0:00 / 1:13

Transcript

  1. Core Web Vitals are three measurements of how your page actually feels to a real visitor: how fast the main content loads, how quickly it reacts to a tap, and whether things jump around while it loads. Google measures them on real visits, not in a lab.
  2. They are a ranking signal and a conversion signal at once. A slow, jumpy page ranks lower and loses the hungry guest who bounces before your menu even appears. Speed is not vanity here — it is covers.
  3. In plain terms: LCP — did the big thing, your hero photo or menu, show up fast, under about two and a half seconds? INP — when they tapped “Reserve,” did it respond right away? CLS — did the button stay put, or shift as the page loaded so they mis-tapped?
  4. The fixes are unglamorous: compress the giant hero image, cut the heaviest scripts, and set sizes on images so nothing jumps. You rarely need a rebuild — you need a diet.
  5. Your visitor never reads a speed score. They feel it, and they leave if it drags. Build for the thumb on a phone in a parking lot.
