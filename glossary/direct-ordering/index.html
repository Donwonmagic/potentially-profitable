Direct ordering

Example: A Brooklyn pizzeria nudges guests toward ordering on its own site with a QR code on every table and free loyalty for direct orders, keeping the platform commission it would otherwise hand an aggregator on each pie.

orders through your own site

Restaurants
Operations & Margin

A customer ordering through your own website or POS (Toast, Square, Olo, ChowNow), bypassing aggregators. You pay only the card-processing fee — no platform commission.

Why it matters

The primary margin-recovery lever available to most restaurants. Every shifted order keeps the commission that would have gone to a platform. Two patterns work: aggressive website CTAs with obviously-better unit economics (e.g., free loyalty on direct), and in-restaurant nudges (QR codes on menus, signage near the door) pointing to the direct path.

The 90-second explainer

Direct ordering, in 90 seconds.

Taking the order yourself instead of renting the customer. · 74s

0:00 / 1:14

Transcript

  1. Direct ordering is the guest ordering through a channel you own — your site, your POS link, your app — instead of a marketplace. The same food, maybe even the same delivery, but the order, and the customer, come to you.
  2. A marketplace takes 15 to 30 percent of every order. Direct ordering costs you payment processing — a few percent — and that is it. On a $40 order, that gap is roughly ten dollars that stays in the house.
  3. The money is the small win. The big one: you get the customer. Their name, their email, their reorder — yours to bring back, instead of the aggregator’s to re-market to the place down the street.
  4. You do not have to drop the marketplaces. Use them to get found, then convert: a card in every bag, a better price on your own page, a reason to skip the app next time.
  5. The marketplace rents you an order. Direct ordering keeps the customer. Get found there; get them back here.
