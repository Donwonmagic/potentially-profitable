Prime cost

food cost + labor cost, as a % of sales

90s · explainerRestaurants
Operations & Margin

Your food cost plus your labor cost, expressed as a percentage of sales. Healthy independents land between 55–65%; above 70% is operationally unsustainable without a hard change.

Why it matters

Prime cost is the single best one-number pulse of a restaurant's operating health. Everything else (rent, insurance, SaaS, loan payments) is more or less fixed — prime is the biggest lever you can actually move week to week. The Prime Cost Check in Margin Math plots your two inputs against the healthy band.

Prime cost, in 90 seconds.

The one-number pulse of operational health. · 90s

0:00 / 1:30

Transcript

  1. Two restaurants on the same corner. Same revenue, $80,000 a month. One is growing. The other loses money every Monday. Why? Prime cost.
  2. Prime cost is just two numbers. Food cost plus labor cost, expressed as a percentage of sales. The whole formula fits on a napkin.
  3. There’s a band. Healthy independents land between fifty-five and sixty-five percent. Over seventy is not slow — it’s unsustainable. Every point over target is margin walking out the kitchen door.
  4. Restaurant A lives at 58 percent. Food at 30, labor at 28. Restaurant B is at 71. Food at 36, labor at 35. Same revenue. One loses every month, the other pays rent.
  5. Watch what happens when you act. Cut food cost three points — portion specs, waste logs, a supplier audit. Flatten Wednesday’s lunch shift. You drop to 65. Same covers. Margin back.
  6. Prime cost is the one weekly lever you can actually move. Know the number. Decide toward it.
