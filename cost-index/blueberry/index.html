What is the cheapest month to buy blueberries?

The cheapest month for blueberries is usually August, and the priciest is October — a clear seasonal window: about 57% cheaper at its August low than at its October peak, across the multi-year public history (USDA, BLS, FRED). That is calendar co-occurrence, not a forecast; read it against your own invoice.

Why it's moving

Upstream inputs right now: Diesel (down), which moves alongside food costs. Association, not cause.

Why it matters

Blueberries trades per carton as a wholesale reference. That is the market's figure, not the delivered price at your door — your invoice adds freight, the vendor's margin, and your volume. Always compare in the same unit.

The price moves with forces upstream of your kitchen — chiefly diesel. That is an association, not a direct cause, but when those climb, blueberries tends to follow. So a higher invoice is not always the vendor's doing: sometimes the whole market moved.

For category context: fresh fruits & vegetables as a whole moved -49.4% over the window (BLS PPI, fresh fruits & vegetables). That's the broad produce market's direction — not blueberries's own read, which is above.

What this means for your kitchen

Blueberries: a highly volatile line.
