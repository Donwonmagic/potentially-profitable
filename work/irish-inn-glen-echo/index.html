The Irish Inn at Glen Echo.
A landmark, managed from the inside.

A year and a half of in-house work at a 1931 Washington-area dining institution — menu design, logo development, seasonal campaigns, and a website redesign concept built from direct knowledge of the kitchen, the room, and the regulars.

Project overview

A 1931 landmark,
hiding behind a template.

The Irish Inn at Glen Echo has been a Washington-area dining institution for nearly a century — featured by the Washington Post, Bethesda Magazine, and Washingtonian, with a split pub/dining-room experience served off a single unified menu and a sourcing story built on Maryland and Virginia farms. The current website buries all of it.

The challenge

A landmark represented by a template

The existing site treats the Inn like any other restaurant — a hero photo, a PDF menu, and some hours. Nothing communicates the dual pub/dining-room experience, the sourcing philosophy, or the press coverage that brings regulars back for decades.

The approach

Rebuild from the story out

New information architecture surfacing the split Pub and Dining Room as two entry points to a single menu. Sourcing pillars get their own section. Press coverage moves above the fold as a quiet ticker. Built on vanilla HTML, CSS, and JS — no framework overhead, instant mobile load, easy for the team to maintain.

The outcome

A proposed replacement that matches the room

A site that looks and reads the way the Inn itself feels — historic, rooted, warm, and still lively. Sub-two-second mobile load, a menu system the team can update in 30 seconds, and an editorial-grade typography system built on Playfair Display and Lato.

Brand palette

Six colors.
All of them earned.

The palette leans on two deep greens — the color of a tavern booth and the light through a cut-glass bottle — offset by warm gold, cream backgrounds, and a quiet neutral type color. No bright accents, no loud gradients. The room does the talking.

Typography

Playfair Display
and Lato.

A serif with enough presence to carry a 1931 date and italics that still feel warm, paired with a neutral humanist sans-serif that disappears into the body copy. The editorial rhythm of a magazine — not a restaurant template.

Display — Playfair Display, Bold

Stewards of History.

Body — Lato, Regular

Like the great saloons of Washington D.C., The Irish Inn is more than a restaurant; it is a gathering place woven into the neighborhood. Since 1931, the Hughes family has kept the lights on and the doors open — a landmark that happens to serve dinner.

Information architecture

Two experiences.
One menu.

The Inn is really two rooms: the Pub, loud and local; and the Dining Room, historic and elegant. Both are served from the same kitchen, but the current site treats them as one blurry experience. The redesign leads with the split — and then collapses it back together at the menu.

Sourcing

Three pillars.
Not a marketing line.

The Inn actually buys from the farms and boats its menu names. The redesign gives that story its own section instead of hiding it in a footer — because for a 2026 diner, sourcing is a reason to book.

Farmers

Local Roots

Produce and dairy sourced from Maryland and Virginia farms, most of them within a two-hour drive. Seasonal specials move with what's actually coming in from the fields that week.

Ethically raised

Heritage Meats

Pasture-raised beef, lamb, and pork from small regional producers breeding heritage stock. No commodity feedlot meat on the menu, ever — and the menu says who raised it.

Responsible fisheries

Sustainable Seafood

Line-caught and responsibly sourced fish from fisheries with verifiable chain-of-custody. Seasonal menu rotation reflects what's actually in good shape to catch.

Press

Coverage the site
doesn't currently use.

The Irish Inn has been written up by every major Washington-area publication that covers food. The redesign puts that above the fold as a quiet ticker — no logos, no slider, just the names.
