The evidence behind the library

Every claim,
traced to its source.

When a Muntin article or tool cites a study, the study gets its own research note here — a plain-English summary of the finding, how it applies to a restaurant, and a link to the original. No "trust me, Google says so." Just the receipts, organized.

Research notes

  • Think with Google · 2017

    The 3-second mobile load rule.

    The majority of mobile visitors leave pages that take longer than 3 seconds to load. Google's benchmark study and the research that's reinforced it ever since.

    Cited in 2 articles
  • Nielsen Norman Group

    What people actually look for on a local business website.

    The most-searched-for information on small-business websites is hours and location — and those are the most commonly hidden items. NNG's usability research.

    Cited in 1 article
  • Nielsen Norman Group

    Fitts's Law — why button placement decides button use.

    The harder a button is to reach, the less often it gets pressed. A 1954 UX principle that explains most of what goes wrong with reservation CTAs.

    Cited in 1 article
  • Baymard Institute

    The 70% cart abandonment rate (and why it applies to reservations).

    Online checkout forms lose ~70% of shoppers before completion. Reservation widgets are checkout forms wearing a different hat — and they suffer the same dynamics.

    Cited in 1 article
  • Chrome developer docs

    How Google's Lighthouse actually scores performance.

    The formula behind every "your site scored 47/100" report. Which metrics Lighthouse weights, how the final number is calculated, and what the score really means for a restaurant site.

    Cited in 1 article

How Muntin research notes are written

Every entry here started as an external link inside a Muntin article or tool. Rather than send you straight to a 4,000-word PDF from 2017, the research note gives you the headline finding, the key numbers, and a restaurant-specific translation in about a minute of reading — and a single link if you want to read the original.

No study is misquoted. If Muntin's framing drifts from the source's language, that's noted explicitly in "Don's note." When a study is outdated, we say so.
