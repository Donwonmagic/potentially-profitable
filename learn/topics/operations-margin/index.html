Topic essay

The honest math of running an independent restaurant.

Operations and margin sit at the unglamorous center of restaurant work. Nobody opens a place because they love spreadsheet rows about prime cost. But the operators still in business after year five are the ones who run the math — usually monthly, sometimes weekly when food prices spike — and adjust before the slide becomes a crisis.

Three numbers do most of the work: prime cost (food + labor), contribution margin per item, and the percentage of your menu mix that lands in the high-margin quadrant. Get those three honest, look at them quarterly, and the rest of the operational drama gets quieter. Ignore them, and every supplier price hike becomes a surprise.

The path through this cluster:

  • Start here: How to Raise Menu Prices Without Losing Reservations — the price-raise playbook most operators delay 12 months too long.
  • Next: Five Website Changes That Recover 1% Margin — operations isn't only the kitchen; the website moves money too.
  • Advanced: Is DoorDash Worth It in 2026? — the third-party-platform decision is downstream of your prime cost.

Tools that pair: Margin Math (prime cost + price-raise simulation), Menu Engineering (the star/dog/puzzle/plowhorse classification), Plate Cost (per-item food cost with yield + waste). Save your scenarios; re-run when costs move ≥1.5%.

Sister topics: Conversions & Content (because better menu copy + photos lift the high-margin items) and Trust & Reviews (because the operators with the strongest review systems can hold higher prices longer).
