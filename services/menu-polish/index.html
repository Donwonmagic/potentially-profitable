Polish keeps the dishes. It tightens type, trims word count to what a guest reads in one glance, drops the trailing “.00”, and lets whitespace carry the page.

What you get back.

Press-ready PDF/X-3

ISO-coated CMYK, fonts embedded, bleed marks set. Hand it to any commercial printer in the country.

Web JPG + PNG

Color-managed and right-sized for the website, the GBP photo slot, and the email blast.

InDesign source

Yours to keep. If next round goes in-house, your nephew starts from the same place I did.

Typography refined

Type pair tightened, sizes balanced, leading set against a real grid. Section heads aligned to the eye, not just the math.

Layout balanced

Whitespace where the menu earns it; density where it can carry it. Descriptions trimmed (with your sign-off) so the page doesn’t fight itself.

EN + ES typography pass

Both columns get the same care — widow control, accent kerning, no tilde orphaned at the end of a line.

What a press-ready PDF/X-3 actually means.

What I accept as input.

Whatever shape your draft is in, I can work from it. Cleaner inputs mean fewer back-and-forth rounds — but I won’t bounce you for sending a Word doc.

Word / Google Doc Existing PDF Clear photo of a printed menu InDesign / Affinity source Canva, Figma, anywhere else

If what you send is too far from a starting point — a pile of dish names with no structure, a scan I can’t read — I’ll refund the $249 the same day and tell you why a $1,500 Menu Drop-In is the better fit.

You drafted a menu and it’s 80% there. You can feel the 20% but can’t name it.
