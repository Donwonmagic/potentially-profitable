The DMV math moved. Three deadlines, one June.

Three hard dates inside the next six weeks and one cost spike across all of them. The June wave: four pieces on what changed, what to do, and the order to do it in.

In short

  • Three DMV pressures stack in the next six weeks: DC's wage step on July 1 ($18.40 / $10.30 tipped), the streatery program's end, and food costs (beef ~$6.90/lb, FAFH +3.6%).
  • Four pieces, designed to be read in order: the wage deadline, the lost patio seats, the food re-cost, and the June calendar.
  • One muscle wins all four — margin math, a complete Google profile, and a clean reservation link.

Three pressures land on a DMV restaurant in the next six weeks, and they don’t politely take turns. July 1: DC’s minimum wage steps to $18.40 and the tipped base to $10.30. This spring already: the temporary streateries are gone, with many operators down a chunk of patio seats just as summer peaks. And underneath both: ground beef at a record near $6.90 a pound and food-away-from-home prices forecast up 3.6% across the year. None of them is the worst news on its own. Stacked, they ask one question — what do you do about it before they all show up in the August prime cost?

This wave is four pieces, meant to be read in order, that walk that question. None of them ask for an ad budget; all of them pay you in margin.

One thesis under all four

Read together, the four point at a single muscle: the operator who knows their numbers and runs them on the same tools wins all four. The margin math that prices the July 1 wage step is the same math that prices the food-cost re-cost. The Google Business Profile that catches the diner who lost a streatery seat is the same profile that catches the Father’s Day booking. A clean reservation link is the single most-used asset across three of the four pieces.

Run the GBP grader and the margin math once, this week, and three of the four moves are already set up. The fourth — re-costing the plates that moved — needs a recipe-cost card per plate, no more.

Three deadlines. One June. Four pieces, in order — ready when you are.

Key takeaways

  • The DC wage step is the hardest deadline; price for it before July 1, not after.
  • Streatery losses move covers indoors and online — the Google profile and the reservation link are the catch.
  • Don't raise the whole menu for the food spike; re-cost the three to five plates that actually moved.
  • June rewards the findable, bookable restaurant — not the cheapest one.
