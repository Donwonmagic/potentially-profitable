The Restaurant Cost Index — July 2026 edition. The panel reads hold.

One honesty paragraph before the numbers, because it changes how you read every one of them. Each ingredient's percentage here is its read against its own tracked baseline window — a dated snapshot, not a month average. The month arcs below are dated committed reads from a single named source series, windowed from 2026-06-08. The basket's data anchor has not refreshed since the prior edition (2026-06-18; still anchored 2026-05-01), so there is no new basket move to report — rather than invent one, I say so. The per-ingredient month arcs below are current and dated. And every figure is a public wholesale level, never your delivered price: this is a read on the cost index, not a line for your food cost sheet. The point is direction and gap, not a number to paste into a cost sheet.

The biggest individual pulls

The basket is a weighted blend of 16 staples, and its headline is a weighted median — so no list of parts adds up to it. What the parts DO show is who tugged hardest. Two views: the spread and basket rings, then the loudest individual pulls.

The lead story is hold

Every edition of this dispatch leads with the ingredients that cleared the panel's own false-discovery gate — the moving-block bootstrap null that separates a real move from what an item's ordinary week-to-week noise produces on its own. This month, across all 81 panel members, none did. That is not a gap in the coverage; that is the read.

The loudest raw gap on the board is Green beans, reading +125.4% against its own tracked window — about $40.75/carton wholesale (range $36.50–$44.75) — a gap that item's own history produces by chance, which is exactly what the gate exists to catch. Behind it sits Romaine lettuce at −55.9%, same verdict. The board below carries each item's dated month arc, so you can see the shape of the wander for yourself.

The licensed action is hold. Hold your prices, and know why: prices wandered this month, and none of the wander is distinguishable from ordinary volatility. If a vendor quotes a jump “because the market moved,” this panel says the market did not clear its own noise — that is a conversation worth having with the invoice on the table.

The record

This dispatch grades itself in public, misses first. Direction calls land 53.6% against a 50.2% majority baseline over 1859 calls; only the strongest signal tier beats the coin flip (58.3% on 873 calls), and the weakest runs 47.8% — below the baseline, which is why the arrows on this page never lean on that tier.

And first prints revise. The public revision ledger holds 1255 revisions and 825 withdrawals; the median revision moves a published read 14%, and one in ten moves it more than 53%. Every dollar figure in this edition is a first print unless dated otherwise — the market's read today, not a settled fact.
