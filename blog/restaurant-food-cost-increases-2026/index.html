  5. Re-cost the plates that moved

How-to · June 1, 2026 · 6 min read · By Don Goldstein

Beef hit $6.90 a pound. Re-cost the five plates that moved.

Ground beef is at a record near $6.90 a pound and food-away-from-home prices are forecast up 3.6% this year. The move isn't to raise the whole menu — it's to find the few plates that actually moved and re-cost those.

In short

  • Ground beef hit a record near $6.90/lb (BLS, April 2026) and the USDA forecasts food-away-from-home prices up 3.6% for 2026.
  • Don't raise the whole menu — re-cost the three to five plates whose inputs actually moved, and pick the lever per plate.
  • Price into a cautious diner: the NRA found 85% pulling back on spending, but 61% still call dining out essential.

Ground beef crossed a record this spring — about $6.90 a pound in April — and the USDA expects food-away-from-home prices up 3.6% across 2026. Coffee, oils, and eggs have moved too. The reflex is to raise the whole menu. Don’t. The whole menu didn’t move; three to five plates did. Re-cost those.

Source: BLS (beef); USDA ERS (food-away-from-home)

U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — average price, ground beef (series APU0000703112): ~$6.90/lb, April 2026, at/near a record. USDA ERS — Food Price Outlook: food-away-from-home prices forecast +3.6% for 2026.

Beef and the FAFH forecast are confirmed to the primary series; the moves in coffee, oils, and eggs are real but the exact year-over-year figures vary by index, so they’re named directionally here, not quantified.

A menu-wide raise is the expensive shortcut. It punishes the items that are still fine, it reads as greed to a diner who is already watching the check, and it doesn’t actually fix the plates bleeding margin — it hides them in an average.

Re-cost the five that moved

Price into a cautious diner

The timing is the hard part, because the 2026 diner is watching the check. The National Restaurant Association found 85% of consumers pulling back on spending this year — and, at the same time, 61% saying dining out is essential to how they live. Both are true. They will still come; they will also notice a clumsy raise. So move the price where the value is obvious — the signature plate, the one with the best photo and description — and protect the average check rather than chasing it.

Source: NRA 2026 State of the Industry

National Restaurant Association — 2026 State of the Restaurant Industry (released Feb 2026): ~85% of consumers pulling back on spending; ~61% say restaurants are essential to their lifestyle; industry sales ~$1.55T.

Figures are the NRA’s reported survey numbers; treat consumer-sentiment framing as “cautious,” not a hard record.

The other input that moved

If you’re in DC, labor moved too — the July 1 wage step stacks on top of food cost. Re-cost and re-price once, for both, rather than twice. Track it going forward with cost pulse so the next spike shows up in a week, not a quarter, and the full pricing playbook is in raising prices without losing reservations.

Re-cost the five that moved. Hold the rest. That is the whole move.

Key takeaways

  • A menu-wide raise punishes the items that are fine and hides the real problem in an average.
  • Re-cost the moved plates with a recipe-cost card; the plate cost shows the new contribution margin.
  • Move the price where the value is obvious — the signature plate — and protect the average check.
  • In DC, the July 1 wage step stacks on food cost; re-cost and re-price once for both.
