  5. The May 2026 wave

Library Letter · May 10, 2026 · 5 min read · By Don Goldstein

Nine pieces. One operating thesis.

Search results aren’t ten blue links anymore. They’re a paragraph Google wrote, citing two or three sources — and for restaurant queries that paragraph now appears above the map pack. The nine pieces below are the operator’s response to that shift, in the order to read them.

In short

  • Week 1 of a new editorial cadence — nine pieces shipped together as a Sunday-night-through-Monday-morning batch, English and Spanish, anchored to publicly-verifiable platform docs, vendor pricing, and search-industry analyses.
  • Through-line: search results aren’t ten blue links anymore — they’re a paragraph Google wrote, citing two or three sources. For restaurant queries that paragraph now appears above the map pack, and if you’re not one of the cited sources, you’re not in the conversation.
  • Read the AI Overview citation piece first; the other eight ladder from it in this order: schema example, GMaps invisibility, review playbook, Instagram-as-SEO, three-platform delivery math, the 30-day case study, loyalty programs, service charges vs tipping.

This is the first weekly batch under a new editorial cadence. Each batch ships Sunday night through Monday morning, with this overview as the entry point. Future batches will hook to whatever’s moving in the restaurant industry that week — a Google product update, a delivery-platform policy change, a wage-law phase-in. This week, the batch is bigger than usual because it lays down the foundation the future ones build on: AI Overview citation is the new discovery surface, and almost no independent restaurant writes for it yet.

The through-line

Two things are happening at the same time, and they compound. One: Google’s search-result page is a paragraph the model wrote, citing two or three sources. As of March 2025 it triggered on 13.14% of US desktop searches; by spring 2026 it’s approaching one in five, and restaurant queries trigger it more often than the average. Two: the map pack still sits beneath that paragraph, and it’s still where most local-intent traffic clicks. So you’re competing on two surfaces at once — one new and shaped by paragraph quality, one old and shaped by Google Business Profile health.

Source: Search Engine Land, March 2025 AI Overview share

Search Engine Land — “Google AI Overviews are now showing for 13.14% of US queries, up from 6.49% in January” (March 31, 2025). Analysis of US desktop search results across a broad query basket. Most-cited measurement of AI Overview prevalence; the post-March-2025 trajectory is reported qualitatively across industry coverage rather than from a single consolidated follow-up measurement.

Five of the nine pieces address those two surfaces directly. The remaining four address the operating-economics decisions that determine what your restaurant actually has to say on those surfaces — what your menu costs to put in front of a guest, who handles the discovery, what compensation model your kitchen runs on.

The reading order

Each piece stands alone, but they were written to ladder. The order below is the order they reward most.

What the claims in these pieces are anchored to

Every operating-floor number across the nine articles is anchored to a publicly verifiable source — platform-published commission tiers, vendor pricing pages, search-industry analyses, and Google’s own help documentation — or to an illustrative range that’s identified as such in the piece itself. Specific anchors include:

  • Platform commission and fee structures. DoorDash Marketplace tiers (Basic 15% / Plus 25% / Premier 30%), Uber Eats and Grubhub published rates, payment-processing rates — all from the platforms’ own merchant documentation. Powers the DoorDash margin walk, the three-platform comparison, the 30-day playbook.
  • AI Overview share of US search. The March 2025 Search Engine Land analysis (13.14% of US desktop queries) is the most-cited measurement; subsequent direction is qualitative. Powers the AI Overview citation article’s framing.
  • Loyalty + email vendor pricing. Toast Rewards, Square Loyalty, Fivestars/Como, Mailchimp, Klaviyo monthly costs are taken from each vendor’s current public pricing page. Powers the loyalty comparison.
  • DMV Google Business Profile audit, 2023–2026. Diagnostics on a sample of independent DMV restaurants — the audit Muntin uses to scope client work. Informs the four-cause distribution discussed in the Google Maps invisibility piece.

What’s coming next

Future weekly batches will mostly be smaller than this one — one or two pieces, an overview, and the issue that batch is tied to. The cadence is roughly Sunday night to Monday morning, with batches keyed to a Google update, a delivery-platform policy change, a wage-law inflection, or whatever’s genuinely moving in the industry that week. The May 2026 wave is large because it’s the foundation. The June 7 batch will be small because, with luck, June 7 will look like a normal Sunday.

The single operating principle behind all nine pieces: ranking and being cited are not the same job. The first independent restaurant in a given market to write for the citation, not just the rank, owns the answer box for the next twelve months. Most operators haven’t made that shift yet. The window for being the first is open right now.

Key takeaways

  • Nine pieces, three operating themes: search citation (4 pieces), social discovery (1), delivery economics and operations (4).
  • Operating-floor claims across the wave are anchored to publicly-verifiable sources: platform commission tiers from each platform’s merchant docs, vendor pricing from each vendor’s pricing page, AI Overview share from the March 2025 Search Engine Land analysis.
  • Future batches will mostly be smaller and hook to current events (Google updates, delivery-platform policy changes, wage-law inflections) rather than calendar.
  • All ten pieces ship audio in six languages — English, Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese, Mandarin.
  • Single operating principle: ranking and being cited are not the same job — the first restaurant in a market to write for the citation owns the answer box for the next twelve months.
