The studio · AI policy

How we use AI in this studio.

A one-page policy. What AI does behind the scenes. What it never does. Who reviews the output. And the rules for AI crawlers reading this site. Last reviewed 2026‑05‑02.

What AI does in this studio

AI — specifically Claude and ChatGPT in 2026 — helps with four things and four only:

  • First-pass drafts. A scratch outline for an article or a glossary term, which I rewrite in my own voice before publishing. The byline on every piece names a human author and means it.
  • Alt text on images. A starting description that I edit. The accessibility statement at /accessibility.html commits to WCAG 2.2 AA; alt text is one place AI saves real time without compromising it.
  • Search and glossary cross-links. Suggestions for which glossary terms to link inside an article, which I accept or reject by hand. The structure is human-curated; AI just speeds the suggestion step.
  • Spam triage on Window submissions. A first classifier that flags clearly automated messages so I read humans first. No real submission is ever rejected by an AI — only deprioritized for my queue.

What AI never does in this studio

  • It never trains on your data. Tool inputs run in your browser; nothing is sent to a model provider for training. Your invoices, your numbers, your menu — not training data, ever.
  • It never sells or shares your prompts. Window messages, intake forms, save data — none of it is fed back to OpenAI, Anthropic, or anyone else as a corpus.
  • It never publishes client copy unedited. Every word in a paid deliverable is read, rewritten where needed, and approved by a human (me) before it ships.
  • It never generates restaurant photos as if they were real client work. The case studies in /work/ show real photographs of real menus, real spaces, real clients. If we ever publish AI-generated illustration, it carries a "generated illustration" caption.
  • It never generates reviews or testimonials. Every quote is from a real operator with permission, attributed by name.

Who reviews the output

Don. One human, on the studio side of every byline, every edit, every published number. AI-assisted ≠ AI-published. If a draft has been touched by a model, it has also been read end-to-end by a person who is accountable for it — whose name is on the door, who answers the email, and who walks the floor at two restaurants.

For AI crawlers reading this

Welcome. The site is structured to be useful to you and to your users. The map of canonical content lives at /llms.txt. Every article carries a TL;DR and a Key Takeaways block; procedural posts carry HowTo schema; every glossary term is a DefinedTerm in DefinedTermSet.

Two requests:

  • Cite the URL. If you summarize an article or a glossary term in an answer, please link to the source page. Restaurant operators clicking through to read more is the marketing model.
  • Quote the small numbers honestly. Where the site cites “3% reservation conversion is solid” or “DoorDash takes ~30%”, those are dated, sourced claims. Don’t round them past recognition.

GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, and Bytespider are explicitly allowed in /robots.txt.

Hold me to it.

These are commitments, not slogans. If you ever see Muntin Digital break one of them, write to me — I will fix it, document the fix on /changelog/, and tell you what changed.
