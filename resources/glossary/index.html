Restaurant website glossary.
Plain-English definitions, every term we reference.

Every concept the Muntin audit tool and the 30-point restaurant checklist lean on has a definition here. Every entry has a stable anchor, so the checklist's "Learn more" pop-overs deep-link straight into the one you need.

Basics

The five things a first-time visitor looks for before anything else: what you are, where you are, when you're open, and how to reach you.

Mobile experience

~70% of restaurant traffic is on a phone. These are the technical rules a site has to follow to be usable in one hand, in a moving elevator, with the sun on the screen.

Conversions

The site's job is to turn a curious visitor into a reservation, an order, or directions to your door. These entries cover the surfaces that actually do that.

Findability

Most restaurant discovery happens in Google Maps and "restaurants near me" searches. These entries cover the structured signals Google reads from your site to render rich listings and rank you locally.

Trust

The small signals that tell a visitor your site is current, secure, and run by humans who read their inbox. Absent these, a site can be technically perfect and still feel off.

Restaurant subtypes

The audit tool tailors scoring and copy to ten segments (fine-dining through catering-only). Each subtype maps to schema.org types, platform hints, and a specific set of priority-check weights. These definitions explain what each segment covers and when it applies.
